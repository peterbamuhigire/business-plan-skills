# CLTD Logistics, Inventory, and Transportation Extraction

Transformed guidance from CLTD 2022 Modules 2, 6, and 8 for business planning. Use this when a company buys, stores, moves, distributes, exports, imports, or delivers physical goods.

## Planning Lens

Logistics design is the operating bridge between market promises and financial reality. A credible plan must show how customer service levels are achieved through a workable mix of inventory, warehouse, transportation, suppliers, systems, and risk controls. The cheapest network is not always the best network; the right design is the lowest total-cost way to meet the chosen service promise.

## Module 2: Logistics Network Design

- Map the current or proposed network before discussing growth: suppliers, ports, borders, factories, warehouses, cross-docks, branches, customers, carriers, trade lanes, and information flows.
- Test each service promise against the total cost of transportation, warehousing, safety stock, handling, order frequency, delivery timing, and administration.
- Explain why the network exists in its selected form: customer geography, freight volume, supplier location, product mix, infrastructure, trade routes, regulation, and expansion timing.
- Revisit the network when customer expectations, product lines, supplier base, freight volume, trade policy, demographics, or service standards change.
- Include reverse logistics where returns, repairs, recalls, reusable packaging, recycling, or disposal affect cost or customer trust.
- Treat international movement as an operating system, not an afterthought: Incoterms, customs brokers, duties, documentation, inspections, and risk transfer shape cash flow and accountability.
- Include an implementation sequence for network changes: which facility, supplier, system, route, carrier, or control changes happen first and how progress is measured.

## Module 6: Inventory Management

- Inventory is a service-level and working-capital decision. It protects the customer promise but ties up cash, space, handling labour, insurance, and management attention.
- Separate cycle stock, safety stock, work in process, finished goods, packaging, spares, returns, and postponement inventory where they behave differently.
- Explain the inventory review method. Perpetual review fits high-value or fast-moving SKUs; periodic review may fit low-value stable items if counts and controls are reliable.
- Use ABC analysis to focus management attention on high-value, high-risk, high-volume, or service-critical SKUs.
- Show how lead time, demand variation, transportation delay, supplier reliability, MOQ, duties, and route risk affect reorder points and safety stock.
- Track stockouts, backorders, excess stock, slow stock, expiry, shrinkage, and in-transit inventory as operational facts, not just finance symptoms.
- Connect inventory policy to the forecast and cash flow forecast. A sales plan that requires stock the business cannot finance, store, move, or replenish is not operationally credible.

## Module 8: Transportation

- Transportation is often the largest logistics cost, so the plan must identify the transport model: own fleet, hired carriers, courier/parcel, freight forwarder, 3PL, 4PL, lead logistics provider, or hybrid.
- Mode, carrier, route, schedule, consolidation, load factor, and delivery frequency should be selected against the service promise and margin model.
- For owned fleets, include vehicle capacity, maintenance, driver availability, utilisation, backhaul potential, fuel, insurance, licensing, compliance, and dispatch control.
- For outsourced transport, include carrier selection, rate basis, service-level agreement, claims process, track-and-trace capability, exception handling, and fallback carriers.
- Document core transport documents and controls where relevant: freight bills, bills of lading, waybills, packing lists, commercial invoices, certificates, customs declarations, and proof of delivery.
- Build exception workflows for delay, damage, failed delivery, refused goods, short shipment, over shipment, wrong item, lost shipment, and expediting.
- Track transportation performance using both cost and service metrics: on-time pickup, on-time delivery, OTIF, cost per order, cost per unit, cost per km, load utilisation, route adherence, freight claims, damage rate, and carrier scorecard.

## Business Plan Applications

### Operations Plan

Use the logistics network to explain how the business will fulfil orders, replenish stock, manage facilities, control quality, handle returns, and maintain service during growth. For distribution-heavy businesses, the operations plan should include both a physical flow and an information flow.

### Financial Plan

Translate the logistics design into working capital, transport cost, storage cost, handling labour, packaging cost, loss/damage provision, insurance, duties, broker fees, system cost, and capital expenditure. Inventory and freight assumptions must reconcile with revenue timing and gross margin.

### Risk Plan

Include risks from supplier failure, port or border delay, route disruption, carrier failure, fleet downtime, stockouts, excess stock, regulatory documentation errors, hazardous materials handling, customs disputes, and reverse-logistics obligations.

### Growth Plan

Show what changes at 2x, 5x, and 10x volume: more frequent replenishment, extra warehouse space, slotting changes, carrier renegotiation, cross-docking, regional stocking points, route redesign, WMS/TMS adoption, or outsourced logistics.

## Minimum Metrics for Physical-Goods Businesses

- Fill rate and stockout rate
- Backorder rate and average backorder age
- Inventory days and inventory turnover
- Safety stock coverage by critical SKU
- Slow-moving and obsolete stock value
- Dock-to-stock time and order fulfilment cycle time
- Pick accuracy and inventory accuracy
- On-time pickup, on-time delivery, and OTIF
- Transportation cost per order, unit, km, or tonne-km
- Load utilisation and route adherence
- Freight claims, damage rate, and return rate
- Carrier or fleet scorecard

## Planning Red Flags

- Revenue growth assumes faster delivery but the plan has no warehouse, route, carrier, or inventory change.
- Inventory is forecast as one line item with no policy for safety stock, reorder points, expiry, spares, packaging, or slow stock.
- Transport cost is assumed as a flat percentage without mode, distance, frequency, load, or carrier logic.
- International trade is planned without Incoterms, customs broker, duties, documentation, clearance lead time, or risk-transfer assumptions.
- Own-fleet plans ignore maintenance downtime, utilisation, backhaul, driver availability, compliance, or replacement capital.
- Outsourced logistics plans ignore service levels, claims, tracking, escalation, and carrier concentration risk.
