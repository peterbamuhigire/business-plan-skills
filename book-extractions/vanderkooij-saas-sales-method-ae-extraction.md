# Book Extraction: Jacco van der Kooij & Winning By Design — The SaaS Sales Method for Account Executives

**Source:** van der Kooij, Jacco et al. *The SaaS Sales Method for Account Executives: How to Win Customers* (Winning By Design, 2018). Part of the Winning By Design (WBD) blueprint series.

**Why this matters:** This is the most disciplined operating manual for designing a SaaS sales motion, and the canonical source for sizing a sales organisation (quota, ramp, attainment, OTE, hiring profile). For any SaaS business plan whose ARR projection >$1M, the sales-team design must reconcile with the financial model. WBD's frameworks (transactional / solution / consultative / provocative; 9-step winning process; impact proposal; trade-not-give negotiation) are the de-facto standard in modern B2B SaaS GTM design.

---

## 1. The Four B2B SaaS Sales Methodologies

WBD's central framing: there is no universal SaaS sales motion. The right methodology depends on (a) customer's prior knowledge of their problem, and (b) ACV / deal size. The four:

| Methodology | Customer State | ACV Range | Cycle Length | Deals/Month/AE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DIY self-service** | Customer knows everything; web buys | <$500 | Minutes | Hundreds via web |
| **Transactional** | Customer knows what they want, price-shopping | <$1k | <30 days | 20+ |
| **Solution** | Customer knows their problem, comparing 2–3 vendors | $5k | ~30 days | 5–10 |
| **Consultative** | Customer doesn't fully understand the problem | $20–100k | 6–18 months | 1–3/quarter |
| **Provocative** | Customer doesn't know they have a problem yet | $250k+ | 6–9 months | 1–2/quarter |

**Plan implication:** Section 07 (Marketing/Sales) must declare which methodology the business will use. The methodology determines: marketing channel mix, AE profile, quota, sales-cycle length, win-rate assumptions, headcount needed for any ARR target. Plans that mix methodologies (e.g. "we'll sell $50k ACV in 30 days") are fundamentally broken.

## 2. The Simplified Sales Process (4 macro-stages)

1. **Educating** — help market understand the problem, impact, and possible solutions (content, thought-leadership, events).
2. **Prospecting** — reach out to fit customers with pain; set up discovery calls.
3. **Winning** — diagnose, prescribe, propose, trade, commit. (9 micro-steps in the WBD winning process.)
4. **Growing** — onboard, drive adoption, expand, renew.

**Plan implication:** Section 07 must show the full pipeline (Education + Prospecting feeding the funnel, Winning processing it, Growing producing NRR). Most plans only model Winning; they miss Education (the brand/content moat) and Growing (the NRR engine).

## 3. The 9-Step Winning Process (the AE blueprint)

1. **Identify** — find similar accounts based on the just-won customer (market segmentation, regional segmentation, product segmentation, impact segmentation, personal network)
2. **Diagnose** — surface pain, critical event, impact on business
3. **Prescribe** — demo, workshop, proof of concept
4. **Assist** — help decision criteria, trial, competitive positioning
5. **Recommend** — quote, impact proposal, business case
6. **Pause / Go Dark** — set alerts, keep educating (FOMO), nurture
7. **Trade** — negotiate, handle price objections, "Crazy Ivan" (deliberate pause to test commitment), accelerated close
8. **Commit** — P/O, MSA, SOW
9. **Not a Fit** — debrief, confirm loss, learn

**Plan implication:** Section 07 sales playbook must define artefacts for each step. Section 09 (Management Team) job descriptions for AEs and SDRs must map to which steps they own.

## 4. Identify — Working Backwards from Won Customers

After every COMMIT, the AE runs the Identify exercise: build a target list of next-prospects with **relevance** to the just-won customer:
- Other companies in same market segment
- Other companies in same geography
- Other companies with same problem (across segments)
- Other companies seeking same impact
- Personal network of the winning buyer (CIO knows other CIOs)

**Plan implication:** the engine should treat customer-acquisition as a relevance chain, not a cold-list spray. Section 07 must show the relevance-based identification logic explicitly.

## 5. Diagnose — Pain, Critical Event, Impact

Three diagnostic questions answer the qualifying logic:
- **Pain**: what specifically is hurting?
- **Critical Event**: what's the deadline / forcing function?
- **Impact**: what does this cost in $$$, time, risk, opportunity?

No critical event = no deal urgency. Plans must train AEs to surface or co-create the critical event.

## 6. Impact Proposal (replaces the price-list quote)

The Impact Proposal structure:
1. The customer's pain (in their language)
2. The critical event and timeline
3. The quantified impact of inaction (cost of doing nothing)
4. The proposed solution (specific to their pain)
5. The quantified impact of action (ROI)
6. The investment (price as a fraction of the impact)
7. The commercial terms (3 options usually: base, recommended, premium)

This is far stronger than a feature/price quote. It positions the deal as a business decision rather than a vendor selection.

## 7. The Trade-not-Give Negotiation Discipline

Every concession trades for value. The seven trade currencies:
1. **Term length** — multi-year contract for price concession
2. **Prepayment** — annual / multi-annual upfront for discount
3. **Logo / case study consent** — discount for the right to publicise
4. **Reference call** — discount for being a reference customer
5. **Co-marketing** — joint case study, joint webinar
6. **Expansion scope** — adding seats / modules to extend deal value
7. **Speed** — close-this-quarter incentive for time-bound concession

Never give without getting. Predictable discounting is poison (Cotton's earlier rule).

## 8. The "Crazy Ivan" — Deliberate Pause

WBD's controversial technique: late in the cycle, deliberately pause / "go dark" to test commitment. If the buyer pings you concerned, the deal is real. If they don't, the deal was not as advanced as you thought. Use sparingly; useful for late-stage qualification.

## 9. Pause / Go Dark — the Productive Wait

Not every cycle closes on first attempt. WBD's Pause framework:
- Set alerts (CRM triggers if buyer revisits website, opens email, etc.)
- Keep educating (drip content, FOMO emails about competitor wins)
- Nurture until critical event re-emerges
- Don't beg — wait, then re-engage with new insight

## 10. Sales Capacity Modelling — the Quota / Ramp / Attainment Logic

For Section 10 (Financial Projections) to reconcile with Section 07 (Sales Strategy), the sales-capacity formula must be explicit:

**Bookings = #AEs × Quota × Attainment % × (1 − Ramp Discount)**

Where:
- **Quota** is annualised new-ARR target per AE
- **Attainment** is % of reps making quota (60–70% healthy)
- **Ramp Discount** is reduced productivity in the first 3–9 months (typically 50% in months 1–3, 75% in 4–6, 100% by month 9 for enterprise; faster for transactional)

Industry benchmarks (Mersch + WBD):
- SMB AE quota: $500k–$1M ARR / year, ramp 3–6 months
- Mid-market AE: $1M–$1.5M ARR / year, ramp 6 months
- Enterprise AE: $1M–$2M ARR / year, ramp 9–12 months

**Plan implication:** the engine's `saas-sales-capacity-and-ramp-model` must produce this calculation for every projected year.

## 11. Sales Org Structure by Stage

- **0–$1M ARR**: founder-led sales; 1–2 sales-assisted founders
- **$1M–$3M ARR**: 2–4 AEs + 1 SDR; founder still in big deals
- **$3M–$10M ARR**: 5–15 AEs, dedicated SDRs, first sales manager, beginnings of CS
- **$10M–$30M ARR**: pods (AE + SDR + CSM), 2–3 sales managers, RevOps function
- **$30M–$100M ARR**: regions, verticals, segmentation by ICP

Each stage has different hiring profile, comp plan, management ratio.

## 12. SDR / BDR Model — Pipeline Generation Factory

Sales Development Reps (inbound qualification) and Business Development Reps (outbound prospecting) feed AEs. Ratios:
- Outbound-heavy: 1 BDR per AE
- Inbound-heavy: 1 SDR per 2–3 AEs
- Hybrid: 1 per 1.5

Each SDR/BDR generates 8–15 qualified opportunities per month. Cost: $50–80k base + $20–30k variable in mature markets; $15–30k loaded in African markets.

## 13. Customer Success — the NRR Engine

CS is not support. CS is revenue-driving. Headcount ratio:
- High-touch (>$50k ACV): 1 CSM per 20–30 customers
- Mid-touch ($10–50k): 1 CSM per 50–100 customers
- Tech-touch (<$10k): 1 CSM per 200–500 customers + automation

CSM ownership: onboarding, adoption, expansion, renewal, advocacy.

## 14. Hardening Rules for the Business-Plan Engine

- Section 07 must declare sales methodology (transactional / solution / consultative / provocative).
- Sales methodology must reconcile with ACV and deal-cycle length.
- Section 07 must show the 4-stage pipeline (Educate / Prospect / Win / Grow).
- Section 09 / 10 must show sales capacity = #AEs × quota × attainment × ramp.
- Section 09 must show org evolution by ARR milestone.
- SDR / BDR / AE / CSM ratios must be explicit.
- Discounting must use trade-not-give discipline (Section 07).
- Impact Proposal structure (not just price quote) for material deals.

## 15. Uganda / East Africa / Africa Application Notes

- Provocative selling is harder in African enterprise markets because of risk-aversion in incumbents and slow corporate decision-making. Consultative is the dominant method for $20k+ ACV.
- Sales cycles in African enterprise SaaS run 50–100% longer than US benchmarks (12–24 months for $50k+ deals) because of procurement bureaucracy, multi-stakeholder consensus, and budget-cycle dependence on government grants / donor cycles.
- AE compensation: total OTE in African markets typically $15–30k for SMB, $25–50k for mid-market, $40–80k for enterprise (compared with US $80k / $150k / $300k). Quota scales accordingly.
- SDR cost-of-acquisition is significantly lower in Africa, making outbound prospecting more viable than inbound-content economics. Many successful African SaaS run outbound-first with content as supporting (the inverse of US SaaS).
- CS in Africa often doubles as field-implementation because customers expect onsite onboarding for $20k+ deals. Build "high-touch CSM" as default and migrate to tech-touch as product matures.
- WhatsApp Business is a core SaaS sales channel in Africa — Impact Proposal delivered via WhatsApp PDF; meetings booked via WhatsApp; CRM integration to WhatsApp essential.
- The Critical Event in African enterprise sales is often: (a) regulatory deadline (URA EFRIS, KRA eTIMS), (b) funding-cycle approval, (c) executive change. Train AEs to map these.
