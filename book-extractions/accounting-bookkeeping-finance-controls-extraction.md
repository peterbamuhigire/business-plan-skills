# Accounting, Bookkeeping, ERP Finance, and Financial Modelling Extraction

Transformed guidance from management accounting, bookkeeping, financial accounting, ERP finance configuration, and Excel finance modelling sources. Use this in business plans where financial credibility, controls, systems, or management accounting materially affect viability.

## Business Planning Lens

Financial projections are stronger when they are grounded in how the business will keep records, control money, recognise revenue/costs, reconcile balances, and review performance. A bankable plan should not only show projected statements; it should show the financial operating system that will produce trustworthy results after launch.

## Bookkeeping and Financial Accounting

- Use the accounting equation as the integrity anchor: assets equal liabilities plus equity.
- Show whether the business will operate cash basis, accrual basis, or a staged transition. For any business with invoices, credit sales, supplier bills, payroll, inventory, taxes, loans, or fixed assets, accrual accounting is the credible target.
- Identify the basic books and schedules: sales, purchases, cashbook, payroll, inventory, fixed assets, tax, receivables, payables, and general ledger.
- Explain how source documents will be retained: invoices, receipts, bills, payment confirmations, delivery notes, payroll records, bank statements, tax returns, and contracts.
- Separate profit from cash. Sales on credit, stock purchases, loan repayments, tax timing, capex, and supplier terms all affect cash differently from accounting profit.
- Include reconciliation discipline: bank, mobile money, receivables, payables, inventory, tax, payroll, and fixed assets.

## ERP Finance and Controls

- For software-enabled or ERP-reliant businesses, describe the finance module as more than invoice entry. It should support chart of accounts, subledgers, posting rules, fiscal periods, audit trail, approvals, reversals, period close, and management reporting.
- Use control accounts for AR, AP, inventory, tax, payroll, fixed assets, and bank/cash where relevant.
- Avoid direct posting to control accounts except through controlled adjustments with reason and approval.
- Record company/branch/cost centre/profit centre/project/location dimensions where management needs performance visibility.
- Use maker-checker approvals for high-risk actions: payments, write-offs, stock adjustments, manual journals, payroll approval, supplier bank changes, and closed-period changes.
- Period close should be a repeatable checklist, not a memory exercise.

## Management and Cost Accounting

- Separate cost types: fixed vs variable, direct vs indirect, controllable vs non-controllable, product vs period, one-time vs recurring.
- Use contribution margin for break-even, pricing, capacity, and product/service mix decisions.
- Use cost centres and profit centres when departments, branches, clinics, campuses, production lines, or projects need accountability.
- For manufacturing, processing, construction, services, and clinics, show how direct labour, materials, overhead, wastage, rework, and capacity are costed.
- For repeatable products or production, use standard cost and variance logic: price/rate variance, usage/efficiency variance, volume variance, yield/mix variance, and overhead variance where applicable.
- Use flexible budgets for monthly control so actual spend is compared to the cost expected at actual activity level.

## Excel and Analytics Expectations

- Financial workbooks should separate Inputs, Data, Calculations, Checks, Reports, and Dashboard.
- Include checks for trial balance, balance sheet, cash roll-forward, AR/AP control, inventory control, fixed assets, and formula errors.
- Use driver-based models: customers, volume, price, utilisation, COGS rate, staff, inventory days, receivable days, payable days, capex, and tax timing.
- Use scenarios and sensitivity analysis for the few assumptions that move funding need, DSCR, cash runway, or profitability.
- Use variance dashboards to compare actual vs budget by month, YTD, and rolling forecast.

## Plan Sections That Should Reflect This

### Operations Plan

State how transactions are captured at source: POS, invoices, purchase orders, stock movements, payroll, patient billing, fee billing, production orders, delivery notes, and payments.

### Financial Projections

Make working capital, stock, receivables, payables, tax, depreciation, loan repayment, capex, and closing cash explicit. Add accounting-control assumptions where the reader must trust the numbers.

### Risk Analysis

Include risks from weak bookkeeping, delayed reconciliations, cash leakage, tax non-compliance, inventory misstatement, debtor collection failure, supplier payment errors, payroll errors, fraud, and spreadsheet model errors.

### Implementation Plan

Add the finance operating cadence: daily cashup, weekly bank/mobile money reconciliation, monthly AR/AP review, monthly stock count or cycle count, monthly management accounts, quarterly tax review, and annual close.

## Red Flags

- The plan shows strong profit but no cash-flow timing.
- Inventory-heavy businesses have no inventory valuation or stock-count process.
- Credit sales are projected but receivable days and bad debts are ignored.
- Supplier credit is assumed but payable terms and payment discipline are not shown.
- Payroll is included as a cost but statutory deductions and approval workflow are absent.
- ERP/software is mentioned but no chart of accounts, posting rules, audit trail, or period close capability is described.
- Excel models lack checks, assumptions, and reconciliation to the statements.
