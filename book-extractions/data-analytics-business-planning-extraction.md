# Data Analytics for Business Planning Extraction

Sources:

- *Mathematical Foundations of Big Data Analytics*
- *AI-Based Data Analytics: Applications for Business Management*
- *The Data Analytics Advantage*
- *Data Analytics using Python*
- *Introduction to Data Analytics*

Use this extraction to improve business plans where market sizing, forecasts, surveys,
financial projections, dashboards, KPIs, AI analytics, or management information systems
affect bankability.

## Business Planning Analytics Ladder

| Analytics type | Business-plan use | Evidence standard |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | market size, historical sales, customer counts, costs, KPI baselines | sourced data, clear definitions, clean tables |
| Diagnostic | reasons for variance, market gaps, operational bottlenecks, customer churn | segmentation, driver analysis, comparisons |
| Predictive | demand, revenue, cash flow, stockouts, churn, loan repayment risk | historical depth, assumptions, backtesting where possible |
| Prescriptive | scenario choice, pricing, inventory levels, staffing, expansion timing | constraints, sensitivity analysis, trade-offs |

Do not make predictive claims from purely descriptive data. State when numbers are
estimates and show the basis.

## Data Quality Requirements

Before using figures in a plan:

- Confirm source, date, geography, period, and unit.
- Check completeness, validity, consistency, timeliness, and relevance.
- Build a data dictionary for any primary dataset.
- Preserve assumptions and transformations.
- Flag missing, outdated, or weak data rather than hiding it.

## Market Analysis

Use analytics to strengthen:

- TAM/SAM/SOM calculations.
- Customer segment sizing.
- Growth-rate calculations.
- Market trend visualization.
- Competitor benchmarking.
- Demand drivers and constraints.

Best practice:

- Use bottom-up sizing where possible.
- Triangulate top-down, bottom-up, and expert/benchmark logic.
- Use CAGR/geometric mean for multi-year growth.
- Add confidence intervals to primary survey results.
- Show uncertainty when market data is thin.

## Financial Projections

Analytics strengthens financial modelling through:

- Driver-based revenue assumptions.
- Sensitivity and scenario analysis.
- Regression or time-series forecasting where historical data exists.
- Variance and coefficient-of-variation checks for unstable revenues or costs.
- Working-capital analytics for receivables, payables, inventory, and cash conversion.
- Dashboard metrics: runway, DSCR, gross margin, CAC, LTV, stockout risk, and budget vs
  actual variance.

Financial analytics should always distinguish forecast, target, and plan.

## Demand and Operations

Use data analytics for:

- Sales rate by branch, channel, SKU, customer segment, or period.
- Stockout and overstock risk.
- Lead-time and reorder-point calculation.
- Capacity utilization.
- Defect, scrap, rework, and yield analysis.
- Delivery performance and cycle time.

Forecasts should include the time horizon, method, history used, exclusions, and error
metric where backtesting is possible.

## AI and Business Management

AI analytics can be proposed when it has a clear management use:

- predictive maintenance;
- customer segmentation;
- churn or retention risk;
- credit or receivables risk;
- anomaly detection;
- social/media sentiment;
- demand forecasting;
- automated narrative reporting.

Controls required:

- Explainability.
- Human oversight.
- Privacy and authorization.
- Model monitoring.
- Data-quality checks.
- Avoidance of unsupported causal claims.

## KPI and Dashboard Standard

Every KPI in a plan should define:

- formula;
- source;
- owner;
- frequency;
- baseline;
- target;
- threshold;
- action if outside threshold.

Dashboards should support a decision, not just display numbers.
