# Book Extraction: Jacco van der Kooij & Winning By Design — The SaaS Sales Method Fundamentals

**Source:** van der Kooij, Jacco et al. *The SaaS Sales Method Fundamentals* (Winning By Design, 2018). The foundation volume in WBD's blueprint series.

**Why this matters:** Where the AE volume codifies the sales motion, the Fundamentals volume codifies the **communication tradecraft** that determines whether the motion works — the customer-centric communication discipline (T-A-L-K-E-R framework), questioning technique (open/closed × situational/pain), the six forms of interaction, and the rhythm by which interactions string into sequences into sales plays into meetings. This is the operating discipline that prevents African SaaS sales hiring from being a hopeful exercise — it gives the founder a teachable, replicable, hiring-able sales-craft standard.

---

## 1. The Customer-Centric Foundation

Three foundations before any sales conversation:
1. **Customer-centric methodology** — put yourself in their shoes; every conversation framed by their context.
2. **Understand your customer** — role, persona, industry, use-cases, customer stories.
3. **Be a customer-centric professional** — show up on time, ready, and properly set up (LinkedIn profile, environment, materials).

**Plan implication:** Section 07 and Section 09 must specify how sales hires are onboarded into this customer-centric discipline. Most African SaaS sales teams default to product-pitching rather than customer-questioning; this is a leakage point.

## 2. The Six Forms of Interaction

WBD's interaction taxonomy (each requires different skill):
1. **Phone** — voice as primary instrument
2. **Email** — written, asynchronous, structured
3. **Online meeting** (Zoom / Teams / Google Meet) — visual + verbal
4. **In-person meeting** — full sensory presence
5. **Text / messaging** (SMS, WhatsApp, Slack) — short, real-time
6. **Social** (LinkedIn, Twitter) — public, indirect

Each interaction has a place in the customer journey. A sales plan that assumes "we'll do calls and demos" misses 70% of the touchpoint surface.

## 3. The T-A-L-K-E-R Communication Framework

Customer-centric communication keys:
- **T**one of voice
- **A**sk questions
- **L**isten to what your client says
- **K**eep notes (include customer's tone words verbatim)
- **E**laborate (dig deeper with intent to understand)
- **R**epeat back (confirm understanding before prescribing)

The "ABC of customer-centric communication": **Always Be Curious** (Jacco's reframe of Glengarry's "Always Be Closing").

## 4. Question Architecture — Speed and Quality

**Speed** is controlled by the open/closed dimension:
- **Closed-ended** (yes/no, single fact) — "How many people on your team?" → "6"
- **Open-ended** (requires elaboration) — "Walk me through how you handle X today"

**Quality** is controlled by the situational/pain dimension:
- **Situational** — facts about their current setup
- **Pain** — questions that surface friction, cost, frustration, risk

Skilled AEs shift gears: open situational → open pain → closed pain (commitment) → closed situational (clarifying).

**Plan implication:** sales-hire onboarding scripts and discovery-call templates should be explicitly designed with these four gears. The engine's reference files should include sample question banks.

## 5. The Doctor Metaphor

WBD's central pedagogy: a great salesperson is like an off-duty doctor seeing a stranger limp. You don't open with "let me sell you a cast" — you ask what happened. You diagnose before prescribing. Diagnosing without prescribing is the discipline that earns trust.

**The fastest way to earn trust is to provide valuable insight by asking the right kind of questions.** — Dan Smith / WBD

## 6. Interaction Sequences and Sales Plays

Interactions don't stand alone. They string into **sequences** (e.g. cold-prospecting sequence = LinkedIn view → InMail → email → call → second email → WhatsApp / SMS / second call → break → re-engagement). Sequences combine into **sales plays** (e.g. "ABM play for enterprise CTO" = personalised content asset + executive briefing offer + multi-channel orchestration). Plays produce **meetings**.

**Plan implication:** Section 07 must specify the named sequences and plays the business will use, with conversion benchmarks at each step. Plans saying "we'll do outreach" without the play architecture lack operational reality.

## 7. The Customer Engagement Funnel

WBD's pipeline view:
- **Top of funnel** — visit (educate, awareness)
- **Middle** — engage (interact, qualify)
- **Bottom** — propose / win / commit

Three corresponding interaction modes:
- **Visiting** customers online — knowing where they live (LinkedIn, sector forums, communities)
- **Listening** to customers online — what they discuss, complain about, ask about
- **Engaging** with customers online — joining the conversation, providing value, building relevance

## 8. Real-Time Messaging Discipline

Messaging (Slack, WhatsApp, SMS) is now a primary B2B sales channel. Rules:
- Short, specific
- Time-of-day sensitive (don't WhatsApp a CIO at 11pm)
- Permission-based after first phone or email contact
- Use messaging for status, scheduling, micro-confirmations — not for first-touch pitches

**Plan implication:** Section 07 must specify WhatsApp Business / messaging policy as a sales channel, particularly in African markets where WhatsApp is the dominant B2B channel.

## 9. Online Meeting Discipline

WBD's online-meeting protocol:
- **Schedule** (clear agenda, duration, attendees, outcome)
- **Invite** (calendar invite with agenda)
- **Prepare** (research, materials, customer-specific deck)
- **Confirm** 24 hours before
- **Open** with relevance ("Last time we discussed X — today we're going to cover Y")
- **Run** with explicit air-time discipline (60% customer talking, 40% rep talking in discovery; reversed in demo)
- **Close** with explicit next-action commitment
- **Follow up** within 24 hours with summary + agreed next steps

## 10. Tab-Based Presenting (in lieu of slide-decks)

For demos and product walks, WBD recommends "tab-based presenting" — open the product in browser tabs, walk through the workflow live, contextualised to the customer's named use-case. This dramatically outperforms slide-deck demos because it shows the product solving the customer's specific problem in real time.

## 11. The Listening Skills (the under-trained discipline)

Three levels of listening:
- **Level 1**: hearing the words
- **Level 2**: hearing the words plus the feeling behind them (tone, hesitation)
- **Level 3**: hearing what's not being said (the unspoken concern, the political reality)

Great AEs operate at Level 2 and 3. Most AEs operate at Level 1.

**Plan implication:** sales onboarding curriculum (Section 09) should include listening-skill development, not just product training.

## 12. Hardening Rules for the Business-Plan Engine

- Section 07 must list named sequences and sales plays, not generic "outreach."
- Sales hires must be onboarded into the T-A-L-K-E-R framework.
- Sales-script library must include question banks by gear (open/closed × situational/pain).
- WhatsApp / messaging policy must be explicit (especially Africa).
- Demo discipline (tab-based presenting) preferred over deck-demos.
- Online-meeting protocol (schedule → confirm → open → run → close → follow up) standard.
- Cycle-conversion benchmarks at each play step must appear in Section 10's sales-funnel model.

## 13. Uganda / East Africa / Africa Application Notes

- WhatsApp Business is the dominant B2B sales channel in African markets. A SaaS sales plan that doesn't include WhatsApp at every funnel stage is operating at half-strength.
- Voice (phone) calling remains powerful in African markets because email-fatigue is lower and decision-makers often prefer voice for material decisions. Most US SaaS playbooks under-weight phone.
- LinkedIn engagement works for tier-1 corporate African markets (Nigeria, South Africa, Kenya, Egypt) but is sparse in tier-2 / SME / public-sector. WhatsApp + sector-specific Facebook groups + Telegram fill the gap.
- In-person meetings remain disproportionately important in African B2B sales for material deals. Plans should budget for travel within country and across the EAC / ECOWAS / SADC regions.
- Listening-Level-3 (the unspoken political reality) is critical in African enterprise sales where deal sponsorship often depends on inter-personal politics, family / ethnic networks, and donor-cycle dynamics. Sales-hire onboarding should include explicit training on local political-economy reading.
- Language: a multi-language sales conversation skill (English + local) is a moat. Plans should specify which languages the sales team must cover.
