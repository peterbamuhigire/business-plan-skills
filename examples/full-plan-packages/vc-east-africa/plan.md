# LakeLink Growth Round — VC exemplar

> Fictional worked example. No market size, traction, multiple or valuation is asserted as fact.

## 1. Decision and executive case

LakeLink hypothetically seeks equity to scale a repeatable B2B monitoring service across selected
East African corridors. The decision is whether customer evidence, retention, unit economics,
product defensibility and capital-efficient expansion justify progression to real investor review.

## 2. Customer problem and product

Distributors need timely incident alerts and a usable operating record. The product combines
devices, connectivity, workflow and reporting. A real pack demonstrates the problem through
customer records and shows which product behaviour—not generic AI language—changes the outcome.

## 3. Traction and cohort evidence

The exemplar assumes pilot customers but treats all growth and retention values as illustrative.
Required evidence is customer-level recurring revenue, activation, logo and revenue retention,
contraction, expansion, churn reasons and collection quality by cohort.

## 4. Reachable market method

Market sizing is bottom-up: eligible customer type × serviceable sites × evidenced price, with
adoption constraints and overlap removed. A top-down regional total may contextualise but cannot
drive the investment case without a dated source and warrant.

## 5. Business model and unit economics

The overlay separates hardware deployment, subscription margin, support, connectivity, acquisition
and replacement. CAC payback and LTV are decision aids only when definitions, cohort horizon and
retention evidence are explicit. Cash and revenue timing remain separate.

## 6. Go-to-market and expansion

Expansion follows verified customer segments and partner economics, not country count. Each market
requires a sales cycle, implementation capacity, payment route, support coverage and sector-gate
screen. The plan stops expansion where one of those controls is unresolved.

## 7. Product moat and technical risk

Defensibility is tested through workflow integration, operating data quality, switching cost and
delivery learning. Commodity devices, generic dashboards and unverified model claims are not a
moat. Data rights, reliability and security have owners and measurable acceptance tests.

## 8. Team and governance

The case needs evidence of selling, deployment, finance and product ownership. The round adds
finance control, security and customer-success capacity only when volume triggers require it.
Governance covers reserved matters, reporting, conflicts and option-pool assumptions.

## 9. Financial plan, round and dilution

Uses fund product reliability, evidence-backed acquisition, deployment capacity and runway. The
model includes base, slower-sales and retention-downside cases. Valuation is a range to be supported
by current evidence; dilution and follow-on needs are shown, not hidden by a single headline value.

## 10. Risks and next gate

Controlling risks are weak retention evidence, hardware working capital, concentration, regional
execution, security and an overstated moat. Progress requires formula audit, cohort evidence,
sector screens, a reconciled capital plan and a committee result that preserves dissent.
