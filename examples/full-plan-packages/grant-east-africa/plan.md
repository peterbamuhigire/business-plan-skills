# LakeLink Cooperative Pilot — grant exemplar

> Fictional worked example. Figures are planning assumptions, not evidence of need or impact.

## 1. Decision and executive case

The hypothetical request funds a 12-month pilot testing whether alerting and response protocols can
reduce avoidable cold-chain incidents among participating cooperatives. The decision is eligibility
and learning value, not commercial debt capacity or venture-scale growth.

## 2. Need and beneficiary definition

The pack defines beneficiaries as participating cooperative operators and their member suppliers.
A real submission needs a dated baseline, selection method, disaggregation, consent and evidence
that the proposed constraint is material. Regional statistics cannot substitute for that baseline.

## 3. Intervention and theory of change

Inputs fund devices, onboarding, response training and monitoring. Activities produce installed
sites, trained operators and incident-response records. The intended outcome is fewer unaddressed
temperature excursions. Attribution is tested through baseline/endline comparison and documented
confounders; impact is not promised in advance.

## 4. Delivery model and partners

Cooperatives nominate sites; LakeLink installs and trains; an independent MEL role checks data
quality. Partner agreements define asset custody, support, safeguarding, data use and exit. No
partner commitment is inferred without a signed record.

## 5. MEL and learning

Indicators include installation acceptance, alert acknowledgement, response completion, device
uptime and defined incident outcomes. Each needs definition, denominator, source, frequency, owner
and data-quality check. Missing baseline or inaccessible logs remain `not assessed`.

## 6. Safeguarding, data and sector gates

The pilot screens ICT/data, product/import, environmental/e-waste and donor-control gates. It
minimises personal data, defines incident escalation and prohibits unassessed work involving
vulnerable people. Competent review is required where local country rules differ.

## 7. Budget and restricted-fund controls

The model overlay separates allowable direct cost, approved indirect cost, co-financing and
contingency. Restricted cash, procurement, asset custody and expenditure evidence reconcile.
Illustrative amounts do not establish donor allowability.

## 8. Sustainability and exit

Continuation is conditional on measured operating value, cooperative willingness to pay, support
capacity and an affordable replacement route. The grant does not assume permanent subsidy or
claim that a pilot proves scale.

## 9. Risks and adaptive decisions

Risks include weak baseline data, selection bias, low alert response, device loss, partner delay and
unsupported causal claims. Pre-agreed decisions include redesign, smaller cohorts, additional
training or stop. Changes retain approval and budget traces.

## 10. Implementation and next gate

The sequence is eligibility, partner diligence, baseline, sector gates, procurement, installation,
training, monitored operation, midline decision, endline and close-out. Submission requires the
evidence manifest, reconciled budget, MEL definitions, safeguards and committee simulation.
