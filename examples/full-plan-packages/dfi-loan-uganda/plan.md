# LakeLink Cold-Chain Systems — DFI debt exemplar

> Fictional worked example. All figures are labelled assumptions; no external statistic is asserted.

## 1. Decision and executive case

LakeLink seeks a hypothetical five-year asset-backed facility to deploy temperature-monitoring
equipment and fund installation working capital for contracted business customers in Uganda. The
decision is whether recurring service cash flow, asset deployment controls and downside coverage
justify progression to human DFI credit review. Approval is not claimed.

## 2. Company, problem and solution

LakeLink installs sensors, provides alerts and maintains an audit trail for distributors handling
temperature-sensitive goods. The customer problem is spoilage and weak incident evidence. The
case depends on signed contracts and measured loss reduction; neither is inferred from sector size.

## 3. Customer and market evidence

The exemplar assumes three contracted distributors and a qualified pipeline. A real pack must attach
contracts, site counts, renewal terms, interview records and a bottom-up reachable-customer method.
National market statistics alone cannot prove willingness to pay.

## 4. Business and revenue model

Revenue comprises installation and monthly monitoring fees. The model separates billing, cash
collection, service delivery and accounting recognition. Price, device life, replacement rate,
connectivity cost, credit loss and churn are editable assumptions with owners.

## 5. Delivery and management

Deployment is gated by customer site survey, equipment acceptance, installation sign-off and live
alert testing. The operations lead owns installation capacity; finance owns receivables and asset
register reconciliation; the managing director owns contracts and covenant reporting.

## 6. Regulatory and environmental/social gates

ICT/data, imported-equipment/product, customs and site/environment screens apply. Each remains
`not assessed` until the competent route is confirmed. The plan does not describe LakeLink as
licensed or environmentally cleared. Customer data, e-waste, worker safety and grievance handling
enter the DFI action plan before first drawdown.

## 7. Financial case and debt service

The overlay uses illustrative price, volume, cost and debt terms. Formula-map, scenario and balance
checks are mandatory. Credit review tests repayment from operating cash, not terminal value. The
downside delays deployment, increases collection days and replacement cost, and tests covenant
headroom. Any uncovered cash deficit defers the case until funded.

## 8. Funding request and use

Illustrative uses are devices, installation equipment, deployment working capital, monitoring
systems and a controlled contingency. Each disbursement needs supplier evidence, customer site
readiness and a procurement check. General corporate use is excluded from this exemplar.

## 9. Risks, conditions and recovery

Key risks are contract concentration, delayed sites, currency exposure on imported devices,
connectivity failure, weak collections and unresolved regulatory perimeter. Recovery options are
staged drawdown, customer deposits, alternative suppliers, smaller deployment cohorts and covenant
reforecasting. No mitigation is treated as effective without an owner and trigger.

## 10. Implementation, evidence and next gate

The sequence is diligence, formula audit, sector screens, environmental/social action plan,
contract verification, procurement, pilot cohort, acceptance, then scaled drawdown. The next gate
is a real DFI review only after every blocker in `evidence-manifest.json` is resolved and the model
overlay is reconciled to signed terms.
