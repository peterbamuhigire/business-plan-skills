### 3.2 Mineral fact base

**Industrial and non-metallic minerals**
- Soda ash: major global producer; source Lake Magadi; used in chemicals and glass; significant national export contributor.
- Titanium (ilmenite, rutile, zircon): largest and most successful recent project (Base Titanium, 2011-2024, Kwale Mineral Sands; wider deposits in the Kilifi-Kwale basins). Initial investment USD 350M; peak employment 1,600 (99% Kenyan; 70%+ from Kwale County); 10-year output 5M tonnes worth about USD 2B; first shipment February 2014 via Likoni Port. Legacy infrastructure: 16 km 132 kV power line, 8.6 million m3 dam, 8 km access road, port facility, training centre.
- Limestone: widely distributed; feeds cement and construction materials.
- Fluorspar: western Kenya; major mine suspended in recent years, revival potential; uses in steel, chemicals and refrigerants.
- Other industrial minerals: diatomite, gypsum, salt, silica sand, talc, dolomite, vermiculite, baryte, bentonite, graphite.
- Graphite: Chawia (Taita-Taveta), Samburu, Kitui, Turkana, Kamstoi, Tsavo; disseminated flakes in meta-sedimentary gneisses (Mozambique Belt, Nyanzian, Kavirondian rocks); viable open-pit; crystalline graphite for batteries and lubricants; 2023 airborne survey recommended ground-truthing in Turkana and Taita-Taveta; limited processing capacity and investment.

**Metallic minerals**
- Gold, Archean-hosted: Migori, Kakamega, Vihiga, Siaya (Nyanzian Craton), analogous to Canada's Abitibi and Tanzania's Lake Victoria goldfields. Migori Greenstone Belt about 245 km2 with over 1.2 million ounces (JORC-compliant, as stated; verify), drilling of 8-12 g/t at some targets, feasibility in preparation. Shanta Gold West Kenya Project: 14.05 Mt at 1.56 g/t (705,000 oz), advanced exploration and feasibility reports available.
- Gold, orogenic-hosted: Turkana, Marsabit, Isiolo, West Pokot (Mozambique Mobile Belt); ASGM at Lochoremoit, Namoruputh, Lokiriama, Ng'akoriyiek, Naduat; recent discoveries in Marsabit, Isiolo, West Pokot.
- Niobium, Mrima Hills (Kwale): Cretaceous alkaline carbonatite. JORC resource estimate July 2013. At 1.0% Nb2O5 cut-off: measured plus indicated 5.8 Mt at 1.41% (181 million lb); inferred 17.5 Mt at 1.41% (547 million lb). At 0.2% cut-off: measured plus indicated 47.8 Mt at 0.66% (699 million lb); inferred 94.4 Mt at 0.73% (1,525 million lb). Strategic value stated as KES 5.4-8.18 trillion (about USD 40-60B at 2025 rates; an indicative figure, not a valuation). Associated minerals: apatite, pyrochlore, barite, florencite, monazite, rutile, sphalerite, galena, goyazite, hematite, magnetite, anatase, ilmenite, pandaite. Other prospects: Homa Mountain (carbonatites, fluorite, light REEs), Rangwa (Homa Bay; niobium and REE), Sukulu (Eastern Rift; phosphate and niobium), unexplored Buru (Kericho), Ruri, Homa, Kuge (Homa Bay), Tharaka Nithi.
- REEs, Mrima Hills (July 2013 JORC estimate): at 5.8% TREO cut-off, measured plus indicated 11.2 Mt at 6.93% TREO (0.78% CREO); inferred 15.6 Mt at 7.12% TREO (1.11% CREO). At 1.0% TREO: measured plus indicated 48.7 Mt at 4.4% TREO (0.91% CREO); inferred 110.7 Mt at 3.61% TREO (0.8% CREO). Same indicative value range as niobium (do not add the two).
- Copper: early-stage; Meru, Embu, Tharaka Nithi, Kitui, Kajiado, Samburu, Turkana, Isiolo; rift-related intrusions and metamorphic belts; surface grades to about 8%; artisanal activity suggests potential; NAMICO Special Prospecting Licence aiming at mining-lease conversion.
- Iron ore:

  Some deposits grade 62-64% Fe, comparable to international standards.
- Others: manganese, chromite, lead, silver, zinc, cobalt occurrences. Nickel-chromite emerging: 15 counties flagged by the 2023 airborne survey for ground-truthing (West Pokot, Samburu, Marsabit, Kitui, Turkana, Taita-Taveta), with nickel typically alongside copper and cobalt in mafic-ultramafic rocks, small chromite bodies in ultramafic zones, battery and renewable applications.

**Gemstones**
- Coloured stones: amethyst, aquamarine, cordierite, garnet (including tsavorite), ruby, sapphire, spinel, tourmaline, rhodolite; most exported before cutting and polishing.
- Tsavorite (high-value niche): Taita-Taveta, Samburu, Kajiado. Gems Africa and Moyo Gems Market Days give artisanal miners direct access to regional and international buyers; AWEIK supports processing, branding, marketing and export readiness.

**Fossil fuels and radioactive or strategic minerals**
- Crude petroleum discovered; production fluctuates; exploration continuing. Lignite: known deposits (including Mui Basin).
- Monazite, uranium, thorium: Tharaka-Nithi, Turkana. Lithium-beryllium: Tharaka-Nithi, Marsabit, Samburu, Tana River. Tin: Tharaka-Nithi, Wajir.

### 3.3 Geology and data availability (reduces exploration risk)
- Kenya lies on the East African Rift System, where the Somali and Nubian plates are pulling apart; rifting has been active for about 30 million years. Volcanism and faulting produce alkaline igneous rocks and carbonatites (REE and niobium sources); Mozambique Mobile Belt metamorphic rocks (600-500 million years old) concentrate base and precious metals; Archean greenstone belts host western Kenya gold. Active rifting and weathering expose deposits; proximity to Mombasa and Lamu ports helps viability.
- Airborne data: 1977 Terra Survey and 1982 Geosurvey International surveys; the 2018-2023 Kenya National Airborne Geophysics Survey (KNAGS), interpretable at 1:20,000. Specification: 80 m nominal height, 200 m line spacing, 2,000 m tie-line spacing, 260 km/h, sampling magnetics 25 Hz, radiometrics 2 Hz, GPS 10 Hz, figure of merit 0.4-1.2 nT. It identified 970 mineral anomalies and added oil, gas and coal (Mui Basin) prospects.
- Available data products: geological maps (catalogue at https://eprocedures.investkenya.go.ke/), drill logs and reports, borehole completion and groundwater reports, termly exploration and mining company reports, memoirs and bulletins.

## 5. Licensing and Regulatory Pathway

### 5.1 Licence types
- Large scale: reconnaissance licence (early exploration), prospecting licence (detailed exploration), retention licence (holding claims during feasibility and financing), mining licence (extraction, duration up to 25 years, renewable subject to performance and compliance).
- Small scale: prospecting permits and mining permits.
- Artisanal: artisanal mining permits. Formalisation pathway: artisanal permit, then prospecting permit, then mining permit.

### 5.2 Application dossier for a mining licence
1. **Identity and tax compliance:** registration certificate not more than 1 year old (incorporated company); or list of partners (business name); or list of members (community-based organisation); or list of elected officials with current membership roll (cooperative). A valid tax compliance certificate is mandatory.
2. **Technical capacity:** CVs of key personnel; equipment owned; equipment to be hired or leased; operational track record.
3. **Financial capacity:** audited financial report (preferred), or 6-month bank statement, or bank guarantee letter, or pre-arranged credit facility.
4. **Operating and environmental plans:** detailed mining programme with activities and budgets; feasibility study by a competent expert; mine closure plan with costing; environmental licence obtained separately from NEMA.
5. **Employment and procurement:** proposal for employing and training Kenyan citizens by cadre with annual budget; proposal for local goods and services procurement with annual budget.
6. **Land and community:** landowner consent in the prescribed form (private or community land); for community land a Community Development Agreement (CDA) covering socially responsible investment, local employment and skills, and revenue sharing.
7. **Term requested:** not exceeding 25 years.

Processing timeline and fees are not stated in the source: obtain the current schedule from KenInvest or the Mining Cadastre Office.

### 5.3 Phased entry path (planning timeline; verify)

| Phase | Indicative duration | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Regulatory readiness | 0-3 months | Obtain geological data and historical reports (KenInvest and geophysical archives); engage a licensed tax agent and mining-experienced counsel; register the company (eCitizen, KRA PIN, bank account); preliminary EIA scoping with NEMA |
| 2. Prospecting application | 3-6 months | Technical feasibility assessment by a qualified engineer; financial capability proof; landowner or community consent; reconnaissance licence or prospecting permit application through the Mining Cadastre Office |
| 3. Exploration and feasibility | 6-18 months | Airborne surveys (use KNAGS data, supplement as needed); drilling and JORC-compliant resource estimation; environmental and social baseline; preliminary feasibility study |
| 4. Mining licence application | 18-24 months | Complete the dossier in 5.2; detailed feasibility study; NEMA EIA approval; CDA finalised; application to the Mining Cadastre Office |
| 5. Finance and development | 24+ months | Debt and equity (DFI interest: IFC, AfDB, government support); power, water and access roads; local supply-chain engagement; workforce recruitment and training |

## 6. Fiscal Terms and Incentives (verify every rate against current law)

### 6.1 Royalties
Prescribed by the Mining Act, 2016; calculated on gross value of sales at mineral-specific rates. Distribution as stated in the source: 70% national government, 20% county government, 10% local community. Planning range used in the source: 0-5% of gross sales, mineral-dependent.

### 6.2 General mining incentives
- Capital allowances: 100% deduction over 4 years on machinery, buildings and equipment, covering exploration and development-phase expenditure; accelerated depreciation.
- Investment Deduction Allowance: up to 150% on qualifying capital expenditure, higher for projects in underdeveloped areas.
- Withholding tax: reduced rates on interest to foreign lenders (aligned with double taxation treaties) and on specialised service fees.
- Loss carry-forward: up to 10 years.
- Import duty exemption on specialised mining equipment; VAT exemption on inputs for exploration and development.

### 6.3 EPZ and SEZ regimes (export-oriented mining companies may qualify)

| Tax element | EPZ regime | SEZ regime | Standard Kenya corporate |
|---|---|---|---|
| VAT (16%) | Exempt on raw materials, machinery, inputs; supply to zone zero-rated | Same | Standard 16%; no mining exemptions |
| Import duty | None on basic raw materials; VAT on machinery | None on basic raw materials and machinery | Standard duty by HS code |
| Corporate income tax | Exempt first 10 years from first sale; 25% next 10 years; 30% after | 10% first 10 years; 15% next 10 years; 30% after | 30% |
| Withholding tax | 10-year holiday on payments to non-residents; then interest 15%, dividend 10%, management fees 20%, rent immovable 30%, rent movable 15%, royalties 20% | No holiday; interest 5%, dividend 15%, management fees 5%, rent immovable 10%, rent movable 10%, royalties 5% | Interest about 15%, dividend 15%, management fees 20%, royalties 20% |
| Investment deduction | 50% year 1 plus 25% residual per year (max 100% on buildings and machinery) | 50% year 1 plus 25% residual | Standard 50% plus 25% residual |
| Import declaration fee | 3.5% normally; exempt for EPZ | Exempt | 3.5% |
| Stamp duty | Normally 1-4%; exempt | Exempt | Standard |
| Railway Development Levy | 2.5%; applicable | Applicable | 2.5% |

Non-fiscal EPZ and SEZ benefits: single unified licence; fast-track approval within 30 days; one-stop shop and after-care; ready infrastructure (factory buildings, water, sewerage, electricity, landscaping, waste management); 24-hour security, illuminated perimeter and on-site customs documentation; work-permit facilitation for up to 20% of full-time employees (SEZ).

Regime choice: the source finds SEZ more favourable for mining on withholding tax for interest (5% vs 15%) and royalties (5% vs 20%), while EPZ is better on dividends (10% vs 15%); model both and confirm eligibility.

## 7. Planning Ranges for the Financial Model (assumptions, all to verify)

**Capital expenditure split:** exploration 5-15% of total project cost (varies by maturity); mine development 30-50% (large-scale); infrastructure and facilities 15-25%; working capital 10-20%. Tax shield on capex from capital allowances and IDA is estimated at 25-35% of eligible spending.

**Operating expenditure:** mining OpEx 30-50% of revenue; VAT recovery on imported inputs possible for licensed operations; royalty as in 6.1.

**Wage benchmarks (Kenya mining sector, 2025, per month, as stated in source; the source labels them monthly, so check for plausibility):**

| Cadre | KES per month |
|---|---|
| Entry-level or artisanal | 200K-500K |
| Semi-skilled operators | 500K-1.2M |
| Technicians | 1.2M-2.5M |
| Engineers and supervisors | 2.5M-5M+ |
| Expatriate specialists | 5M-10M+ (work permit and withholding taxes apply) |

**Valuation:** revenue multiples 1-3x; EBITDA multiples 4-8x for mature operations (2-4x exploration stage); DCF WACC 8-12% (Kenya country risk premium 4-6%, base rate 4-6%, leverage adjustment).

**Price sensitivity (revenue change per 1% price move):** gold high (5-15%); base metals such as copper and iron medium-high (6-12%); industrial minerals such as soda ash and limestone medium (3-8%); REEs and niobium very high (10-20% margin expansion potential).

**Cash-flow tax notes:** corporate tax 30% standard, 10-15% under SEZ for the first 10-20 years; withholding 10-20% on dividends, interest, royalties (treaty-dependent); capital gains tax typically not applicable to mineral extraction (check current law); VAT recovery 90-100% on export-oriented mining.

## 8. Investor Protection, Foreign Exchange and Land

- Foreign Investments Protection Act (FIPA): guarantees against expropriation and legal recourse for unlawful seizure. The 2010 Constitution protects private property rights, enforced by an independent judiciary.
- ICSID: Kenya is a member, giving a neutral forum for investor-state arbitration.
- MIGA: Kenya is a member; political-risk insurance for private investors, foreign lenders and member-country enterprises covering currency inconvertibility, expropriation, political violence and breach of contract.
- ATIA (Africa Trade Insurance Agency): Kenya is a member; export credit, political-risk and investment insurance.
- Foreign exchange: no exchange controls; full repatriation of profits and capital and free currency conversion for legitimate business, conditional on tax compliance with KRA (withholding tax by income type; compliance certified by a licensed tax agent or KRA endorsement).
- Land: non-citizens hold leasehold only, maximum 99 years; the Ministry of Lands registers interests (procedures and fees at https://lands.go.ke/). Access: private land needs owner consent in the prescribed form; community land needs community consent under the Land Act 2012 and Community Land Act 2016; public land needs National or County government approval through the National Land Commission.

## 9. Environmental, Social and Governance Requirements

- EIA: mandatory under the Environment (Impact Assessment and Audit) Regulations, 2003; no project implementation without a completed EIA; regulated by NEMA (http://www.nema.go.ke/). A mine closure plan with cost estimate and rehabilitation plan is required for all licences, and long-term liability should be provided for.
- Occupational health and safety: Occupational Safety and Health Act, 2007 and the Mining Act, 2016 provisions; international best practice expected.
- Community Development Agreements: mandatory for mining licences (especially large scale). Typical elements: local employment preference, skills transfer and career pathways, community infrastructure, health, education and livelihoods, direct revenue sharing, and grievance mechanisms.
- Responsible-mining leverage: growing investor demand for ESG-aligned projects and DFI preference for responsible operators; AWEIK programme model, proven CDAs, rehabilitation record, safety excellence.
- Local content and industrialisation: supplier integration with micro and small enterprises (Base Titanium reference), skills programmes, Vision 2030 local content targets, county partnership opportunities.
- ASM formalisation: opportunity for aggregation, value addition and formalisation as a social-enterprise model (permit pathway in 5.1).
- Infrastructure and regional development: mining projects stimulate roads, power, water and training facilities; county co-investment; benefits for Laikipia, West Pokot and other underserved regions.
