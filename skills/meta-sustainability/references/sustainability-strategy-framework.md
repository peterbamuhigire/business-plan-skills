# Sustainability Strategy Framework

Task reference for building the strategy behind Section 16: choose a few material issues, resource them, prove the business case, and align with lender and SDG expectations. Draws on the vectoring approach of Leleux and van der Kaaij (2019) and standard integrated-reporting, circular-economy and SDG practice.

## 1. Vectoring: direction plus speed

A winning strategy is a vector. Direction is a focus on the few issues that are material; speed is execution momentum. Strong direction without momentum stalls; fast movement in the wrong direction creates risk. Most programmes fail by spreading effort over too many issues. Rule: do less, but do it completely.

- Direction: pick two or three material issues. Materiality is sector- and location-specific (a food processor: food safety and water; a hotel: energy and waste; a construction firm: worker safety and land).
- Speed: commit budget, people and time; set dated targets; assign accountability; report progress. Avoid the reporting trap (measuring and reporting without changing operations).

Application to the plan:
1. Section 16 names two or three material issues, not all 17 SDGs.
2. Each issue has a measurable target, a budget line and a named owner.
3. Progress KPIs appear in the monitoring and evaluation plan.
4. Audit with the Sustainability Readiness Score in `meta-sustainability/SKILL.md` to confirm direction and speed are both present.

Benchmarks that frontrunners outperform peers on eco-efficiency and climate strategy are reported in the source literature; cite a current dated source if you use such a figure.

## 2. Four archetypes

Classify the plan on two axes: how far material risks are reduced, and how actively sustainability opportunities are captured.

| Archetype | Risk reduced? | Opportunity captured? | Traits | Lender view |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional | No | Low | Regulatory minimum only; no resources or targets | Unacceptable for DFI funding; high ESG risk |
| Communicative | Yes | Low | Strong reporting; little operational change; reporting trap | Acceptable to commercial banks if compliant; weak for DFIs |
| Opportunistic | No | High | Captures green revenue while core risks remain; public relations outpaces performance; green products beside high-harm operations | Mixed; incoherent portfolio flagged |
| Transformational | Yes | High | Embedded in the business model; cuts cost, grows revenue, builds reputation; material, resourced targets | Preferred by DFIs and impact investors; high social-licence score in CAMPARI |

Diagnosis questions:
1. Does sustainability appear only in the risk section, or also in strategy and operations as an opportunity?
2. Is there a sustainability budget line? If not, the plan is Traditional or Communicative.
3. Are KPIs in the M&E plan with named accountability? If not, momentum is absent.
4. Does the narrative link to specific revenue, cost or reputation outcomes? If not, it is probably Communicative.

Target the Transformational archetype, or a credible trajectory towards it.

## 3. Materiality matrix

A sustainability issue is material when it significantly affects the ability to create value over time. Score two dimensions: relevance to the business (financial impact, dependency, regulatory exposure, competitive risk) and relevance to stakeholders (customers, employees, communities, lenders, regulators, suppliers).

Procedure:
1. List 10 to 15 candidate issues for the sector (use the sector table in `meta-sustainability/SKILL.md`).
2. Score business relevance 1 to 5 (5 = could materially affect revenue, operating cost or regulatory status; 3 = moderate; 1 = minor).
3. Score stakeholder relevance 1 to 5 on the same scale.
4. Plot on a 5 by 5 grid; issues scoring 4 or more on both axes are primary.
5. Select the top two or three; acknowledge the rest without prioritising them.

Illustration (dairy farm): water scarcity (5, 4), manure management (4, 5) and milk food safety (5, 5) are primary; worker safety (3, 4) and animal welfare (3, 3) are secondary; packaging (2, 2) is low. Lenders, NEMA inspectors and communities will interrogate the primary issues.

Lesson: one material issue, fully addressed, beats a generic report. A cold-storage business, for instance, should answer one question well: energy reliability and refrigerant management under outages and rising temperatures.

## 4. Business case for sustainability

Reports without action provide none of the four building blocks below and are a liability.

1. Cost savings from eco-efficiency. Reduce energy, water and raw-material inputs and waste outputs. Examples: biogas digester on a dairy farm; process redesign to cut water use; LED lighting and solar water heating in hotels; driver eco-efficiency training in transport. Model as lower cost of sales or overheads, with investment and payback period. Savings percentages vary by site; state them as assumptions supported by a dated quote or audit.
2. Revenue from sustainable innovation. Certification (organic, fair trade) for export markets and price premiums; green building for institutional clients; by-product lines (maize bran to feed, coffee husks to briquettes).
3. Reputation and social licence to operate. Continuing acceptance by communities, employees, customers and regulators. Critical for shared water sources, nuisances (noise, odour, dust), large local workforces and hazardous materials. In CAMPARI scoring (`meta-bankability-scoring/SKILL.md`) unresolved community conflict weakens Character and Ability.
4. Risk reduction and lower cost of capital. Unmanaged risks become financial risks: NEMA enforcement and shutdown, community disruption, climate shocks on unprotected inputs. Lenders price ESG risk; UDB green-lending facilities may offer concessional rates for climate-smart investments (verify current terms).

## 5. Six capitals of value creation (Integrated Reporting framework)

Ask which capitals the business consumes and which it creates.

| Capital | Meaning | SME example |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | Debt, equity, retained earnings, grants | Bank loan, DFI grant, SACCO savings |
| Manufactured | Buildings, equipment, vehicles, infrastructure | Machinery, cold storage, vehicles |
| Intellectual | Knowledge, brand, IP, processes | Proprietary recipe, registered trademark |
| Human | Skills, experience, motivation, culture | Trained technicians, sales team |
| Social and relationship | Stakeholder relationships and social licence | Farmer supply network, regulator relationships |
| Natural | Water, soil, biodiversity, air, ecosystem services | Watershed access, fertile out-grower land |

Financial projections usually capture only the first two; Section 16 accounts for the other four. A farm building soil health, staff skills and farmer relationships is more resilient than one depleting soil for short-term yield.

## 6. Circular economy

Linear: extract, make, sell, dispose. Circular: design out waste, keep materials in use at highest value, regenerate natural systems (Ellen MacArthur Foundation). Waste is a design failure rather than an end-of-pipe problem.

| Business type | Circular application | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Dairy | Biogas from manure; digestate as fertiliser or fodder-crop input | Lower energy cost; compost revenue |
| Agri-processing | Sell husks (briquettes, feed), waste starch to industry; zero-waste target | Disposal cost avoided; secondary revenue |
| Poultry | Manure to biogas or compost; feathers to crafts or insulation | Lower disposal cost; extra income |
| Coffee | Husks to biochar or briquettes; treated pulp water reused or composted | Energy savings; less effluent |
| Hospitality | Food waste to compost or biogas; greywater for gardens; solar PV | Lower utility and disposal costs |
| Plastics and packaging retail | Collection point for recyclers; return incentives; biodegradable range for premium segment | Premium pricing; lower compliance risk |
| Textiles | Off-cuts to accessories or stuffing; second-hand resale | No off-cut disposal; new revenue line |
| Construction | Reclaimed timber and steel; rubble as aggregate; soil reuse | Lower materials and disposal costs |
| Healthcare | Licensed contractor for sharps and biohazard waste (mandatory); sterilise and reuse equipment | Compliance; equipment savings |

Why it pays for SMEs: input costs are volatile (exchange rate and supply risk); waste regulation and enforcement are tightening, so non-compliance costs more than circular design; by-products have real local demand.

## 7. SDG integration

The SDGs (2015 to 2030) carry a large annual financing gap, and private-sector delivery is expected to fill much of it (quote the current estimate with a dated source). SDG alignment increasingly conditions development finance (UDB, aBi Finance, UNCDF, EU), impact investment, public procurement and international buyers.

Selection procedure:
1. List the five or six SDGs most affected by operations, outputs or supply chain.
2. Assess alignment with existing activity.
3. Separate positive contribution from negative impact to manage.
4. Set measurable, dated targets for the top two or three.
5. Do not claim superficial alignment; lenders scrutinise it.

| Sector | Primary SDGs | Example targets |
|---|---|---|
| Food and agriculture | 2, 12 | 2.3 smallholder incomes; 12.3 food waste |
| Healthcare and pharmacy | 3, 10 | 3.8 universal health coverage; 10.2 inclusion |
| Construction | 11, 13 | 11.6 urban environmental impact; 13.1 climate resilience |
| Education and training | 4, 10 | 4.4 employment skills; 10.2 inclusion |
| Technology and digital | 9, 17 | 9.c internet access; 17.16 partnerships |
| Financial services | 1, 8 | 1.4 access to financial services; 8.10 banking access |
| Energy | 7, 13 | 7.2 renewables share; 13.2 climate policy |
| Water and sanitation | 6, 3 | 6.1 safe water; 3.3 waterborne disease |

Common mistakes: listing all 17 goals; claiming SDG 8 without employment count and wage level; claiming SDG 13 without a specific climate measure; claiming any SDG without a measurable target and date.

## 8. Localisation: Uganda and East Africa

Climate adaptation framing. Uganda's share of global emissions is very small, so frame sustainability as adaptation (resilience to rainfall variability, drought, flooding, temperature shifts). Mitigation matters mainly where deforestation risk (charcoal, tobacco curing, artisanal timber) or high energy intensity (industrial processing, cold chain) also raises cost.

Adaptation priorities:
- Agriculture: drought-tolerant varieties, rainwater harvesting, on-farm storage, crop diversification, post-harvest storage against flush-season price falls.
- Food processing: flood-safe siting, backup power for refrigeration, dry-season input procurement.
- Construction: flood-zone setbacks, seasonal materials storage, natural ventilation.
- Tourism: biodiversity protection; flood resilience at lake or forest sites.
- Transport: seasonal route planning; maintenance for temperature extremes.

Recurring materialities:

| Materiality | Sectors | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Water scarcity | Dairy, agri-processing, hospitality, horticulture | Efficiency targets; dry-season abstraction plan; permit compliance |
| Soil degradation | Farming and supply chains | Soil-health programme; organic matter return; out-grower support |
| Informal waste streams | Processing, construction, retail | Waste plan with licensed contractor; NEMA compliance |
| Land tenure disputes | Any land-based business | Title verification; documented community consent; Resettlement Action Plan where needed |
| Food safety | Food businesses | UNBS certification; HACCP or GMP; cold-chain integrity |
| Worker safety | Construction, manufacturing, agri-processing | PPE; induction; incident reporting; NSSF compliance |

Lender expectations (confirm current policy with each lender):

| Lender | ESMP expectation | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| UDB | Mandatory | UDB Environmental and Social Policy |
| aBi Finance | Mandatory for agri-lending | aBi Finance ESG framework |
| UNCDF, EU Development Fund | Mandatory, aligned with IFC Performance Standards | IFC Performance Standards (2012) |
| Commercial banks | Strongly recommended for larger loans | Bank E&S policies |
| SACCOs and microfinance | Usually not required for small loans | Self-certification |

Use `11-funding-request/references/esmp-template.md` for DFI applications.

Green finance: check current national climate and green-growth policy (Green Growth Development Strategy, climate finance strategy, climate-smart agriculture investment plan), UDB green-lending terms, EU blended-finance windows and AfDB climate funds. Terms change; cite a dated source.

Sustainability as advantage (illustrative patterns, model with own numbers): biogas cutting electricity and manure costs and unlocking concessional lending; husks sold to feed manufacturers; solar PV plus rainwater harvesting in hotels; biodegradable packaging with a price premium among willing urban customers (test with a pilot); reclaimed-materials certification winning tenders with ESG procurement rules.
