# Sustainability Indicators and Measurement

Task reference for choosing, baselining and monitoring sustainability KPIs in a business plan. Use it for the KPI table in Section 16 and the monitoring plan.

## 1. Purpose and quality tests

Indicators serve four functions: monitor change, assess progress, support decisions, and communicate with stakeholders. Most indicators fail in practice because they are irrelevant, unmeasurable or not actionable. Accept an indicator only if it is:

1. Scientifically valid: measurable and replicable.
2. Policy-relevant: tied to a decision someone can make.
3. Understandable to non-experts.
4. Timely: available often enough to guide action.
5. Cost-effective: benefit exceeds collection cost.

## 2. Indicator types

| Type | Use | Plan application |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | State of a system (litres per day) | Year 0 baseline |
| Performance | Output against input or target (water per kg) | Year-on-year comparison |
| Efficiency | Resource productivity | Cost reduction; benchmarking |
| Policy effectiveness | Whether an action achieves its intended outcome | ESMP compliance; programme evaluation |
| Welfare or outcome | Wellbeing outcomes for people or ecosystems | SDG and DFI impact reporting |
| Sentinel | Early warning of limits or tipping points | Climate adaptation, soil health, water table |

## 3. DPSIR analysis

Map the issue along Driving forces, Pressures, State, Impacts, Responses (a development of the Pressure-State-Response model). Use it to locate where the business sits in the chain and where intervention is most effective.

| Component | Meaning | Uganda illustration |
|---|---|---|
| Driving forces | Socio-economic activity causing change | Population growth, urbanisation, agricultural intensification |
| Pressures | Direct stressors | Water abstraction, pesticide use, deforestation |
| State | Environmental condition | Declining lake levels, degrading soil |
| Impacts | Effects on people and ecosystems | Lower fish catch, yield decline, flooding |
| Responses | Policy or business action | Water permits, organic farming, watershed restoration |

## 4. Individual indicators versus composite indices

Individual indicators are precise and actionable but cover one dimension. Composite indices are easier to communicate but can mask trade-offs. Well-known composites and their plan relevance:

| Index | Measures | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Dow Jones Sustainability Index | ESG performance of large listed firms | Benchmark for large firms; rarely applicable to SMEs |
| Human Development Index | Life expectancy, education, income | National context |
| Environmental Performance Index | Environmental health and ecosystem vitality | Country benchmark |
| Genuine Progress Indicator | GDP net of social and environmental costs | Alternative framing for social or environmental claims |
| Custom composite | Own aggregation of KPIs | Sustainability Readiness Score in `meta-sustainability/SKILL.md` |

Country scores change each release; quote the current value with a dated source. For SMEs, use the Sustainability Readiness Score as the composite.

## 5. Build a KPI set in five steps

1. Define the decision the indicator informs (for example, cut water cost by a target percentage).
2. Choose the single metric that best captures progress (litres per kg; percentage of women in the workforce).
3. Set the baseline before any intervention.
4. Set targets for year 1 and year 3.
5. Define the measurement method: who collects, how, how often, at what cost.

Avoid: metrics that are easy but immaterial (office paper when water is the main impact); targets without baselines; more than about ten KPIs for an SME; indicators with no accountable owner.

## 6. Minimum viable KPI set (six to eight KPIs)

| Pillar | KPI | Method | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Environmental | Water per unit output (litres per kg) | Meter reading divided by production records | Monthly |
| Environmental | Energy per unit output or per revenue | Electricity bill divided by output or revenue | Monthly |
| Environmental | Waste to disposal (kg per month) | Waste collection manifest | Monthly |
| Environmental | NEMA permit compliance | Permit on file | Annual |
| Social | Women in workforce (%) | Payroll records | Quarterly |
| Social | Lost-time injury rate per 100 employees | Accident register | Quarterly |
| Social | Local sourcing (% of inputs by value) | Purchase records | Quarterly |
| Economic | Revenue per unit of resource input | Management accounts | Monthly |

## 7. Monitoring system design

- Name one data collector (may be part of another role).
- Record in a simple spreadsheet or mobile data entry.
- Collect monthly; review quarterly with management; report annually to the DFI or lender.
- Escalation trigger: any KPI more than 20% off target prompts a management review within 30 days (planning rule; adjust to materiality).
- For DFI loans, budget for an annual third-party environmental and social audit (planning assumption; obtain a dated quote).

## 8. Localisation: Uganda and East Africa

- Data sources: UBOS (Statistical Abstract), NEMA (State of Environment Report), MAAIF (agriculture), Ministry of Health. National data is improving but limited; professional monitoring services in Kampala are an annual cost to quote.
- Low-cost tools: mechanical water flow meter; electricity bill for kWh, plus sub-meters for major equipment; a kitchen or platform scale for waste. Obtain current supplier prices.
- DFI reporting: UDB, aBi Finance and EU-funded programmes commonly require periodic environmental and social reports for larger loans; confirm thresholds and format with the lender. Template: `meta-monitoring-evaluation/references/funder-reporting-requirements.md`.
- Sentinel indicator: hydropower depends on lake levels, which affect electricity tariffs and load-shedding. Electricity-dependent businesses should watch tariff announcements and outage schedules as early warnings.
