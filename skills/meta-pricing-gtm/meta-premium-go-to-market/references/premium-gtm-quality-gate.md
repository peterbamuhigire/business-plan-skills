# Premium GTM Quality Gate

Use this gate for high-ticket, luxury, affluent, executive, enterprise, and premium-service business plans.

## Positioning

- Define the premium buyer narrowly. "Everyone who can afford it" is not a segment.
- State the category and the premium contrast: what ordinary alternatives do, what this offer does better, and why that difference matters.
- Name the value driver: status, confidence, time, revenue, margin, risk reduction, access, convenience, discretion, transformation, or strategic control.
- Avoid low-price language, generic quality claims, and commodity comparisons.

## Offer And Product

- The core offer must include service design, not only product features.
- Include onboarding, consultation, customisation, account ownership, aftercare, reporting, and escalation where premium buyers expect support.
- Use scarcity or exclusivity only when operationally true: limited slots, invitation, application, qualification, waitlist, membership, or bespoke production.
- For services, make the invisible visible: process, milestones, deliverables, proof, review cadence, and success measures.
- Treat packaging as a value driver: name, presentation, onboarding, website, proposal, demo, environment, documentation, and customer communications must support the price premium.
- Simplicity is part of premium quality: reduce buyer effort, make decisions clear, and avoid complex bundles that hide the value proposition.

## Pricing

- Use value-based pricing or tiered packages where the offer is differentiated.
- Do not discount without a scope, timing, payment, or commitment trade-off.
- Show buyer ROI or consequence of inaction where credible.
- Protect margin by modelling acquisition cost, senior staff time, onboarding, revisions, aftercare, and premium support.

## Lead Generation

- For enterprise/high-ticket B2B, build named-account lists, executive triggers, referral paths, and account-specific outreach.
- For affluent/luxury consumers, build trust through brand world, aspiration, service proof, privacy, and experience.
- Use lead magnets only if they signal expertise and attract the right buyer; avoid generic giveaways that attract low-fit leads.
- Use nurture sequences to educate, build authority, handle risk, and invite a specific next step.
- Use digital PR and content assets to earn authority: original data, expert commentary, buyer guides, diagnostics, benchmarks, case studies, and campaignable stories.
- For high-value meeting generation, create a reason worth attention: relevant trigger, insight, referral, benchmark, diagnostic, or executive briefing. Do not ask for time with a generic introduction.

## Sales Execution

- Use discovery before pitching: situation, problem, implication, payoff, decision process, budget, timing, and risk.
- Ask for meaningful commitments: diagnostic, meeting, data access, stakeholder introduction, proposal review, pilot, or deposit.
- Build proposals around outcomes, risk reduction, proof, delivery plan, and next decision.
- Capture objections as signals about missing value, weak proof, timing risk, stakeholder concerns, or price anchoring.

## Launch And Retention

- Validate demand before scaling: interviews, pilots, pre-orders, waitlist, paid discovery, or founder-led selling.
- Launch with a clear audience, channel, message, offer, proof, deadline, and follow-up sequence.
- Retain premium customers with review meetings, proactive recommendations, early access, private updates, relationship care, and referral invitations.

## Red Flags

- Premium pricing with ordinary delivery.
- Luxury language with no exclusivity, service, or proof.
- Enterprise sales plan with no stakeholder map or business case.
- Marketing plan focused on awareness but no conversion/follow-up system.
- Financial projections assume premium margins without premium acquisition and service costs.
