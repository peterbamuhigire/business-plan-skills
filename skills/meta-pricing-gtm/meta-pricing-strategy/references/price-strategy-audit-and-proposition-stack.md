When to read: before accepting any price, fee, discount or pricing narrative in a plan, and whenever the plan relies on cost-plus, industry-norm or competitor-match pricing.

# Price strategy audit and proposition stack

This is the engine's own pricing review for business plans. It asks three questions in order: is the price built on sound arithmetic, is it protected by a proposition the buyer cannot compare like for like, and will the business hold the price in practice? Several ideas are adapted from the direct-response pricing literature, notably Kennedy, D. S. and Marrs, J. (2011) *No B.S. Price Strategy*, Entrepreneur Press, which frames under-pricing as a set of recurring owner failures and argues for building several "propositions" around a price. The grouping, tests, thresholds and examples below are this engine's. Book case figures are not used.

## 1. Diagnose the current price (twelve checks in three groups)

Mark each check **clear**, **weak** or **failing**. Any failing check blocks a "pricing is sound" conclusion until the plan states the fix.

**A. Arithmetic**

| Check | Failing sign in the draft | Required fix |
|---|---|---|
| A1 Price origin | "Cost plus 30%", "the going rate", or no stated method | Work back from the required return (section 3) and use cost only as a floor |
| A2 Unit economics | No contribution per unit, break-even volume or cash cycle | Show contribution, break-even and working-capital effect |
| A3 Discount cost | Promotions with no stated volume needed to recover margin | State the extra units needed to hold contribution before approval |
| A4 Funding the business | Margin too thin to pay for marketing, good staff and improvement | Record the under-pricing cascade as a risk (section 7) |

**B. Positioning**

| Check | Failing sign | Required fix |
|---|---|---|
| B1 Comparability | Same package, wording and delivery as rivals | Change bundle, guarantee, delivery, name or niche (section 4) |
| B2 Difference | "Affordable", "quality service", "reliable" | One specific, testable difference tied to a customer outcome |
| B3 Buyer mix | Growth plan depends on discount-seekers | Name the buyer who pays for certainty, speed or outcome |
| B4 Price ladder | One price or one tier only | Add a higher tier that sets the ceiling and signals quality |

**C. Behaviour**

| Check | Failing sign | Required fix |
|---|---|---|
| C1 Owner belief | "Our customers will never pay more" with no test | Plan a test that includes a higher price |
| C2 Fear of the cheapest rival | Price justified only against the lowest competitor | Show which segment is not choosing on price |
| C3 Front-line discipline | Staff free to negotiate down; incentives on revenue only | Approval matrix, margin-based incentives, scripted price talk |
| C4 Confidence | Apologetic wording in proposals and price lists | Written price policy and plain, confident wording |

Most failing checks trace to owner belief rather than tactics, so the fix is usually a written policy, not a one-off price change.

## 2. Build the protection around the price

A buyer judges price against what surrounds it. Draft each layer; lead with the strongest for the segment:

| Layer | Buyer's question | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| Distinction | Why this provider rather than any alternative, including doing nothing? | Positioning line and the one difference that matters |
| Worth | Why is this worth clearly more than it costs? | Outcome arithmetic in the buyer's money (hours saved, losses avoided, revenue gained) |
| Offer | Why act now? | Core offer, relevant extras, a genuine deadline or capacity limit |
| Safety | Why is trying it low-risk? | Guarantee or warranty, trial, references, peer proof |
| Experience | What will buying and using it feel like? | Onboarding, service moments, after-sales follow-through |

Test: does the material give the buyer a reason to stop comparing on price alone? If not, a layer is missing.

**Worked example (illustrative).** A Mbarara veterinary-drug supplier sells the same products as three rivals. Distinction: farm-visit dosing advice by a qualified technician. Worth: one avoided calf loss outweighs a season's price difference. Offer: a quarterly herd-health pack booked before the rains. Safety: replacement of any product that fails a cold-chain check. Experience: a WhatsApp reminder before each dosing date.

## 3. Set the price

| Method | Use |
|---|---|
| Required return | Starting point: fix the return the business needs, then solve for price, volume and cost |
| Customer value | Ceiling: the buyer's best alternative and the outcome value |
| Cost-plus | Floor check only |
| Industry norm or rival match | Context, never the method; it locks the business at parity |
| Buyer-dictated or guess | Reject |

Choose the movement over time: **skim** (launch high, step down for later segments) or **penetrate** (launch low for share, with a dated plan for raising price, because climbing out of a low price is hard). Test at least one higher price point before projecting.

## 4. Remove like-for-like comparison

- Bundle complementary goods or services rivals do not offer together.
- Change one visible element: delivery method, packaging, guarantee, speed, service hours.
- Give the method or package a name so it becomes its own category.
- Specialise the presentation for a defined profession, industry or community; the same product built around that group's needs can carry a higher price when it truly serves them.

## 5. Discount policy

1. Every discount has a stated reason (volume, prepayment, season, pre-order, desired behaviour).
2. Every concession is traded for something of value: earlier commitment, prepayment, multi-period booking, referral, case-study consent.
3. Promotions vary in timing, reason and size so buyers do not learn to wait.
4. Acquisition discounts are confined to one designated entry product.
5. Prestige products are never discounted or given away; add value (warranty, service, financing, access) instead.
6. Each discount records its margin cost, the recovery volume and its likely effect on perceived quality.

## 6. Build pricing power over time

Pricing power rests on three things the plan must evidence: **expertise and service** that resist comparison (methods, trained staff, named processes); **visible presence** in the channels the niche uses; and **standing in the community** served (credible sponsorship, teaching, thought leadership). Kennedy and Marrs describe a similar idea, crediting a practitioner source. Add two supports:

- **Enquiry surplus:** run more enquiry sources than capacity needs, so poor-fit clients can be declined rather than discounted; never depend on one introducer.
- **Association:** show credible clients, partners or recognition, with written permission, so the price is compared with premium peers.

## 7. Situations that need special handling

- **Free or subsidised rivals** (public services, donor programmes, freemium tools): list the hidden costs of the free option (waiting, paperwork, uneven quality, travel, privacy), make them visible without disparaging named providers, target buyers who pay for certainty, give decision-helping information freely and charge for delivery and results, and avoid training buyers to expect free.
- **Price as a quality signal:** a price far below comparables can create doubt, especially where counterfeits are common; test whether a higher price improves response.
- **Upstream signals:** buyers form a price expectation before the quote from the media the brand appears in, the company it keeps, premises, website, staff presentation and proposal packaging. Fix mismatches before raising price.
- **Under-pricing cascade:** thin margin → small marketing budget → weak enquiry flow → pressure to discount; thin margin also limits staff quality (service failures, churn) and product investment (drift to commodity). Show it in the model if margins cannot fund all three.
- **Several decision-makers:** households and firms often include a sceptic, a cost controller, an enthusiast and a final decider. Give each the evidence they need; close with the decider.
- **Downturns:** do not cut price by default. First improve value, shed poor-fit customers, redirect marketing to the segment still buying, and hold price where weaker rivals are leaving. Show the downside in the stress test.

## 8. Sequence for a pricing workstream

Diagnose (section 1) → choose the niche and the lead protection layer (section 2) → evidence pricing power (section 6) → set and test the price (section 3) → remove comparison (section 4) → write the discount policy (section 5) → train staff and set the approval matrix → plan downturn behaviour (section 7).

## 9. East African checks (to run, not facts)

- Confirm that a segment paying for trusted providers exists in the named town, with a dated source.
- Apply section 7 where the business competes with public schools, clinics, security or savings groups.
- Test origin or niche premiums (single-origin produce, crafts, specialist training) with real price evidence before projecting them.
- Hold written permission for any logo or association claim.
- Route tax on prices and discounts (VAT treatment, withholding) to the Chwezi finance doctrine.

## Evidence to record in the plan

Check results (clear/weak/failing) with fixes; lead protection layer; price method and test plan; discount policy with reasons, trades and margin effect; skim or penetration path; approval-matrix owner; downturn policy.
