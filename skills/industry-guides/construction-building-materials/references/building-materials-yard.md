# Building Materials Yard (Sand, Ballast, Stone): Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers a yard that buys aggregates in lorry loads and breaks bulk for small and mid-scale builders.

## Market and customer
- Customers: small and mid-size builders, contractors, fundis and site managers who need one to ten tonnes (too little for direct lorry delivery, too much for a hardware shop); secondary buyers include small brokers and transporters.
- Demand tracks residential construction; yards near active housing development, plot subdivision and infill zones outperform yards in settled areas. Evidence the demand with counts of active sites in the catchment.
- The market is informal: weights and grades are unregulated and units (wheelbarrows, "tonnes") vary by place. Competition is on price, convenience and trust (honest measure, consistent quality).

## Operating model
- Chain: quarry or river source, transporter or lorry, yard, builder.
- Stock tiers: fast movers (ballast, river sand, quarry chips, building sand); secondary (stones by size, hardcore, murram, red soil).
- Buy by the lorry load (lorry capacities vary by type) and sell in one-to-five-tonne lots.
- Site: a fenced yard of modest size with a gate, a shelter or small office, a hard floor for loading, good road access and drainage. Staff: owner or supervisor, two or more loaders, a watchman.
- Add complementary lines (cement, nails, timber, steel bars) to raise revenue per customer.

## Equipment and capex checklist
Fencing and gate, land clearing, shelter, floor, signage, wheelbarrows, shovels, scale or agreed measure; permits; rent deposit; opening stock (about one to three lorry loads across materials); wages and rent for the first months.

## Unit economics drivers
- Margin per tonne = selling price minus (purchase price + haulage + loading and shrinkage).
- Profit per batch = tonnes x margin per tonne; annual profit = batches per year x profit per batch minus rent, labour, permits and security.
- Inventory turnover is the critical variable: a load sold in days is profitable; the same load sold over months is a loss after rent and labour. Model days-to-sell per load.
- Prices are location-specific and volatile (fuel, quarry distance, season); use dated quotations from local transporters.

## Regulation and licensing checks
Trading licence; environmental compliance near wetlands or protected zones; mineral-dealer or sand and stone licence in some districts (Uganda); quarry source legitimacy. Check district, city (KCCA) and environmental authority requirements.

## Localisation
Kampala sources: quarry areas around Mukono, Lugazi and Kampala outskirts; rents fall towards city edges. Kigali and Dar es Salaam show similar construction-driven demand.

## Risks
Slow turnover, fuel and haulage price swings, weather (rainy-season sales drop), short-measure disputes, regulatory action on sand extraction, theft.

## KPIs
Days to sell a load, gross margin per tonne, tonnes sold per week, share of repeat contractors, spoilage or loss percentage.

## Plan-section prompts
Which materials and why? What supply route and lorry size? How many days to sell a load? Which sites will buy in the first quarter?

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya, materials yard guide (2018); figures dated and must be re-quoted locally.
