# Tiles Retail: Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers a shop selling floor and wall tiles to home builders, renovators and contractors. Stock dominates capital; competitive advantage comes from supplier variety, product knowledge and contractor accounts.

## Market and customer
- Segments: individual home builders (small quantities), building contractors (large, multi-unit accounts, the most valuable), and renovation buyers (single cartons).
- Demand tracks construction activity; sales are typically stronger in the second half of the year and depend on the mix of residential and commercial building nearby. Estimate daily boxes sold from a traffic and site survey rather than assuming the top of any range.
- Trends to test: more specialist tile shops, growing customer brand and quality knowledge, price-based competition, importers entering, constant new designs. The shift from floor-only to full wall-and-floor tiling in homes widens the market.
- Independent shops win through location near active construction, specialisation (premium or decorative), or contractor networks; large hardware chains and low-overhead importer-retailers are the main threats.

## Product range
- Types: ceramic (widest, affordable), porcelain (harder, premium, high traffic), PVC (budget, lightweight).
- Classify stock by size, material, origin (local, Chinese, Indian), purpose (floor, wall, border, bathroom) and brand.
- Floor tiles usually outsell wall tiles because many buyers paint walls. Lower-price imports sell most; pieces per box affect coverage per purchase.

## Operating model
- Suppliers: several importers and manufacturers, giving variety. Typical wholesale terms: minimum opening order in cartons (smaller on repeat orders), an account requiring business permit, ID and tax number, transport sometimes included, and credit after a track record with prepayment at first. Maintain consistent purchasing volume to keep the account.
- Setup sequence: choose location, compare wholesalers on price, minimum quantities and terms, renovate, obtain licences, stock the most popular sizes with design variety, open.
- Avoid substandard tiles; quality failures damage reputation. Provide advice on quantities, adhesive and laying.

## Equipment and capex checklist
Display stands (often supplied free), pallets to prevent breakage, signboard, fire extinguisher, renovation, licences, optional company registration (some wholesalers require it), stock, rent deposit and months of rent.

## Unit economics drivers
- Stock capital = cartons x wholesale price per carton; halve the opening order and stock capital roughly halves, but do not sacrifice variety excessively.
- Gross margin per box = retail minus wholesale minus breakage and freight; margins vary by type, supplier and location.
- Break-even boxes per month = fixed costs / margin per box; add carrying cost for slow designs.
- Sensitivity: exchange rate and shipping costs, breakage, slow-moving designs, contractor discounts.

## Regulation and licensing checks
Business permit, fire safety licence, tax registration; import certification and standards marks (TBS in Tanzania, RBS in Rwanda are enforced for imports); registration bodies (KCCA and URA in Uganda, BRELA and TRA in Tanzania, RDB in Rwanda).

## Localisation
Kampala clusters and Kigali (Nyabugogo, Kimironko) buy mainly imports; Rwanda enforces certification strictly and adds transport cost to landed price; Tanzania contractors often procure centrally from one supplier; premium and hotel segments favour designer tiles.

## Risks
Capital tied in stock, breakage, competition from chains, exchange-rate and freight swings, contractor credit.

## KPIs
Boxes sold per day, gross margin, stock turn by design, contractor share, breakage rate, receivable days.

## Plan-section prompts
Which suppliers and terms? What opening stock and why? Which contractors are targeted? Where does the shop differentiate?
