# Timber Yard Retail: Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers a yard selling sawn timber to contractors, builders and carpenters. The main margin variable is where you enter the supply chain: the closer to the forest or sawmill, the higher the margin.

## Market and customer
- Customers: building contractors (high volume, formwork and roofing) and jua kali carpenters (steady volume, furniture and joinery).
- Demand follows construction; concrete-frame building reduces structural timber but raises formwork and interior joinery demand. Established urban areas are crowded; newer towns are less competitive but smaller.
- Differentiation is limited; price and reliability dominate. Long-term relationships with carpenters and contractors are assets.

## Product range
- Fast-moving cheap species (for shuttering and temporary works) sold in bulk; premium softwood (roofing, frames) at higher margin; eucalyptus for furniture and roofing (note moisture sensitivity and species differences); poles; avoid low-quality pine in initial stock.
- Timber sells by the linear foot in standard sections (for example 2x2, 3x2, 4x2, 6x2 inches). Wholesale purchases are in larger sections that the yard splits into smaller retail sections, so a saw is essential.
- Start with lower-cost, fast-moving species while relationships form, then add premium stock.

## Supply chain and sourcing
- Chain: forests or private farms or imports, sawmillers or traders, brokers or wholesalers, retail yards, consumers.
- Sources: local plantations and sawmill regions; imported softwood and hardwood across borders. Practical steps: survey local retail prices (your price ceiling), visit large established yards (some also wholesale), contact freelance brokers, insist on seeing timber before paying (cash on delivery for new suppliers), keep several suppliers.
- Fraud risk: short measure at wholesale is common. Inspect and measure every delivery.
- Broker credit terms may extend working capital; model them carefully.

## Equipment and capex checklist
Multipurpose circular saw and surface planer (the essential machine), band saw for large sections, optional electric saw and lathe, treatment or paint trough, fire extinguisher and blanket, signboard, sheds and small office, security, licences, stock, rent, wages. Avoid second-hand machinery unless quality is guaranteed.

## Unit economics drivers
- Margin per foot = retail price per foot (after splitting) minus wholesale cost per foot of the parent section; splitting a wide section into several narrow ones raises revenue per foot bought.
- Gross margin depends on supply source, pricing versus competitors, volume (lorry-load buying), and product mix.
- Revenue = feet sold per day x price per foot; break-even feet per month = fixed costs / average margin per foot.
- Include wastage from sawing, warping and moisture, and financing cost of stock.

## Regulation and licensing checks
Trading licence, business registration, fire inspection, signboard, machinery licence, forest-service transport permits for each load and import licences where relevant. Uganda: forestry authority permits and protected hardwood rules; Tanzania: forest service permits, and plantation supply advantage; Rwanda: limited domestic timber, so imports carry a large landed premium. Confirm current requirements.

## Risks
Illegal-timber exposure, measure fraud, price and licence enforcement swings, fire, theft, slow-moving species, rainy-season damage.

## KPIs
Feet sold per day, margin per foot, stock turn, wastage percentage, share of contractor sales, days of broker credit.

## Plan-section prompts
Where in the supply chain do we buy? Which species mix first? Who are the first ten customers? How is delivery measurement verified?
