# Packaged Dairy Distributorship: Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers becoming a distributor between a milk processor and retail shops in a defined area. Margins are a small percentage per packet, so profit is volume-driven and depends on retailer relationships.

## Market and customer
- Supply chain: farm, broker or cooperative, processor, agent or distributor, retailer (kiosk, shop, supermarket), consumer. Supermarkets buy direct from processors, so they undercut kiosks; distributors serve the kiosk and small-shop tier.
- Demand patterns: small consumer purchases, evening then morning peaks, higher consumption when schools are on holiday. Fastest-growing segments are yoghurt and flavoured dairy.
- Threats: milk dispensers (no packaging cost), informal raw-milk trade, and rival brands. Brand strength helps volumes, but poor retailer relations defeat even a strong brand.

## How to become a distributor
- Entry routes: processor advertisement, being approached by the processor, approaching the area manager with a market proposal, or taking over an exiting distributor.
- Typical processor requirements: refundable security deposit (varies widely by company and area), evidence of local market knowledge, delivery capacity (bicycles, motorcycles or vehicle), storage premises, possibly cold storage for full dairy range, tax identification, sometimes a certificate of good conduct. Some prefer applicants already trading in the area.

## Daily operating cycle
Early morning truck delivery to your depot; sort by retailer; morning delivery rounds; late morning collect payment for credit sales; afternoon deposit of the full wholesale value with the processor and place the next order. Commission is normally paid at month end, so daily cash must fund the deposit.
- Extend at most about one day of credit to retailers; beyond that exposure grows.
- Fresh milk is highly perishable (short shelf life); agree return or spoilage rules with the processor and plan for losses.

## Unit economics drivers
- Distributor margin per packet = selling price to retailer minus buying price from processor; retailer margin is similar in size, and yoghurt margins per cup are higher.
- Monthly gross profit = packets per day x 30 x margin; deduct wages, transport, rent, spoilage and bad debt.
- Capital: security deposit (largest and locked until contract end), delivery assets, premises deposit, working capital to cover the deposit cycle and credit. Break-even is typically months, longer for lesser-known brands; state the ramp-up curve (retailers signed x average order).
- Sensitivity: volume per route, spoilage percentage, deposit size, credit days.

## Critical success factors
Retailer relationships and reliable early delivery; a long-term view and cash to sustain the ramp-up; a good relationship with the processor's delivery staff; disciplined working capital.

## Regulation and licensing checks
Dairy regulator licence for distributors (Kenya Dairy Board in Kenya; Dairy Development Authority in Uganda; TFDA in Tanzania; RFDA and RBS in Rwanda), county or city business permit, tax identification. Confirm current requirements and processor contract terms.

## Localisation
Uganda: processors and route-delivery model similar; boda boda and bicycles can replace vans in dense estates; mobile money can support day-end deposits. Tanzania: weaker cold chain outside big cities, so long-life products matter more on rural routes. Rwanda: compact geography and strong food-safety enforcement, so expired stock carries reputational risk.

## Risks
Spoilage, retailer credit default, processor contract termination and deposit lock-up, competing dispensers, price wars.

## KPIs
Packets per day, retailers served, spoilage percentage, days of credit outstanding, margin per packet, route productivity.

## Plan-section prompts
Which processor and terms? What deposit and how is it funded? How many retailers and what daily volume by month? What is the spoilage and credit policy?

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya, milk distributorship quick guide (c. 2016); deposit sizes, margins and volumes are dated and processor-specific and must be verified.
