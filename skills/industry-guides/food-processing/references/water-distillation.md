# Water Distillation and Purified-Water Operations

**Source attribution:** Independently written synthesis of the Trade Sector
profile labelled `BUSINESS IDEA FOR DISTILATION OF WATER` in the *Compendium of Diaspora
Investment & Business Opportunities, Volume 2*, PDF page 114, as captured in
the inspected raw extraction. The tracked raw extraction is removed; this
reference is not a transcription and does not reproduce its incomplete table.

## When to use

Load this reference when a plan proposes water distillation as a production or
service activity. Use [`bottled-water.md`](bottled-water.md) as an adjacent
reference only when the business also purifies, packages, or delivers drinking
water. Distillation and potable-water packaging are not interchangeable claims.

## Decision sequence

1. Define the intended use: laboratory, industrial, commercial, or drinking
   water. The intended use determines the required quality evidence.
2. Test and document the source water before selecting equipment. Record the
   contaminants or mineral profile the process must address.
3. Compare distillation with other treatment options on energy, throughput,
   maintenance, reject-water handling, storage, and product-quality needs.
4. Design a controlled flow from intake through treatment, storage, testing,
   packaging or delivery, cleaning, and waste handling.
5. Build a batch or lot record that links source, treatment run, test result,
   release decision, and customer delivery.

## Evidence boundary

The source profile supplied a business-idea label and a limited production
description but did not provide a complete equipment schedule, current market
evidence, quality specification, or jurisdiction-specific approval basis. These
are **gaps**. Do not use the historical profile as a current cost, demand,
capacity, safety, or compliance claim.

## Release checks

- A competent reviewer has confirmed the intended use and applicable quality
  requirements.
- Source-water and treated-water tests are retained and tied to batches.
- Energy, maintenance, reject-water, packaging, delivery, and downtime costs
  reconcile with the financial model.
- Any claim that the output is potable, certified, or suitable for a regulated
  use remains blocked until current authority and test evidence are present.
