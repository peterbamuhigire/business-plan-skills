# Chicken Butchery (Retail): Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers a fixed-shop retail outlet selling raw chicken (whole birds, quarters, backs, necks, liver, gizzard, feet) with an optional roasted or fried add-on. It differs from beef butchery in its cold-chain need and perishability.

## Market and customer
- Chicken is a mainstream protein across income levels and dominates institutional feeding (schools, hospitals, canteens). Demand rests on price relative to beef, health preferences and urban growth.
- Retail price must sit sensibly below beef in the same neighbourhood; pricing above it destroys volume. Calibrate to local purchasing power: identical portions sell at different prices in low-, middle- and higher-income areas.
- Where a competitor is in sight, price cutting is common; enter only with a differentiation plan (freshness, cut mix, ready-to-eat items, delivery).
- Niches: village (local-breed) chicken at a premium near affluent areas; fresh whole birds for hotels and restaurants; halal certification in Muslim-majority areas.

## Operating model
- Supply: city wholesale markets, slaughterhouses, hatcheries or peri-urban farms; farm-gate buying can lower cost.
- Cold chain: a deep freezer is essential for any shop that does not sell out daily. A cooler box works only for very low volumes.
- Product ladder by margin: whole birds (lowest margin), backs and necks, liver, feet, gizzard. Selling small ready-to-eat portions of low-margin cuts creates impulse sales. Roasting or frying adds a per-kilogram premium but needs extra equipment and health clearance.
- Staff need valid medical certificates. Scales must be certified by the weights-and-measures authority; uncertified scales create legal risk.

## Equipment and capex checklist
Deep freezer (new preferred over second-hand), digital scale (allows sales by money amount) and manual backup, counter, table and chair, knives, basins, trays, buckets, wrapping; licences; rent deposit and renovation; opening stock; three or more months of wages and utilities.

## Unit economics drivers
- Margin per cut = retail price minus wholesale price minus wastage; compute by cut and weight into a blended margin.
- Model daily volume by day of week and payday cycle; break-even kilograms per day = fixed monthly costs / (30 x blended unit margin).
- Working capital must cover several months of rent, wages and utilities before break-even; undercapitalisation is the main failure cause. Any historical price or margin in older sources is a planning assumption to be re-verified with dated market visits.

## Regulation and licensing checks
Business licence, public health licence, medical certificates, scale certification, signboard permit; meat or poultry inspection where required (KCCA in Kampala, TBS and TFDA in Tanzania, RBS in Rwanda). Confirm current fees.

## Risks
Spoilage and power cuts, price swings and disease outbreaks, competitor price cutting, staff theft, underpricing for the local income level.

## KPIs
Kilograms per day, blended gross margin, wastage percentage, stock days, share of value-added sales, months to break-even.

## Plan-section prompts
Which location and why? What backup exists for power? What cut mix and blended margin? What differentiates the shop from the nearest competitor?
