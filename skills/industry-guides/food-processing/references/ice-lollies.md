# Ice Lollies (Frozen Confectionery) Production: Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers small-scale production and distribution of flavoured frozen water lollies for schoolchildren and impulse buyers. Unit margins are thin, so volume and distribution reach decide viability.

## Market and customer
- Core consumers: school-age children and young adults; best sited in dense, warm areas near primary schools. Peri-urban and smaller towns may lack vendors (first-mover advantage) but have lower purchasing power.
- Demand is seasonal: sales peak in hot months and fall in cold or rainy periods; hold a cash reserve for lean months.
- Competition is location-based with little product differentiation. Novelty (two-tone, new flavours, mould shapes) gives only temporary advantage as it is easily copied.

## Operating model
- Process: mix water, flavour and colour; fill moulds in the freezing unit; freeze; extract; pack into cooler boxes; store surplus in a freezer.
- Machine choice: freezing unit plus separate mould sets; capacity is per cycle (for example 120 or 240 lollies), and realistic output is lower than the nominal figure because of preparation and extraction time. Scale by adding mould sets.
- Buy new with warranty where possible; test second-hand units before purchase; secure a reliable supplier for service. Premises need stable power.
- Distribution models: (1) commission salespeople carrying stocked cooler boxes, returning proceeds and unsold stock daily (wide coverage, needs supervision); (2) prepaid wholesale to retailers (cash up front, less sales push). Assign one salesperson per mapped area to avoid overlap.

## Equipment and capex checklist
Freezing unit, mould sets, storage freezer, cooler boxes, sticks, tables, water storage, fire extinguisher, counter, utensils, initial flavours and colours; licences; rent deposit; three months of labour, electricity and water.

## Unit economics drivers
- Cost per lollie = water + electricity + flavour and colour + stick + packaging; compute from local input prices.
- Contribution per lollie = selling price minus cost minus vendor commission (commission model) or minus retailer margin (wholesale model).
- Break-even lollies per month = fixed costs / contribution per lollie; check that mould capacity and freezing cycle time allow it. Payback depends on coverage area and competition; state it from your own model and treat any historical payback as an assumption.
- Cash discipline: reconcile lollies issued to cash returned per vendor daily.

## Regulation and licensing checks
Business permit (or home operation where permitted), public health certificate for premises, medical certificates for workers, fire inspection, hawker licences for vendors; food-production certification from the standards body or food authority in Tanzania and Rwanda; city-authority hawker licences in Kampala. Confirm current fees.

## Localisation
Local names and currencies differ (freezies in Uganda, barafu in Tanzania); convert prices at the prevailing exchange rate and keep the price within the child impulse-purchase range. Power reliability is a constraint in some areas: plan a backup cooling option. Rwanda secondary towns may offer first-mover openings.

## Risks
Thin margins, seasonality, power outages, vendor dishonesty, hygiene incidents, easy imitation.

## KPIs
Lollies produced and sold per day, cost per lollie, sell-through per vendor, unsold-stock returns, downtime hours, gross margin.

## Plan-section prompts
Which distribution model, and how is cash controlled? Which schools or areas and what population? What machine capacity fits the volume target? What is the lean-season plan?
