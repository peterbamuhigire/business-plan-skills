# Cookie and Biscuit Specialist Bakery: Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers a specialist cookie bakery or shop, home-based cookie production, or wholesale of cookies to shops and supermarkets. It is a market-opportunity and margin reference, not a fixed set-up recipe.

## Market and customer
- Competitor types: large formal bakeries selling through supermarkets; specialist cookie brands with own shops; many home-based bakers working through orders and social media; supermarket in-store bakeries.
- Observed gap: small bakers usually focus on cakes and treat cookies as an extra, and estate-level specialist cookie shops are largely absent. Test this gap locally before relying on it.
- Differentiation options: flavours missing from the local range (for example chocolate chip, peanut butter, oatmeal, banana, mint), personalised cookies for birthdays, sports and corporate gifts, and sale by weight over the counter to signal freshness.
- Price sensitivity: estate consumers accept only a small premium for quality; premium demand clusters in specific urban zones (affluent estates, expatriate and NGO areas).

## Operating model
- Channels: supermarkets (need consistent volume, packaging standards and credit terms), own shop or counter, wholesale to cake shops and kiosks, home delivery and social-media orders.
- Quality depends on flour type, dough mixing, oven temperature control and oiling; skilled bakers are a key dependency, so document recipes and train a second baker.
- Control weak points: ingredient theft, under-reported batches and private sales of surplus. Reconcile ingredients issued to output, and keep the owner present at the bakery if it is separate from the shop.
- Packaging (rigid plastic tubs, paper bags) is a competitive lever as well as a cost.

## Set-up scale (choose a model)
Home-based (small oven, mixer, trays, initial ingredients), shop-front bakery, or production-plus-shop hybrid; capital rises through these three. Cost each from supplier quotations.

## Unit economics drivers
- Cost per pack = ingredients (flour, fat, sugar, eggs, flavourings) + packaging + fuel or energy + labour per batch; state the margin from your own costing.
- Batch yield, oven capacity and bake cycles set daily output; break-even packs per month = fixed costs / unit contribution.
- Labour: model casual bakers by day, shop assistants by month, and a possible per-session or per-batch payment for skilled bakers.

## Regulation and licensing checks
Food-manufacturing standards and premises approval from the standards body and health authority (KEBS, UNBS, TBS, RBS), business licence, staff medical certificates, labelling rules. Verify current requirements before choosing premises.

## Risks
Dependence on one baker, theft, supermarket credit terms and delisting, ingredient price swings, imitation by large brands, thin willingness to pay.

## KPIs
Packs per week, gross margin per pack, batch yield variance, repeat-order share, wholesale share, wastage.

## Plan-section prompts
Which format (home, shop, hybrid) and why? Which flavours and formats are missing locally, with evidence? Which channel carries the first year? How are recipes and stock controlled?
