# Non-Woven Bag Manufacturing

Reference for the business-plan sections of a polypropylene (PP) non-woven bag factory. Demand was created by polythene-bag bans. This is a high-capital, high-volume, factory-scale business. All figures are planning assumptions from a 2017 Kenyan reference; re-verify each.

## 1. Market and customer
- Segments: supermarkets and retail chains (steady branded volume, 30 to 90 day credit; approach mid-tier chains first), mass market through distributors to kiosks and market vendors, and corporate buyers of branded bags (shorter credit, higher unit value).
- Bag types: T-shirt bag (mass market), D-cut (supermarkets, branded retail), handle or tote (corporate, premium), zipper (specialty, reusable). Lighter fabric (about 50 gsm) suits everyday use; heavier grammages are more durable and command a premium.
- Demand is regulatory and durable; the competitive threat is cheap imported bags. Local advantages: faster delivery, no import duty, customisation at low minimum orders, local compliance.

## 2. Operating model
Convert imported PP fabric into bags on cutting, forming and printing lines; pack, store and dispatch. Size capacity as bags per minute x hours per day x working days x realistic efficiency; do not assume full sell-through.

## 3. Inputs and suppliers
- PP non-woven fabric, imported (about 100 light bags per kg). Monthly fabric need = bags per month / bags per kg. A container carries roughly 11 to 12 tonnes; lead time from China about six weeks.
- Hold at least six weeks of stock. Landed fabric cost = FOB + sea freight + inland transport + duty; re-quote every order.
- Planning assumption: fabric traded around USD 1,400 to 1,800 per tonne in 2017; verify.

## 4. Equipment and capex
- Options: separate machines per bag type (highest capital); a combination 5-in-1 machine (recommended entry, covers most mass-market formats); a single T-shirt-bag machine (limits the range). Add a printing machine.
- Premises: minimum about 5,000 sq ft covering production, warehouse, office, toilets, changing and first-aid rooms; machine footprints run 10 to 13 m long, so check hall length.
- Start-up capital checklist: rent deposit, renovation, equipment and shipping, power connection, communications, legal and registration, licences, delivery vehicles, furniture and fittings, stationery, opening fabric stock, first-month wages, transport, insurance, contingency. Working capital and stock, not machines, are the largest items; in the 2017 reference equipment was under half of start-up capital.

## 5. Unit economics drivers
Monthly cost = fabric (dominant, about 85% of total) + wages + rent + power + transport + communications + marketing + other. Margin per bag = factory price - fabric cost per bag - conversion. Break-even bags per month = fixed costs / contribution per bag.
The 2017 reference assumed a minimum factory price of about KES 8 per D-cut bag and 100 bags per minute for 20 hours a day; treat this as a ceiling case and plan on lower utilisation and price competition at launch.

## 6. Staffing
General or operations manager, accountant, marketing and sales manager, salespeople, production manager, administrative manager, machine operators per shift, line packers, store keeper, drivers, caretaker. Use current local wage data.

## 7. Regulation and licensing checklist
Company registration and tax PIN; county business permit (trade, fire, health, advertising); certificate of health; workplace safety approval; environmental licence (Kenya NEMA, Tanzania NEMC); standards quality audit and product certification (KEBS, UNBS, TBS, RBS). Verify current fees and rules; bag-ban rules differ by country and change.

## 8. Distribution
Split the country into regions; appoint regional wholesalers as distributors; accept direct factory orders above a minimum quantity to improve cash flow against long supermarket credit.

## 9. Risks and KPIs
Risks: fabric price and exchange-rate swings, shipping delay, import competition, credit exposure to chains, machine downtime, ban-policy changes, underutilised capacity.
KPIs: bags per shift, machine efficiency, fabric waste percentage, cost per bag, stock weeks, debtor days, sales by segment, on-time delivery.

## 10. East Africa localisation
- Uganda: partial polythene ban since 2009; production in Kampala industrial zones; fabric via Mombasa by road; smaller market, less local competition; cross-border supply from Kenya is possible.
- Tanzania: single-use bag ban from 2019; Dar es Salaam is a large mass-market opportunity; fabric import via Dar port.
- Rwanda: first EA country to ban polythene (2008); smaller market; fabric import is costly; hotel and conference sector offers branded-bag demand; investment agency may waive machinery duty.

## 11. Plan-section prompts
Market (bag types by segment, ban rules); Operations (capacity, layout, shifts); Capex and working capital (start-up checklist); Financials (fabric sensitivity, utilisation scenarios, credit terms); Regulation; Risks.
