# Wire Nail Manufacturing

Reference for the business-plan sections of a small wire-nail plant using imported automatic nail-making machines. Figures are planning assumptions from a 2018 East African reference; re-verify each with a dated quotation.

## 1. Market and customer
Buyers: hardware shops (dominant), building contractors and construction sites, institutional and government tenders, wholesale distributors. Demand tracks construction activity. Nails are near-commodity: consistent quality, reliable supply and hardware-owner relationships matter, although a small per-kg gap can decide wholesale orders. The main threat is cheap imported nails landing close to local cost per kg.

## 2. Operating model
Wire is fed to the machine, cut and headed, then polished (tumbled) and packed by weight. One kg of wire yields about one kg of nails; plan a small waste allowance (about 5 kg per tonne). Model output as machine speed x efficiency x wire diameter x nail length; longer, thicker nails yield more kg per shift.

## 3. Inputs and suppliers
- Finished wire (ready to use): higher price, no drawing machine needed.
- Raw wire (rod): lower price, needs a wire-drawing machine (extra capex).
- Sources: local steel producers, independent suppliers, informal metal markets. Wire is 85 to 90% of production cost, so supplier price, credit terms and steel-price volatility are the key sensitivities. Hold at least four weeks of wire stock.

## 4. Equipment and capex
- Options: Chinese Z-series machines (choose by nail range: small, medium, large; umbrella or roofing nails need a specific model), Indian machines of roughly 350 pieces per minute, plus a polisher and grinder.
- Planning assumptions (2018 FOB; apply an uplift for inflation and freight; re-quote): basic set about USD 8,000 to 11,000; full-range set about USD 15,000 to 18,000; a single Indian machine about USD 6,500.
- Landing costs: sea freight, import declaration fee, railway or port levies, clearing agent, documentation. Machinery may be duty-free while spares and consumables are dutiable; verify current tariff.
- Premises: about 3,000 to 5,000 sq ft; strong floor for machine weights; good lighting, ventilation and noise control; raw-wire and finished-goods stores; admin area; renovation allowance.

## 5. Unit economics drivers
Gross margin per kg = selling price - wire cost per kg. Monthly output = kg per shift x days. Contribution must cover salaries (operators, polisher, packers, manager, sales, guard), rent, power, transport, communication and marketing.
Illustration (2018 Kenyan planning assumptions; re-verify): finished wire about KES 95 to 115/kg, raw wire about KES 70 to 90/kg, nails sold about KES 110/kg, giving margin of about KES 15/kg on finished wire and about KES 30/kg on raw wire; output about 600 to 700 kg per 8-hour shift on one machine. On these figures net profit after overheads is thin on finished wire and only reasonable on raw wire, and only if everything produced is sold. Build the plan on a sensitivity table of wire price against selling price.

## 6. Pricing
Price per kg by nail size, benchmarked against local hardware wholesale prices surveyed at plan date. Offer volume discounts to distributors and contractors; do not lead with the lowest price.

## 7. Regulation and licensing checklist
Company and tax registration; local trade licence; product standards certification for construction fasteners (Kenya KEBS, Uganda UNBS, Tanzania TBS, Rwanda RBS); environmental and occupational safety approvals; investment-agency incentives where available (for example Rwanda RDB machinery duty relief). Verify current requirements.

## 8. Risks
Steel price swings; imported-nail competition; slow-paying distributors; machine wear and spares availability; noise and safety; standards non-compliance; selling less than capacity.

## 9. KPIs
kg produced per shift, machine utilisation, wire waste percentage, margin per kg, wire stock days, debtor days, share of sales by channel.

## 10. East Africa localisation
- Uganda: industrial zones around Kampala; steel input availability and price vary; import via Mombasa; local product certification required.
- Tanzania: strong construction demand; local steel and imported wire; Dar es Salaam port import; standards enforcement increasing.
- Rwanda: most nails imported, so little domestic competition; wire must be imported, adding cost; certified product needed.
- Across the region compete on quality consistency, delivery speed and relationships, not price alone.

## 11. Plan-section prompts
Market (demand by channel, import price benchmark); Operations (layout, shift pattern); Capex (landed-cost build-up from three quotes); Financials (wire versus selling price sensitivity, full-capacity versus realistic sales); Risks.
