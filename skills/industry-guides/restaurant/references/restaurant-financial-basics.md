# Restaurant Financial Statements, Forecasting and Cost Targets

Use this reference to structure the financial statements, revenue forecast and cost-control targets for a restaurant business plan. Ranges are planning assumptions; re-verify them with a dated local source. Verify accounting and tax treatment with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 1. Financial statements

### 1.1 Income statement (restaurant format)

Follow the hospitality uniform-system layout:

```
Sales: food, beverage -> total sales (100%)
Cost of sales: food, beverage -> gross profit
Operating expenses: salaries and wages; employee benefits; direct operating expenses;
  music and entertainment; marketing; utilities; general and administrative;
  repairs and maintenance; occupancy; depreciation; other income
= Operating income
- Interest
= Income before income tax
- Income tax
= Net income   (show each line as a % of total sales)
```

Features to keep: food and beverage reported separately; cost of sales split by food and beverage with gross profit by department; expenses grouped as controllable (payroll through repairs) and non-controllable (occupancy, depreciation); operating income marks the point below which costs are mostly outside the manager's control.

### 1.2 Balance sheet

- **Assets:** current (cash, net receivables, food and beverage inventory, marketable securities, prepaid expenses); fixed (land, buildings, furniture and equipment, leasehold improvements, linen, china, glassware, silverware, uniforms); investments; other (goodwill, liquor licence, deposits, organisation and pre-opening costs, franchise fees).
- **Liabilities:** current (trade and other payables, notes payable, wages payable, payroll and withholding taxes, sales tax, banquet deposits, income tax, current portion of debt); long-term (mortgage, notes, bonds, capital leases).
- **Owners' equity.**
- Check: assets = liabilities + owners' equity.

### 1.3 Cash-flow statement

Three sections: operating (net income plus non-cash items such as depreciation, less gains, adjusted for working-capital changes); investing (purchase and sale of investments and equipment); financing (debt repayment, dividends, share issues). Reconcile the net movement to the change in cash on the balance sheet.

## 2. Revenue forecast

Forecast food, beverage and other income separately, monthly or weekly, then total.

Factors: sales history and trend; local economy and employment; competitor openings, closures and pricing; menu and price changes; planned marketing; capacity changes (seats, meal periods, hours); seasonal patterns.

```
Revenue = seats x seat turnover x average check x days open
Meals per period = days open x seat turnover x seats
Average price per period = revenue for the period / meals in the period
Revenue for a period = total food revenue x share earned in that period
```

Seat turnover differs by concept (fast food highest, fine dining lowest). Planning range for full-service concepts is about 1.5-3.0 per meal period; verify it against a comparable local operator. Set turnover separately for each meal period, day of week and season, and for restaurant versus bar.

## 3. Food cost

```
Food cost % = cost of food sold / food sales x 100
Cost of food sold = opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory - staff meals - food used as beverage garnish
Gross profit (food) = food sales - cost of food sold
Food inventory turnover = cost of food used / average food inventory   (days in stock = 365 / turnover)
```

- Planning range for full-service food cost is about 30-40% of food sales, with many operators using about 40% as a ceiling; re-verify locally.
- Staff meals are a labour cost and garnishes are a beverage cost, so cost of food used differs from cost of food sold.
- Food cost is variable: a 10% rise in food sales should raise cost of food sold by about 10%.
- Causes of a rising food cost percentage: costs rising faster than menu prices; sales mix shifting to dearer dishes; understated closing stock; non-food purchases booked as food; portion inconsistency, waste, spoilage and theft. A shift to a more profitable mix can raise the percentage legitimately.
- Higher inventory turnover is better, provided stock-outs stay rare.

## 4. Labour cost

```
Labour cost % = (payroll + employee benefits + employment taxes) / total sales x 100
Payroll = fixed payroll (salaries) + variable payroll (hourly wages)
```

- Fixed payroll is set by the owner; variable payroll is the manager's main control above minimum staffing.
- Labour expense always exceeds payroll because of benefits and employment taxes; controllable costs are the payroll; statutory or negotiated benefits are not.
- Planning range for total labour is roughly 30-35% of sales for a full-service restaurant; re-verify locally. Labour is usually the second-largest cost after cost of sales.
- Analyse per full-time equivalent: total sales, food sales and beverage sales per FTE; average hourly wage; and payroll by category (service, bar, preparation, sanitation, purchasing and storing, administration, other) as a share of total payroll.

## 5. Prime cost

```
Prime cost = cost of food sold + cost of beverages sold + total labour cost
Prime cost % = prime cost / total sales
```

Planning range is about 60-70% of sales, with the better-run operations at the lower end; re-verify locally. Combine planned food and labour percentages and compare.

### Simple prime-cost pricing

1. Labour cost per guest = budgeted labour cost / expected guests.
2. Prime cost per guest = labour cost per guest + ingredient cost of the dish.
3. Base price = prime cost per guest / desired prime cost %.

Round to a sensible price point and test against competitors.

## 6. Operating expense categories

Controllable: salaries and wages; employee benefits; direct operating expenses (uniforms, laundry, supplies, menus, kitchen fuel, dining-room and storage items); music and entertainment (only if significant); marketing; utilities (except kitchen fuel; record utilities paid on a rented premises as utilities, not rent); general and administrative (office, professional fees, card fees, telephone, postage, insurance other than staff); repairs and maintenance. Non-controllable: occupancy (rent, property tax, property insurance) and depreciation.

Compute each as expense / total revenue. Expenses with a fixed component should fall as a percentage as sales grow. Most restaurants earn a net margin below about 10%, so more than 90% of revenue is consumed by costs; state the target margin and justify it.
