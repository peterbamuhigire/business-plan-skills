# Catering Operations: Staffing, Menu, Scaling and KPIs

Task reference for the operations, staffing and targets sections of a restaurant plan that adds a catering channel. Read with `catering-strategy.md`.

All monetary figures below are illustrative planning assumptions in UGX. Re-verify every one against a dated local source (supplier quotes, competitor price lists, own trial data) before it enters the plan.

## 1. Staffing model

| Role | Job to be done |
|---|---|
| Catering director or owner-manager | Owns catering strategy, P&L and growth targets; documents procedures and trains staff |
| Catering sales lead | Business-to-business (B2B) prospecting by telephone, visit and sampling |
| Customer service representative | Scripted order entry; follow-up call after every order |
| Delivery coordinator | Plans routes the day before; assigns orders to drivers; controls labels and packing slips |
| Driver | Vehicle upkeep; delivery; asks for referrals; helps production between runs |
| Kitchen catering lead | Confirms pre-production; leads assembly, packaging and quality checks |

Decision rules:
- Use the existing labour pool first: add catering tasks to current roles (counter staff prepare beverages and fold boxes in quiet periods).
- Add dedicated posts only when volume justifies them.
- Aim to lower labour cost per unit by spreading labour across retail and catering revenue.
- Multi-outlet or franchise groups: centralise sales, marketing, order taking, payment processing and receivables; outlets focus on order execution (production and distribution).

## 2. Catering menu design

Principle: the catering menu is a subset of what travels well, not an extension of the dine-in menu. Build it from existing ingredients and flavour profiles, keep it simpler, and change packaging, pricing and presentation.

| Format | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Trays and platters | Cornerstone; three sizes (small about 6-10 people, medium 10-15, large 15+); price per platter, not per head |
| Boxed lunches | Worthwhile only at volume; suit tours, schools, seminars; set a minimum order |
| Beverages | Signature drinks sold in bulk containers; limit the range; margins exceed commodity drinks |
| Hot food | Limited menu; needs specialist packaging and holding equipment; not advised for a first launch |

- Start with cold programmes (breakfast platters, fruit, sandwiches, salads). Corporate demand peaks at breakfast and lunch.
- Use branded, custom packaging; compare quotes, because custom corrugated boxes are often cheaper than generic trays and act as an advertisement in offices.
- Remove any item that does not survive transport and holding time.

## 3. Scaling framework

1. Strategy and alignment: secure owner or board agreement, write a catering business plan, document the strategy.
2. Prove the model: run at one or a few outlets first; refine menu, systems and a policy-and-procedure manual from real experience.
3. Scale: roll out across outlets or franchisees; centralise services; outlets execute orders.

Growth drivers: relationship selling, promotional sampling, in-store cross-selling to retail customers, contracts with large accounts, referrals, follow-up on every order.

Scaling risks: catering and retail compete for staff, space and equipment at outlet level; redesign roles, keep the two transaction flows separate, and never let catering calls degrade the walk-in experience.

## 4. Unit economics drivers

Compute targets from drivers rather than copying benchmarks:

```
Daily catering revenue  = orders per day x average order value
Annual revenue          = daily revenue x trading days per week x productive weeks
Catering share of sales = catering revenue / total revenue
```

Illustrative assumptions to test locally: catering at roughly one fifth to one third of sales; contribution to gross margin above its share of sales because it leverages existing premises, equipment and brand; a modest number of orders per day per outlet; average order sized for a typical office group. Replace each with observed data from trial orders.

Sensitivity to show in the plan: one more order per day per outlet; average order value up or down 15%; delivery cost per order.

## 5. KPIs

Operations: catering sales per outlet per day; orders per day; average order value and headcount; on-time delivery rate; order accuracy; menu mix (top items by revenue); pre-production versus day-of assembly efficiency.

Sales and customer: dormant-client rate (no order in 60-90 days); lead-to-first-order time; order frequency per client; retention; referrals generated; position on clients' preferred-supplier rotation; sample-to-customer conversion; follow-up completion.

Financial: catering gross margin percentage against retail; catering share of total gross margin; labour cost per unit (should fall with volume); receivables ageing; incremental cost per shilling of catering revenue; delivery and packaging cost per order.

## 6. Launch checklist

- [ ] Owner or board commitment recorded
- [ ] Catering business plan written
- [ ] Catering lead appointed
- [ ] Simple menu built from existing ingredients
- [ ] Branded, cost-effective packaging sourced
- [ ] Policy-and-procedure manual written
- [ ] Prices set at a premium to retail
- [ ] Invoicing separate from the till; credit and house-account policy set
- [ ] Staff trained in catering terms and scripts
- [ ] In-outlet marketing displayed
- [ ] Sampling package designed
- [ ] B2B prospecting under way
- [ ] Delivery logistics planned
- [ ] Follow-up system running
- [ ] Metrics tracked

## 7. Psychology of the order-placer

The person ordering is accountable inside their organisation and short of time. Reduce their stress by giving: consistent service, accurate orders (dietary needs, timing), a scripted ordering process, proactive communication, flawless execution, and ease (credit accounts, simple menus).

## 8. Catering versus retail

| Dimension | Retail | Catering |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Individual | Organisation, via an order-placer |
| Payment | Cash or mobile money at till | Invoice, credit account, receivables |
| Timing | Immediate | Advance order |
| Size | One person | Groups of 10-50+ |
| Production | Made to order | Bulk assembly |
| Price sensitivity | High | Lower (corporate budget) |
| Selling | Passive | Active outbound |
| Follow-up | Optional | Required on every order |

## 9. Plan-section prompts

- Which outlet, staff and vehicles carry the catering load, and what is the incremental cost?
- What is the target order volume by month, and what evidence supports it?
- What credit terms are offered, and what is the bad-debt allowance?
- Which licences and food-safety certificates cover off-premises service? Verify with the local health authority.
- Tax and accounting treatment of catering revenue: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.
