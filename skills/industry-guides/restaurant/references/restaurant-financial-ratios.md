# Restaurant Ratios, Capital Budgeting and Pricing Formulas

Use this reference for the ratio dashboard, capital-cost treatment and objective pricing methods in a restaurant business plan. Benchmarks are planning ranges; re-verify them with a dated local source and label them as assumptions. Verify depreciation, amortisation and tax treatment with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 1. Ratios

### Liquidity
- Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities.
- Receivables turnover = charge sales / average receivables; collection period = 365 / turnover. Compare with credit terms; a period well above terms signals a collection problem.

### Solvency
- Solvency ratio = total assets / total liabilities (solvent above 1.0).
- Debt-equity ratio = total liabilities / owners' equity; higher means more risk and harder access to more finance.
- Times interest earned = (net income + interest + income tax) / interest.

### Activity
- Food inventory turnover = cost of food used / average food inventory; days in inventory = 365 / turnover.
- Fixed-asset turnover = total revenue / average fixed assets.
- Asset turnover = total revenue / average total assets.
- Seat turnover = guests served / (days open x available seats); calculate by meal period, day, season and profit centre (restaurant versus bar).

### Profitability
- Return on equity = net income / average owners' equity.
- Return on assets = net income / average total assets.
- Profit margin = net income / total revenue (most restaurants earn below about 10%).
- Managerial operating efficiency = (operating income + depreciation + occupancy costs) / total revenue; it isolates what the manager controls.
- Earnings per share (companies) = net income / average shares.

### Operating
- Food and beverage sales mix = each / total sales; a higher beverage share usually lifts profit because of higher beverage margins.
- Sales per seat: food sales / restaurant seats; beverage sales / bar seats (annual).
- Food cost %, beverage cost %, labour cost % as defined in `restaurant-financial-basics.md`.

### Comparison bases (in order of preference)
1. Budget (built from history, competition and expectations).
2. Prior period (limited in a changing market).
3. Industry averages (useful for new operations; broad).
4. Named competitors (limited by data availability).

### Limits
Ratios rest on historical cost; accounting methods differ between businesses; ratios flag problems but do not explain them; a higher food cost percentage can accompany a more profitable mix.

## 3. Capital budgeting for a start-up

### Capital versus revenue expenditure
Capitalise equipment, buildings, land and leasehold improvements; expense repairs, maintenance and supplies. Fixed-asset cost includes purchase price, taxes and duties, freight, transit insurance, installation and, for construction, materials, labour, overheads, architect fees, permits and construction finance.

### Start-up cost treatment (indicative; confirm with the finance engine)
- Organisation costs (legal, promotion, accounting, registration): spread over about 3-5 years.
- Pre-opening costs (pre-opening payroll, training, promotion): spread over about 1-3 years.
- Franchise fee: spread over the franchise term; ongoing royalties are expensed as incurred.
- Liquor licence: record as an other asset and spread over a reasonable period.
- Leasehold improvements: spread over the shorter of lease term and asset life.

### Depreciation
- Straight line: (cost - salvage) / useful life.
- Double declining balance: rate = 2 / years; charge = rate x undepreciated balance (ignore salvage in the calculation; total depreciation must not exceed cost less salvage). This front-loads charges; use one method consistently for each asset.
- Uniforms, linen, china, glassware, silver and utensils: capitalise the initial stock, depreciate over a short period (36 months maximum is a common approach), expense replacements when issued, and hold reserve stock as inventory until used.

## 4. Objective pricing methods

| Method | Formula | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Markup on ingredients | Ingredient cost x (1 / food cost %) | Simple and widely used |
| Contribution margin | Ingredient cost + (non-food costs + profit) / expected guests | Per-guest costs similar except food |
| Ratio pricing | Ingredient cost + ingredient cost x (non-food costs plus profit) / food cost | Reflects the food to non-food cost relationship |
| Simple prime cost | (Ingredient cost + labour per guest) / desired prime cost % | Captures the two largest costs |
| Bottom-up | Start from required return on investment, derive required revenue and average meal price | Ties price to investor return |

- Markup multiplier = 1 / budgeted food cost %; alternatively budgeted food revenue / allowable food cost.
- Allowable food cost = forecast revenue - (all non-food costs + required profit); allowable food cost % = allowable food cost / forecast revenue.
- Food and beverage profit split: the food department covers its own cost plus its prorated share of non-product costs; the beverage department must deliver the remaining required profit, which sets its allowable cost and markup.

## 5. Formula quick list

```
Gross profit = sales - cost of sales
CM ratio = CM / selling price
Break-even meals = fixed costs / CM per meal
Break-even revenue = fixed costs / CM ratio
Margin of safety = actual sales - break-even sales
Prime cost % = (food + beverage cost + labour) / total sales
Effective interest = annual interest / (loan - compensating balance)
Markup multiplier = 1 / desired food cost %
```
