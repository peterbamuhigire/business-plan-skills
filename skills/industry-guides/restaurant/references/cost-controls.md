# Restaurant Cost Controls: Core Formulas, Benchmarks, Income Statement and Cost Determination

Task reference for the costing, margin and staffing-cost sections of a restaurant plan. Pricing, portioning, inventory, break-even, menu engineering and daily tracking are in `cost-controls-advanced.md`.

Percentages below are illustrative planning ranges; re-verify against a dated local source before use. Tax and accounting treatment: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 1. Core formulas

Costing triangle (each formula rearranges into the others):

```
Cost / Sales x 100 = Cost %
Cost / Cost % (decimal) = Sales or selling price
Sales x Cost % (decimal) = Cost
```

Apply to food, beverage and labour. Also:

```
Prime cost = food cost + beverage cost + labour cost (wages, salaries, benefits)
Overhead   = every other cost (rent, utilities, insurance, depreciation, interest)
Average check = total sales / covers
Average sale per server = server's sales / customers served
Sales per seat = annual sales / seats
Seat turnover = customers / seats
Sales mix % = units of item sold / total units sold x 100
Covers per hour, per day, per server = covers / hours, days, servers
```

## 2. Benchmark structures (planning ranges)

| Line | Quick-service pattern | Full-service pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Food and beverage cost | higher share of sales | lower share of sales |
| Labour | lower share | higher share |
| Other costs | similar | similar |
| Profit before tax | thin | thin |

The two patterns trade food cost against labour cost, with prime cost commonly in the region of 60-70% of sales. A well-run operation holds prime cost near the low end and earns a pre-tax margin of about 10%; a poorly run one shows food and labour both high and margin in low single digits. Typical ranges to test locally: food cost 25-42% of food sales; beverage cost roughly 18-35% (spirits lowest, wine and beer higher); labour 15-45% of sales. Costs as percentages of sales must total below 100% for the plan to be profitable, and each restaurant's structure must be set from its own concept.

## 3. Income statement layout

1. Sales: food, beverage, total.
2. Cost of sales: food, beverage, total. Gross profit.
3. Controllable expenses: salaries and wages, employee benefits, other controllable expenses.
4. Income before occupancy, interest, depreciation and tax.
5. Occupancy, interest, depreciation.
6. Restaurant profit.

Fixed versus variable split (needed for break-even): treat occupancy, other controllable costs, interest, depreciation and the fixed portion of payroll (managers, chefs, bookkeepers) as fixed; food and beverage cost and the volume-linked portion of payroll (servers, dishwashers, prep cooks) as variable, with benefits allocated in proportion. Derive the split from the plan's own staffing table.

## 4. Monthly food cost determination

```
Opening inventory + purchases = total available
- closing inventory = cost of food issued
+ cooking liquor and transfers in
- food to bar, transfers out, staff-sale food, complimentary bar food, promotion food
= cost of food consumed
- employee meals
= cost of food sold
Food cost % = cost of food sold / food sales x 100
```

Employee meals can be costed by separate requisitions, a set amount per meal, a set amount per period, or sales value x average food cost %. Choose one and apply it consistently.

## 5. Monthly beverage cost determination

```
Opening beverage inventory + purchases - closing inventory = issued to bar
+/- bar inventory differential (opening bar stock - closing bar stock)
= cost of beverages consumed
```

Adjust by adding food items sent to the bar (fruit, cream), storeroom issues (olives, garnishes) and mixers, and subtracting cooking liquor, managers' drinks and special promotions. Compute beverage cost % by category (spirits, wine, beer) as well as overall.

Three monitoring approaches:
1. Cost approach: actual cost against standard cost.
2. Liquid measure approach: volume sold against volume consumed.
3. Sales value approach: potential sales value against actual takings.

Standard cost method: for each drink, units sold x standard cost per drink; sum, then compare with actual cost to find variances. Track cumulatively day by day as for food.

## 6. Labour cost control

```
Labour cost % = (salaries + wages + benefits) / total sales x 100
Standard work hours = employees x hours worked
Covers per standard work hour = covers / standard work hours
Standard labour cost = standard work hours x hourly rate
```

- Classify staff as variable (servers, bussers, dishwashers, prep cooks) or fixed (managers, chefs, cashiers, bookkeepers).
- Build a standard staffing table by cover band (servers, warewashers, prep staff) from observed trading, and a matching standard work-hours table.
- Scheduling: keep daily and hourly cover records; use the median (not the mean) volume; derive staffing tables from a test period; schedule hourly to demand; use split shifts and part-timers for peaks; record maximum, minimum and median hourly volumes.
- Working guide: about 20 covers per server per hour, varying by service style.
- Verify statutory wage, pension and leave costs with current local law and the Chwezi finance engine.

Sources consulted: Dittmer, P.R. and Keefe, J.D., *Principles of Food, Beverage, and Labor Cost Controls*, 9th ed. (2009).
