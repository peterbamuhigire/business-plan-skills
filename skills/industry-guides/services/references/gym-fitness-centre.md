# Gym / Fitness Centre

Use this reference when a plan proposes a gym, from a strength-only or aerobics-only studio to a full combination gym. Capital is high and break-even needs patience.

## 1. Market and customer

Health awareness and social pressure are growing demand, but gym users remain a small share of the urban population, so headroom exists. Competition is intensifying in urban estates. Gyms in higher-income areas or near corporate offices sustain higher fees; peri-urban towns near administrative centres offer less competition and urbanising consumers. Test the catchment: households and offices within a short travel time, competitor prices and peak-hour crowding.

## 2. Gym types

| Type | Focus | Key equipment |
|---|---|---|
| Strength or muscle gym | Weightlifting, bodybuilding | Racks, benches, barbells, weight plates, cable machine |
| Aerobics gym | Cardio and dance fitness | Mats, steps, mirrors, sound system |
| Combination (standard) | Strength, cardio and aerobics | All of the above plus treadmills, bikes, rowing machine |

A single-focus gym needs far less capital than a combination gym; choose by demand evidence.

## 3. Equipment and premises

- Buy commercial-grade equipment: home-grade fails quickly under daily multi-user load and costs more over time.
- New versus second-hand: quality second-hand imports can be good value versus cheap new imports; compare warranties and spares.
- Flooring: commercial rubber flooring is non-negotiable (protects equipment, absorbs impact, prevents slips); compare branded and general-purpose rubber for cost and supply consistency.
- Equipment checklist: benches (flat, incline, decline, Olympic, preacher), leg press, squat and power racks, barbells and curl bars, dumbbells and plates (priced by weight), cable machine, rowing machine, spin or exercise bikes, treadmills, aerobics steps, skipping ropes, exercise balls, mirrors, sound system, television, reception counter, lockers, dumbbell racks, fire extinguisher, fans and fittings, signage.
- Premises: about 100 square metres for a full combination gym (planning assumption); partitioning, flooring installation, changing rooms (mandatory), showers where possible, ventilation, power capacity, sound insulation for neighbours.
- Obtain multiple supplier quotes for each item and date them; treadmill and cable-machine prices vary widely.

## 4. Operating model

- Opening hours: early morning (about 5 am) to late evening; morning and evening are peak; early opening is a competitive advantage.
- Membership: monthly subscriptions dominate, with daily passes for casual users; consider quarterly and annual discounts.
- Sign-in book or access-control software: essential for revenue tracking and membership management, especially for absentee owners.
- Aerobics classes need a qualified instructor; instructor quality is the main reason customers choose aerobics gyms.
- Staffing: trainers, front desk, cleaner; personal-training revenue share.

## 5. Unit economics drivers

- Monthly revenue = members x monthly fee + daily passes x day rate + personal training + retail (drinks, supplements).
- Fixed costs: rent, payroll, electricity, licences, equipment maintenance and replacement reserve.
- Break-even members = fixed costs / (fee - variable cost per member).
- Model membership ramp and churn honestly; plan for a long break-even (assumption to test: 12-18 months for a full set-up).
- Capital recovery = capex / monthly net contribution.
- Test smaller starts: a selective purchase of equipment for one category lowers capital sharply.

## 6. Regulation and licensing checks

Local authority single business permit; music copyright licence where music is played (Kenya: collecting-society licences; check local equivalents); signboard permit; fire clearance certificate; employment contracts and social-security registration for staff (NSSF in Uganda, RSSB in Rwanda); business registration (BRELA in Tanzania). Add public-liability and equipment insurance. Verify tax treatment with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 7. Risks

Injury and liability claims; high capital and slow payback; membership churn and seasonality; competitor price cutting; equipment failure; rent rises; power outages; reliance on one instructor or trainer.

## 8. KPIs

Active members; new members and churn per month; average revenue per member; peak-hour utilisation; class attendance; equipment downtime; revenue per square metre.

## 9. Localisation

- Uganda: Kampala estates and Entebbe; many gyms combine with juice bars or sports shops; equipment is widely available from importers.
- Tanzania: growing in Dar es Salaam and Arusha; high-end hotel gyms coexist with informal estate gyms.
- Rwanda: active markets in Kigali estates; employment contracts and social-security registration are consistently enforced; compact city means wider catchment.

## 10. Plan-section prompts

Gym type and evidence of demand; catchment and competitor pricing; equipment list with dated quotes; membership plan and ramp; staffing and instructor plan; break-even members; funding and downside case.

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya (CAB), modern gym business guide, 2016; general fitness-industry practice.
