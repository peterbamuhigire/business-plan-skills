# Bank Agency (Agent Banking)

Use this reference when a plan proposes operating an agent-banking outlet on behalf of a licensed bank. The model is commission-based, cash-flow intensive and works best as an add-on to an existing business.

## 1. Business model

A neighbourhood outlet, authorised under the central bank agent-banking framework and contracted by a commercial bank, offers deposits, withdrawals, account opening, balance enquiries, airtime, utility payments and loan repayments. Income is per-transaction commission only.

## 2. Market and customer

Agency banking closes last-mile access gaps for unbanked and under-banked customers where branches are scarce. Best sites: markets, trading centres, bus stages, residential estates, institutions and areas where mobile money already draws foot traffic. Mobile money (M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, Airtel Money) is complementary; many customers use both, so successful agents often offer both.

## 3. Eligibility (typical bank requirements; confirm with the chosen bank)

- Existing business trading for about a year or more.
- Strategic premises with adequate footfall.
- Valid business permit.
- Several months of current-account statements.
- National ID and certificate of good conduct.
- Clean credit reference bureau report.
- For companies or partnerships: constitutional documents, board resolution, financial statements.

## 4. Operating model

- The bank supplies a POS terminal, registers and branding, and trains the agent in transaction processing, fraud prevention and customer service.
- Working hours follow the host business.
- Float management is the core discipline: hold enough cash for withdrawals and enough e-float for deposits; running out turns customers away permanently.
- Security: cash-handling procedures, secure storage, counting routine, reconciliation at close.

## 5. Unit economics drivers

- Monthly commission = sum over transaction types of (transactions per day x operating days x commission per transaction).
- Commission schedules are set per bank and per transaction band, and change periodically. Obtain the current schedule in writing and date it in the plan.
- Model urban high-footfall, peri-urban and rural cases separately; rural outlets carry markedly lower daily volumes.
- Capital: the equity or float deposit required by the bank, application fees, plus cash float for withdrawals sized to peak daily withdrawals. Treat all figures as assumptions to verify.
- Payback: incremental commission versus incremental costs (float financing cost, security, extra staff time).

## 6. Regulation and licensing checks

- Kenya: Central Bank of Kenya agent-banking guidelines.
- Uganda: Bank of Uganda agent-banking regulations (2017); local authority trading licence.
- Tanzania: Bank of Tanzania agent-banking framework.
- Rwanda: National Bank of Rwanda agent-banking regulations.
- Verify accounting and tax treatment with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 7. Risks

Float shortages; robbery and fraud; commission changes by the bank; low volumes at poor sites; dependence on one bank; regulatory change; competition from mobile money and other agents.

## 8. Critical success factors

High-traffic location; a well-run anchor business (shop, pharmacy, mobile-money outlet); float discipline; choice of the bank with the largest local customer base and mobile-money integration.

## 9. KPIs

Transactions per day by type; commission per day; float-outs per month; cash variance; customer return rate; commission as a share of outlet income.

## 10. Plan-section prompts

State the anchor business, the chosen bank and why, site footfall evidence, the dated commission schedule, float sizing, security controls, and a downside case at low volume.

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya (CAB), agency banking profile, 2017; central bank agent-banking guidelines (Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda), to be re-read for current text.
