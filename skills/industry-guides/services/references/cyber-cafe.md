# Cyber Cafe (Internet and Digital Services Centre)

Use this reference when a plan proposes an internet cafe or digital service bureau (browsing, printing, copying, scanning, lamination, binding, photographs, assisted government e-services).

## 1. Market and customer

Demand segments: students (printing, downloads, project work), non-digital users needing help with government portals, bettors and gamers, casual browsers, shift workers and off-peak users. Browsing has been eroded by smartphones and cheaper mobile data; the durable opportunity is the service-bureau model. Plan the mix accordingly: pure browsing is a declining line; printing, binding, scanning, copying, photographs and assisted e-government services are the growth lines. Online betting brings reliable footfall but carries reputational risk in conservative communities: assess the local context before including it.

## 2. Location test

College proximity is the strongest single driver; government-office proximity brings assisted-service customers; extended hours (up to 24 hours in central districts) capture shift workers. Count competitor cafes and machine counts in the catchment; compare secondary towns (often stronger for cafes) with capital cities (smartphone-saturated).

## 3. Service lines and pricing

Browsing (per minute or block); printing (black-and-white, colour); photocopying; scanning; lamination; binding; passport photographs; assisted government e-services (fee for staff navigating portals). Price from a dated survey of three local competitors; printing and binding carry higher margin than browsing. Document assisted e-services as a distinct service line.

## 4. Equipment and operations

- Computers: buy fewer, reliable machines rather than more, cheaper ones; reliability is the customer's first concern. Use a supported operating system.
- Printer: a quality laser printer or multifunction copier for student volume; inkjets fail under heavy use.
- Internet: compare providers on reliability over price; unreliable internet destroys the business immediately; size bandwidth to seat count; consider a backup line.
- Power: UPS per workstation and generator or inverter backup.
- Other: binding and lamination machines, card readers, headphones, router and switches, cabling, furniture, signage, fan, fire extinguisher.
- Session logging and cash discipline: staff theft is a real risk; use cafe-management software and end-of-shift reconciliation.

## 5. Start-up cost checklist

Licences; computers, printer, networking, furniture; premises (deposit, rent, renovation); internet connection; working capital for about three months. Obtain current quotes for the chosen seat count and date them.

## 6. Regulation and licensing checks

- Local authority business permit (often banded by number of computers).
- Music or entertainment copyright licence if playing music (Kenya: collecting-society licences; check local equivalents).
- Reprographic (copying) licence where a reprographic rights body exists.
- Signboard and fire-compliance permits.
- Uganda: local trading licence; Tanzania: business licence and communications regulator rules; Rwanda: registration rules.
- Verify tax treatment with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 7. Unit economics drivers

- Revenue per day = seats x utilisation x average revenue per seat-hour + printing and other service revenue.
- Fixed costs: rent, staff, internet, licences, utilities; these are low, so daily revenue consistency is the main break-even lever.
- Break-even days of revenue per month = monthly fixed costs / average daily revenue.
- Compare college-adjacent and government-adjacent cases; test browsing rate and utilisation as sensitivities; add equipment replacement provision.

## 8. Risks

Continued erosion of browsing by mobile data; internet or power failure; staff theft; equipment failure and replacement cost; reputational risk from betting; licensing lapses; competition from phone-based services; rent rises.

## 9. KPIs

Revenue per seat per day; utilisation percentage; printing revenue share; assisted-service revenue share; internet uptime; cash variance; customer count per day.

## 10. Localisation

- Uganda: national ID, company registration, tax and social security services accessible through portals generate demand for assisted services.
- Tanzania: registration, tax and immigration services are demand drivers; portal adoption slower than Kenya.
- Rwanda: high smartphone penetration reduces browsing demand; assisted digital services remain in lower-income areas; secondary towns are stronger.

## 11. Plan-section prompts

Segment and location evidence; service mix with pivot away from browsing; competitor survey; seat and equipment plan; uptime plan; break-even days; downside case.

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya (CAB), cyber cafe business profile, 2017; general ICT-services practice.
