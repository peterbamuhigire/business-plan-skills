# Independent Petrol Station: Business-Plan Reference

Use this reference to plan an independently owned fuel station (not franchised to an oil marketing company). It is capital-intensive and technically demanding; it needs experienced management and a long lease. All figures are planning assumptions to be re-verified with dated quotes and the current regulator's price and licence schedules.

## 1. Market and customer
- Customers: passing motorists, commercial and freight vehicles, motorcycle taxis, and fleets.
- Site test: measure vehicle counts by type and time of day on the road, growth in the town, competitor stations and their volumes, and entry and exit geometry.
- Volume scale: stations range from poor to excellent by monthly litres; set a break-even volume and check it against realistic traffic projections. Allow at least a year to reach target volumes as a new independent.

## 2. Operating model
- Sale of fuel plus lubricants, with optional service bay, tyre centre, car wash, tyre inflation and a small shop.
- The service-centre model is the standard margin-improvement route because fuel margins per litre are thin.
- Buy fuel from brokers or wholesalers, or direct from marketers where the volume allows; brokers suit new entrants (small minimum loads, delivery, credit after a track record).

## 3. Fuel supply chain and buying procedure
- Typical chain: import terminal or refinery, pipeline depots, marketer depots, brokers and wholesalers, then independent stations. Regional variants exist (see section 10).
- On every delivery: check quantity (calibrated dipstick) and quality (testing kit); have a trusted person accompany the tanker from the depot; record reading before and after.
- Negotiate firmly on current market rates; brokers charge inexperienced buyers more.
- Maintain more than one supplier to avoid stock-outs.
- Where retail prices are regulated, the maximum pump price is a formula (wholesale price + permitted margin + delivery cost from the nearest depot); apply the current formula.

## 4. Equipment and capital (checklist)
- Approvals and professional fees: environmental impact assessment and consultancy, energy-regulator licence, construction permits, fire licence, public-health licence, outdoor advertising, company registration, weights-and-measures certification of dispensers, business permit, lawyers, architects, surveyors, insurance.
- Forecourt: fuel dispensers (single or double nozzle), underground tanks (size by days of cover), testing kits and dip sticks, pipes, generator.
- Office: underground safe, furniture, stationery.
- Construction: civil works, canopy, installation and piping, power, water and sewer connections, signboards, alarm and CCTV.
- Optional service centre: air compressor, wheel balancer, greasing machine, tyre changer, car-wash equipment, water storage.
- Opening stock: petrol, diesel, lubricants, and transport.
- Working capital for about three months: wages, rent, power, water.
- Capital = approvals + forecourt + office + construction + service centre + stock + working capital. Land purchase is excluded if the site is leased. A minimal one- or two-pump rural station costs far less; scale each line.

## 5. Unit economics and pricing
- Gross profit = litres sold x margin per litre. Margins per litre are thin and vary widely; plan conservatively at the low end and verify the current allowed margin and broker prices.
- Net margin per litre = pump price - purchase price - inbound transport per litre.
- Monthly break-even litres = monthly operating costs / net margin per litre.
- Volume benchmark tiers: poor, average, good, excellent; model at least three and stress the low tier.
- Operating expenses (mid-size station): labour (attendants, supervisor, manager), fuel delivery transport, rent, electricity (large kWh load; get a real bill estimate), security, water, miscellaneous.
- Non-fuel revenue: lubricants (margin far above fuel; VAT-registered; fiscal receipt machine may be required), car wash, wheel balancing and tyre services per job, air inflation (sometimes free to draw footfall).

## 6. Location principles
- High-traffic corridors, growing towns and major roads; easy entry and exit; proximity to depots to reduce transport per litre.
- Lease of at least ten years to recover capital (marketers may require longer for franchise conversion).
- Reject sites where break-even volume is unreachable on realistic traffic.

## 7. Operational risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Fuel adulteration by supplier | Accompany or observe loading; test on arrival |
| Revenue leakage by attendants | Underground safe, cash-drop protocol, shift-end reconciliation, pump versus tank reading cross-check |
| Dispenser calibration errors | Regular calibration jar tests on all dispensers |
| Stock-out | Reorder ahead; multiple suppliers |
| Robbery | Safe, guards, CCTV, limited cash on the forecourt |
| Power failure | Generator; essential for overnight trading |
| Fire and spill | Certified equipment, trained staff, environmental compliance, insurance |
| Price regulation shocks | Margin sensitivity; stock timing around price reviews |

## 8. Regulation and licensing checks
- Environmental impact assessment, energy or petroleum regulator licence, fire, public health, weights and measures, construction and planning approvals, business permit, tax registration.
- Kenya: environmental authority and energy regulator. Uganda: Petroleum Authority of Uganda, local trade licence, fire certificate, URA TIN; pump prices are market-set. Tanzania: EWURA dealer licence; EWURA sets monthly prices; BRELA and TRA registration; TBS standards for lubricants. Rwanda: RURA fuel retail licence; RDB and RRA registration.
- Tax and fiscal device rules: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 9. Staffing (planning drivers)
- Roles: attendants, supervisor, manager, security. Pay by local market rate; hire the manager for experience and honesty controls.

## 10. Regional localisation
- Uganda: supply largely via Mombasa with some lake routes; major marketers include Total, Vivo, Stabex and Gulf.
- Tanzania: refined products via Dar es Salaam; pipeline to the south-west; monthly regulated price.
- Rwanda: all products imported by road or pipeline; compact geography reduces transport cost differences; competitive Kigali market.
- Fundamentals are the same everywhere: thin margins, high volume, quality risk, leakage control.

## 11. KPIs
- Litres per day by product, margin per litre, dip variance (book versus physical stock), shrinkage, non-fuel revenue share, cost per litre sold, days of stock cover.

## 12. Plan-section prompts
- Site and traffic evidence; break-even litres.
- Licence and environmental programme with timings.
- Supply and quality control; capital table; scenarios; controls against leakage.
