# Chemist / Retail Pharmacy

Use this reference when a plan proposes a licensed retail chemist or pharmacy selling prescription and over-the-counter medicines, supplements and health products.

## 1. Business model and the licensing gate

A retail chemist must be superintended by a registered pharmacist or enrolled pharmaceutical technologist named on the premises licence. Non-professional investors therefore either employ or partner with a qualified professional. Decide the structure first because it drives cost and legal exposure:

- Employed or retained professional (monthly fee): common; state the fee as a planning assumption.
- Licence rental (a professional lends the licence for a fee, monthly or annual lump sum): widespread but legally risky; in Uganda it is explicitly illegal and both parties can be prosecuted; enforcement is stricter in Rwanda. A lump-sum arrangement gives the professional little incentive to renew. Do not recommend licence rental in a plan; present a compliant structure.
- Equity partnership with the pharmacist: aligns interests long term; document the terms.

## 2. Market and customer

Demand grows with urbanisation, chronic disease (diabetes, hypertension) and health awareness. Estate chemists face intense price competition; captive sites (hospital compounds, college campuses, malls) command higher margins but require relationships to secure. Many unregistered outlets operate in some markets; enforcement raids occur periodically and can lead to stock confiscation and prosecution, so plan on full compliance.

## 3. Regulation and licensing checks

- Kenya: Pharmacy and Poisons Board (PPB) retail licence and premises registration (Pharmacy and Poisons Act), county business permit, public health licence, signboard permit.
- Uganda: National Drug Authority (NDA); a Responsible Pharmacist is required; "drug shop" (limited over-the-counter) and "pharmacy" (full dispensing) are distinct categories.
- Tanzania: Tanzania Medicines and Medical Devices Authority (formerly TFDA) regulates; classes include full pharmacy and over-the-counter drug shops (duka la dawa baridi).
- Rwanda: Rwanda Food and Drugs Authority; pharmacist licence required; stricter enforcement.
- Confirm fees, category rules and premises standards with the regulator; fees are revised periodically.
- Company registration and tax: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 4. Operating model

- Range: prescription, OTC, supplements, baby and personal care; adjust to catchment (chronic-care in older estates, family products near schools).
- Stock strategy: baseline stock for a standard estate chemist versus a much larger range for a central or mall site; size stock to catchment demand and cash.
- Suppliers: several licensed wholesalers; compare prices, credit terms, delivery, authenticity guarantees; buy only from licensed sources to avoid counterfeits.
- Pricing: wholesale price lists carry a recommended retail (trade) price that is not binding; competitive estates trade at or below it, captive sites above. Same-product price gaps between neighbouring shops are common and customers learn them.
- Inspections: regulators inspect periodically; keep licences, registers, storage conditions and expiry controls audit-ready.
- Cold chain, controlled and prescription-only drug registers, expiry rotation (FEFO), and dispensing records.

## 5. Capital checklist

Licences and company registration; computer and POS or stock system; furniture, dispenser, signboard; premises (rent deposit and advance, renovation, counters and shelving); opening stock; working capital for about three months (staff, professional fee, utilities). Obtain current quotes and date them.

## 6. Unit economics drivers

- Gross margin by category = (retail price - landed cost) / retail price; margins range very widely by product, competition and location, so model categories separately (fast-moving generics, branded, chronic-care, OTC, cosmetics).
- Revenue = daily transactions x average basket x days.
- Contribution = revenue x blended margin - discounts - expiry write-offs - shrinkage.
- Fixed costs: rent, salaries, professional fee, licences, utilities.
- Break-even revenue = fixed costs / blended margin; add stock-holding cost (stock days x cost of capital).

## 7. Risks

Licensing breaches and raids; counterfeit or expired stock; price competition; credit sales; theft; over-stocking of slow lines; dependence on the licence holder leaving; regulatory change.

## 8. KPIs

Daily sales and transaction count; average basket; gross margin by category; stock days; expired stock as percentage of purchases; stock-outs on top 50 lines; prescription share.

## 9. Plan-section prompts

State the licensing structure and the named professional; premises class; catchment and competitor price survey; stock plan; supplier terms; compliance calendar; downside case.
