# Informal and Non-Deposit Money Lending: Business-Plan Reference

Use this reference to plan a licensed or lawfully registered lending business that does not take deposits. Rates, thresholds and legal positions below are planning assumptions; confirm them with a local lawyer and the current regulator position before operating. Lending law differs sharply by country.

## 1. Market and customer
- Segments: salaried employees needing advances, micro and small traders needing working capital, individuals needing short-term cash, owners of collateral (electronics, vehicles), and small companies needing invoice finance.
- Competitors: banks, microfinance institutions, SACCOs and chamas (see the SACCO reference), mobile and app lenders, and other informal lenders. Competition is the main moderator of rates.

## 2. Loan products

| Product | Description | Typical security |
|---|---|---|
| Salary advance | Short loan repaid from next salary | Employer letter, payslip, payroll deduction |
| Working capital | Loan for restocking or operations | Business assets, post-dated cheques |
| General personal | Short personal loan | Varies |
| Collateral-backed electronics | Loan against deposited electronics held by lender | The item itself |
| Mobile or app loans | Score-based, no collateral | Phone or mobile-money behavioural score |
| Invoice discounting | Short advance against unpaid invoices | Invoice and client relationship |
| Logbook loan | Loan against vehicle registration | Logbook |
| Corporate | Larger business loans | Business assets, directors' guarantees |

Documented delivery models worth studying: loans scored from mobile-money transaction history with automatic repayment deduction; daily or weekly collection from market traders on very small tickets; app-based lending scored on phone data; USSD micro-advances with a short repayment window; and peer-to-peer platforms that match lenders with verified borrowers for a fee.

## 3. Credit assessment: the Five Cs
- Character: repayment history, reputation, references.
- Capacity: income, cash flow, existing debts.
- Collateral: assets securing the loan.
- Credit record: track record with earlier lenders, formal or informal.
- Capital: the borrower's own stake.
Procedure: verify identity documents, inspect collateral physically, take references, compute repayment capacity, then set the amount.

## 4. Loan structuring and pricing
- Secured loans: cap the advance at a fraction of realisable collateral value (typically 30 to 50 per cent). Hold the collateral or register the lien. Verify ownership.
- Unsecured loans: start with small amounts, build limits with repayment history; payroll deduction reduces default risk.
- Interest: compute per period. Example calculation: advance x rate x months = interest; repayment = principal + interest. Informal rates quoted per month can be very high; compare with the effective annual rate and with legal limits where they exist.
- Rate planning range in the source market was widely spread (roughly 10 to 30 per cent per month for informal models, lower for app cycles); treat as a market observation, not a recommendation. Where a usury cap or licence-condition rate applies, use it.
- Portfolio arithmetic: expected yield = interest income x (1 - default rate) - cost of funds - operating cost. Model default rate explicitly.

## 5. Risk management

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Default, unsecured | Character and capacity assessment; small starting tickets; step-up limits |
| Default, secured | Hold collateral; value conservatively; verify title |
| Fraud | Identity verification, inspection, references |
| Legal dispute | Written, signed, witnessed contract; clear repayment schedule |
| Borrower insolvency or concentration | Diversify across borrowers and sectors |
- A written, signed contract is essential; oral agreements are difficult to enforce.
- Collection conduct: follow lawful methods only; harassment and unlawful seizure expose the lender to criminal and civil liability.

## 6. Capital and scale (checklist)
- Working capital is the business. Entry tiers: micro (personal network, salary advances, small vendor loans), small (mixed secured and unsecured, a few dozen active loans), medium (working-capital and logbook loans, company registration advised).
- Add: registration costs where applicable, business permit for premises, record-keeping system, legal templates, and a loss reserve.

## 7. Legal and regulatory checks
- Determine whether taking deposits is involved (this triggers banking or microfinance licensing) and whether lending as a business requires registration or a licence.
- Kenya: banking and microfinance law apply to deposit-takers; pure lenders rely on contract law and business registration, subject to current consumer-protection and digital-lender rules that must be verified. Uganda: the Tier 4 Microfinance Institutions and Money Lenders Act requires registration or licensing with the Uganda Microfinance Regulatory Authority even for non-deposit lenders. Tanzania: the Microfinance Act and Bank of Tanzania rules require registration for non-deposit lenders; BRELA registration. Rwanda: central bank and microfinance law; RDB registration; Umurenge SACCOs are a large rural credit source.
- Data protection and KYC obligations apply to credit scoring and borrower data.
- Tax and accounting (interest income, loan-loss provisioning under IFRS 9): verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 8. Regional localisation
- Mobile-money equivalents: Uganda MTN Mobile Money and Airtel Money; Tanzania Vodacom M-Pesa, Tigo (Mixx by Yas) and Airtel Money; Rwanda MTN Mobile Money and Airtel Money. All support collecting repayments and verifying transaction history through merchant or agent interfaces.
- Fintech credit scoring on mobile-money data is active in the region; study it as a competitor and as a method.
- Rates are lower where regulation and competition are stronger (for example Rwanda).

## 9. KPIs
- Portfolio at risk (30 and 90 days), default and write-off rate, yield on portfolio, average ticket, repayment rate, collateral cover, cost of funds, loans per staff member.

## 10. Plan-section prompts
- Legal basis: which law and licence apply and how the lender complies.
- Products: which loan types, tickets, tenors, security.
- Risk: scoring method, default assumptions, collection policy.
- Financials: capital deployed, expected yield net of default, cost of funds, break-even, scenarios.
