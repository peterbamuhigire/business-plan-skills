# Boda Boda (Motorcycle Taxi)

Use this reference when a plan proposes motorcycle passenger or goods transport, whether owner-operated, hired-rider or a fleet.

## 1. Market and customer

Boda bodas provide last-mile transport in urban estates, market towns and rural areas across East Africa. Growth followed tax relief on smaller motorcycles. Demand is passengers plus a growing goods and delivery stream (pharmacies, supermarkets, restaurants). Regulation is tightening (helmets, stage or SACCO registration, route limits), which raises compliance cost without stopping demand. Investor failure rates are high, mainly from rider dishonesty, breakdowns and accidents.

## 2. Business models

1. Owner-operator: the owner rides and keeps income after fuel and maintenance; most control, most personal time.
2. Owner plus hired rider: the rider pays a fixed daily remittance or shares revenue; income is passive but depends on rider honesty and reliability.
3. Fleet on hire purchase: several motorcycles financed and riders engaged; margin is rider payment less loan repayment, maintenance and losses; scalable but management-heavy.

## 3. Equipment: choosing the motorcycle

- Categories: budget new brands (lower resale, variable parts supply), mid-tier (good urban performance, strong resale), established premium makes (best reliability and resale), second-hand (cheapest, high risk without mechanic inspection).
- Engine size: 100-125 cc for fuel economy in town; about 150 cc for hills or goods.
- Choose the brand most liquid on the local resale market with parts nearby.
- Safety kit: helmets (rider and passenger), reflective jacket, toolkit.
- Financing: dealers, banks, SACCOs and microfinance lenders offer daily-repayment schemes; compare total cost, not the daily figure.

## 4. Start-up cost checklist

Motorcycle; insurance; stage or SACCO registration fee; riding licence if not held; helmet, jacket, toolkit; working capital for fuel and repairs. Obtain current dealer quotes and date them; all figures are assumptions.

## 5. Licensing and regulation checks

Riding licence and any permit for passenger carriage; vehicle registration; at least third-party insurance; local authority or county licence (annual); stage or SACCO membership (Kampala requires SACCO registration; Rwanda enforces uniform registration, numbered reflective vests and SACCOs); helmet-law compliance. Confirm current fees per jurisdiction.

## 6. Unit economics drivers

- Daily gross = trips per day x average fare, plus goods runs.
- Owner-operator daily net = gross - fuel - maintenance provision - stage fees - insurance and licence provision.
- Hired-rider model: owner net = daily remittance x working days - maintenance - insurance - licences - loan repayment - loss allowance (missed remittances, theft, accidents).
- Set remittance from the rider's realistic net; otherwise riders under-report or default.
- Add depreciation and replacement timeline to compute payback.

## 7. Risks and controls

- Dishonest riders: guarantors, logbook custody, GPS tracker, daily reconciliation.
- Breakdowns: service schedule, spare-parts fund.
- Accidents: insurance, helmets, rider training.
- Theft; police and licensing enforcement; fuel price rises; rider turnover.
- Owner presence or reliable supervision is critical.

## 8. KPIs

Trips per day; remittance collection rate; downtime days per month; fuel per km; maintenance cost per month; accident and claim count; rider retention.

## 9. Localisation

- Uganda: dominant in Kampala and all towns; SACCO registration; helmet law inconsistently enforced.
- Tanzania: competes with motorised tricycles (bajaji) in Dar es Salaam.
- Rwanda: strictest regulation and uniform registration; fares are higher.
- Goods boda: choose carrying capacity accordingly.

## 10. Plan-section prompts

Model selected and why; fleet size and financing schedule; rider vetting and controls; per-day cash model with downside; compliance list.
