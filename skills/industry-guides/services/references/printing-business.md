# Commercial Printing Business: Business-Plan Reference

Use this reference to choose printing technologies, size capital and plan a print shop. Printing spans several distinct technologies with different equipment, customers and capital needs. Costs below are ranges of magnitude, not quotes; re-verify with current dealer and importer prices. Most sustainable shops focus on one or two technologies and subcontract the rest.

## 1. Market and customer
- Customer groups: businesses (stationery, signage, packaging), politicians and campaigns (garments, posters), schools and institutions (uniforms, books), NGOs and event organisers (banners, branded items), households (documents, photos, custom gifts).
- Demand is won through networks and referrals rather than walk-in trade; marketing is the largest success factor.
- Demand seasons: signage is steady; garment and poster demand spikes in election years; custom gifts are trend-driven.
- Capacity: small and medium offset markets often have idle capacity; a new entrant needs an active sales plan.

## 2. Choosing technology (decision rules)
- Start from the customers you already have or can reach: businesses lean to offset or large format; campaigns and institutions lean to screen printing.
- Buy what capital allows; subcontract the rest; use subcontracted orders to measure demand before investing; upgrade as revenue grows.
- Rural towns: laser and screen printing for local demand; cities: all types can work.

| Product | Best technology |
|---|---|
| Books, brochures and flyers in hundreds of copies | Offset |
| Business cards (about 100 or more) | Offset or laser |
| Small-run posters | Laser or large format |
| Banners, billboards, outdoor signage | Large format |
| Garments in bulk (about 50 or more identical) | Screen printing |
| Custom garments (1 to about 20) | Heat transfer |
| Vehicle branding | Large format plus vinyl cutter |
| Mugs, cups, tiles, phone cases | Sublimation or heat transfer |
| Photocopies and documents | Laser |

## 3. Technology profiles

### Offset
- Uses: books, brochures, stationery, magazines, labels, high-volume runs. Dominant above roughly hundreds of copies where digital cost per unit is higher.
- Equipment: press (used or new; brand and age change price greatly), plate-making (computer-to-plate or older film and plate burner), guillotine, graphics computer, film-output inkjet, finishing (folders, binders, stitchers), premises, working capital.
- Capital is the highest of all technologies; a full operation sits in the millions of local currency units. Goodwill and rent in prime locations add substantially.

### Laser
- Uses: photocopying, short-run documents, business cards, certificates, stickers, small-run posters.
- Equipment: A3-capable laser machine, computer, toner stock. It is the default bureau technology; lowest-capital entry when combined with design services. Laser beats inkjet on cost per page for jobs above about 20 pages.
- Maximise the machine: sell design and specialty jobs, not only copies.

### Inkjet
- Uses: small documents, photos, disc-label printing, heat-transfer paper. Cannot stand alone; pair with a photo studio, typing bureau or heat-transfer set-up.

### Large format (outdoor)
- Uses: banners, billboards, vehicle branding, posters, signage, backlit displays, floor graphics, wallpaper.
- Equipment: large-format eco-solvent or UV printer (popular ranges up to about 64 inches wide), vinyl cutter or plotter (servo-motor preferred over stepper), RIP computer, media rolls (blackback banner, backlit, reflective vinyl, satin paper, one-way vision).
- Vehicle branding: print vinyl, then have a specialist apply it (price application per square metre).
- Extension: some printers acquire billboard sites and sell advertising space.
- Differentiators: ink quality, drying time, machine uptime. Check spare-parts availability and local technician support before buying.

### Screen printing
- Uses: garments, branded bags, caps, aprons, uniforms.
- Equipment: multi-station press, screens, vacuum exposure unit, squeegees, flash and blow dryers (tunnel dryer at volume), emulsion, inks and chemicals, computer and film-output inkjet.
- Process: artwork design; colour separation; film positives; screen making (coat, expose, wash); printing; drying.
- Most profitable at 50 or more identical items; below about 20 items, heat transfer or laser is cheaper. A cheap machine can produce commercial work if mechanically sound; test before purchase.

### Heat transfer and sublimation
- Uses: custom garments in small quantities, caps, mugs, phone cases, mouse pads, tiles.
- Equipment: multi-function heat press, vinyl cutter, inkjet, computer.
- Vinyl handles simple text and graphics only, not photographs. Sublimation gives photographic quality on polyester and coated items using sublimation paper and inks.
- Entry cost is low and so are margins per item; trends move quickly.

## 4. Capital build-up (checklist)
- Equipment for the chosen technology; computer and design software; premises deposit, rent and possible goodwill; licences; consumables and media stock; miscellaneous; working capital for several months.
- Compare new versus used machines and local versus imported ones (price against ink cost and support).

## 5. Unit economics and pricing
- Price per job = materials + machine time + labour + design time + margin. Compute cost per square metre for large format, cost per copy for laser and offset, cost per garment for screen and heat transfer.
- Track consumable cost drivers: toner, ink, media, plates, emulsion, energy.
- Utilisation drives profit: idle capacity is the main risk with expensive machines. Model utilisation percentages and a break-even job volume.

## 6. Regulation and licensing checks
- Business permit, signage licence, fire licence, waste management licence (Nairobi example); local equivalents elsewhere.
- Uganda: KCCA trade licence and URA TIN. Tanzania: BRELA and TRA; TBS certification may apply to food packaging. Rwanda: RDB registration; higher quality expectations.
- Content compliance: do not print counterfeit currency, forged documents or copyrighted material without permission; election printing may have local rules.
- Tax: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 7. Risks
- Machine downtime and lack of local technician support, high consumable prices, under-utilised capacity, election-year volatility, technology obsolescence, credit risk from institutional customers, quality problems.

## 8. Regional localisation
- Uganda: Kampala Road, Nasser Road and Ben Kiwanuka Street cluster; campaign, school, church and NGO garments; election years lift demand.
- Tanzania: Kariakoo and Kisutu cluster; strong NGO demand; screen printing spreading to district towns.
- Rwanda: Nyarugenge and Kacyiru; conference and events demand for high-quality large format.
- Universally applicable: large-format signage and screen-printed garments; laser plus design is the lowest-capital entry.

## 9. KPIs
- Jobs per month, revenue by technology, machine utilisation, consumable cost per unit, reprint or waste rate, turnaround time, customer repeat rate, quote-to-order conversion.

## 10. Plan-section prompts
- Which technologies, why (customer evidence), and what is subcontracted.
- Capital table per technology and upgrade path.
- Sales and referral plan; seasonality; utilisation and break-even scenarios.
