# Movie and Media Shop (Disc, USB and Digital Media Retail): Business-Plan Reference

Use this reference to plan a small media retail outlet that sells films and programmes on disc, memory stick or loaded hard disk. All figures are planning assumptions to be re-verified with a current field survey.

## 1. Legal position (read first)
- Copying and selling films or music without the rights-holder's permission infringes copyright and is unlawful in every East African market; business licences do not authorise it. Do not build a plan on unlicensed duplication.
- Plan the business on lawful content: legitimately purchased or licensed discs, content licensed from local producers, distributors or aggregators, public-domain and freely licensed material, and locally produced content under a written agreement.
- The lawful model has lower margins than the informal market but avoids seizure, prosecution and licence loss. Have a lawyer confirm the content-licensing route before capital is committed.

## 2. Market and customer
- Customers: households seeking family viewing, viewers of local-language and narrated films, and repeat buyers who purchase several titles per visit (more at weekends and month-end).
- Physical media faces structural decline from broadband, smartphones, streaming and digital broadcasting. Treat the plan as short- to medium-term unless it pivots (section 9).
- Saturation: central business districts can hold several shops within 50 metres, estates fewer, upcountry towns fewer still. Survey shop density, openings and closures locally.
- Exit signals: low returns, losses, better alternatives, and staffing problems. A steady stream of shops advertised for sale indicates weak returns; ask why.

## 3. Operating model
- Counter or kiosk with a catalogue (printed, displayed or on screen), a preview screen and player, and a sales attendant.
- Service speed is a satisfaction driver: keep popular titles pre-packaged and use enough drives or copies to shorten waiting time.
- Attendant knowledge of titles and customer rapport drives repeat business. Pay either a fixed salary or a commission on sales; commission pay needs a reliable sales-count control.

## 4. Inputs and suppliers
- Licensed content sources, blank or pre-packaged media, storage devices, and a wholesale channel for local narrated releases where licensed.
- Catalogue depth: a library in the low thousands of titles, including current releases and back catalogue, supports variety.

## 5. Equipment and capital (checklist)
- Minimal set-up: computer, media drive or player, table, seating, small library, simple lit sign, media stock, rent deposit.
- Fuller set-up: preview screen, player, internet access, extra storage, counter and shelving, advertising board.
- Buying a going concern (equipment, library, fixtures, advertising board) can be cheaper than building; verify the content in the library is lawfully held.
- Capital tiers: basic (single workstation in an estate) and intermediate (multi-drive equipment, full fit-out). Cost each item locally and add rent and staff for three months.

## 6. Unit economics and pricing
- Gross margin per unit = selling price - media and content cost. Fixed costs: rent, wages, internet, licences.
- Revenue = customers per day x titles per customer x price. Model low, base and high days; daily revenue varies by more than an order of magnitude.
- Price competition: in the market surveyed, shops at a lower price earned no more than shops at the standard price, so discounting did not pay. A bundle offer (for example a free title with every five) lifted daily revenue modestly; test locally.
- Transfers to customers' own storage devices can be a secondary priced line where lawful.

## 7. Niche positioning (differentiation checklist)
- Family-friendly and curated content for conservative parents.
- Art and independent films for a small loyal audience.
- Classic titles neglected by shops chasing new releases.
- Narrated or dubbed local-language films popular in peri-urban and rural areas.
- Home and office delivery within a defined radius, with a minimum order.
- Genre specialisation builds repeat customers and reduces head-on competition.

## 8. Regulation and licensing checks
- Local trade licence; film classification or exhibition licence from the national film board or classification body; public performance and music licences from the collecting societies; performers' rights licences where applicable.
- Kenya: county trade licence, Kenya Film Classification Board, and the music and performers' collecting societies. Uganda: KCCA or local trade licence, film classification requirements, collecting-society licence for in-store music. Tanzania: film board licensing, BRELA and TRA registration. Rwanda: RDB registration and stricter enforcement of intellectual property and business rules.
- Tax treatment: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 9. Long-term risks and pivots
- Risks: copyright enforcement, broadband and streaming growth, digital broadcasting, saturation, falling revenue per shop.
- Pivots: pre-loaded USB or hard-disk libraries of licensed content, a licensed local-language content niche, a Wi-Fi or download point for licensed streaming, or bundling with phone accessories and repairs.
- Streaming transition is slower where broadband is weaker, so runway differs by country and city; assess locally.

## 10. KPIs
- Customers per day, titles per customer, average ticket, repeat-customer rate, waiting time, catalogue size, gross margin, months to break-even.

## 11. Plan-section prompts
- Legal: which licensed content sources and licences you hold.
- Market: competitor density and niche chosen.
- Financials: capital tier, margin per unit, daily scenarios, break-even.
- Strategy: the pivot if physical media demand falls.

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya / Insightful Ventures, The Movie Shop Quick Business Guide; general practice and copyright law principles.
