# Mobile Money Sub-Agency: Business-Plan Reference

Use this reference to plan an outlet that earns commission on customer cash-in and cash-out for a mobile money network. All figures are planning assumptions; commission schedules in particular change often, so re-verify against the operator's current rate card before committing capital.

## 1. Market and customer
- Customers: walk-in users depositing and withdrawing small amounts; the large majority of transactions are small-value.
- Urban markets are heavily saturated (several outlets within 50 metres in busy areas), new outlets open monthly, and a meaningful share of outlets close each year.
- Demand test: count outlets within 50 to 300 metres, observe transactions per hour at peak, and note which outlets customers trust for float.
- Main reasons outlets close: low returns or losses, disputes with the agent above them, and account freezes by the operator or agent.

## 2. Operating model and agency structure
- Three tiers: super agents (banks, large retailers, large float per store), agents or aggregators (recruit sub-agents, operate multiple tills, mid-level float), and sub-agents (retail outlets under an agent, smallest float).
- Ways to obtain a till:
  1. Through an agent, paying goodwill (a sum that varies widely by location).
  2. Buying from an exiting sub-agent: ensure the registration transfers to your name and sign a new contract with the agent first.
- Formal aggregation: if you are registered directly with the operator, you keep the official share. If you are not, all commission passes through the agent, who may take a much larger cut than the recommended split. Negotiate for formal registration and put the split in writing.

## 3. Float management (procedure)
- Hold two balances: electronic float and cash.
- Withdrawal: e-float rises, cash falls. Deposit: cash rises, e-float falls.
- If e-float runs out, you cannot accept deposits; top up by depositing cash into your agent or store account. If cash runs out, you cannot pay withdrawals; rebalance by withdrawing e-float to cash.
- Bank float-management credit products can finance rebalancing.
- Reconcile float and cash records daily; float reliability is the top loyalty driver.

## 4. Capital (checklist)
- Goodwill for the till, float (the critical variable: larger float allows larger-value transactions and better commission), premises rent, branding, business permit, plus a three-month working-capital buffer. Regulator or operator minimum float applies; check it.
- Float can be partly financed by bank credit lines.

## 5. Unit economics
- Revenue = sum of commissions across transactions in each value band; commission is banded by transaction size and differs by whether the customer is registered and by transaction type (withdrawal versus deposit).
- Net commission = gross commission x (1 - agent share). Commissions are usually stated gross and pre-tax.
- Model: daily transactions x average commission x days, minus agent share, rent, wages and float financing cost.
- Volumes vary widely (from a handful to hundreds of transactions a day); month-end and payday peaks are pronounced.
- Run a standalone outlet only if the volume case supports rent and wages; otherwise combine it with a shop, airtime sales or a bank agency to share fixed costs.

## 6. Competition and differentiation
- Products are identical, so differentiation rests on float reliability first and location second (walk-in convenience).
- Loyalty programmes exist on some networks (for example points per amount transacted); check current schemes.
- Cross-network transfers that are cheap or free for customers can help agents on the receiving network.

## 7. Fraud risks and controls

| Fraud | Method | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Impersonation call | Caller claims to be operator staff and extracts PIN and agent ID | Never share PIN or ID by phone; official contact only |
| Erroneous transfer scam | Fraudster sends money then asks for a "reversal" | Route all reversals through the operator's official customer service |
| Counterfeit currency | Fake notes in large cash deals | Use a counterfeit detector for larger notes |

## 8. Regulation and licensing
- Business permit and tax registration; agent registration and KYC with the operator; the financial-services regulator's agent rules.
- Kenya: M-Pesa (Safaricom), Airtel Money. Uganda: MTN Mobile Money (dominant), Airtel Money; KCCA trade licence and URA TIN. Tanzania: Vodacom M-Pesa, Tigo (Yas/Mixx by Yas), Airtel Money; TCRA regulates; BRELA and TRA registration. Rwanda: MTN Mobile Money (dominant), Airtel; RDB and RRA registration.
- Tax treatment of commissions: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 9. Risks
- Saturation, low commission relative to fixed costs, float shortage, fraud, commission-rate cuts by the operator, agent-share disputes, account freezes, cash-in-transit theft.

## 10. Regional localisation
- Uganda: cash-intensive economy outside Kampala makes float logistics harder; goodwill is typically far lower in absolute terms than Nairobi CBD; check MTN and Airtel rate cards.
- Tanzania: more distributed market across operators; commission structures differ by platform and are set separately from Kenya.
- Rwanda: compact geography simplifies float restocking; commission rates are published by the dominant operator.
- Platforms all offer agent apps and USSD interfaces for transaction recording and float control.

## 11. KPIs
- Transactions per day, average value, gross and net commission per day, float turnover, stock-outs of float or cash per week, agent-share percentage, cost per transaction.

## 12. Plan-section prompts
- Market: outlets within 50 metres, transaction volume observed, unique advantage.
- Structure: which tier, who is the agent, is registration direct, the written commission split.
- Financials: float requirement, commission by band, net-of-agent income, break-even, standalone versus combined.

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya, Money and Mobile: A Report on the M-Pesa Sub Agent Business (2013); general practice.
