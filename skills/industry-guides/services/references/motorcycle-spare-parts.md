# Motorcycle (Bodaboda) Spare-Parts Retail: Business-Plan Reference

Use this reference to plan a retail shop serving motorcycle-taxi riders. Figures are planning assumptions to be re-verified locally with dated quotes.

## 1. Market and customer
- Customers: motorcycle-taxi riders and private owners; mechanics who recommend shops.
- Demand drivers: fleet size and growth in the catchment, replacement frequency (tyres, brake shoes, plugs), and rider income (urban riders typically earn about double rural riders, so rural spend per rider is lower).
- Demand test: count motorcycles in the area and shops per motorcycle. A ratio of roughly one shop per 85 motorcycles supported a mid-sized county-town shop in the source data; treat it as an indicative check only.

## 2. Operating model
- Small retail counter with shelves organised by fast-moving item, and staff able to identify parts by model.
- Referral channel: local mechanics. Offer fair prices, genuine parts and technical help. Riders often buy the part themselves after a mechanic diagnoses the fault.

## 3. Inputs, suppliers and sourcing

| Source | Advantage | Consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Capital-city wholesalers | Variety, reliability | Higher prices |
| Cross-border sourcing (for example Kampala for Kenyan shops) | Substantially lower prices where import duties are lower | Travel, accommodation, relationship building; first trip needs guidance |
| Overseas sourcing (for example Dubai) | Highest margin; allows wholesaling | Large capital, flights, big minimum quantities |
| Local upcountry wholesalers | Fast restocking | Higher cost, lower margin |

- Once a supplier relationship exists, pay by mobile money or bank transfer and ship by bus or courier, subject to a minimum order.
- Joint purchases: several retailers pool funds to obtain wholesale prices without carrying full capital.
- Counterfeits of trusted brands are common and shorten part life; buy from verified wholesalers or the brand's own distributor to protect reputation.

## 4. Stock strategy
1. Identify the dominant motorcycle brands locally (Chinese and Indian makes need different parts).
2. Stock universal items first (tyres, tubes, spokes, bulbs, chains, brake fluid) as they are cross-compatible; stock brand-specific parts (pistons, gaskets, clutch assemblies) only for dominant models.
3. Identify locally trusted part brands; trust is cultural and local, not purely quality-based.
- Fast-moving list: tyres and tubes, rims, side mirrors and holders, brake shoes, indicator lamps, spark plugs, front and rear sprockets, engine oil, cables, return springs, head cases, dimmer switches. A rider replaces a tyre roughly every five months, so demand persists even from a stable fleet, and new motorcycles add to it.

## 5. Capital (checklist)
- Business permit; initial stock (the largest item); shelving, fit-out and branding; deposit plus first rent; about three months of wages, transport, power and sundries.
- Stock variety wins customers from established shops, so set a minimum viable stock value from your survey rather than the lowest possible figure.

## 6. Unit economics and pricing
- Margin = (retail - wholesale) / wholesale, computed per item. Small parts (springs, gaskets, plugs, rings) carry very high mark-ups; batteries and tyres carry low ones. Build the plan on a blended average margin and stress-test it downwards.
- Daily revenue varies enormously between shops; model low, base and high, and base a new shop on the low case.
- Working capital sits in stock: track stock turns and dead stock.
- Shops in small towns with few competitors can price nearer the ceiling; competitive urban shops price lower. Survey local prices.

## 7. Regulation and licensing
- Business permit (cost varies by council and premises size); tax registration.
- Tanzania: TBS certification for safety-critical parts (brakes, tyres); BRELA and TRA registration. Rwanda: operators must register and meet equipment standards, which favours retailers of certified, documented parts; RDB registration. Uganda: safety-equipment rules with inconsistent enforcement.
- Import duty and cross-border sourcing: check customs rules; tax treatment: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 8. Risks
- Counterfeit stock, wrong-model stock, dead stock as models change, price undercutting, travel and cross-border risks, theft, capital tied in slow lines.

## 9. Regional localisation
- Uganda: mature bodaboda market; Nakawa and Kikuubo areas of Kampala host wholesalers; lower import taxes.
- Tanzania: Dar es Salaam (Kariakoo) and Mwanza hubs; Chinese and Indian brands dominate.
- Rwanda: Nyabugogo area hub; strict operator registration.
- Opportunity in secondary cities (Mbarara, Mwanza, Huye, Kisumu, Nakuru) where fleets are growing and shops are few.

## 10. KPIs
- Daily revenue, blended margin, stock turns, fill rate on fast movers, dead-stock share, mechanic referrals, repeat riders.

## 11. Plan-section prompts
- Market: fleet count, dominant models, shops per motorcycle.
- Sourcing: primary and backup suppliers, counterfeit controls.
- Financials: stock build, margin by category, scenarios, break-even.
