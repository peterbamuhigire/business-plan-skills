# Mitumba (Second-Hand Clothing) Retail

Use this reference to plan retail of imported second-hand clothing sold from bales. All figures are planning assumptions to re-verify against current wholesale prices.

## 1. Supply chain

Garments are donated abroad, collected by charities, graded, sorted and baled by reclamation companies, exported, bought by large importers (a small number control much port volume), sold on to wholesalers in the major markets, then to retailers and sub-retailers. A container holds mixed bale types, so importers cannot choose only fast-moving categories. A standard bale weighs about 45 kg; small wholesalers sell single bales, large importers sell in lots of dozens of bales. Bale prices vary widely by category (slow categories are cheaper).

## 2. Grading a bale (the "camera" system)

On opening a bale, sort items into three groups: first camera (best, near-new, fashionable; a small share of the bale; premium price, prominent display), second camera (average; the bulk; standard price) and clearance (worn or dated; sold cheaply or in lots). First-camera items often recover the bale cost, second camera carries most profit, clearance recovers residual value. Record the split per bale to learn which bale types pay.

## 3. Stock options and capital

| Option | Risk | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Single tested items | Low | Limited selection, lower profit |
| One bale | Medium | Bale quality variable; higher return per unit |
| Several bales | Higher but diversified | Proportionally higher profit |

Keep a reserve of about one extra bale's cost so restocking need not wait for current stock to clear (assumption).

## 4. Unit economics

- Bale profit = sum(pieces x realised price by camera group) - bale cost - transport and market costs.
- Model an optimistic, base and pessimistic case. Realised prices in mixed-quality bales are lower than an "ideal" all-at-target-price case.
- Planning assumption: average profit per bale is modest and highly variable (a range from small to several times the average); it depends on bale selection skill, which improves with experience. Plan conservatively.
- A wholesaler moving hundreds of bales a month has a very different profile from a single-stall retailer; do not mix them in one model.

## 5. Selecting the right bale

Decide by: category (dresses, trousers, coats, shirts, children's, shoes; each has its own turnover and margin); season (buy ahead of season; warm items sell slowly in cold months); location demand (rural against central market, school area against commercial area); demographics (workwear for working-class estates, branded-look for youth, premium first-camera for upmarket areas).

## 6. Licences

One local-government licence is usually all that is required: a business permit for fixed premises, or a monthly or daily open-market fee for stalls (the smallest entry point). Retailers need no import licence; importers handle customs. Confirm current fees locally.

## 7. Markets and competition

Key hubs: Gikomba, Kariokor, Kongowea and Toi (Kenya); Owino and regional markets (Uganda); Kariakoo (Tanzania). Competition is location-based and products are undifferentiated, so the winner has the best variety, price and selection. Repeat business depends on the first purchase experience. Online selling through social groups and classifieds supplements stalls where prime locations are unavailable.

## 8. Regulatory and policy risk

East African Community discussions about banning or taxing second-hand clothing recur. Rwanda restricted imports from 2016, which sharply reduced its formal market; verify the current rule before planning there. Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania have so far resisted full bans, but the policy environment is volatile: review policy updates yearly. Standards-body rules apply in principle in Tanzania, with inconsistent retail enforcement. Tax: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 9. Risks and KPIs

Risks: poor bale selection, policy change, currency and freight swings, theft, damp or damaged stock. KPIs: profit per bale, share of first-camera items, days to clear a bale, gross margin, repeat-customer rate.
