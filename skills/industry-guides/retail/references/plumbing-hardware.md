# Plumbing Hardware Retail: Sector Reference

Use this reference for a specialist shop selling pipes, fittings, valves, taps and bathroom or kitchen plumbing items to plumbers, contractors and households. All monetary figures are planning assumptions from a dated East African field survey; re-verify each with a current, dated supplier quotation before it enters a plan.

## 1. Market and customer

- Buyers: jobbing plumbers (highest repeat value), site foremen and contractors, landlords and households doing repairs or fit-outs.
- Demand drivers: new housing and construction activity, piped-water connections, and repair demand in older buildings.
- Trend to reflect in the assortment: PPR (polypropylene random) pipe displaces galvanised iron (GI) in new construction; CPVC grows for hot-water use; GI persists for repairs.
- Plumbers and foremen steer client spending. Treat relationships with them as the primary marketing channel.
- Market-entry options (choose one and justify it in the plan):

| Strategy | Description | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | Large stock and shop, strong first impression | High capital; hard to sustain |
| Economies of location | Open inside a known hardware cluster | Built-in traffic, intense competition |
| Market gap | Area with high demand and little supply | Best early margin; attracts entrants fast |
| Specialisation | Pipes, fittings or sanitary ware only | Needs enough demand; avoid gaps in related items |
| Variety | Broad range following local demand | Guards against dead stock |

## 2. Location

- Near active construction or dense residential areas with piped water; hardware clusters supply ready foot traffic.
- Check visibility, access and nearby services (mobile-money agents, transport routes).
- Avoid premises too large for the stock level: empty shelves undermine credibility.

## 3. Operating model

- Hours: early opening (from about 08:00 or earlier) catches plumbers starting morning jobs.
- Records: stock card or ledger per category as the minimum; weekly stock-to-sales reconciliation; monthly full stocktake.
- Staff: shop assistants on commission, or salary plus bonus; test the rate against local wages.
- Relationship management: keep a contact list of regular plumbers and foremen.

## 4. Assortment and sourcing

Priority (fast-moving) lines:
1. PPR pipes and fittings (sockets, elbows, unions, tees, adapters; about 20 to 110 mm).
2. GI pipes and fittings (bends, sockets, unions, tees, elbows; about half-inch to four inches).
3. Waste and drainage pipes (about one to four inches).
4. CPVC pipes and fittings.
5. Taps.
6. Basic toilet and bathroom fittings (flush valves, shower heads, angle valves, stop cocks).
7. Gate valves and ball valves.

Secondary lines, added once the core sells: baths, basins, shower mixers, ceramic cisterns, toilet seats, urinal bowls, soap dishes, towel holders, bottle traps, kitchen sinks, further shower-head ranges.

Rules:
- Stock both branded original items and lower-priced alternatives; customers ask for both and plumbers buy to the client's budget. Counterfeits of major brands exist; train staff to recognise them and specify brand and grade on orders.
- Sourcing: compare at least three to five wholesalers per line. A few currency units per piece matter at volume. Ask about delivery terms (free transport above a minimum order is common) and build long-term relationships for credit and priority access.
- Regional supply route: East African inland markets import through the Mombasa or Dar es Salaam ports; include transport and duty in landed cost (check the current East African Community common external tariff).

## 5. Startup capital: the cost headings

Build the capex and working-capital schedule from these headings, each quoted locally:

| Heading | Items |
|---|---|
| Licences | Single business permit or trading licence; signboard licence; fire or safety certificate |
| Equipment | Fire extinguisher, signboard, shelving and counter, receipt and record books |
| Premises | Deposit plus first month rent; branding and renovation |
| Stock | Mixed plumbing items; the largest single line |
| Working capital (three months) | Salary, marketing, miscellaneous, and fixed costs until break-even |

Planning assumptions from the survey (verify before use): a full setup of the order of USD 6,000; a minimum viable entry of roughly a third of that, with stock limited to fast-moving pipes and fittings and range widened from revenue; stock is roughly half of the total.

## 6. Unit economics

- Gross margin: survey average about 40 per cent, with a range of about 30 to 100 per cent across lines.
- Fittings pricing: add a fixed minimum per piece over wholesale (a small absolute amount, tested against the local market), with a higher mark-up on larger or slower items to cover holding cost.
- Daily turnover: survey range of a few tens to a few hundred US dollars for most shops, with rare outliers far higher; break-even averaged about four months.
- Break-even formula: monthly break-even sales = monthly fixed costs / gross margin. Check it against expected daily turnover.
- Price data: any wholesale or retail price table is directional only; rebuild it from current quotations by pipe size and fitting type.

## 7. Regulation and licensing checks

- Trading licence from the local authority, tax registration, and business registration under the country's registry.
- Fire safety certificate and extinguisher.
- Product standards: check the national bureau of standards for water-fitting requirements (stricter enforcement in some markets, for example Rwanda; standards apply on some public projects in Tanzania).
- Verify with the Chwezi finance engine for tax, VAT and duty treatment.

## 8. Country notes (East Africa)

- Uganda: hardware clusters in central Kampala; PPR dominant in new work; imports arrive via Mombasa or Dar es Salaam; municipal trade licence and tax registration required.
- Tanzania: clusters in Dar es Salaam; strong construction demand; business registry and revenue-authority registration; check duty on imported sanitary ware.
- Rwanda: clusters in Kigali markets; standards bureau active on imported fittings; premium sanitary ware finds a market in higher-income districts.

## 9. Risks

- Counterfeit or off-grade fittings; dead stock from following supplier push rather than local demand; price competition inside clusters; currency movement on imported lines; theft and shrink on small items; oversized premises.
- Mitigation: focused core range, weekly reconciliation, multi-supplier quotes, plumber loyalty programme.

## 10. KPIs

Gross margin by category; stock turn; dead-stock share; daily sales and average basket; share of turnover from repeat plumbers and contractors; supplier price variance; weeks of cover on fast-moving lines.

## 11. Plan-section prompts

- Market: who buys, how many plumbers and building sites are within reach, and what competitors stock.
- Operations: hours, staffing, record system, supplier list with terms.
- Financials: capex schedule, opening stock plan by category, margin by category, break-even, three-month working capital.
- Risks: as section 9, each with an owner and a control.

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya, Plumbing Hardware Business Guide (2018), used as a field-survey input only; general retail practice. Data are dated and regional; re-verify all figures.
