# Retail Operating Model and Engine-Ready Business Plan Reference

Use this reference when a retail business plan, investor plan, bankable plan, strategy paper, or turnaround plan needs to cover modern retail operations beyond "shop, stock, rent, staff, sales".

## Planning Principle

A serious retail plan must connect the customer proposition to the operating model. The plan should show how the business will choose products, price them, promote them, hold and move stock, fulfil orders, handle returns, manage stores, control shrink, negotiate suppliers, review KPIs, and reconcile finance. If those systems are absent, the plan may look viable in the income statement while failing in execution.

## Section Injection Guide

| Plan section | Retail-specific content to add |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | State the retail format, channel role, category focus, margin/turnover logic, and operating-model advantage. |
| Products/services | Define categories, item roles, private label if any, assortment breadth/depth, and product-data standards. |
| Market analysis | Separate customer mission, shopping occasion, channel preference, trade area, and category demand. Do not invent market-size figures. |
| Marketing and sales | Cover pricing, promotions, loyalty, CRM, search/content, launch calendar, retention, and discount guardrails. |
| Operations plan | Cover buying, receiving, inventory, fulfilment, returns, store routines, labour, SOPs, audits, and vendor management. |
| Technology plan | Cover POS, inventory, e-commerce, payments, loyalty/CRM, analytics, accounting, data security, and integration needs. |
| Financial projections | Model sales, gross margin, markdowns, returns, shrink, fulfilment cost, labour, rent/occupancy, vendor funding, working capital, and stock turns. |
| Risk analysis | Include stockouts, excess stock, margin erosion, promotion dependency, returns, shrink, supplier concentration, data quality, and channel conflict. |
| Implementation plan | Sequence setup, buying calendar, product data, systems, staff training, pilot/opening, dashboard, and weekly review cadence. |

## Financial Assumption Checklist

Retail projections should explicitly state:

- Sales build-up logic: footfall x conversion x average transaction, website sessions x conversion x average order value, or channel-specific equivalent.
- Gross margin by category or format, not one blended rate where the range is material.
- Planned reductions: markdowns, promotions, employee discounts, returns, allowances, shrink, damaged goods.
- Inventory investment: opening stock, safety stock, reorder point, lead time, seasonal stock build, stock turn, and slow-moving stock policy.
- Fulfilment cost: delivery, pickup handling, packaging, payment fees, return logistics, replacement, disposal.
- Labour: store coverage, fulfilment labour, management, security, training, opening support.
- Vendor funding: rebates, allowances, co-op funding, claim timing, recovery risk.
- Working capital: supplier credit, inventory days, cash/card/mobile-money settlement timing, VAT/tax timing where applicable.
- Dashboard cadence: daily trading, weekly business review, monthly management accounts, quarterly strategy review.

## Operating Plan Minimum Standard

The plan is not retail-ready unless it covers:

1. Merchandising and assortment governance.
2. Pricing, promotion, and markdown rules.
3. Inventory, fulfilment, and returns workflows.
4. Store labour, SOPs, audits, and training.
5. Shrink/loss-prevention controls.
6. Vendor terms, trade spend, and recovery logic where suppliers are material.
7. KPI dashboard and weekly business review cadence.
8. Finance/accounting controls for POS, stock, refunds, discounts, vendor funding, and management reports.

## Anti-Patterns

- Treating "retail" as rent plus stock plus staff.
- Using one gross margin for all categories without explaining mix.
- Showing sales growth without stock, fulfilment, labour, and cash-flow capacity.
- Modelling promotions as revenue growth without margin, funding, and cannibalisation logic.
- Ignoring returns, markdowns, shrink, damaged goods, and slow movers.
- Treating e-commerce as an add-on without product data, search, checkout, fulfilment, returns, analytics, and customer service.
- Claiming vendor funding or rebates without agreement evidence and recovery timing.
- Including dashboards without metric definitions, owners, and action cadence.
