# Strategic Management for Agribusiness: Plan-Section Reference

Use for the strategy, industry-context and structure sections of an agribusiness plan.

## Sector context to establish
- State agriculture's share of GDP and employment for the plan's country from a dated official source (for example the national statistics bureau or World Bank); do not reuse older figures.
- Compare efficiency indicators across countries where useful: labour share in agriculture, agriculture share of GDP, farm size and share of household income spent on food. The pattern to expect: as economies develop, labour share in agriculture falls, farm size rises and the food share of spending falls.
- Value chain participants: input suppliers, producers, processors and manufacturers, distributors and wholesalers, food retailers and food service. Map where the business sits and who holds power on each side.
- The farm value share of the consumer food shilling is a minority share and declining, and the marketing bill (processing, packaging, transport, retail) is the larger share. Use this to show where margin can be captured.
- Retail trends to check locally: growth in food away from home, supermarket expansion versus traditional markets, e-commerce and delivery in cities.

## Strategic planning frameworks
- Management functions: planning, organising, directing, controlling.
- Planning levels: strategic (several years, mission and broad goals), tactical (one to three years, departmental objectives), operational (up to a year, specific actions). Show all three in the plan.
- SWOT for agribusiness: internal strengths and weaknesses (finance, quality and brand, customer relationships, technical capability, distribution reach, management, staff) against external opportunities (organic and sustainable demand, regional export growth, precision technology, value-added products, acquisitions) and threats (commodity price volatility, regulation, climate, input cost rises, new entrants and substitutes).
- Management by exception: set an allowable deviation (for example plus or minus a stated percentage). Deviation percentage = (actual minus forecast) / forecast x 100. Inside the threshold, take no action; outside it, review inventory, production scheduling, capital, labour, pricing and promotion.

## Economics for strategy
- Accounting profit = revenue minus explicit costs. Economic profit = accounting profit minus implicit costs (foregone interest on owner capital, unrealised rent on owned property, foregone owner income). Positive economic profit signals a viable use of resources.
- Price elasticity of demand (arc form) = [(Q2 minus Q1) / (Q2 + Q1)] x [(P2 + P1) / (P2 minus P1)]. Elastic demand (absolute value above one): a price cut raises revenue; unit elastic: no change; inelastic: revenue falls. Staples tend to be inelastic; luxuries and goods with many substitutes elastic.
- Derived demand: input demand follows demand and prices for the final product, so track consumer and commodity trends to anticipate input sales.

## Business organisation forms
Compare sole proprietorship, partnership, company, cooperative and limited-liability forms on formation complexity, liability, tax treatment, access to capital, control and continuity. Confirm local company, tax and cooperative law and verify tax treatment with the Chwezi finance engine.
- Cooperative principles: user-owner, user-control (one member, one vote), user-benefit (patronage returns). Types: marketing, purchasing or supply, service, bargaining and new-generation cooperatives (delivery rights tied to equity).

## International expansion
- Reasons: mature domestic market, growing neighbouring markets, competitor entry, scale, risk diversification.
- Entry methods by risk, investment and control: exporting, licensing or franchising, joint ventures, strategic alliances, direct investment.
- Screen countries with SLEPT (social, legal, economic, political, technological).
- Developed versus developing markets: food budget share, diet composition, supply-chain fragmentation, consumer priorities and retail structure differ; design the offer accordingly.

## Trends to address
Consolidation, technology (precision agriculture, biotechnology, information systems, e-commerce), globalisation and population growth, consumer preference for organic, local and traceable food, bioenergy demand, climate and environmental regulation.

## Plan-section prompts
Where does the business sit on the value chain and what share of the consumer shilling can it capture? What is the SWOT and the strategy at each planning level? What legal form and why? Is expansion into a regional market justified?

Sources consulted: Barnard, Akridge, Dooley and Foltz, Agribusiness Management, 4th edn (Routledge, 2012). National statistics must be re-sourced with dates.
