# Direct Marketing for Small Farms: Channels, Models and Pricing

Use for the market and sales section of a plan for a market garden or small market farm selling to consumers, restaurants and institutions. The principle: prosperity depends more on finding and keeping profitable customers, knowing true costs and matching channels to production capacity than on growing skill.

## Farm scale categories (plan against one)
Kitchen garden (household use), market garden (roughly half an acre to three acres; direct sales, box schemes, markets), market farm (three to twelve acres; subscriptions, restaurants, wholesale, markets), small vegetable farm (larger; wholesale plus direct hybrid). Most smallholder commercial vegetable operations in East Africa sit in the first two commercial categories, serving nearby urban markets.

## Why direct channels
Each intermediary removed raises the farmer's share of the retail price. Direct sale to consumers captures the whole retail price; restaurants pay a share of retail; retailers and distributors pay progressively less. Quantify the farm-gate-to-retail price gap for the crops and city in question from a dated market visit before claiming a premium.

## Channels
### Farmers markets and street stalls
- Strengths: highest price per unit, same-day cash, customer feedback, brand building, low entry barrier. Weaknesses: a full day per market, weather risk, limited volume per stand, need for steady weekly supply.
- Model: target gross sales per market day scaled to the farm; carry roughly eight to fifteen items for abundance and cross-selling; budget display kit (tables, signs, scales, bags, canopy); include the stall fee (percentage or fixed) and transport and labour. Break-even: day sales must exceed stall fee plus transport plus labour.
- Match the market to the produce: middle-class urban markets pay premiums for clean, fresh, high-quality produce; informal daily markets serve price-driven buyers.
- Market-day routine: arrive early for a good position; display abundantly; pre-wash, trim and present; price clearly; offer recipe suggestions; collect contacts for subscriptions and pre-orders.

### Subscription boxes (community supported agriculture, CSA)
- Customers pay at or before season start for weekly boxes; risk is shared and the farm gains upfront capital.
- Share price = (target CSA revenue + delivery and drop-point cost) / number of members; weekly box value = share price / weeks. Keep the value delivered each week close to the average weekly payment; consistent over-filling in peak season erodes value and leads to smaller renewals.
- Requirements: transparent communication in light weeks, planned variety (a dozen or more crops across the season), a weekly newsletter or crop note, drop-point logistics and reusable crates.
- Cooperative version: several farms pool production into one share with clear contribution and revenue-sharing agreements.
- East African adaptation: shorter seasons (about twelve to sixteen weeks), mobile-money upfront or monthly payment, a messaging group per drop-point, initial focus on expatriate, NGO and urban middle-class households.

### Restaurants and institutions
- Buyer criteria: consistency, reliability (deliver what you commit), quality and appearance, communication (warn before a shortfall). Price between wholesale and retail; avoid buyers who want farmer-gate prices and do not value the relationship. Start with a sample box; supply specialty items unavailable in the main market; agree flexible delivery days to suit kitchen schedules; offer farm attribution on menus.
- Institutions (schools, hospitals, NGO canteens): tender-based procurement, invoice and receipt documentation, payment often thirty to sixty days after delivery (plan working capital), higher volume but lower margin, pauses during school holidays.

### Pick-your-own and agritourism
- Pick-your-own transfers harvest labour to the customer and works for high-volume easy crops near cities; invest in safety, pathways, sanitation and a welcome area.
- Agritourism streams: farm tours, farm stays, farm dinners, school excursions, cooking classes, event hire. Revenue can stack (a stay buys produce, a dinner and a tour). It requires hosting skills, infrastructure and marketing.

### Online and messaging sales
Broadcast lists and business messaging accounts for weekly availability and pre-orders, simple forms for box orders, mobile-money payment before delivery, and social media for produce photography and farm stories. State a weekly cut-off for orders.

## Channel diversification
- Rule: no single channel should exceed about half of revenue. Indicative mix to test: subscription members, farmers market, restaurants, institutions, each with a stated share and rationale.
- Diversify crops as well: early, mid and late season crops, some perennials, some hardy low-input crops, and a minimum number of crops in production at any time so a single failure does not sink revenue.

## True cost and pricing
- Count all costs: seed and transplants, soil amendments, labour including own labour at market rate, equipment and depreciation, fuel and transport, packaging, market fees, insurance, water and irrigation, pest management inputs, infrastructure maintenance.
- Break-even price = total cost per unit / marketable yield. Target sale price = break-even price x (1 + target margin), with a margin sufficient to cover surprises and give business profit.
- Three-year viability questions: what minimum revenue covers all costs including living expenses; which crop and channel combination delivers it on the land available; what is the path to profit in years two and three as fertility improves and infrastructure is written off.
- Build a scenario table: revenue by channel (weekly, monthly, annual), costs as a share of revenue, and net operating income. Use current local prices; treat all figures as assumptions.

## Customer loyalty
- Relationship reduces price sensitivity: learn names and preferences, share honest farm stories, offer tastings of unfamiliar produce, teach variety and cooking methods, and show up consistently with the same quality.
- Complaint handling: apologise and thank, replace without requiring return, add a small goodwill extra, and follow up the next week.

## Plan-section prompts
Which channels and in what shares? What is the true cost and break-even price per crop? How is subscription value matched to payment? Which restaurants or institutions are targeted, on what payment terms? How is loyalty measured?
