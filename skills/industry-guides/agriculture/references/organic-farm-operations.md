# Vegetable Farm Operations (Organic and Diversified): Plan-Section Reference

Use for the operations section of a vegetable or mixed-crop farm plan: planning tools, weed control, bed preparation, seeding, marketing, staffing, records, packing, greenhouse work and business-plan structure. Statutory payroll items: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## Planning from profit to field
Four linked tools: (1) a marketing chart (accounts across, products down, quantities and values in each cell; grand total equals projected gross sales), (2) a production plan (crop, yield per bed, number of beds, variety, spacing), (3) a farm map showing where each crop goes and when, with prior years' maps kept for rotation, (4) a seedling calendar back-calculated from transplant dates by the nursery lead time.
- Bed arithmetic: beds per acre = 43,560 sq ft / (bed length x bed centre-to-centre width). Use consistent bed widths matched to machinery wheel spacing.
- Spacing system: multiple rows per bed for small crops (carrots, beets, lettuce, spinach), two outer rows for peppers and aubergines, single row for vines. Keep a crop budget for each main crop (labour hours, inputs, yield, price).

## Weed control
- Compare hand weeding alone against mechanical cultivation plus flame weeding on hours per acre and cost per acre; mechanical methods usually save a large share of weeding hours.
- Tine weeders: shallow scratching over the whole bed including the crop row, best on well-rooted crops.
- Basket weeders: from bed formation until plants reach a modest height; double-pass offset method removes most of the area needing hand weeding.
- Sweeps (V-shaped): for taller crops, burying small in-row weeds.
- Stale-bed seeding plus flame weeding: form the bed, let weeds germinate, sow, cover small sections to watch emergence, and flame the bed immediately when the crop starts to emerge; suited to slow-germinating crops.
- Other tactics: time plantings around weed peaks, stimulate weed germination before planting with irrigation or covers, use mulches, and use crops that suppress weeds.

## Bed preparation
Sequence: soil test and amend (compost, mineral amendments); incorporate residues (disc or chain harrow); chisel to aerate deeply without forming a plough pan; form beds with a bed-former; mark rows. Finished bed: raised, flat topped, firm to walk on, with visible row marks. Avoid chronic rotary tilling (plough pan, rapid organic-matter loss, beds settling); if used, precede with a chisel pass and run at slow rotor speed.

## Seeding and transplanting
- Push seeders: lightweight plate types for general use; heavier adjustable types for precise density. Calibrate against germination percentage by counting seeds per length over a tarp or by a set wheel rotation; record plate or hole setting on the seed bag.
- Transplanting: hand crews (two people, several hundred plants per hour each) versus a tractor-mounted transplanter (higher output, more people and set-up time). Compute the break-even number of plants that justifies the machine. Transplant in the afternoon or on cloudy days and water in.

## Marketing and customer service
- Channel comparison by price level: grower cooperative or large wholesale (lowest), local wholesale (stores, restaurants, co-ops, invoiced), retail direct (farm stand, subscriptions, markets; highest).
- Marketing circle, four questions: how will the market learn I have what it needs; to whom will I market; why will they want my product; what will I market (demanded and profitable).
- Subscription (CSA) management: keep average weekly share value equal to the average weekly payment; do not overfill at peak; sell surplus elsewhere; run at least one other channel alongside.
- Professional conduct rules: deliver in full and on time, place and confirm orders at agreed times, reply promptly, ask buyers what works, keep quality and packaging consistent.
- Brand identity: consistent colour, lettering and logo on invoices, signage, bags, case labels, vehicle and clothing.

## Staff management
- Types: casual (hourly, weekly pay, timesheets) and regular (periodic pay, full tax and social-security compliance).
- Employer calendar: confirm employee tax details and registration at year start, file annual returns by the statutory deadline, and remit payroll deductions monthly; verify local dates and rates.
- Employer additions to base wage (social-security contribution, insurance) commonly add about a tenth to a fifth.
- Production standards: keep a standards page of benchmark rates (bunching, picking, potting, hoeing, trellising) measured on your own farm; the farm earns by the piece rather than by the hour.
- Practices: written job descriptions, morning task planning, periodic staff meetings, alternating dull and engaging tasks, temporary hands to avoid falling behind, and calm feedback that cites target rates.

## Record-keeping
- Crop journal: one page per crop, recorded the same day: soil preparation, seeding, cultivation, harvesting, and rates (quantity per area, quantity per time, time per area). These feed budgets and scaling.
- Office paper flow: unpaid bills, paid bills, unpaid invoices, payments received, deposit bundles. Process bills on a fixed weekly slot. Use multi-line register entries to itemise expense types. Store annual records for the statutory retention period.

## Harvest and pack-out
- Aids: bed-lifters for roots, chain diggers for whole-bed lifting, barrel washers.
- Grade one grade at a time rather than judging each piece; use bucket-brigade unloading, hand trucks for stackable containers, bundle tasks (assemble all boxes, then pick all of one crop), and count bunches in tens against orders.

## Greenhouse operations
Use one house for seedlings and benches and one for in-ground high-value crops if scale allows. Efficiency options: overhead trolley rail, roof trapdoor for potting mix, timer-controlled overhead sprinklers (automate most watering, hand-water the rest), bottom-watering benches and ebb-and-flow tables. Recirculating water can spread disease; group plants of similar size. Watering is skilled work.

## Business plan structure
Cover page, contents, executive summary (written last, with mission and key financials), farm and product descriptions (marketing chart or sales spreadsheet), analyses (SWOT, management, market by venue, enterprise budgets for top items), planning (strategies and timeline with who, when, how, why, budget), financial forms (P&L, balance sheet, three-to-five-year cash-flow projection). Aim for a concise document.

## Deer and wildlife fencing (low-cost method)
Two-strand electric fence, fibreglass posts angled outwards, low and high wires at set heights, a strong charger with good earthing, and a visual marker at first placement. Adapt to the local wildlife and permit requirements.

## Plan-section prompts
Which planning tools and beds per crop? Which weed-control system and its hours per acre? What labour standards are used? What controls and records exist? Which channels and in what proportions?
