# Farm Planning and Strategy: Plan-Section Reference

Use for the vision, strategy, risk, structure and succession sections of a farm business plan.

## Planning framework
Whole-farm planning integrates vision, resources, markets and finances across six domains: define vision, values, mission and goals; secure land, equipment, infrastructure and labour; develop markets; manage finances, budgets and records; plan production and ecosystem management; and protect the business through structure, risk management and succession.
- Development stages of a beginning farm: exploring (learn and test), planning (business plan, resources), establishing (build markets, refine systems), growing (expand and prepare succession). Expect several years to reach full profitability and reflect that in the cash plan.

## Goals and SWOT
- Write goals that are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound (SMART): give a quantity, a baseline, a date and the means (for example added beds or a tunnel).
- SWOT: brainstorm internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats; prioritise by impact and likelihood; match strengths to opportunities for growth strategies; address weaknesses that amplify threats.

## Farm types and business models (choose and justify)
Market garden (small, intensive, direct sales), diversified vegetable farm (multiple crops, mixed channels), small-ruminant livestock, mixed crop-livestock (internal nutrient cycling), commodity row crops (wholesale markets). Scale, revenue range and labour differ; take ranges from current local sources.
- Direct-market revenue model: gross revenue = yield per unit area x planted area x marketable share x price x sell-through rate. Use it to test whether a plan's area and price assumptions reconcile with the sales target.

## Risk management
- Five steps: identify, analyse probability and impact, control through prevention and mitigation, reduce financial loss (insurance, contracts, diversification), and implement and review annually.
- Risk register: production (weather, pests, disease: diversify crops, season extension, insurance), market (price drops, lost accounts: multiple channels, contracts, value adding), financial (cash gaps, debt service: reserves, credit lines, conservative budgets), legal and regulatory (compliance, liability: insurance, counsel, food-safety plans), human (injury, key-person loss, burnout: workers' compensation, cross-training, succession plan).
- Insurance types to consider: general liability, product liability, property and casualty, crop insurance where available, workers' compensation, business interruption. Obtain current quotations.

## Business structure
Compare sole proprietorship, general partnership, limited partnership and limited-liability company on owners, liability, taxation and formation paperwork. Prefer limited liability for farms with employees, agritourism or direct food sales; consider estate-planning benefits of family structures. Confirm local company and tax law and cost of formation.

## Succession and estate planning
- Succession planning (business continuity, start well before transition) differs from estate planning (asset transfer at death; wills, trusts, powers of attorney). 
- Steps: start early; identify successors (family, employees or outside buyers); develop them; obtain a professional valuation; structure the transfer (sale, instalment purchase, lease-to-own, gift); address non-farming heirs fairly; document agreements, operating manuals and key relationships.
- Family business risks: unclear roles and unspoken expectations, and mixing family and business decisions. Hold regular family business meetings that separate the two.

## Government and support programmes; licences to check
- Programme types: development-bank agricultural lending, microfinance and savings groups, input-distribution programmes, organic-certification support, commodity authority extension, agricultural credit facilities, youth livelihood programmes. Verify eligibility and current terms.
- Licences and registrations: business or trading licence, food-handler permit for direct food sales, organic certification for organic claims, pesticide applicator licence, water abstraction permit for larger irrigation, employer registrations (tax, PAYE, social security, local service tax) and food-safety compliance with the national standards body.

## Plan-section prompts
What are the SMART goals and the SWOT? Which business model and why? What are the top risks and mitigations? Which legal structure? Who succeeds and how?

Sources consulted: Stephenson, Whole Farm Management (Storey Publishing, 2019). Cost and revenue ranges omitted; verify locally.
