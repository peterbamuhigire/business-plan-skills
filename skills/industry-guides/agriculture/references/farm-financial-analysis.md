# Farm Financial Analysis: Plan-Section Reference

Use for the financial-plan section of a farm business plan. Tax, depreciation and accounting rules are jurisdiction-specific: verify with the Chwezi finance engine and the local revenue authority.

## Financial statements to include
- Profit and loss (income statement): profitability over a period; monthly and annual.
- Cash flow statement: actual cash movement; monthly.
- Balance sheet: assets, liabilities and equity at a date; at least annually.
- Lay out the P&L by expense line and show each line as a percentage of income; note that owner labour must be costed (owner draw) or profit is overstated.
- Cash versus accrual: cash basis records on payment (simple, small farms); accrual records when earned or incurred (better period matching, needed for larger farms and produce delivered but unpaid at period end).

## Budgets (four types)
1. Income budget by crop or product and market channel.
2. Expense budget for operating and capital costs.
3. Cash-flow budget by month.
4. Projected balance sheet (assets, liabilities, equity).
- Build the monthly cash-flow budget first for seasonal farms: costs peak before harvest, income after. Find the peak cumulative deficit and size a credit line or reserve to cover it, with a margin.
- Variance analysis: variance = actual minus budget; variance percentage = (actual minus budget) / budget x 100. Set an investigation threshold (for example around ten per cent) and record the cause and action.

## Depreciation and cost classification
- Straight line: annual depreciation = (purchase price minus salvage value) / useful life; book value = price minus accumulated depreciation.
- Tax depreciation systems may differ (straight line, reducing balance, initial allowance, full write-off for small items): follow local tax law.
- Operating expense (short life, below capitalisation limit, deducted in year) versus capital expense (longer life, above limit, depreciated).

## Enterprise analysis and pricing
- Enterprise budget isolates one crop or product: profit = gross revenue minus (direct costs + allocated overhead). Gross revenue = yield x marketable share x price. Direct costs typically include planting material, fertiliser, pest management, irrigation, harvest labour, packing and transport.
- Pricing methods: cost-based (unit cost x (1 + markup)), competition-based, value-based. Compute unit cost first, then compare with the achievable market price and state the margin.

## Ratios and benchmark bands (use as screening rules, then adjust to the sector)
- Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities (above about 1.5 comfortable, near 1.0 caution, below 1.0 trouble).
- Debt to assets (under about 0.3 comfortable; above about 0.5 trouble); debt to equity (under about 0.5 comfortable; above about 1.0 trouble).
- Operating expense ratio = operating expenses / gross revenue (under about 0.65 comfortable; above about 0.80 trouble).
- Net farm income ratio = net farm income / gross revenue (above about 0.25 strong; below about 0.10 weak).
- Asset turnover, return on assets (above about 5 per cent) and return on equity (above about 10 per cent).

## Capital budgeting
- Net present value = minus initial investment + sum of cash flow in each year discounted at the required rate; accept if positive. Include salvage value in the final year.
- Payback = initial investment / annual net cash flow.
- IRR = the discount rate at which NPV is zero; accept if above the cost of capital.
- Apply to investments such as tunnels, irrigation, cold rooms and machinery.

## Credit and financing
- Source types: development banks, commercial agricultural lending, microfinance and SACCOs, agricultural credit facilities, development-partner schemes, vendor financing. Record rate, term and collateral from current quotes; do not reuse dated rates.
- Five Cs of credit: character, capacity (debt-service coverage ratio above about 1.25), capital, collateral, conditions. Prepare the plan so each is evidenced.

## Break-even and sensitivity
- Break-even volume = fixed costs / (price per unit minus variable cost per unit); break-even revenue = fixed costs / (1 minus variable cost ratio).
- Sensitivity table: base case plus price down, volume down, cost up, combined worst case and best case. Identify the variable to which profit is most sensitive (often price) and state the mitigation (brand, loyalty, contracts). Direct-subscription models (for example box schemes) use break-even shares = fixed costs / (share price minus variable cost per share).

## Tax and statutory items to list for verification
Income tax filing, presumptive tax for small businesses, rental income tax if leasing land, withholding tax on supplies to public bodies, VAT thresholds and exemptions for agricultural produce, and PAYE and social-security obligations. Confirm current rates with the Chwezi finance engine and the revenue authority.

## Plan-section prompts
Which statements and budgets are included and at what frequency? What is the peak cash deficit and how is it funded? Which enterprises earn their overhead? Which investment metrics justify major assets? What sensitivities matter most?
