# Agrovet (Farm Inputs Retail): Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers a retail shop selling seeds, fertilisers, crop-protection chemicals, animal feeds, veterinary supplies and small sprayers to farmers.

## Market and customer
- Demand scales with commercial farming intensity: zones with intensive horticulture, tea, coffee or NGO-supported outgrower schemes generate the highest input spend per farmer.
- The category is crowded in mature farming areas (often one shop within every few kilometres). Differentiate through technical advice, genuine (non-counterfeit) products, and deep stock of locally preferred brands.
- Site for high farmer density and a clear gap to the nearest strong competitor. Estimate demand from farmers in catchment x average input spend per season x share captured.

## Operating model
- Group products by use (fertilisers, seeds, agrochemicals, acaricides, veterinary supplies, feeds) with the shop visible from the road.
- Stock to the dominant local crops and the farming calendar; pre-position fertilisers and seed four to six weeks before rains; avoid tying capital in slow lines.
- Supply: county or town distributors; obtain credit terms only after a record of consistent cash purchases.
- Credit policy: extending credit to farmers is the main cause of agrovet cash-flow collapse. If you must, cap it, document it and time it to harvest.
- Advisory service is part of the offer; train staff or partner with an extension officer or agronomist.
- Growth path: with volume and capital, become a sub-distributor or wholesaler for input companies, which usually require substantial security deposits.

## Equipment and capex checklist
Shelving, counter, weighing and measuring tools, signage, renovation, licences, opening stock (the largest item), rent deposit, first months of wages.

## Unit economics drivers
- Margin differs by category: fertilisers carry thinner percentage margins; agrochemicals and foliar feeds carry wider margins. Blend the mix to reach a target average and model it by category.
- Revenue = customers per day x average basket, with seasonal peaks; use monthly seasonality rather than a flat daily figure.
- Break-even sales = fixed costs / blended gross margin percentage; include shrinkage from expiry and damage.
- Fertiliser prices are volatile because of import cost, subsidy programmes and demand seasonality; re-verify current buying prices with local distributors.

## Regulation and licensing checks
County or district trading licence plus product-specific registration: seed dealers (seed regulator), agrochemical dealers (pesticide regulator), veterinary chemical dealers (veterinary regulator). Examples: Kenya, KEPHIS and PCPB; Uganda, veterinary drug authority and agriculture ministry seed certification; Tanzania, pesticide research institute and seed certification institute; Rwanda, the agriculture board, with subsidy voucher schemes affecting buying patterns. Confirm current licensing and fees.

## Risks
Farmer credit default, counterfeit or expired stock, seasonal cash troughs, subsidy or import-policy changes, weather and crop failure, price undercutting.

## KPIs
Gross margin by category, stock turn and expiry write-offs, credit outstanding as a share of sales, seasonal sales versus plan, repeat farmer share.

## Plan-section prompts
Which crops and farming calendar define the stock? What is the advisory offer? What credit rule protects cash? What is the route to a sub-distributor role?
