# Operations and Financial Management for Agribusiness: Plan-Section Reference

Use for the operations, finance and people sections of a plan for a distributor, processor, input supplier or similar agribusiness. For tax and accounting treatment, verify with the Chwezi finance engine.

## Financial statements to include
Balance sheet (current assets, fixed assets, current and long-term liabilities, owner equity) and income statement (net sales, cost of goods sold, gross margin, operating expenses, operating profit, interest, tax, net income) with a common-size view (each line as a percentage of net sales).

## Ratio analysis
- Profitability: return on sales = net income / net sales; return on assets = net income / total assets; return on equity = net income / owner equity.
- Liquidity: current ratio = current assets / current liabilities (about two or above is healthy); quick ratio = (current assets minus inventory) / current liabilities (below one signals risk if inventory is slow).
- Solvency: debt to equity = total liabilities / owner equity; equity to assets = owner equity / total assets.
- Efficiency: asset turnover = net sales / total assets; inventory turnover = cost of goods sold / average inventory; receivable turnover = net sales / receivables; wage efficiency = labour cost / net sales.
- DuPont model: ROE = return on sales x asset turnover x leverage (assets / equity). Diagnose which path (margin, asset efficiency, debt) drives performance; more debt raises leverage but increases interest and lowers margin.
- Credit management: bad debts as a percentage of credit sales; compute the extra sales needed to recover a bad debt = loss / margin percentage, which shows why low-margin businesses cannot absorb bad debt. Hold cash reserves as a stated percentage of current liabilities.

## Volume-cost (break-even) analysis
- Classify costs as fixed, variable or semi-variable.
- Contribution to overhead (CTO) per unit of sales = 1 minus variable costs as a share of sales. Break-even sales = fixed costs / CTO.
- Profit planning: sales needed for a target profit = (fixed costs + target profit) / CTO.
- Sensitivity: new fixed cost (for example a truck) requires extra sales = cost / CTO; a one-point rise in variable cost share raises break-even; a price cut lowers CTO sharply and can raise break-even by a large percentage. Rule: never sell below variable cost except as a deliberate loss leader or to clear obsolete stock.

## Capital investment analysis
- Payback = initial investment / annual net cash inflow (ignores time value).
- Simple rate of return = average annual net income / initial investment.
- Net present value = sum of net cash flows discounted at the cost of capital minus initial investment; accept if positive.
- Internal rate of return = the discount rate where NPV is zero (interpolate between two rates); accept if above the required return.
- Compounding: FV = PV x (1 + i)^n; PV = FV / (1 + i)^n.

## Financing
- Short-term loans for seasonal inventory and receivables; intermediate loans for equipment; long-term loans for land and buildings; equity for permanent capital.
- Build an amortisation schedule (payment, interest, principal, balance) for each loan.
- Sources: commercial banks, agricultural credit institutions and development banks, government or youth programmes, leasing (operating versus capital), bonds and debentures, equity and retained earnings. Match term to asset life.

## Operations management
- Facility location factors: proximity to raw materials and markets, labour, transport, utilities, regulation, community.
- Layout: process, product, hybrid, fixed-position.
- Job design: meet output and quality goals; make jobs safe and motivating; consider enlargement, enrichment and self-directed teams.
- Agricultural specifics: seasonality (size for peak load), perishability and cold chain, input variability, bulk handling, food safety (HACCP, traceability), weather dependence.

## Supply chain and inventory
- Components: master production schedule, materials requirements planning, inventory management, distribution.
- Reasons to hold inventory: buffer against uncertainty, seasonal production, quantity discounts, pipeline stock, speculation. Review systems: periodic (low-value, stable items) or continuous with reorder point (high-value, variable items).
- Warehouse placement: forward (near customers) versus backward (near production). Choose transport mode (truck, rail, barge, container) on cost, flexibility and speed.
- Move from lowest purchase price to total cost of ownership, fewer deeper supplier partnerships, information sharing and supplier quality responsibility.

## Human resources
- Structure: line, line-and-staff, functional; choose by size.
- HR process: job analysis, description, specification, recruitment, selection, training, performance evaluation, compensation.
- Leadership styles (autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire, transformational) and when each fits.
- Motivation: Maslow's hierarchy and Herzberg's two-factor theory. For seasonal and entry-level staff address hygiene factors (pay, conditions, security) first; for skilled managers emphasise recognition, autonomy and growth.

## Plan-section prompts
Which ratios and what targets? What is the break-even and its sensitivities? Which investment metrics justify major assets? What financing mix and schedule? What inventory system and peak-season capacity? How are staff recruited and retained?

Sources consulted: Barnard, Akridge, Dooley and Foltz, Agribusiness Management, 4th edn (Routledge, 2012), plus standard corporate-finance and operations texts. Worked figures omitted; recompute with the plan's own data.
