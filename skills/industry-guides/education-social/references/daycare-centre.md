# Daycare Centre (Infants and Toddlers): Plan-Section Reference

Use when the plan covers a home-based or premises-based day care for children from about six months to three years in urban and peri-urban areas.

## Market and customer
- Demand drivers: more women in formal work and enterprise, unreliable domestic help, minimum-wage rules raising the cost of home care, and nuclear families without extended-family support.
- Tiers: low-end (convenience, basic facilities), mid-level (better toys, some training, cleaner setting), high-end (trained caregivers, insurance, activities, transport). Fees, locations and differentiators differ by tier.
- Price anchor: parents compare daycare fees with what they pay a house help. Learn the local house-help rate before setting price.
- Competition: typically many centres per estate; churn is high, and the leading failure cause is running out of capital before enrolment reaches break-even, not lack of demand. Allow weeks to months to build trust and minimum enrolment.
- Seasonal enrolment peaks at school-term starts and around mid-year job changes.
- Adding basic early childhood education (songs, colours, letters) supports higher fees and reduces competition from pure minding.

## Operating model
- Trust is the product: named, consistent caregivers who know each child, prompt communication on illness or incidents, a visibly clean environment, happy children at pickup, and word of mouth among mothers.
- Staffing: about one caregiver per six to eight children; burnout and quality decline above about fifteen. Hire for care and temperament first. Fixed salary supports retention better than daily wage or commission. Caregiver turnover is very high in the sector and is the top operational risk: pay fairly, train, and have relief cover.
- Hours are long (for example morning to early evening, six days); include meals, naps, hygiene, play and learning routines; keep incident and attendance registers.

## Equipment and capex checklist
Mattresses, toys, utensils and cooking gear, potties, basic play equipment, television (optional), tables and chairs, charts and books, first-aid kit and fire extinguisher, rent deposit and renovation, initial staff and utilities.

## Unit economics drivers
- Revenue = children x daily fee x days per month. Cost = rent (or allocated home space), caregiver pay, food, utilities, supplies, licences.
- Break-even children per day = monthly fixed and variable costs / (fee per day x operating days); test at low, base and high enrolment. Below the break-even count the centre loses money.
- Compare parent monthly fee with the house-help benchmark to check affordability.
- Set opening capital to cover the trust-building period.

## Regulation and licensing checks
County or city business permit, public health licence (premises, toilets, kitchen), caregiver health certificates, and early-childhood education or child-care registration where the authority requires it (rules differ by county and country). Uganda: pre-primary registration through district education offices; Tanzania: education ministry registration and social-security registration for caregivers; Rwanda: the gender and child-protection ministry and district offices regulate child-care centres with stricter enforcement. Verify with local authorities before opening; operating without a permit carries enforcement risk.

## Safeguarding
Child-protection policy, staff vetting, safe premises, administering medicines, emergency and fire procedures, and pickup authorisation.

## Risks
Undercapitalisation, high staff turnover, safeguarding incident, regulatory action, low enrolment, parent late payment.

## KPIs
Enrolment versus break-even, attendance rate, monthly fee collection rate, staff turnover, parent satisfaction, incidents.

## Plan-section prompts
Which tier and what fee versus the local house-help rate? How many children break even? What capital covers ramp-up? How are caregivers recruited, paid and retained? Which licences apply here?

Sources consulted: Crack A Business Kenya, daycare market survey (c. 2014); single-case figures omitted and must be re-collected locally.
