# Wines and Spirits Retail Shop

Use this reference when a plan covers a shop selling spirits, wines and beer for takeaway or brief on-premises consumption. Demand for affordable spirits grows when beer prices rise, but the business is volatile: licensing regimes change, authorities intervene, and counterfeit spirits are a health and legal hazard. Figures are planning assumptions from an older regional case; verify licences, prices and rules locally.

## 1. Market and customer

- Demand drivers: rising beer prices pushing beer drinkers towards spirits; lower-income consumers moving from draught beer to spirits after price increases.
- Customers: estate residents, workers on the way home, students (in low-income areas), younger drinkers (in trendy formats).
- Competition: several outlets per estate; many open and close each year. Count outlets within a fixed radius, note hours and licence type.

## 2. Business models

1. Strictly takeaway: buy and leave; faces competition from supermarkets; found in town centres and mid-income areas.
2. Flash and standing: brief consumption on premises, minimal seating.
3. Sit-in: chairs, tables, television and music, close to a bar in feel; specialises in spirits while stocking beer.
Add-ons: hot water and lemons for mixing, draught beer, snacks, soft drinks; a diversified revenue mix improves stability.

## 3. Inputs and suppliers

Core spirits by local brand tiers, a minimum beer range even in a spirits shop, mixers. Buy from authorised distributors only; counterfeit spirits circulate and some leak even into mainstream channels, so verify authenticity (seals, excise stamps, supplier invoices) and keep receipts. Most wholesalers sell in small quantities (several bottles per transaction), so full cartons are not needed initially. Stock to your target market's income level; avoid premium brands in low-income areas.

## 4. Equipment and capex (categories)

Chairs and tables, television and pay-TV subscription, music system, glasses and basins, counter and shelves, signboard. A bare-bones outlet (permit, counter, few chairs, basic music, no television) costs far less but suits only low-income and student markets. Plan: licences; equipment; premises (deposit, first rent, renovation); initial stock; three months of wages, power and miscellaneous.

## 5. Unit economics drivers

- Margin per bottle = retail - wholesale; compute per brand from a live price list.
- Break-even period: an older regional average was about eight months; compute yours as capital / monthly net contribution and be conservative.
- Fixed costs: rent, wages, power, licences, pay-TV; variable: stock, breakage.
- Watch for stock shrinkage; count daily.
- The high margin on cheap counterfeit product is a trap: it exposes owners to confiscation, prosecution and harm to customers.

## 6. Licence types (illustrative structure; verify the current law)

| Type | Hours | Note |
|---|---|---|
| General retail alcohol licence | Restricted evening hours, longer at weekends | Correct for takeaway in many regimes |
| Wholesale licence | No hour restriction (technically wholesale) | Often preferred by retailers for longer hours; confirm the licensing office accepts it for your activity |
| Off-licence | Very limited hours | Usually too restrictive |
Fee schedules differ by city, urban, and rural classification and by half-year or annual term; counties often apply their own schedule. Prefer an annual licence. Confirm the class, fee, hours and renewal with the licensing office.

## 7. Other permits

Business permit (usually a prerequisite for the alcohol licence), public health licence after premises inspection, signboard permit, worker medical certificates, music licences if music is played, environmental noise compliance, tax registration. Country notes: Uganda (city authority trade licence, tax registration; strict excise enforcement so avoid illicit stock); Tanzania (business registration, tax number, local alcohol licence, product-standards inspection); Rwanda (registration, tax number, product standards, regulated hours, one of the strictest regimes).

## 8. Operational risks and mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Authority intervention and unpredictable licensing | Hold all lawful permits, know the local licensing office and cooperate with inspections; do not plan informal payments |
| Counterfeit spirits | Buy from authorised distributors; check seals and excise stamps; keep invoices |
| Tax enforcement | Keep receipts; know stock provenance |
| Agencies (health, standards, drug-abuse control, music rights) | Obtain the relevant licences; cooperate |
| County policy change (sudden closures of all outlets) | Diversify revenue; keep cash reserves; monitor local politics |
| Security | Guard or security arrangement; cash handling routine |

## 9. Location selection

Assess area income and drinking culture, competitor count within about 500 metres, permitted hours locally, proximity to security offices (more visible means more scrutiny), customer access and visibility, and renovation cost.

## 10. KPIs

Bottles sold per day by category; gross margin; stock variance; days to break-even; share of sales by product line; complaints; days closed.

## 11. Plan-section prompts

- Which model and licence class, and are they accepted locally?
- How is authentic supply assured?
- What is the regulatory shock scenario and the cash buffer?
- What is the location evidence and competitor density?
- How will revenue be diversified?
