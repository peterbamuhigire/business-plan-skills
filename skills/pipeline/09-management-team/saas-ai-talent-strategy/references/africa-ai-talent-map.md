## 4. Retention levers (the operating reality)

Top African AI talent is recruited aggressively by US/EU companies at international comp. Realistic retention levers:

1. **Meaningful equity** — vesting schedule that produces real wealth at exit
2. **Mission alignment** — DFI / AI-for-good story matters to talent who chose African deployment
3. **Technical leadership track** — engineers grow without being forced into management
4. **Research / publishing time** — 10-20% time for papers, open-source, conferences
5. **Conference budget** — Deep Learning Indaba, IndabaX, NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR attendance
6. **University affiliation** — AIMS visiting, AMMI affiliate, university teaching opportunities
7. **Sabbatical policy** — 6-12 weeks per 3-4 years
8. **Distributed-team flexibility** — pick the city that fits life
9. **Local-context career arc** — building AI that understands cooperatives, mobile money, USSD, local languages is a non-replicable career path
10. **Mentorship infrastructure** — senior international AI advisors / board members accessible to AI team

## 5. Diversity / inclusion targets

DFI-grade plans should set explicit targets:
- Gender ratio in AI team: typical target 40%+ women in AI engineering (continental average is far lower; this is a real commitment)
- Country diversity: pan-African plans aim for 3+ African countries represented in AI team
- Language coverage in team: internal representation of operating-country languages
- Junior-to-senior ratio: appropriate for stage
- Disability inclusion: accessibility hiring practice

## 6. Key-person contingency

For each AI lead:
- Documentation discipline (architecture, decisions, eval methodologies all written; no tribal knowledge)
- Backup ownership (a second person can run the function 2-4 weeks)
- IP assignment + NDA in employment contract
- Notice period 3-6 months for Head of AI
- Departure included in `meta-living-plan-governance` trigger-replan event list
