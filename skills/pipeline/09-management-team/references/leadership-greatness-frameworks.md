# Leadership and Strategic Discipline Frameworks for Business Plans

Use this reference to test a plan for leadership quality, team-building logic, strategic focus, honest risk handling, disciplined execution, compounding growth and technology choices. The frameworks come from Collins's research on sustained-performance companies (Level 5 leadership, "First Who", Hedgehog Concept, Stockdale Paradox, Flywheel and Doom Loop); the application to plans is this engine's own.

## 1. Leadership character (Level 5)

Five-level ladder: capable individual; contributing team member; competent manager; effective leader (drives a compelling vision); Level 5 executive, who builds enduring performance by combining personal humility with professional will.

| Professional will | Personal humility |
|---|---|
| Delivers superb results and catalyses change | Modest, shuns adulation, never boastful |
| Resolves to do whatever must be done | Acts with quiet determination, relies on standards, not charisma |
| Builds something that lasts and readies successors | Directs ambition to the company, not the self |
| Takes responsibility for poor results ("mirror") | Credits others and luck for success ("window") |

Apply in the plan:
- Founder bios: emphasise what was built and what the team achieved; avoid "I"-saturated narrative.
- Tone: use "we"; credit team, advisers, mentors.
- Succession: show that the venture does not depend on one person; describe how leadership capability is being built at several levels.
- Risk section: own past failures honestly.
- Vision: express ambition for the company, not personal wealth or fame.
Red flag for funders: a plan full of personal hero narrative signals a celebrity-led venture.

## 2. People before direction ("First Who, Then What")

Principle: secure the right people, remove the wrong people, place people in the right roles, then fix the direction.
Why: people who joined for who is on the team adapt when direction changes; the right people need little pushing; a vision without the right people is irrelevant.
Rigorous, not ruthless: apply exacting standards consistently.
1. When in doubt, do not hire; keep looking.
2. When a people change is needed, act promptly, since keeping the wrong person is unfair to the right ones.
3. Put the best people on the biggest opportunities, not the biggest problems.
Weight character (work ethic, intelligence, dedication, values) over CV; skills can be taught.

Apply in the plan:
| Section | Application |
|---|---|
| Management team | Lead with who people are (character, values, complementary strengths), then titles |
| Hiring plan | State standards and process; accept slower growth over compromised hiring |
| Skills-gap analysis | Name gaps honestly; acknowledging them signals rigour |
| Compensation | Fair pay plus mission motivation; do not claim pay design alone produces results |
| Organisation chart | Show who fills key seats; if empty, say so and give the plan and criteria |
Template line: "The head of operations built the supply chain of [type of distributor] from [starting volume] to [ending volume] over [years] and joined for [mission reason]. We would rather leave the finance seat empty for [months] than fill it without both financial rigour and mission alignment." (Fill with verified facts only.)

## 3. Strategic focus (Hedgehog Concept)

A simple, clear concept from the intersection of three questions (after Berlin's hedgehog and fox):
1. What can we be the best at, and what can we not be the best at? (Competence alone is not enough.)
2. What single ratio, "profit per X", drives our economics?
3. What are we deeply passionate about? (Discover it; do not manufacture it.)
All three are needed: passion without economics is a hobby; economics without best-in-class potential is mediocrity; best-in-class without passion is unsustainable.

Method:
1. Convene a small council (5 to 12 people).
2. Debate the three questions with evidence.
3. Decide, observe results, learn.
4. Repeat. Clarity typically takes years, so if the concept is not yet clear, say so and describe the refinement process.
Apply: filter every initiative through the three tests; track and forecast the chosen ratio in the financial model; write a "will not do" list; reject diversification where the business cannot be best.

Worked pattern (agri-processing, Uganda): best at converting a local staple into shelf-stable products; economic engine is profit per tonne processed (drivers: fixed-cost absorption above a throughput threshold, yield, post-harvest loss reduced); passion is community and crop. The plan then rejects unrelated crops. All figures must come from the plan's own verified data.

## 4. Facing reality (Stockdale Paradox)

Hold two things together: unwavering faith that the business will prevail and disciplined confrontation of the current brutal facts. Faith without facts is delusion; facts without faith is despair.
Four practices for a climate where truth is heard: lead with questions; hold dialogue and debate, not coercion; run no-blame reviews (what happened, not who); build red-flag mechanisms that surface bad news automatically.
Apply:
| Section | Application |
|---|---|
| Risk analysis | List the hard facts unsanitised, then show how the business prevails |
| Market analysis | Include unfavourable data, then explain why the business succeeds anyway |
| Financials | Include a genuinely harsh downside scenario and show survival |
| SWOT and competitor analysis | State weaknesses; funders will find them anyway |
| Executive summary | Conviction backed by evidence |
Test: read the risk section aloud; if it reads like marketing, rewrite.

## 5. Disciplined execution

Matrix of entrepreneurship and discipline: high entrepreneurship and low discipline is a fragile start-up; low entrepreneurship and low discipline is bureaucracy; low entrepreneurship and high discipline is a rigid hierarchy; the target is both, creative discipline.
- Small habits compound: show measurable routines (weekly unit-economics review, shared KPI dashboards, systematic daily waste audits).
- Concrete objectives: set measurable milestones with dates and metrics at the start of the year; report against exactly those, with no retrospective redefinition; if a target was missed, say so and state the lesson.

| Milestone | Target | Date | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| First production run | Volume processed | Quarter | Units of output |
| Break-even month | Revenue at least costs | Month N | Net monthly profit |
| Key hire | Appointed and onboarded | Quarter | Role filled |

- Culture, not a tyrant: results that depend on one forceful leader collapse when the leader leaves. Describe systems and processes that enforce discipline independently of any one person.

## 6. Momentum (Flywheel and Doom Loop)

Flywheel: sustained results come from many consistent pushes in one direction, not one miracle event. Pattern: a buildup phase (often several years) of quiet accumulation (right people, facts faced, focus clarified), then a breakthrough in which results accelerate; outsiders see overnight success.
Doom loop: launch a big programme, fail to get sustained results, lurch to a new direction, leader or programme, repeat. Signs: chronic inconsistency, skipping buildup, a new leader resetting direction, acquisitions used to create rather than accelerate momentum.
Apply:
| Section | Application |
|---|---|
| Implementation timeline | Sequence milestones as turns of a flywheel, each building on the last; avoid disconnected big-bang initiatives |
| Financial projections | Model buildup then acceleration; a hockey-stick is credible only with the mechanics shown |
| Growth strategy | Show compounding (first customers validate the model, referrals supply the next tranche, proven record unlocks partnerships and finance) |
| Investor narrative | State which turns are complete and what breakthrough looks like |
| Acquisitions | Position as accelerators of a flywheel already turning |
Illustrative sequence for a logistics start-up: anchor clients prove the model; performance data negotiates lower fuel cost and lifts margin; reinvested margin adds a vehicle and capacity; capacity attracts referral clients; proven model unlocks asset finance; regional expansion produces breakthrough. Use the plan's own verified numbers.

## 7. Technology as an accelerator

Test: does the technology fit directly within the focus concept? If yes, pioneer its application; if no, ignore it or match parity. Technology accelerates momentum; it does not create it, and cannot rescue a poor strategy. Contrast: momentum-led firms adopt selectively and let technology follow strategy; doom-loop firms chase every trend out of fear.
Procedure:
1. State the focus concept.
2. For each technology, ask whether it helps the business be best at its core activity, improves the profit-per-X ratio, and fits the team's passion.
3. Yes to all three: invest aggressively. Otherwise defer or ignore.
Illustration: quality grading that raises accuracy and lowers rejection improves profit per tonne (pioneer); traceability that adds transparency with marginal profit impact (match parity); an internal HR chatbot with no link to the core (ignore for now).
Investor message: technology spending is filtered by the focus test so scarce capital accelerates the flywheel.

## 8. Plan audit checklist

| Plan section | Framework | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Executive summary | Level 5; Stockdale | Ambition for the company with honest acknowledgement of challenges? |
| Company description | Focus concept | Can the concept be stated in one sentence? |
| Products and services | Focus (best at) | Are products ones the business can be best at? |
| Market analysis | Facing reality | Is the market shown as it is, including inconvenient data? |
| Target market | Focus (economic ratio) | Is the profit-per-X ratio identified? |
| Competitive analysis | Focus; facing reality | Honest about where competitors are better? |
| Marketing and sales | Flywheel | Does the growth model compound? |
| Operations | Disciplined execution | Is discipline embedded in the system? |
| Management team | First Who; Level 5 | Leads with who; "we" language? |
| Financials | Flywheel; facing reality | Realistic buildup, tracked ratio, honest downside? |
| Funding request | Flywheel | Fuel for a turning flywheel, not a rescue of a stationary one? |
| Risk analysis | Stockdale | Both brutal honesty and faith in the route? |
| Implementation | Flywheel; discipline | Sequential turns; fixed, concrete targets? |
| Technology | Accelerator test | Does each investment pass the three tests? |

Five "stop doing" questions: which activities sit outside the three tests (remove); where does "I" hide a team effort (fix); where was a hard fact softened (restore); where is a big-bang initiative that should be incremental (restructure); where does technology fail the test (cut or justify).
Final test: would a humble, determined, hype-averse reader fund this plan?

Sources consulted: Collins, Good to Great, 2001; Berlin, The Hedgehog and the Fox, 1953.
