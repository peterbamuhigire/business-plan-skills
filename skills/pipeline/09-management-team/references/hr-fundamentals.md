# HR Fundamentals for the Management Plan (Uganda-Adapted)

Use this reference when Section 09 (and the operations and financial sections that cost people) must set out how the business plans its workforce, recruits, onboards, appraises, pays, disciplines, retains and exits staff, and how it meets Ugandan employment obligations. Statutory rates, thresholds, penalties and section numbers below are planning assumptions recorded at drafting; re-verify each with a dated official source (Ministry of Gender, Labour and Social Development, NSSF, URA, legislation text) and with the Chwezi finance engine for tax and payroll before they enter the plan. See also `uganda-labour-market-2025.md` and `payroll-hr.md` in this folder.

## 1. Workforce planning and job design

### 1.1 Before opening a vacancy
Answer these planning questions:
1. Title and reporting line: align the title with grade and responsibility; titles carry social weight with clients.
2. Timeline and budget: a wrong hire costs more than a temporary vacancy.
3. Interview panel: name everyone who must meet the candidate; peers help assess fit and present the organisation.
4. Skills, education and experience: separate must-have from desirable before advertising.
5. Vacancy context: find out why the last holder left (promotion, skills gap, misconduct) before rewriting the role.

### 1.2 Job descriptions and person specifications
A job description records title, short summary, reporting line, interacting departments, main responsibilities and minimum qualifications. It supports appraisal, grading, legal statements and any later disciplinary or redundancy process. Review it annually with the employee. A person specification records the ideal candidate (competencies, attributes, full experience range). Keep the two separate so that specifications do not slip into discriminatory or irrelevant criteria (age, physical attributes).
Legal check: Ugandan law requires a written statement of the main particulars of employment shortly after start (assumed within eight weeks; verify). Include title, nature and place of work, hours, pay and method, leave, notice and probation.

### 1.3 Labour market context to plan for
- Large informal sector; formal employment brings contract, social-security and tax duties, and informal arrangements carry legal risk.
- Technical and professional skills concentrate in the capital; budget relocation or training for up-country sites.
- Young population: entry-level supply is plentiful, experienced middle managers are scarce.
- Universities and institutes supply graduates; internship links improve the pipeline.

## 2. Recruitment and selection

### 2.1 Sourcing
Networking through the hiring manager is the most productive first step. Channel choice:
| Channel | Best for |
|---|---|
| Messaging groups (industry, alumni, professional) | All levels, fastest reach |
| Social media pages and job groups | Entry to mid-level, low cost |
| Professional networking site | Professional and management roles |
| National newspapers (print and online) | Broad awareness, senior roles |
| University job boards | Graduates and interns |
| Professional associations | Specialist roles |
| Employee referral | All levels; cheapest and strongest cultural-fit signal |
| Trade unions and sector bodies | Skilled trades |
Advertisement: reflect the role and culture, state key requirements, give application method, deadline and a named contact; for lower-level posts, accept a phone or messaging contact instead of a CV.

### 2.2 Referral programme
Communicate openings; state the reward before referral; give prompt feedback; reward visibly; target hard-to-fill roles if budget is limited. A modest cash amount or non-cash recognition (public thanks, airtime) can work; size any reward as a planning assumption.

### 2.3 Phone screen
Check: name, role applied for, current employer and role, reason for leaving, availability, salary expectation (accept a range), required licences or skills, motive, interview availability. Close politely if the candidate is unsuitable and send written rejections to unsuccessful candidates.

### 2.4 Interviews
Structured interviews beat unstructured chats. Formats: behavioural (past situations, using situation-task-action-result), competency-based (questions built from the role's competency list), situational (realistic scenarios, for example a supplier short-delivering and blaming transport). Use at least two interviewers scoring independently before comparing. Avoid questions on marital status, children, tribe, religion, pregnancy, disability (unless directly job-relevant with accommodation) or HIV status.

### 2.5 References and background checks
Speak directly to previous supervisors; for managers, also to peers and reports; ask open questions on results; record date, person and content. Telephone reference topics: dates and positions; quality of work with an example; relationships with colleagues and supervisor; greatest accomplishment; what they would want changed; anything else relevant.
Local checks: police clearance for finance, security and child-facing roles; verification of academic certificates with the examination and higher-education authorities where credentials are critical (certificate fraud occurs); credit checks only with written consent.

### 2.6 Offers and contracts
Follow verbal offers promptly with a written letter; verify every detail; say whether the letter is a contract; never email it to the candidate's current employer; set an acceptance deadline. Senior roles need a lawyer-drafted contract covering notice, restraint clauses, confidentiality and termination grounds. Avoid verbal job-security promises that create implied terms.

## 3. Onboarding

### 3.1 First day
Tour and introductions; access and keys; job description, expectations and short-term goals; technical set-up; payroll and statutory forms; explanation of jargon; a meal with the team; assign a patient buddy (not necessarily the top performer). End the day with a manager review.

### 3.2 Orientation programme
Content: history, mission, values and direction; structure and where the role fits; handbook; benefits (social security, health cover, leave); safety and evacuation; the appraisal process, with a blank form. Run it one-to-one or in small groups within about two weeks; a structured 90-minute session reduces early turnover and policy misunderstandings.

### 3.3 Probation
Tell the employee the duration and what is assessed (offer letter and day one). Legal ceiling assumed at six months with notice as per contract (verify). Evaluate formally before the end; do not extend probation as a "second chance"; end the contract during probation where basic conduct rules are broken.
30/60/90-day plan: days 1 to 30 learning (role, team, systems, compliance); days 31 to 60 contributing (first independent deliverables, relationships); days 61 to 90 performing (targets met without close supervision).

### 3.4 Personnel file checklist
Signed contract or offer acknowledgement; signed handbook acknowledgement; national identity copy; certificate copies; photographs; police clearance where required; social-security number or registration; tax identification number; bank details; next of kin; medical certificate where the role requires it (food handling, driving).

## 4. Performance management

1. System: a continuous cycle of goals, feedback, coaching and review that closes gaps, uses strengths, links individual to organisational goals and supports pay decisions transparently. Design with managers and employees; secure top-management support and train managers or it becomes paperwork.
2. Goals: SMART (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound) or objectives and key results (objective plus measurable results). Agree three to five priority objectives, success measures, resources and tracking.
3. One-to-ones: weekly 15 to 30 minutes (status, obstacles, priorities); monthly 45 to 60 minutes (progress, development, feedback); quarterly formal progress review. Give specific feedback ("three errors in last week's supplier reconciliation") and ask the employee to assess first.
4. Formal review: annual or semi-annual (the latter allows mid-year correction). Elements: standards tied to the job description; rating scale; written comment required at either extreme; employee self-appraisal; development suggestions; review of last period's goals; next goals; signatures. Keep the form to two to four pages.
5. Multi-rater review: for cross-functional structures, at least six raters, facilitated, used for development more than pay.
6. Coaching before formal action: explain the gap, hear the reason, ask for the employee's fix, agree measurable actions, express confidence, record the plan as a coaching note. If no improvement, run an improvement plan: standard not met, examples with dates, a 30 to 60 day period, required improvement and measure, consequences, signatures. It is a genuine last attempt, not a file-building step; close it formally if the standard is met.
7. Calibration: train managers on every rating level; the top rating must be awardable; hold calibration sessions to reduce leniency, harshness and halo effects; include timely completion of appraisals in manager objectives.
8. Merit pay link (illustrative bands to be set by the plan): scale increases with rating, none for unsatisfactory; separate merit from cost-of-living adjustment, because a merit rise below inflation is a real pay cut.

## 5. Compensation and benefits

### 5.1 Compensation philosophy
Write one page answering: affordability at target margins; positioning against the local market; bonus or incentive use; cost-of-living policy; benefits budget and its effect on pay; who decides pay; how it is communicated.

### 5.2 Grade structure
Group jobs of similar value into grades with minimum, midpoint (market rate for full competence) and maximum. Typical SME structure: about seven grades from general labour and support through officers, supervisors, managers, heads of department to directors. Set each grade's monthly range from current market data; do not reuse indicative ranges from other documents without re-benchmarking. Sources: national labour-force surveys, manufacturers' and sector association surveys, consultancy salary surveys, and advertised salaries on job boards. Expect capital-city rates to exceed up-country rates materially for the same role.

### 5.3 Statutory contributions and tax (assumptions to verify)
- Social security: employers above a size threshold (assumed five employees) register and remit monthly; assumed rates 10% employer and 5% employee of gross wages, due by the 15th of the following month; late interest applies; keep receipts for several years.
- Pay-as-you-earn: register before the first payroll; every employee needs a tax identification number; withhold each pay run; file and remit by the 15th of the following month; annual return by year end; penalties for lateness. Use the current progressive monthly bands from the revenue authority. Do not hard-code bands in the plan.

### 5.4 Leave (assumed statutory minimums; verify)
Annual leave after 12 months' service (assumed 21 working days, pro rata); sick leave (assumed one month full pay and two months half pay, medically certified); maternity leave (assumed 60 working days, full pay); paternity leave (assumed four working days); compassionate leave at employer discretion; gazetted public holidays with compensatory time or premium pay when worked.
Practice: keep a leave register; require two weeks' notice for annual leave; cap carry-over (for example 10 days) to limit leave liability; pay untaken leave at termination.

### 5.5 Incentives
- Annual bonus: tied to individual rating and company profit, as a percentage of salary, paid when promised.
- Commission: percentage of sales, with draw for new sellers and hold-back where returns are high.
- Goal-based bonus: paid for specified company, team or project results; state eligibility and calculation before the period.
- Sign-on bonus: paid over 6 to 12 months with clawback if the employee leaves early.
Principles: document each plan; distribute it; pay when promised because withheld bonuses destroy trust; evaluate annually against intended behaviour.

## 6. Employee relations and discipline

### 6.1 Communication
Poor communication drives most employee-relations problems; staff who feel uninformed are much more likely to consider leaving (survey evidence; verify current data). Communicate strategy, results, changes (before rumour spreads) and achievements. Channels: team messaging group, monthly all-staff meeting, notice board, private one-to-ones for sensitive matters.

### 6.2 Recognition
Visible, timely, specific recognition beats infrequent large payments. Link to behaviours; make the criteria known; recognise publicly; mix monetary (airtime, vouchers, cash) and non-monetary (praise in meetings, certificates, training support, an early finish, an extra leave day).

### 6.3 Progressive discipline
Purpose: correct, not build a termination case.
| Step | Action | Record |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verbal warning | Dated note on file |
| 2 | First written warning | Letter, employee signs receipt |
| 3 | Final written warning | Letter stating termination follows without improvement |
| 4 | Termination | Statutory process |
Match the sanction to the offence and to precedent; inconsistent treatment grounds an unfair-termination claim. Gross misconduct (theft, fraud, sexual harassment, violence, intoxication at work, wilful damage), wilful disobedience of a lawful order and unexplained absence (assumed three consecutive days) may justify summary dismissal, but still hold a brief investigation.

### 6.4 Termination (assumed rules; verify)
Notice: scales with pay period (day, week, fortnight, month), with two weeks' written notice for those employed six months or more on any basis; pay in lieu if notice is not worked. For cause: written reasons, a chance to respond, consideration of the response, a termination letter with reason, date and entitlements. Severance: assumed at least one month's pay per completed year for redundancy after six months' service. Probation: shorter notice per contract, no full progressive process.

### 6.5 Grievances
Three steps: verbal to the supervisor (reply within 48 hours); written to HR or a senior manager (meeting within five working days); escalation to the managing director or appeals body (decision within five working days). Confidentiality and a written non-retaliation policy are essential. External routes: labour officers for mediation and the industrial court.

### 6.6 Harassment and equal opportunity
Policy elements: zero tolerance; definitions (sexual harassment, bullying, tribal discrimination); at least two named reporting contacts of different genders; confidentiality and non-retaliation; investigation process; consequences. Train managers annually. Prohibited grounds under Ugandan law include sex, tribe, race, colour, religion, political opinion, national extraction, social origin and disability; verify current statutes.

## 8. Handbook

Purpose: inform rights and duties, give managers a consistent framework, protect the business in disputes, standardise treatment, and convey culture. Write for the business; do not copy another firm's handbook.
Contents:
1. Welcome: history, mission, values, founder message.
2. Equal opportunity and anti-harassment.
3. Employment and pay: hours, overtime, probation, pay period, grades and review policy, appraisal, attendance, dress.
4. Leave: annual, sick, maternity, paternity, compassionate, public holidays, application process.
5. Benefits: social security, medical cover, airtime, transport, others.
6. Conduct: professional standards, confidentiality, conflicts of interest, company assets, social media, gifts.
7. Discipline and grievance: progressive steps, gross-misconduct list, grievance route, whistleblowing.
8. Termination: notice, resignation, exit interview, return of property, final pay.
9. Safety: rules, accident reporting, emergency contacts and evacuation.
Add a signed acknowledgement form and a disclaimer that the handbook is not a contract and may be updated with notice. Review whenever legislation changes and at least every two years; announce changes in writing.

## 9. HR metrics

| Metric | Formula | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Turnover | leavers in period / average headcount x 100 | Separate voluntary (early warning of management or engagement problems) from involuntary; replacement cost is often a large share of annual salary (estimate from own data) |
| Time-to-hire | days from vacancy approval to signed acceptance | Track by level and stage; long times disrupt operations |
| Cost-per-hire | total recruitment cost / hires | Include advertising, agency fees, panel time, assessments, checks, onboarding materials; referrals and university links lower it |
| Absenteeism | days absent / (employees x working days) x 100 | Split sick from unplanned; rising unplanned absence signals disengagement or financial stress |
| Training hours per employee | total training hours / average headcount | Low values signal underinvestment; set a target in the plan |
| Revenue per employee | annual revenue / average headcount | Links people investment to productivity |
| HR-to-employee ratio | HR staff / employees | Small firms use a generalist and outsource payroll and statutory filings |
Set benchmarks from local sector data, not from this file. Local patterns to watch: absence around public holidays and school-term starts; turnover driven by donor grant cycles.

## 10. Exit management

Exit interviews: run by HR or a neutral manager, never the direct supervisor; private; verbal or written. Questions: reasons for joining; clarity of expectations; support received; most and least enjoyed; main reason for leaving; what could have prevented it; whether they would recommend the business. Aggregate quarterly to spot patterns (for example several exits citing one manager). Treat any disclosed harassment or illegality as a live complaint and investigate.
Exit checklist: final pay including accrued leave; collect property (laptop, phone, vehicle, access cards, keys, uniform); revoke system access on the last day; issue a certificate of service; confirm contact address; process social-security paperwork. References: give neutral factual references (name, title, dates) unless the employee consents to more.
