# Purpose, Mission and Vision Statements (Golden Circle Method)

**Use for:** the company overview section, where the plan must show why the business exists, not only what it sells. The Golden Circle (Sinek) is applied as a drafting and testing method.

## 1. The three layers

| Layer | Content | Plan location |
|---|---|---|
| Why | Purpose, cause or belief; independent of products; not profit | Opening of company overview |
| How | Values and operating principles, written as verbs | Operating approach, quality standards, staff values |
| What | Products and services that prove the Why | Products and revenue lines |

- Profit is a result, not a purpose.
- Write values as actions ("speak plainly to customers", "fix errors before the customer notices"), not nouns ("integrity", "quality"). Actions can be hired for, trained and measured.
- A genuine Why survives changes in product line.
- Lead with Why, then How, then What. Readers, including lenders and investors, decide on trust first and rationalise with features afterwards.

## 2. Three conditions for coherence

1. **Clarity of Why:** stated in plain language. Generic wording ("serve our customers") is functionally absent.
2. **Discipline of How:** hold to the principles under growth or profit pressure.
3. **Consistency of What:** products, pricing, marketing, hiring and service all visibly prove the Why. Authenticity is alignment between what is said and what is done.

## 3. Discovering the Why (look back, not forward)

The Why is discovered from history, not designed from market research. Sources: the founders' formative experiences, a problem or injustice they witnessed, values from family or community, the moment they decided something must change.

Ask the founder(s):
1. Why did you start; what were you trying to change or fix?
2. What problem could you not ignore?
3. What do you believe about the people you serve that others overlook?
4. When has the work felt most meaningful?
5. What would the community lose without the business?
6. What pattern runs through your life history?

Look for the recurring theme; surface it rather than invent it.

## 4. Decision filter test

Test any decision (new product, partnership, price, hire, grant, channel): does it prove what we believe?
- Yes: proceed.
- No: proceed only for short-term reasons such as survival cash flow, and record it as a temporary departure.

Illustrative Uganda applications (illustrative only): a milling cooperative whose Why is fair access to quality inputs passes a bulk input scheme and fails a luxury gift line; a clinic committed to dignified care for all passes informal-settlement outreach and fails a premium-only ward.

Use three to five recent or planned decisions to test the draft Why; revise until it sorts right decisions from wrong ones.

## 5. Adoption strategy link

Innovators and early adopters (roughly the first 16 per cent under Rogers' diffusion curve) buy on belief; the early and late majority want proof and peer recommendation. Sequence: state the Why clearly, attract believers, let them advocate to the majority, and reach the tipping point through referral. Compare Moore's chasm concept. Without a clear Why, early adopters cannot explain the product and the firm ends up buying trial with discounts, which builds transactions rather than loyalty.

## 6. Internal use

A clear Why tells staff why they come to work beyond pay, supports hiring for attitude and belief as well as skill, lets people at any level make founder-quality decisions, and keeps culture self-sustaining.

## 7. Drift: when purpose is lost

Triggers: rapid growth with professional managers focused on efficiency; departure of the founder who embodied the purpose; financial targets becoming the purpose; investor short-termism.

Warning signs: generic or rewritten mission; decisions defaulting to financial metrics; staff unable to state why they work there; price and feature competition only; inconsistent new lines or partnerships; culture shifts after leadership change.

Recovery question: why did we start doing this, and how could we bring that cause to life with today's technology and market?
For new firms, prevent drift by writing the Why into founding documents and culture before scale dilutes it.

## 8. Definitions

| Statement | Describes | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Why (purpose) | Belief or cause | Belief-based; about others; authentic |
| Vision | Future state if the Why is realised | Aspirational and specific enough to recognise |
| Mission | How the organisation pursues the vision daily (Why + How + What) | Actionable; guides decisions and hiring |

Common error: a mission that is only a What ("we provide quality maize flour") or only aspiration without conviction.

## 9. Mission-writing procedure

1. Discover the Why (section 3).
2. Draft the Why statement: "We believe [belief about people or the world]. So we [contribution]." or "To [contribution] so that [change]." It must be about others, express a belief, be memorable, be true, and pass the customer's "so what".
3. Write three to five How principles as verbs.
4. Confirm each What proves the Why; treat exceptions as acknowledged short-term items or a sign the Why is unclear.
5. Combine into a formal statement of two to four sentences and a one-sentence tagline.
6. Read aloud to an outsider and ask what the business believes; they should answer without naming the product.
7. Run the decision filter (section 4).

## 10. Templates

- **Why:** "We believe that [how people or communities deserve to be treated or served]. So everything we do is aimed at [specific change the business makes]."
- **Mission:** "[Organisation] exists to [Why]. We do this by [two or three How principles]. Our [products or services] are proof of that commitment."
- **Vision:** "[Timeframe], [community, sector or world transformed], because [organisations like this] showed it was possible."

## 11. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Problem |
|---|---|
| Profit or market rank as purpose | A result; inspires no one |
| Generic virtue lists | Every firm claims them; no identity |
| Product as purpose | A What, not a Why |
| Aspiration without conviction ("strive", "world-class solutions") | Untestable |
| Written for investors only | Inauthenticity is detectable |
| Too long to recite | Cannot guide decisions |
| Noun-based values | Nobody can be held accountable to a noun |
| Copying competitors | Shows no distinct Why |

## 12. Uganda and East Africa application

Cultural themes that frequently anchor a genuine Why:

| Theme | Expression | Typical fit |
|---|---|---|
| Ubuntu (shared humanity) | One person's success matters when it lifts others | Cooperatives, savings groups, shared-value firms |
| Family legacy | Building something children will inherit | Family firms formalising; succession planning |
| Community uplift | The neighbourhood deserves equal quality | Rural, peri-urban and refugee-settlement services |
| Pride in craft | Ugandan-made means world-class | Manufacturing, food processing, export crafts |
| Closing the gap | Access to health, education, water or finance should not depend on income or location | Social enterprises, impact SMEs |
| Redemptive purpose | Founder's own hardship drives the aim that others avoid it | Businesses founded from lived hardship |

Drafting notes for Uganda plans:
1. The Why should reflect real motivation, not what sounds good to a donor or bank.
2. The How should include Uganda-specific practice: worker treatment (local labour law and ILO standards), community engagement and financial transparency (URA and EFRIS compliance; verify current rules with the Chwezi finance engine).
3. Each What line should visibly pass the decision filter.
4. Draft the Why first in the founder's mother tongue (Luganda, Acholi, Runyankole, etc.) then translate; if the English version feels hollow, check whether it has been softened for an assumed audience.
5. For bank plans, put the mission early in the company overview, then legal structure, registration and history; the Why supports the "character" element of lender assessment (CAMPARI).

## 13. Quick reference

- Why: We believe [fundamental belief].
- How: We [verb-based principles].
- What: Our [products/services] prove it.

Sources consulted: Sinek, S. (2009), *Start With Why*, Portfolio/Penguin; Rogers, E. (1962), *Diffusion of Innovations*; Moore, G. (1991), *Crossing the Chasm*. Cite in the plan where the mission is introduced.
