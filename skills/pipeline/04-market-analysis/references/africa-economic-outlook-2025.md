Country reading notes:
- **Uganda:** one of 10 African countries with a growth rise above 1.0 pp between 2023 and 2024; driven by public investment in domestic value chains and energy; largest current account deficit in the peer group; moderate inflation; fiscal deficit expected to ease as oil revenue arrives (2026–27).
- **Kenya:** shilling appreciated 3.1% in 2024 after 2023 depreciation; improved sentiment; tax-administration digitalisation precedent (+12.7% revenue increase after the 2016/17 reforms); Project Mocha (blockchain tokenisation of coffee trees for smallholders) as a pilot innovation; smallest current account deficit in the peer group.
- **Tanzania:** lowest inflation in East Africa (3.3%); best fiscal position in the peer group (-3.0%); greatest room for public investment.
- **East Africa resilience:** diversified economies with a growing manufactured share in intra-regional trade; 2023 total EAC trade USD 12.1 billion (+13.1% year on year); intra-EAC share 15%.

### Domestic resource mobilisation (opportunity sizing)
Additional domestic resources of about USD 1.43 trillion are estimated, above the USD 1.3 trillion annual SDG financing gap.

| Capital type | Potential | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Fiscal | USD 469.4 billion per year (14.4% of GDP, 2025–29) | Digitalise tax administration, broaden the base, enforce existing rules |
| Natural | 30-fold value uplift | Local mineral processing (DRC bauxite to aluminium: USD 65 to USD 2,335 per tonne) |
| Financial | USD 70 billion (pension funds, 6 countries) plus USD 411.9 billion forex reserves | Ring-fence 1% of pension assets; use central bank reserves |
| Business | USD 560 billion AfCFTA export increase; USD 125.3 billion per year if informality falls 10 pp | Regional value chains; SME formalisation |
| Human | 1–2% GDP growth per 10 pp informality reduction | Demographic dividend (60%+ under 25) |

Related: illicit flows, corruption and profit shifting leak about USD 275 billion per year. Revenue-to-GDP: Africa 19.8% (+2.3 pp since 2015) against Europe/Central Asia 41.0%, Latin America 28.6%, East Asia 26.2%.

## 4. Risks and scenarios

**Tier 1, external shocks**
- **US tariffs (April 2025):** average 10% announced, 90-day pause; US is 5% of Africa's goods trade; regional growth revisions of -0.2 to -0.4 pp; highest exposure Lesotho (apparel 45% of exports to the US) and Botswana (diamonds); Uganda and Tanzania have low direct exposure.
- **Aid cuts:** DAC ODA to Africa USD 35.9 billion (2023); anticipated 7% reduction in 2025 versus 2023, led by US cuts; low-income, aid-dependent countries hit hardest.

**Tier 2, macroeconomic:** persistent inflation (supply bottlenecks, weak monetary transmission, exchange-rate pass-through); widening current account deficits (-2.6% GDP in 2025–26) and weaker investor appetite; exchange-rate volatility.

**Tier 3, structural:** debt distress and high debt service; regional conflict (DRC, Sahel) lowering revenue and raising military spending; climate and agricultural vulnerability, with food supply shocks the main inflation driver.

**Opportunities 2025–2030:** full AfCFTA implementation (USD 560 billion export increase, mostly manufacturing, 173M+ EAC market); local beneficiation of green minerals (cobalt, lithium, manganese); formalisation of the 50–65% informal economy; lower remittance costs and diaspora bonds.

## 5. Implications by plan section

**Market analysis**
- Use East Africa 5.9% as the regional growth anchor for 2025–26 (label as an AfDB projection, verify).
- Uganda 6.8% compares with Africa 3.9% and world 2.8%.
- Tanzania is the most stable East African market on fiscal discipline (-3.0%) and inflation (3.3%).

**Risk section**
- US tariff risk for Uganda and Tanzania is indirect: global slowdown, softer commodity prices, pressure on export revenue.
- Aid cuts: development project delays and payment risk on government contracts.
- Climate: food supply shocks keep inflation risk to the upside despite tight policy.

**Financial projections and funding**
- The USD 1.43 trillion domestic mobilisation estimate supports DFI appetite for East African deals.
- The Kenya tax-digitalisation precedent supports tax-compliance assumptions.
- Uganda remittances of about USD 1.4 billion per year support consumer income and demand assumptions (verify against a current national source).
