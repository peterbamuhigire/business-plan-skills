## 4. Risks and scenarios

**Sovereign ratings (September 2024)**

| Country | Previous | Current | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uganda | B2 (Negative) | B3 (Stable, Moody's) | Downgraded, fiscal deterioration; sub-investment grade |
| Tanzania | B2 (Positive) | B1 (Stable) | Upgraded |
| Kenya | not stated | not stated | Outlook revised to positive |

Planning rule of thumb: add a country risk premium of about 4–6% in WACC for Uganda (consistent with the Damodaran approach; treat as an assumption to verify).

**Inflation and debt stress**
- Africa inflation 19.3% in 2024; Eastern Africa 21.2% to 20.9% (still elevated); non-resource-intensive economies 7.8% (from 12.5% in 2022); Southern Africa 38.9% to 20.1% (Zimbabwe effect). Root causes: currency depreciation against the dollar and agricultural disruption.
- Eastern Africa debt 52.2% of GDP in 2024 (about 60% in 2023), excluding Sudan (354% of GDP).
- Fiscal space is constrained across Africa: plans that rely on government contracts should assume payment delays.

## 5. Implications by plan section

**Market size framing**
- Position Uganda and East Africa in the fastest-growing sub-region (Eastern Africa 4.4%).
- Uganda's 2025 forecast of 6.8% is well above the continental 3.4%.
- Uganda inflation is easing (4.0% in 2024; about 4.9% forecast for 2025), a relatively stable cost environment.

**Trade-exposed businesses**
- AfCFTA is lowering barriers for EAC exporters; East African intra-regional exports of USD 23.3 billion favour services and manufactured-goods exporters.
- Coffee and gold price gains in 2024 improve foreign-exchange availability.

**Risk section**
- Incorporate the B3 rating and a country risk premium.
- Uganda's deficit is improving (forecast -1.0% by 2027) but is -4.9% now.
- High regional inflation (20.9% in Eastern Africa) means input-cost uncertainty despite Uganda's lower domestic rate.
