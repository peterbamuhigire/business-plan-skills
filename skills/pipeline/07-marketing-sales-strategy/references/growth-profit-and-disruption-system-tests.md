# Growth, Profit, and Disruption System Tests

This reference consolidates methods paraphrased from five sources: *Growth Engineering*; *Remarkable Business Growth*; *Make Phenomenal Profits*; *Dare to Disrupt*; and *Think Deeper* (author and publisher details were not recorded when the sources were first ingested; confirm them before citing in a client document). It treats growth as a managed system, not a list of tactics.

## 1. The growth-system model

A credible growth claim connects seven parts:

1. A sharply defined customer and problem.
2. A differentiated offer and position.
3. A measured loop of acquisition, activation, retention, referral, and revenue.
4. An experimentation rhythm.
5. A data model and dashboard showing what works.
6. Delivery that keeps the promise after the sale.
7. Leadership willing to challenge current practice.

## 2. Product and data growth (digital products)

For SaaS, apps, platforms, marketplaces, and AI-enabled products:

- Define the growth loop: trigger, user action, moment of value, retention hook, referral or expansion path.
- Define the data model: users, accounts, events, campaigns, subscriptions, conversions, cohorts, revenue facts.
- Govern experiments: hypothesis, metric, sample, duration, decision rule, follow-up action.
- Separate vanity measures from decision measures; traffic is not growth unless it leads to activation, retention, or revenue.
- Include AI only where it improves acquisition, onboarding, personalisation, insight, support, or speed.

Measures: activation rate and time to first value; cohort retention by day, week, and month; conversion by source; expansion revenue and net revenue retention; experiment win rate and cycle time; product-qualified leads; feature adoption and habit frequency.

## 3. Systemise before optimising

- Show target market, position, offer, sales plan, marketing plan, delivery, support, and measures as one operating system.
- Apply the specialist test: if the business can serve everyone, the position is not sharp enough.
- Make marketing a lead-generation system: useful content, a conversion-focused website, email nurture, social proof, sales follow-up.
- Build dashboards for revenue, website, marketing, sales, service, and customer success.
- Treat service and support as part of growth, because retention protects acquisition cost and produces referrals.

## 4. Profit-lever arithmetic

Profit growth usually comes from several small improvements that multiply. Model a modest improvement (the source uses 15 per cent as an illustration) in each lever and show the compound effect:

| Lever | Example test |
|---|---|
| Leads | New source, better hand-raiser |
| Conversion | Proof, offer, follow-up |
| Price | Tier, bundle, premium option |
| Purchase frequency | Replenishment, subscription |
| Retention | Onboarding, service recovery |
| Cost discipline | Waste, supplier terms |

Also test offer mix (product, price, packaging, pitch, proof, service experience) and pricing options such as monthly against annual, bundle against single item, and premium against entry tier. State the sales goal with gross margin, contribution margin, break-even volume, and cash conversion. Treat retention as a profit centre.

## 5. Disruption test

- Separate evolution (longer hours, more advertising, more staff) from transformation (a changed business model, customer experience, delivery system, or basis of competition).
- Check the transformation pillars: leadership, culture, people, systems, customer experience, technology, execution rhythm.
- Name where the business changes the rules: access model, pricing model, delivery model, customer journey, service speed, ownership structure.
- Measure: new-offer adoption, new-segment revenue, retention, employee engagement, productivity, testing rhythm, speed to launch.
- Describe the execution system: milestones, owners, feedback loops, decision rights.

## 6. Structured questioning before accepting a strategy

Ask: what assumption is hidden; what would an outsider do; what would make this obsolete; what would we do with half the budget; what would we do if output had to double? Then view the plan in turn as a customer, competitor, regulator, employee, supplier, investor, and future entrant would. Create a path from idea to experiment, experiment to decision, and decision to operating change. Do not present creativity as a culture claim; tie it to decision rhythm, product tests, process redesign, and customer feedback.

## 7. Section application

| Section | Apply |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | Name the growth engine, the profit levers, and why the timing is right. |
| Market analysis | Identify growth pressure, category disruption, and unmet customer jobs. |
| Target market | Narrow to the first segment most likely to activate, stay, and refer. |
| Competitive analysis | Show how the business changes the basis of competition. |
| Products and services | Packaging, habit loops, data capture, AI-enabled value, premium tiers. |
| Marketing and sales | Acquisition, conversion, retention, referral, and experiment system. |
| Operations | Delivery that protects the customer experience as demand grows. |
| Financial projections | Revenue linked to funnel, retention, price, frequency, and margin assumptions. |
| Risk | Weak activation, churn, channel dependence, poor data, failed experiments, execution fatigue. |

## 8. Growth-system checklist

- [ ] Clear customer and problem.
- [ ] Specialist position.
- [ ] Offer states a visible customer or profit outcome.
- [ ] Acquisition, activation, retention, referral, and revenue loops specified.
- [ ] Growth measures tied to the financial projections.
- [ ] Experiments have hypotheses and decision rules.
- [ ] Customer success and support feed retention and referrals.
- [ ] Pricing and packaging tested, or a test plan exists.
- [ ] The plan states which changes are evolution and which are transformation.
- [ ] Every major growth lever has a dashboard and an owner.
