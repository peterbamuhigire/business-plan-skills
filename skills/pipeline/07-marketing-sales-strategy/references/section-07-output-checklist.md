# Section 07 Output Checklist

Use this checklist to draft or review the marketing and sales strategy section without loading every specialist reference.

## Required Elements

1. **Marketing objectives**: specific, measurable goals in SMART format.
2. **Marketing evolution stage**: where the business sits on the Kotler 1.0-6.0 spectrum and where it should move over three years.
3. **Positioning statement**: how the brand should be perceived relative to competitors.
4. **Marketing mix**: 4Ps for products or 7Ps for services.
5. **Sales strategy and process**: how leads become customers, step by step.
6. **Customer acquisition funnel**: AARRR or equivalent stage metrics.
7. **Digital channel strategy**: website, social, content, SEO, paid, email, and analytics where relevant.
8. **Guerrilla or low-budget tactics**: only where budget constraints make them appropriate.
9. **Marketing budget**: allocation by channel with expected CAC, customer volume, and ROI logic.
10. **Retention strategy**: loyalty, support, upsell, cross-sell, referral, and review cadence.
11. **Metrics dashboard**: acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue, and sales pipeline measures.
12. **Attraction-conversion-retention system**: how prospects become leads, customers, repeat buyers, and referrers.
13. **Lead magnet or hand-raiser**: the low-friction offer that earns permission to follow up.
14. **Narrow market focus**: the sharpest viable beachhead segment the business can credibly win first.
15. **Growth system**: loops, tests, owners, and learning cadence rather than isolated campaigns.
16. **Premium or high-ticket logic**: buyer profile, value proof, consultative discovery, proposal, objections, follow-up, and VIP retention where relevant.
17. **Website and SEO asset logic**: target pages, search intent, conversion paths, analytics events, launch distribution, maintenance, and cost assumptions where relevant.

## Generation Process

1. Confirm business model, target segment, price point, primary channels, sales model, budget range, and country context.
2. Position the business on the marketing evolution spectrum.
3. Define positioning statement, USP, strapline, and StoryBrand one-liner.
4. Build the 4Ps or 7Ps.
5. Map the AARRR funnel with tactics, owner, metric, target, budget, and risk at each stage.
6. Select channels using buyer behaviour, budget, speed-to-result, and measurement quality.
7. Include guerrilla, alliance, PR, referral, and owned-audience tactics for budget-constrained businesses.
8. Allocate marketing budget by channel with expected CAC, customers, payback, and ROI.
9. Define retention, upsell, review, complaint-handling, and referral mechanisms.
10. Quantify the gap between customer current state and desired future state.
11. Choose the right sales process: B2C funnel, B2B discovery, founder-led startup selling, service-business selling, high-touch persuasion, or self-serve digital conversion.
12. For B2B, specify prospecting cadence, first-meeting structure, pipeline ratios, relationship progression, and average time in stage.
13. For startups, identify founder-led selling stage and the next sales hiring gate.
14. Set measurable KPIs for each channel and funnel stage, including sales velocity when sales-led.
15. Define the monthly test-and-measure review process.

## Quality Criteria

- Strategy is specific to this business, not generic marketing advice.
- Channel choices match buyer behaviour, budget, geography, and sales capacity.
- USP is measurable, differentiated, and connected to customer pain or aspiration.
- Budget is realistic and traceable to revenue assumptions.
- Sales process has named stages, conversion targets, owners, and pipeline ratios.
- AARRR or equivalent funnel has specific tactics and metrics at each stage.
- Online and offline channels are both considered where relevant.
- All three growth levers are addressed: new customers, spend per customer, and purchase frequency.
- Retention includes communication cadence, review process, complaint handling, and upsell/referral logic.
- Gap analysis quantifies current state, future state, and purchase urgency.
- Startup sales plans show stage-appropriate hiring, not premature VP Sales assumptions.
- Growth is presented as loops, tests, metrics, and owners, not a pile of campaign ideas.
- Product-led, AI-enabled, or platform growth claims identify behaviour, data signal, experiment, and proof metric.
- StoryBrand one-liner is present and can be repeated in 30 seconds.
- Brand is positioned as the guide and the customer as the hero.
- Promotional copy uses benefit, feature, and proof, not feature-first claims.
- Direct-response tactics identify list, offer, message, reply path, and follow-up cadence.
