# Institutional Health Trust Communication

This reference is a self-contained synthesis prepared from Sara Rubinelli,
*Institutional Health Communication in the Information Age*. It is for planning
communication work; health claims, clinical advice, and current public guidance
require qualified review and authoritative sources.

## Strategy frame

Define the public-health or institutional objective, audience, evidence, action,
uncertainty, equity risk, channel, feedback route, and review owner. Treat the
public as a participant with knowledge and preferences, not only a recipient.

## Message quality

- Translate technical evidence without deleting material limits.
- Explain mixed evidence as an expected feature of inquiry, not as a reason to invent certainty.
- State what is known, unknown, changing, and safe to do now.
- Use accessible language, local context, and a clear route to professional help.
- Listen through structured feedback and change the message when evidence or audience needs change.

Measure comprehension, trust signals, appropriate action, reach across affected
groups, correction time, and harmful misunderstanding. Do not optimise reach at
the expense of evidence or dignity.
