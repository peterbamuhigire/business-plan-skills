# Sales Process and Messaging Frameworks

Use this reference when Section 07 needs a practical sales path, pipeline metrics, founder-led sales staging, or messaging checks.

## Gap Selling Principle

Every sale exists in the gap between the customer's current state and desired future state. The larger and more expensive the gap, the more urgency to buy and the less price-sensitive the buyer becomes.

For each customer segment, define:

- Current state: facts, problems, business impact, root cause, and emotional impact.
- Future state: desired outcomes, new capabilities, and success metrics.
- Gap size: quantified difference, monetary where possible.
- Gap validation: whether the gap is large enough to drive action. As a rough check, price should usually be below 20-30% of quantified gap value.

The real competitor is often the status quo, not another vendor.

## B2C Funnel

Use a simple funnel where buying is low-touch:

```text
Awareness -> Interest -> Consideration -> Purchase -> Loyalty -> Advocacy
```

For each stage, specify channel, message, offer, metric, owner, conversion target, and follow-up mechanism.

## B2B Four-Step Sales Process

Use for sales-led B2B, professional services, high-value equipment, enterprise software, or institutional buyers.

1. **Opening**: prospecting, cold calls, introductions, and first contact. Objective: secure agreement to a meaningful interview.
2. **Interviewing**: discovery and information gathering. Objective: understand the buyer's work, problems, decision path, budget, timing, and status quo.
3. **Presentation**: tailored proposal. Objective: present a plan based on verified information. Add a verification step before the final proposal.
4. **Closing**: decision and next commitment. Objective: make the next action explicit and diagnose objections without defensiveness.

Track responsible person, tools, conversion target, and average time in stage.

## First Meeting Structure

Use PIPA for initial prospect meetings:

1. **Present**: brief 2-3 sentence introduction.
2. **Interview**: move quickly into do-based questions about current and future work.
3. **Present next step**: propose a specific next action with date and time.
4. **Agreement**: confirm the prospect accepts the next step.

Key rule: listen more than talk. Avoid product dumping before discovery.

## Pipeline Ratios

Track and improve these ratios continuously:

| Ratio | What to Track |
|---|---|
| Dials to discussions | Calls required to reach one live person |
| Discussions to appointments | Conversations required to book one meeting |
| First to second appointments | First meetings that advance |
| Visits to sales | Total meetings per closed deal |
| Average deal value | Revenue per closed sale |

Prospecting cannot stop after a sale. A plan must show enough top-of-funnel activity to support the revenue forecast.

## Prospect Probability Tiers

| Prospect Type | Close Probability | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Existing customer | About 50% | Highest for upsell and cross-sell |
| Past customer | About 25% | High; maintain contact |
| New cold prospect | About 5% | Volume-dependent |
| Fallback prospect | 5-10% on revisit | Revisit periodically |

## Relationship Progression

Move important accounts toward partner status:

| Level | Role | Risk | How to Advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seller | Price-only peddler | Extreme | Build repeat business |
| Supplier | Regular vendor | High | Learn the account deeply |
| Vendor | Trusted provider | Moderate | Deliver consistently |
| Partner | Strategic ally | Low | Join planning and provide strategic input |

## Service-Business Sales Tactics

For service businesses and small B2B:

1. Send a warm introduction before the call.
2. Research and qualify the prospect.
3. Offer a useful consultation, assessment, or diagnostic.
4. Build rapport without forcing false intimacy.
5. Discover pain, cost, urgency, and decision process.
6. Tailor the proposition using the customer's language.
7. Keep proposals short: situation, objective, solution, value, price, plan.
8. Follow up with discipline until there is a clear conclusion.

## Persuasion-Based Sales

For high-touch sales, organise the process into involvement, qualification, and urgency:

1. Prepare around client, competitors, and likely objections.
2. Create trust and rapport.
3. Qualify wants, pain, needs, income or budget, and decision process.
4. Build belief using proof, reasons to act, and test closes.
5. Make the outcome tangible.
6. Transform objections into commitments.
7. Prevent buyer's remorse and ask for referrals.

Use `persuasion-sales-process.md` for detailed scripts and objection handling.

## Founder-Led Selling Stages

For startups, sales hiring should follow evidence.

| Stage | Team | Exit Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Founder only | 20-30 customers acquired and getting value |
| 2 | Founder, SDR, and 2 AEs | AEs close at the founder's rate |
| 3 | Sales manager and team | Repeatable hiring and quota attainment |
| 4 | VP Sales and scaled team | Predictable revenue engine |

Anti-pattern: hiring a VP Sales before the founder has found repeatable sales motion.

## Sales Velocity

```text
Sales Velocity = (Opportunities x Win Rate x Average Deal Size) / Average Sales Cycle in Days
```

Use sales velocity to test whether the pipeline can support the revenue forecast.

## Core Dashboard Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|---|---|---|
| CAC | Total marketing spend / new customers | Below one-third of CLV |
| CLV:CAC | Customer lifetime value / CAC | Above 3:1 |
| Marketing ROI | (Revenue from marketing - cost) / cost | Positive and improving |
| Conversion rate | Customers / leads | Benchmarked by channel |
| Churn rate | Lost customers / total customers | Low enough for the model |
| NPS | Promoters minus detractors | Positive and improving |
| CAC payback | CAC / monthly margin per customer | Usually below 12 months |

## StoryBrand Messaging Checks

Use a clear one-liner:

```text
We help [character] who [problem] [achieve result or transformation].
```

The BrandScript must define character, problem, guide, plan, direct CTA, transitional CTA, failure stakes, success, and identity transformation. The brand should be the guide, not the hero.

Website non-negotiables:

1. Offer above the fold.
2. Obvious direct CTA.
3. Images or proof of successful customers.
4. Bite-sized product or service breakdown.
5. Minimal words.

Email nurture should mix problem education, plan, life after resolution, offer, transformation, and direct CTA.
