# Selling system for the sales section

This is the engine's method for writing the "how we sell" part of Section 07, a sales script or a sales-training plan. It follows the buyer's path from first noticing the business to recommending it. It draws on the personal-selling literature, in particular Kennedy, D. (2004) *No B.S. Sales Success*, 3rd edn, Entrepreneur Press, which argues for positioning over prospecting, information-led lead generation, a disciplined sales conversation and deliberate post-sale reassurance. The structure, tests and examples are this engine's; the book's anecdotes and response figures are not used. Every tactic is bound by the ethics rules in section 8.

## 1. Be found before you chase

Decide which authority assets the business will build, give each an owner and budget, and set a 90-day output:

| Asset | Typical output in East Africa | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Written expertise | Guides, articles in trade or association newsletters, LinkedIn articles | Enquiries citing the piece |
| Speaking | Association meetings, bank SME forums, radio business shows, webinars | Contacts collected per talk |
| Media coverage | Business pages, talk radio, niche outlets | Referral visits and enquiries |
| Narrow specialism | Being known for one problem in one sector | Share of enquiries arriving by referral |

Test: would a prospect call the business "a supplier" or "the people who wrote the guide on this problem"?

## 2. Invite interest with useful information

1. Advertise an offer of useful information (checklist, guide, short course, calculator), not the product.
2. The prospect requests it and gives contact details plus a few qualifying fields (role, organisation, size).
3. A follow-up sequence delivers the information, shows competence and sorts prospects.
4. The prospect asks for the sales conversation, or is invited once they meet the criteria.

Test several headlines, because the headline decides most of the response. Candidate channels: association and trade media, search and social ads to a landing page, messaging-app opt-ins, direct mail to past buyers. Check current platform and data-protection rules before specifying targeting or messaging (see the marketing orchestrator's location and scope calibration).

## 3. Qualify before you present

- Write who the offer is for and who it is not for ("firms with at least ten staff on payroll"; "not suitable for one-off events").
- Use capacity limits only when they are true ("we onboard six new clients a quarter").
- Decline poor fits and return any deposit; selectivity is part of the positioning.
- After a yes, ask the buyer to confirm they meet the criteria.

## 4. The sales conversation (script skeleton)

| Stage | Purpose | What goes in the script |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement to proceed | The buyer says they want to discuss a solution now | An opening question; if the answer is no, arrange a later contact and leave |
| Discovery | Understand the problem, its cost and who decides | Question list, note template, decision-maker map |
| Complete offer | State price, terms, timing and deliverables plainly | One-page offer sheet |
| Fit to the problem | Show how the offer solves the discovered problem, with process and proof | Proof pack, less obvious benefits |
| Reasons | First the motive (relief, safety, pride, avoided loss), then the justification (return, guarantee, comparison) | Value arithmetic in the buyer's money |
| Decision | Ask for a decision with a concrete question | "Shall we start on the 1st or the 15th?"; "Option A or B?"; "Does this make sense for you?" |
| After the yes | Within 24 hours reassure, confirm, set next steps; later ask for a referral or testimonial | Welcome message template, onboarding checklist |

The last stage reduces cancellations, reversed mobile-money payments and lost referrals.

## 5. Proof

Assemble a proof pack and use the strongest specific items first: named outcomes with time frames; before-and-after records; figures from the business's own records; third-party recognition; a demonstration; a guarantee; client names used with permission. Stop when proof turns into generic clutter. Never invent or embellish a testimonial or figure.

## 6. Price conversations

- State the price without apology.
- Express the fee as an investment that returns more than it costs, with the arithmetic.
- Add low-cost value (template, audit, extra session, community access) instead of cutting price.
- Change the comparison: bundle, guarantee, timeline or outcome measure.
- Use a valuable bonus to close hard sales or support retention; ask suppliers to fund it.
- Walk away when fit is poor.

Detailed rules: `../../../meta-pricing-gtm/meta-pricing-strategy/references/price-strategy-audit-and-proposition-stack.md`.

## 7. Review questions for the sales section

1. When a prospect says no, does the plan diagnose why (timing, form, fit, information, wrong person) and schedule a revisit?
2. Are likely objections, competitor counters and delivery failures listed with responses?
3. Does the script make the seller listen (restate, pause, "tell me more")?
4. Is personal presentation (dress, vehicle, card, signature, profile photo, office) treated as sales material?
5. Does every meeting aim at a decision, not only a pleasant conversation?
6. Is the language the buyer's own, describing outcomes rather than process, with clear yes-or-no questions?
7. Is there a named method the business can explain openly while keeping its craft?
8. Does follow-up change approach when repetition is not working?
9. As the deal matures, does contact move closer to in-person (text → email → video → phone → meeting)?
10. Is a CRM used so no follow-up is missed?

What damages sales: call-volume targets over quality; forcing a decision on every visit; rigid scripts; discounting to close; feature lists before discovery; talking more than listening; chasing poor fits from need; stopping follow-up too early. Treat the website as a structured sales document, an email subject line as the envelope and its first lines as the promise.

## 8. Ethics rules

- Deadlines, scarcity and capacity limits must be true.
- Never imitate official, legal or government notices to force attention.
- Automated calls and broadcast voice or text messages are regulated; check current consumer-protection, telecoms and data-protection rules first.
- Emotional appeals must match a real benefit; do not manufacture fear.

## 9. East African tests (to run, not assume)

Whether talks at business associations, service clubs and radio shows create enquiries; whether a clear "who this is not for" stance works where buyers expect to bargain; whether a named method improves response; whether a landing-page-to-WhatsApp opt-in lowers cost per customer compared with direct-sale ads.

**Illustrative example.** A Kampala payroll bureau publishes a free year-end payroll checklist through two HR associations, collects 140 requests, qualifies 35 firms with more than 20 staff, holds 18 conversations and signs 6, each receiving a welcome call within 24 hours and a referral request after the first payroll run.

## Evidence to record in the plan

Authority assets and budget; information offer and channels; qualification criteria; script owner; proof pack; price-conversation rules; CRM and follow-up cadence; ethics check.
