When to read: when the offer sounds broad, undifferentiated, or aimed at "everyone", or when an early-stage plan has not chosen its first customer group.

# Niche-First Positioning Test

This test sharpens audience choice and differentiation before any conversion work. It paraphrases ideas from Godin, S. (2012) *Startup School* (audio programme; transcript compilation). A weak, undifferentiated offer is hard to convert no matter how good the sales process is; run this test before `direct-response-commercial-system.md`.

## 1. Choose the narrowest viable first group

1. Name the first customer group in one sentence: who they are, where they are, and the problem they feel most.
2. Reject descriptions such as "all households", "all SMEs", or "the general public" for an early-stage business.
3. Check that the group is large enough to sustain the first 12 to 24 months of revenue in the model, and small enough to reach with the stated budget.
4. State why this group will care enough to switch, join, or recommend.

## 2. Test for real difference

Ask whether the offer is:

- visibly different from what the group already uses;
- easy to describe in one sentence;
- easy for a satisfied customer to recommend;
- valuable to a defined community.

Treat "better quality", "good service", and "affordable" as insufficient on their own. Replace them with a specific, testable difference.

## 3. Add community and identity logic where it matters

Where the purchase carries identity or belonging (education, creative work, wellness, fashion, lifestyle, mission-led or community-led ventures), state:

- which group the business serves;
- what status, identity, or belonging signal the offer reinforces;
- how word of mouth moves inside that group.

In East Africa, test community channels such as diaspora groups, professional associations, faith networks, school communities, savings groups, and messaging-app communities. These are hypotheses to test, not assumed channels.

## 4. Apply the founder-reality filter

- Do not avoid the audience decision to keep options open.
- Do not wait for permission before a small launch.
- Do not count activity (posts, meetings, sign-ups) as traction; count paying and returning customers.
- Do not build complexity before demand is shown.

## 5. Why niche-first helps constrained businesses

A narrow group reduces wasted acquisition spend and fits fragmented markets where trust and relevance matter more than broad awareness. Record this reasoning in the plan only when the budget and channel assumptions support it.

## Plan evidence to record

First-group definition; size and reach check; one-sentence difference; switching reason; community logic where relevant; traction measure used.
