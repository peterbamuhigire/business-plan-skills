When to read: when drafting or reviewing a long-form sales letter, sales page, video sales script, long email, WhatsApp broadcast series or any multi-message follow-up sequence that a plan relies on.

# Long-form persuasive copy: build and review method

This is the engine's working method for long persuasive copy. It is organised as five work packages (brief, argument, attention, draft and test, sequence) with a review skeleton at the end. The long-copy tradition it draws on is summarised in Kennedy, D. S. (2000) *The Ultimate Sales Letter*, 2nd edn, Adams Media, which sets out a step-by-step drafting system; the packages, questions, tests and examples here are this engine's own. Apply `anti-ai-slop` and the ethics rules in package 6 to every draft.

## Package 1 — Brief: know the reader and the offer

**Reader worksheet** (write short answers before drafting):

| Area | Question |
|---|---|
| Daily reality | What occupies them at the start and end of the day? What worries and frustrates them? |
| Hopes | What do they want, including what they rarely say aloud? |
| Change | Which trends are altering their work or household? |
| Language | Which words and jargon do they use at work, at home, in their groups? |
| Decision style | Analytical, status-conscious, cautious, price-led? |
| Competition | Who else sells to them, how, and who has tried and failed with this offer before? |
| Moment | Where and when will they open this message? Write to that moment. |

If you are outside the audience, read its trade press, attend its meetings and listen to how members talk. For fundraising or sponsorship letters, answer the giver's questions: what they gain, who else has committed, and which budget line the money comes from.

**Offer worksheet:** use the product; list each feature and its benefit to this reader; rank them; then find the **hidden benefit**, the gain the reader values but the specification never mentions (time back with family, standing among peers, avoiding embarrassment, a better start for children). It usually surfaces in informal conversation, not in documents.

## Package 2 — Argument: decide what the letter must prove

1. **Central promise** in one sentence, in the reader's words.
2. **Concede the obvious weakness early** ("We are not the cheapest, and here is why"; "This is only for…"). An honest concession raises trust in everything else.
3. **Objection list:** every reason the reader might not buy, each answered with a specific story or proof, not a general rebuttal.
4. **Price reframing:** compare with the cost of the problem rather than with cheaper substitutes; express cost per day or per use; show the value of extras against the price; offer instalments where they are affordable (mobile money suits this); trade any discount for an early decision or a referral; if the price is higher, say so and explain.
5. **Reasons to act now:** a real deadline, a real quantity limit, an early-response bonus, the stated cost of waiting, a strong guarantee, several easy ways to respond.

Structures to choose from, not stack: attention → interest → desire → (conviction) → action; problem → consequence → solution; before → after; "feature, so that benefit, so that deeper benefit".

## Package 3 — Attention: get it delivered, opened and read

- **Delivered:** physical mail (hand addressing, courier, dimensional items) or digital deliverability (sender reputation, consent-based list).
- **Opened:** an envelope line or subject line that promises a benefit or raises a real question.
- **Read:** a specific headline (numbers, question, promised gain or relief); an opening story, surprising fact or question, never "I am writing to…"; subheads and white space.
- **Skim test:** someone who reads only the headline, subheads and postscript should know the offer and how to respond.

## Package 4 — Draft and test

1. Write a fast, complete first draft.
2. Rewrite for argument: right points, right order, every objection answered.
3. Rewrite for style: "you" clearly outnumbers "we"; specific, verified numbers; transitions used sparingly.
4. Add emphasis, personality and a real story; recheck proof and consent for every testimonial.
5. Read aloud; have one or two members of the target audience read it; note where they stop.
6. Mock up the real format and send it to yourself; leave it for a day or two; edit again.
7. Final check against the review skeleton (package 7), then production and launch decisions.

The postscript is heavily read; give each postscript one job (restate the offer, add a bonus, repeat the deadline, a testimonial, the main objection answered).

## Package 5 — Sequence rather than a single message

A short sequence usually outperforms a single send. A three-step pattern: (1) full offer with deadline; (2) a check-in that answers the main objection and restates the offer; (3) a final notice with the strongest genuine incentive and a clear instruction. Test three to five messages over one to two weeks in relationship-led markets.

| Format | Envelope equivalent | Opening promise | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email | Subject line | Preview text and first two sentences | Letter structure |
| Sales page | Page title and first screen | Headline and sub-headline | Letter paced for scrolling |
| Video script | Thumbnail and first seconds | Hook | Story, problem, solution, offer, value, deadline, action |
| WhatsApp broadcast (opted-in list) | First text | First voice note | Story note, offer message, deadline and payment details |

## Package 6 — Ethics rules

Deadlines, scarcity and testimonials must be true and verifiable. Never imply official status. Never invent results. Keep claims within what the business can prove, and check the current advertising rules of any regulator covering health, finance or education claims. Messaging and email follow data-protection consent and opt-out rules.

## Package 7 — Review skeleton

Headline · opening promise · story or problem · early concession · central promise · proof · offer and value · price reframing · guarantee · genuine deadline or limit · a response route per channel · real name, signature and photograph · one to three postscripts.

**Illustrative example.** A Jinja solar installer's three-part WhatsApp sequence to past enquirers: (1) story of a nearby school that stopped paying for generator fuel, offer of a site survey with a month-end deadline; (2) answer to "what if it breaks?" with the service guarantee; (3) final reminder that survey slots close on the 30th, with the mobile-money deposit instructions.

## Evidence to record in the plan

Reader and offer worksheets; hidden benefit; objection list with answers; sequence plan by channel; reader-test notes; ethics check.
