# Online sales path and offer ladder

This is the engine's method for designing and costing an online or messaging-led sales path in a business plan. It is organised around six design decisions and a testing rule. It draws on the online-funnel literature, including Brunson, R. (n.d.) *Proven Secrets to Double Your Traffic, Conversion & Sales for Any Product or Service Online*, SuccessEtc LLC (the "value ladder" and traffic-temperature ideas), and Eugene Schwartz's awareness levels as cited there. The decisions, arithmetic, tables and examples are this engine's; conversion rates and tools from the source are not used. Every rate below is an assumption to test.

## Decision 1 — Whom, and where they already gather

Write one narrow description of the first customer (segment, size, situation) and list the places they already gather: WhatsApp and Facebook groups, associations, SACCOs, churches, trade fairs, newsletters, radio call-in shows. The first offer goes where they already are.

## Decision 2 — The offer ladder

Arrange offers by price and commitment, each serving the customer better than the last:

| Rung | Role | Example (Kampala bookkeeping firm, illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Free first step | Earns contact details | Year-end tax-filing checklist |
| Low-price entry | Turns a contact into a customer | UGX 50,000 records health-check |
| Core offer | Main revenue | Monthly bookkeeping retainer |
| Premium | Highest value and margin | Bookkeeping plus management accounts and quarterly review |
| Recurring | Predictable income | The retainers themselves, billed monthly by mobile money |

The same knowledge can be sold at several prices by changing format and support level. Ask: what recurring value can the business give each month?

## Decision 3 — Work revenue back to traffic

1. Monthly revenue required.
2. Revenue model: recurring (price × members), one-off core sales (price × sales), or a recurring base plus one-off sales.
3. Entry-level customers needed = core customers required ÷ assumed upgrade rate.
4. Visitors or contacts needed = entry customers ÷ assumed entry conversion rate.
5. Compare with what the budget and channels can plausibly deliver.

Worked example (illustrative): target UGX 12m a month from retainers at UGX 400,000 → 30 retainers. If 20% of health-check buyers upgrade, 150 health-checks are needed over the build-up period. If 5% of engaged contacts buy the health-check, about 3,000 engaged contacts are needed. If that exceeds what the channels can reach, change the model (price, upgrade path, niche), not the effort. Feed these assumptions into Section 10 with their sources.

## Decision 4 — Own the audience

| Attention | Examples | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Paid | Search and social ads, sponsorships, affiliates | Rented: stops when spending stops |
| Earned | Search ranking, social feeds, press | Borrowed: platform or editor controls it |
| Owned | Email, SMS and WhatsApp lists (opted in), customer base | Owned |

Every sign-up step exists to move people into owned audience. Messaging lists are often the main owned channel in East Africa; follow platform business-messaging rules and data-protection consent and objection rules. Growth levers to test: partnerships with owners of relevant lists or groups, affiliate arrangements, community administrators. Platform targeting options change often; verify before specifying.

## Decision 5 — The customer's path

Group the path into five movements and give each a metric:

| Movement | What happens | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Arrive | Visitor comes from a source of known warmth (own list = warm, referral = lukewarm, stranger = cold) | Visitors by source |
| Prepare | A bridging message or page sets context; cold audiences need an extra teaching step before any offer | Click-through to offer |
| Join | Visitor becomes a subscriber | Sign-up rate |
| First purchase | Subscriber buys anything, even the entry product | Subscriber-to-buyer rate |
| Grow | Buyer moves up the ladder and the relationship moves closer (message → call → event → meeting) | Upgrade rate, repeat rate |

Match the message to awareness: a reader unaware of the problem needs story and problem first; a reader who knows the product needs the offer, value and guarantee.

## Decision 6 — The path format

Choose the simplest format that fits price and decision complexity:

| Price and complexity | Format |
|---|---|
| Low price, simple decision | Sign-up page → short sequence → direct offer |
| Physical product | Product page → order form with a relevant add-on → thank-you page with a repeat offer |
| Mid price, some education needed | Teaching session (live or recorded) → offer |
| New product or launch | Short run of teaching content released over days → offer window |
| Membership | Trial or first month → onboarding → monthly value |
| High price, consultative | Application form → qualifying call → proposal call |

Building blocks: bridging page, sign-up page, sales page or video, order form, add-on or upgrade offer after purchase, thank-you page, member area, application form, registration and replay pages, email or WhatsApp sequences, call scripts.

## Persuasive structures used inside the path

- **Long sales page or video:** open with the core desire and why earlier attempts failed → a true story of the presenter or a client facing the problem → proof from others → the offer (what it is, what it does, what is included, honest value, price, guarantee, any genuine limit) → a picture of life after purchase, a clear instruction and a note on who it is not for.
- **Teaching session:** one promise ("how to achieve X without Y"); three points that each correct a limiting belief; then the offer and answers to the usual objections (time, money, confidence, habit).
- **Offer straight after a purchase:** confirm the good decision; offer something that gets the result faster or bigger; say who it suits; guarantee; a clear yes-or-no.
- **High-price application:** first call qualifies (goals, context, capacity to invest, time, decision-maker present); second call confirms and completes the sale.
- **Welcome sequence (five messages):** expectations and introduction → the presenter's relevant story → the insight that links to the offer → less obvious benefits → a genuine deadline and invitation.
- **Ongoing messages:** alternate a short story, a lesson and a practical how-to, each tied to the offer.

## Testing rule

Before buying traffic at scale, push a small fixed batch (for example 100 visitors per source) through the path and record each step's rate. Fix the weakest step first. Study what already works in the market (public ad libraries, competitor pages), build a control, then try to beat it.

## Ethics rules

- Any "total value" figure must be defensible from real prices, not an invented anchor.
- "Not your fault" or common-enemy framing must not misrepresent third parties.
- Scarcity, deadlines, testimonials and stories must be true; the presenter's public character is honest self-presentation, not fiction.
- No income or health claims without evidence and regulator checks.

## East African tests

Whether the ladder suits subscription-style sectors (tuition, fitness, training, savings groups, professional services); whether a five-message welcome sequence works as a WhatsApp broadcast; whether small-batch testing reduces wasted spend where clicks are costly relative to prices.

## Evidence to record in the plan

Customer description and gathering places; offer ladder with prices; revenue-to-traffic arithmetic with a source for each rate; owned-audience target; chosen path format; step-rate assumptions; test plan; ethics check.
