# Growth Strategy Playbook

Use this reference to turn a growth ambition into a sequenced, evidence-tested option portfolio for the marketing, sales and expansion sections of a plan. It applies the widely used "seven degrees of growth" option map (McKinsey-style portfolio thinking) with the engine's own procedures, templates and East African adaptation. Any figures are illustrative planning assumptions; re-verify with dated sources.

## 1. Set the mandate and the targets

### Choose the primary mandate
| Mandate | Typical horizon | Primary metric |
|---|---|---|
| Protect and optimise | 1-2 years | Margin, cash, share held |
| Accelerate the core | 2-3 years | Revenue growth in existing markets |
| Expand adjacencies | 2-4 years | Share in new segments, channels, geographies |
| Transform | 3-5+ years | New business model or market position |

Combine mandates only with explicit sequencing; otherwise priorities blur. Split the plan into Now (0-12 months: core fixes and quick wins), Next (12-36 months: adjacent moves needing new capability) and Later (3+ years: transformational options). Risk tolerance, governance and investment staging differ by horizon.

### Quantify the ambition
A target is measurable, time-bound and decomposable ("grow net new recurring revenue by a stated amount in two years while lifting gross margin by a stated number of points"; "grow faster" is not a target).
1. Set a top-down target to create urgency.
2. Test bottom-up feasibility to surface constraints and capability gaps.
3. Reconcile: close any remaining gap with bigger moves (new channels, acquisitions) or by changing constraints (funding, talent, operating model).
4. Decompose in two layers: by unit, segment, region or channel (allocation); then by economic driver (volume, price, mix, retention, expansion, cost-to-serve).
5. Use ranges when uncertainty is high; commit to a floor and state what must be true for the upside.

### Write the guardrails
Three to seven measurable non-negotiables the strategy will not breach: financial health (cash, margin floors, capex and leverage limits); customer promise (service levels, quality, data trust); risk and compliance; capacity reality (operations, supply, hiring pace); identity and values. Make each measurable (for example "no segment requiring discounts below a stated margin floor" rather than "protect the brand").

### Build the scorecard
North Star metric plus two to four supporting outcomes (margin, cash, share, impact); three to five leading drivers tied to the biggest levers (pipeline, conversion, adoption, retention, price realisation, cost-to-serve); two or three enabler milestones. Cap the scorecard at 8-12 metrics, each with an owner, a review rhythm and the decision that changes if it moves. Guardrail metrics carry intervention thresholds, not just targets.

## 2. Diagnose the growth engine

Most growth plans fail by investing in the wrong lever, underestimating execution difficulty or misreading what customers value. Diagnose before designing.

### Baseline (momentum versus management)
1. Use 24-36 months of actuals; label anomalies.
2. Build a driver waterfall for the business model: recurring (opening base, new, expansion, contraction, churn, price effects); transactional (customers, conversion, order value, frequency, mix, price realisation); sales-led (pipeline creation, stage conversion, win rate, contract value, cycle time, renewals, expansion).
3. Name the two or three drivers explaining most variance; separate repeatable internal gains from external tailwinds.
4. Separate baseline from budget; the gap is where the plan relies on hope.

Output: a one-page baseline brief (trajectory, waterfall, top variance drivers, growth and profit concentration by unit, three to five implications).

### Unit economics
Ask which customers and motions create value and which destroy it. Subscription: CAC by channel and segment, contribution margin, gross and net retention, payback, servicing cost. Transactional: contribution per order and customer, marketing efficiency, repeat and cohort curves, returns and fraud leakage, fulfilment cost. Platform: economics per side, take rate, subsidies, cross-side elasticity. Build a heatmap (segment by channel or product) showing growth rate and contribution margin together. Use cohort analysis as the test of truth (do later cohorts retain worse? does cost-to-serve fall with tenure?). Note second-order effects of each lever: discount discipline can cut volume if value is poorly communicated; retention needs onboarding and product investment; channel shifts can create conflict; service redesign must protect the moments that drive retention.

### Customers and channels
- Segments must imply different offers, messages or motions, or they are not strategic.
- Map the evidence-based journey: discovery and shortlist, evaluation and influencers, purchase and onboarding delays, usage behaviours linked to retention, renewal and advocacy.
- Run a four-view channel diagnostic: contribution (revenue, growth, profit), efficiency (CAC, productivity, payback), capacity (volume the channel can absorb), fit (segments served well or badly). Look for hidden channels (integrators, embedded distribution, marketplaces, procurement frameworks, referral loops).
- Diagnose churn: voluntary versus forced; the moments that matter. Teams often blame price when onboarding, unclear return on investment or unreliable service is the cause.

### Competition
Define the competitive set including substitutes ("what would the customer do without us?"), bundled incumbents, platform players and in-house builds. State the basis of competition ("we win when X matters and lose when Y matters": price and total cost, performance, time-to-value, integration, trust, reach, service outcomes). Use win-loss data (however imperfect), competitor offer structure, and hypotheses on competitor economics and intent.

### Constraints (design inputs, not excuses)
| Constraint | Diagnostic question | Relief |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | If demand doubled in six months, what breaks first? | Find the bottleneck step; cost and time its expansion |
| Talent | Do we have people for the likely options? | Identify gating roles and realistic fill times |
| Capital | If we invest here, what stops or shrinks? | Make trade-offs explicit; avoid growth on top of the day job |
| Brand and trust | Which options break the promise? | State what the strategy will not do |
| Regulation and risk | Which approvals, privacy or safety rules apply? | Engage early; find blockers before scaling |

Build a constraint heatmap per growth vector: which constraint binds first, what relieves it, who owns it.

## 3. Map the option space: seven degrees of growth

Purpose: widen the option set and make trade-offs explicit. Do not pursue all seven at once; each needs different data, talent and decision rhythm. Coherent strategies usually concentrate on two to four. Use the map three times: framing (in scope, explore only, out of scope), option generation (degree by degree, into comparable memos), and portfolio design (mix, sequence, owners, budgets). Before generating options for a degree, write a degree brief: growth hypothesis, value mechanism, assets to leverage, capabilities required, fast proof.

| Degree | What it is | Proof required before scaling | Traps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Deepen current customers and markets | Usage, frequency, share of wallet, retention, account expansion, price and mix; fastest impact, funds the rest | Improving net and gross retention, price realisation, attach rates | Discounting as the main lever; cross-sell before the core job works; buying acquisition while churn is high |
| 2. New segments and channels | Same offer to new buyers (vertical, size, use case) via new routes (inside sales, self-serve, resellers, integrators, marketplaces, embedded, partnerships); a go-to-market redesign | Partner or channel creates pipeline and renews customers profitably | Modelling one set of economics for all segments |
| 3. New geographies | Beachhead segment where advantage transfers, plus a localisation plan; entry by direct, distributor, partnership or acquisition | Beachhead hits target unit economics | Broad launch before proof |
| 4. Extend the offer to current customers | Adjacent job customers already pay to solve; bundling, services attachment, premium tier, modules | Attaches at meaningful rates without raising core churn | Extending before the core is stable |
| 5. Change the profit model | One-time to subscription, project to retainer, feature to outcome or usage-based pricing | Migration pilot with stable churn and better net retention | Treating it as a pricing exercise; it is a systems change (metering, entitlement, sales behaviour, finance, customer success) |
| 6. Change role in the value chain | Move up or downstream, integrate, platform, restructure distribution | Model partner cooperation or retaliation; strong existing position | High execution risk |
| 7. Build new businesses | New lines using corporate assets | Repeatable acquisition and improving cohort economics; wedge use case validates in 90-180 days | Highest uncertainty; stage with explicit kill criteria |

## 4. Generate and document options

### Ideation session
- Prework (48 hours): circulate four pages (ambition and guardrails, baseline, profit-pool map, customer jobs and journey brief) and collect three option starters per participant (verb, customer, mechanism, tagged to a degree).
- Eight to twelve participants across commercial, product, customer success, operations, finance and technology. Roles: facilitator (time and format), scribe (captures live), fact keeper (data and assumptions), rotating red team, executive sponsor (opens with mandate, closes with next steps).
- Rules: options not opinions (disagree with an alternative option, not a debate); one primary mechanism per option; name the target segment and first proof point; tag evidence or label as hypothesis.
- Three-hour agenda: context 15 minutes; insight recap 20; degree sprints 60; cluster and sharpen 35; reality pass 30; commit owners for memos 20. Clean up and publish the inventory within 48 hours.
- Mini-option format: verb plus target, mechanism, first proof point (for example, reduce churn in a named segment by redesigning onboarding to the first success milestone; proof: activation rate improves within eight weeks).

### One-page option memo (same structure for every option)
1 Option statement; 2 degree and horizon; 3 customer and job; 4 value mechanism; 5 economics (range, timing, three key assumptions); 6 investment (people, budget, tools; one-off versus recurring); 7 capabilities and dependencies (build, buy or partner); 8 top three risks with mitigations; 9 first 90 days with leading indicator; 10 owner (sponsor and lead).

Size roughly with simple driver arithmetic and ranges: new segment = reachable accounts x plausible win rate x average contract value; retention recovery = churn reduction in a cohort x revenue per customer, adjusted for service cost; pricing = expected realisation uplift x affected revenue, with conservative volume sensitivity.

Confidence labels: high (internal data or proven execution), medium (evidence and analogues, some new capability), low (hypothesis, needs testing). Quality gate: specific target, one dominant mechanism, explicit assumptions, 90-day proof point, realistic resources, named owner.

## 5. Core growth plays (degrees 1 and 2)

### Pricing, packaging and leakage
Objective: raise net price realisation while reducing buying and renewal friction. Leakage patterns: discount drift (pre-emptive discounting hides willingness to pay); concession creep (free support, custom work, extended terms raise cost-to-serve); renewal reset (renewals become renegotiations because deals were over-customised or value invisible).

Hygiene moves (payback in one to two quarters): discount approval ladder with reason capture; price or bound concessions; fix quote-to-cash errors. Run a weekly deal desk that approves exceptions fast and harvests learning. Package with value-based fences (tiers differ on outcomes customers care about), operational feasibility, and renewal simplicity. Treat rollout as a change programme (internal training and compliance tracking, customer value language and fair transition, end-to-end operational test with pause conditions).

Leakage checklist: documented discount ladder; standard concessions with cost visibility; explicit renewal rules (uplift, true-ups, tier migration) with value proof; entitlement records; share of invoices needing credits and root causes; exceptions feeding back into defaults.

### Retention and expansion
Retention is the quiet multiplier: a small churn improvement often outlasts a large acquisition push. Define lifecycle milestones and owners (acquisition, activation, adoption, value realisation, renewal, expansion). Treat time-to-value as a strategic metric. Segment churn: early (onboarding and expectations), late (reliability, roadmap confidence, embedding), commercial (renewal packaging, migration rules), forced (customer closure). Pick two or three leading indicators (adoption depth, usage frequency, time since last meaningful outcome, support severity, stakeholder engagement) and build an early-warning system. Run four plays (onboarding, adoption, renewal, save), each with cohort, trigger, steps, assets and KPI. Expansion pathways each need an eligibility rule, value story and mechanism: more users or sites, adjacent modules, attached services, true-ups or usage billing. Trade-offs: price uplift needs visible value; usage expansion needs transparent metering; cross-sell needs fit and enablement; surprise true-ups erode trust.

Success plan at onboarding: outcomes in customer language with two or three measures; milestones (first success, steady state, renewal proof); roles (customer owner, sponsor, own owner, escalation); value-proof method and date; next expansion hypothesis.

### Channel performance
Highest return is usually better routing, coverage, partner productivity and measurement, not a new channel. Scorecard per channel: contribution, efficiency, conversion, capacity. Coverage moves: rebalance territories to shifting profit pools; specialise motions for segments needing different service; enforce handoff discipline between marketing, development reps, sellers and customer success. Partners need a reason to sell (margin, services revenue, attach), deal registration, co-selling rules and clean handoffs; measure partner-sourced pipeline, win rates, retention and cohort quality. Attribution over-credits the last touch; triangulate spend against pipeline and cohort outcomes and move budget to channels that create incremental, well-retained pipeline.

### Commercial productivity
Define the sales motion (self-serve, inside, field, partner-led, hybrid), each with its own economics, enablement and incentives. Five drivers: pipeline per rep, stage conversion, win rate, deal size, cycle length; find the binding constraint. Manage by system (weekly leading-indicator reviews, monthly outcomes, coaching loops; mutual action plans for complex deals). Incentive test: if a seller maximises pay by doing the opposite of the strategy, the plan is broken (segment accelerators, discount guardrails, renewal-quality component). Enablement by quality: segment value narrative, proof library, discovery guides, action-plan templates; measure asset use and conversion effect.

Sales motion canvas: target (segments, buyer roles, qualification thresholds); value story and proof per buying role; stages with exit criteria and artefacts; friction removers (security, legal, procurement, implementation); default offer, discount rules and expansion path; KPIs (pipeline, conversion, cycle time, win rate, deal size, margin).

## 6. Adjacent plays (degrees 2-4)

- Segment and channel adjacency: pass three tests (transferable right to win; an affordable route to those customers; unit economics equal to or better than the core). Enter through a beachhead segment, geography or use case. Avoid when degree 1 churn is high, product fit is unproven, or channel economics are structurally worse.
- Geographic expansion: choose entry mode by control, cost and risk (direct: most control and cost; distributor: cheaper, depends on partner quality; partnership: shared investment, needs aligned incentives; acquisition: fastest access, highest risk, needs integration capability). Checklist: does the offer transfer to a beachhead segment; what must be localised (pricing, language, distribution, compliance); can on-the-ground capability be built in affordable time; which mode fits capital and management bandwidth.
- Offer extension: identify jobs best customers already pay to solve. Best adjacencies have existing pain, a data, access or trust advantage, low implementation complexity and high gross margin (service-led extensions often qualify). Do not extend before the core is stable; extensions amplify churn.

## 7. Evaluate and build the portfolio

Score each option on strategic fit (equity story and guardrails), economic impact (revenue, margin, timing, recurring or one-off), execution feasibility (capability, data, talent within the horizon) and risk (top three failure modes, credible mitigations). Shortlist screens: specific target segment or cohort; clear dominant value mechanism; explicit assumptions; a 90-day proof point that produces learning; realistic resources with a named owner.

Portfolio rules: cover now, next and later; balance risk (high-confidence core, medium-confidence adjacent, one or two exploratory bets); keep options mutually reinforcing (degree 1 funds degree 2; degree 2 proves before degree 3 scales); respect capacity (three to five heavy initiatives at once). Sequence: lead with degree 1; stage degrees 2-4 behind proof; fund degrees 5-7 only when the core is stable and cash headroom exists. Sanity check: if you cannot name what stops when a new initiative starts, you are not ready to scale.

## 8. Run the growth operating system

- Governance in three layers: weekly initiative reviews (milestones, leading indicators, decisions needed); monthly portfolio review (reconcile to scorecard, reallocate); quarterly leadership review (capital allocation, trade-offs, mandate refresh). Forum rules: pre-read 24 hours ahead, meetings for decisions, every meeting ends with decisions, owners and deadlines, discussion on what changed, what is at risk, what is needed.
- Test-and-learn gates: hypothesis (0-30 days: hypothesis, minimum test, kill criteria, success metrics); learning (30-90 days: run, gather evidence, go or no-go on leading indicators); proof (90-180 days: repeatable unit economics); scale (180+ days). Set kill criteria in advance; stopping to free capacity is a success. Fast proofs: degree 1 pricing (exception rate, net price realisation, churn threshold); degree 2 (partner-sourced pipeline rate, 90-day renewal); degree 3 (beachhead conversion, cost-to-serve versus core); degree 4 (attach rate, effect on core renewal); degree 5 (migration rate, net retention versus cohort baseline).
- Stakeholder alignment: answer why (customer and market reality, no blame), what (priorities that shift, decisions made differently, what will stop), how (measures, routines, enablement). Tailor to executives (portfolio logic, scorecard, cadence), middle managers (routines, decision rules, exception guidance) and frontline (workflow, tools, escalation). Consistent leader behaviour is the communication.

## 9. East Africa adaptation

| Growth challenge | Degrees | Local notes (verify) |
|---|---|---|
| Grow revenue in current market | 1 | Mobile money enables low-friction retention and expansion plays |
| New segments | 2 | Regional growth within the EAC; income-tier segmentation is critical |
| Regional expansion | 3 | AfCFTA and the EAC Common Market (rules of origin; free movement provisions for EAC nationals; verify current rules and local-value thresholds) |
| Adjacent services | 4 | Health, financial services and agri-inputs are common high-value adjacencies |
| Model shift | 5 | USSD or mobile-first monetisation for low-smartphone markets |

Common constraints and mitigations: low rural smartphone and data penetration (USSD, messaging apps, agent networks, radio and community channels); thin mid-management talent (build before buy, regional talent pool); unreliable power and roads (backup power, hub-and-spoke logistics, last-mile partners); working-capital strain from slow payment cycles (mobile money prepayment incentives, layaway or SACCO financing); regulatory lag (early regulator engagement, sandbox participation where offered).

Pricing and packaging norms: tier prices to income bands; treat mobile money integration as a packaging decision; use trust signals (community endorsement, visible local presence, pay-after-value) to cut purchase risk; translate formal quotes into informal channel equivalents because many small buyers buy through phone conversations.
