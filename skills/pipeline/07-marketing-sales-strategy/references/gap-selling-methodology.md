# Problem-Centred (Gap-Based) Selling for Business Plans

Use this reference when a plan must show why customers will buy and how the sales process will find, quantify and close a customer's problem. The approach is known as gap selling (Keenan); it is named here with attribution and applied to plan sections and to sales-forecast validation.

## 1. Core idea

A sale exists in the gap between the customer's current state (problems, costs, inefficiencies, missed opportunities) and a desired future state (outcomes, improvements, new capabilities). The larger and better quantified the gap, the greater the urgency and the willingness to pay. Every product or service description in a plan should state which gap it closes; investors fund solutions to large gaps, not clever products.

## 2. Selling principles to apply

1. No problem, no sale: lead with the customer's problem, not the product.
2. Every sale involves a quantifiable gap.
3. Buying means change, and people resist change: address switching cost, risk and implementation burden.
4. Purchases carry emotion (frustration, fear, ambition); acknowledge it.
5. Customers accept change only when the return exceeds the total cost of change.
6. Repeated "why" questions uncover root causes rather than symptoms.
7. Describe a vivid, credible future state.
8. Customers care about their problems, not your features.

## 3. Current-state map

| Dimension | Discover | Illustration (agribusiness) |
|---|---|---|
| Facts and data | Measurable situation | Post-harvest loss rate, number of suppliers, monthly revenue |
| Problems | What is going wrong | Inconsistent grading, no cold chain, dependence on intermediaries |
| Impact | Business consequences | Annual revenue lost, blocked market access |
| Root cause | Why it exists | No certification, manual processes, poor market information |
| Emotion | How it feels | Frustration, anxiety about viability |

Problem-identification checklist per customer segment: literal problem; quantified business impact (revenue lost, time wasted, cost incurred); root cause; emotional impact; current workaround; cost of that workaround.

## 4. Future-state map and gap equation

| Dimension | Define |
|---|---|
| New facts and data | Measurable improvements (loss reduced, customers connected, revenue) |
| Problems solved | Which current problems disappear |
| Positive impact | Gains realised |
| New capabilities | What the customer can now do |
| Emotional outcome | Confidence, control, pride |

Gap size = value of the future state - (cost of the current state + cost of change).
- Gap above zero: a sale is possible.
- Large gap: urgent and less price-sensitive.
- Zero or negative: no sale; the problem is too small or the change too costly.
Quantify the gap in money per segment in the plan.

## 5. Discovery process

Ask in order: situation (facts), problem (issues), impact (consequences in revenue, time, reputation), root cause ("why do you think that happens? what have you tried?"), emotion (effect on the person, team and goals).

Rules:
1. Do not pitch before discovery is complete.
2. Ask why at least three times; first answers are symptoms.
3. Quantify everything ("a lot of waste" becomes a monetary annual loss).
4. Use silence.
5. Take visible notes.
6. Confirm understanding by repeating it back.

Discovery record template: customer; current state (facts, problems, impact, root cause, emotion); future state (desired outcomes, new capabilities, success metrics); the gap (size, urgency, decision criteria, decision process including who decides, how and when).

## 6. Comparison with feature-led selling

| Aspect | Feature-led | Gap-led |
|---|---|---|
| Starts with | Product features | Customer problems |
| Discovery | Budget, authority, need, timeline | Current state, impact, root cause, emotion |
| Presentation | Feature and benefit slides | Gap analysis, current to future |
| Objections | Overcome resistance | Revisit discovery; the gap is unclear |
| Closing | Techniques and pressure | Natural once the gap is clear |
| Competition | Other vendors | The status quo |
| Price sensitivity | High | Lower, since price is compared with gap value |

The real competitor is usually the status quo (doing nothing or the current workaround); most lost deals are "no decision".

## 7. Objection reframing

| Objection | Likely meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | Gap not big enough for the price | Return to impact; quantify the cost of not changing |
| "We need to think about it" | Future state not compelling | Clarify desired outcomes |
| "We are happy with the current solution" | Problems not acknowledged | Return to problem identification |
| "Not the right time" | No urgency | Ask what happens if they wait six months |
| "Send me information" | Trust or relevance missing | Ask which information would help and why |

## 8. Outbound prospecting by gap

Structure: name a likely problem (from ideal-customer research); cite its impact; hint at the future state; ask for a discovery conversation, not a pitch.

Message template: subject line naming the specific problem; an observation showing research; the common problem and its quantified cost per period; a comparable customer moved from current to future state in a stated time; a request for a short call to test whether a similar gap exists.

Local adaptation: cite local conditions that create urgency (tightening quality standards, regulatory changes, mobile-money adoption); verify each claim with a dated source before using it.

## 9. Applying it in a plan

Sales strategy section:
1. Define the gap for each segment (current, future, quantified difference).
2. Map the discovery questions salespeople will ask.
3. Build sales materials around before-and-after transformation.
4. Set price as a fraction of gap value.
5. Train discovery before product knowledge.
6. Track gap metrics (average gap per deal; link between gap size and close rate).

Forecast validation:

| Check | Question | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Problem severity | Is it painful enough to drive action? | Nice-to-have, small gap |
| Gap versus price | Is the gap several times the price (roughly three to five times as a rule of thumb)? | Price above about half the gap |
| Status quo strength | How entrenched is the workaround? | Heavy prior investment |
| Urgency | Is there a forcing event (deadline, regulation, growth)? | No timeline pressure |
| Buyer authority | Can this person decide? | Talking to influencers only |
