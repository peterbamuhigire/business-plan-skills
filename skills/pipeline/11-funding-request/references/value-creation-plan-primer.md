# Value Creation Plan Primer

A value creation plan (VCP) turns an investment thesis into named initiatives, metrics, owners and governance that together produce a target return. It is an evidence-based decision system, not a budget. Use it for equity proposals and for any plan where an investor or lender must see how operating actions produce cash, profit and value. All numbers below are illustrative planning assumptions; validate them with the business's own data and, for tax or accounting treatment, with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 1. Build the plan in this order

1. Restate the thesis and the return bridge.
2. Baseline the business and write the do-nothing case.
3. Set the equity story and driver-based targets.
4. Select levers, then convert them into initiatives.
5. Design governance, incentives and change support.
6. Stress-test with scenarios and write contingencies.
7. Plan the first 100 days, then continuous exit readiness.

## 2. Ownership context and common failures

Investor holding periods typically run three to seven years, with most value created early. Leverage amplifies both returns and risk, so cash discipline matters as much as profit growth. Exit value = exit EBITDA x multiple - net debt, and working capital and capex discipline touch all three terms.

| Failure | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Thesis-to-plan gap | Diligence findings never become initiatives |
| Over-initiative | Too many workstreams dilute ownership |
| Governance lag | Monthly-only reviews; problems found too late |
| People mismatch | Leaders judged on past results, not future demands |
| Leakage | Value designed but never captured in the financials |

## 3. Thesis to plan

A thesis has four parts: strategic rationale (why this asset, why now, which position is defensible); value path (which mechanisms move EBITDA, cash and multiple); proof plan (evidence in the first 6-12 months that confirms or refutes it); return bridge (operating levers to money multiple and IRR).

Translate each mechanism into an initiative with owner, milestones and leading indicators. Hold an alignment session on the top three to five value drivers; test alignment by asking each leader separately for the top three priorities (different answers mean no alignment).

Reconcile top-down return needs with bottom-up feasibility: express the required return in drivers (price, volume, margin); run a mechanism-level feasibility check; if a gap remains choose explicitly to raise intensity (more levers, faster, better capability), change the path (add-ons, disposals, horizon) or reset expectations. Stage the plan as a high-confidence base plus upside plays activated by evidence, and define leading indicators that move before the P&L (pipeline creation, conversion, price realisation).

Revisit the thesis only for three reasons: post-close data shows a diligence assumption was materially wrong; external conditions shifted (input costs, regulation, competitors); or strategic actions changed the business (add-on, disposal, new channel model). If the thesis breaks, protect liquidity and controllable levers first, then rebuild a path with fewer, higher-confidence moves.

## 4. Baseline and do-nothing case

Financial baseline
- At least 24 months of monthly data, longer if volatile. Lock definitions (revenue, EBITDA exclusions, working-capital method) and bridge the competing views (management accounts, audited statements, lender covenant reports).
- Split revenue into price, volume and mix by segment (recurring models: bookings, renewals, expansion, churn).
- Build the cash bridge: EBITDA to operating cash flow to free cash flow (tax, interest, working capital, maintenance capex). Compute baseline return on invested capital even if never tracked.

Operational and commercial baseline
- Identify binding constraints (capacity, lead time, quality, pricing discipline, sales coverage).
- Funnel with consistent definitions; for relationship businesses, quantify coverage and capacity.
- Price waterfall by segment: list price, discounts, rebates, freight, net realised price.
- Delivery metrics tied to the customer (schedule adherence, throughput, yield, on-time delivery).
- A "do not break" list: safety, compliance, critical quality, core service levels, key-talent retention.

Do-nothing case
- Include committed actions (signed price changes, approved capex, planned hires), contractual terms (escalators, supplier indexation) and known cost step-ups; exclude speculative improvement needing behaviour change.
- Express as a range. Report all value claims relative to this case, not to last year.

Benchmarking and prize sizing
- Benchmark at three layers (financial: gross and EBITDA margin, working-capital days; operational: adherence, utilisation, yield; commercial: win rate, net price realisation, renewal, churn). Classify gaps as structural, strategic choice or execution weakness; only the last is the opportunity. Internal benchmarks across sites, regions or product lines are especially powerful.
- Size each value pool (price realisation, mix, procurement, labour productivity, overhead, footprint, working-capital release) as a range: current performance x improvement x base x timing. Separate run-rate from in-year impact, and value creation from value transfer.

## 5. Equity story and targets

Equity story (what a future buyer will believe): strategic identity (priority segments and boundaries); economic engine (pricing model, unit economics, capital intensity); competitive advantage (few capabilities that are stronger at exit and hard to copy); valuation readiness (quality of earnings, resilience, repeatable cadence). Archetypes: premium category leader (differentiation and pricing proven in retention and realisation); scaled platform (standardised operating model, integration discipline); operational turnaround (reliability and cash conversion first, growth rebuilt after).

Targets, three to five years
- Build revenue from drivers, not one growth rate; recurring models: opening base, gross and net retention, new bookings, booking-to-revenue lag.
- Separate structural EBITDA gains (pricing governance, procurement, productivity) from volume leverage; apply a quality-of-earnings test (will the mechanism exist at exit?).
- Build cash targets from the EBITDA bridge including working capital, tax, interest, restructuring and maintenance capex. Phase in years 1-3 and pair with leading indicators.

Return link: enterprise value = exit EBITDA x multiple; equity = enterprise value - net debt; money multiple measures size, IRR adds timing (early cash lifts IRR at the same multiple). Run a "what must be true" test stating exit EBITDA, net debt and multiple required for the base-case return.

Sequencing patterns: cash-first stabilisation (bank price, working-capital and productivity gains, then fund growth); growth-led repositioning (invest early when the opportunity is time-sensitive); margin-led funding (harvest structural margin to fund growth). Warning signs of imbalance: service failures, rising discounts, swelling inventory, attrition in critical roles.

## 6. Lever library

Growth levers must state where demand comes from, why the company wins it and what operating change converts it to profitable revenue.
- Market: segment focus on highest willingness to pay and capacity to serve; coverage redesign (reallocate sales capacity, hunter and farmer roles); standard pipeline engine (sources, qualification, cadence); conversion (simpler quotes, shorter cycles, proposal standards); retention (onboarding, customer success, reliability, renewal discipline).
- Pricing (often highest return): price architecture by attribute and segment; discount governance (authority, thresholds, approval workflow, transaction data); sales enablement (value messaging, deal tools, negotiation standards).
- Product: near term, SKU rationalisation, packaging, bundling, service attachment; longer term, new offers, premium tier, mix shift.

Margin levers
- Cost efficiency: procurement (category owners, contract coverage, compliance tracking) and overhead (clarify necessary work, standardise, centralise or automate, then right-size).
- Mix: customer and product rationalisation, tiered service models, channel optimisation.
- Productivity: same output with fewer resources through better processes, standards and asset use; gains are not real unless sustained by daily management, standard work, performance boards, training and accountability.

Capital levers
- Working capital: diagnose receivables (terms, billing accuracy, disputes, collections), inventory (forecast quality, SKU complexity, lead times) and payables (terms, compliance); mechanisms include credit governance, forecast and sales-and-operations planning, SKU reduction, A/B/C inventory segmentation.
- Capex: separate maintenance (reliability, safety) from growth capex (stage behind evidence); govern execution to cut overruns and commissioning time.
- Portfolio moves (disposals, consolidations, add-ons): judge on execution capacity and timing, not just strategic fit; write integration or separation plans.

Multiple levers: strategic positioning (retention engine, proven pricing power, reliability as differentiator, scalable go-to-market); earnings quality (less EBITDA noise, revenue-recognition discipline, no aggressive capitalisation, fewer one-off add-backs); key-person risk (second leadership layer, performance cadence, documented decision routines).

Prioritise each lever with three questions: mechanism and leading indicators; size range and landing date for EBITDA, cash and valuation; requirements and what must stop to make room. A strong portfolio holds at least one growth lever, one structural margin lever, one cash lever and one or two proof-building levers.

## 7. Initiative portfolio

An initiative is a discrete, managed effort changing a defined set of decisions, processes or capabilities to deliver measurable value; it is not "ongoing improvement". Specify object (what changes), population (where), mechanism (why it creates value) and evidence (which metric moves first). Translation checklist: decision change, process change that makes it repeatable, capability change, owner accountable for outcomes, first proof metric moving in 30-90 days.

Scope: a primary objective as a range (for example a stated basis-point gain in net price realisation in a target segment); proof objectives showing durability; boundaries (population, function, time, decision); measurable guardrails (safety, compliance, quality, churn limits); deliverables as installed mechanisms rather than documents. Sanity checks: materiality within the holding period, unambiguous "done", owner with decision rights, baseline and leading indicators.

Impact estimate: addressable base x driver change x adoption curve - leakage. Confidence: high (known base, proven mechanism, clear owner, measurable indicators), medium (controllable but needs capability or behaviour change), low (uncertain market response, complex systems change or multi-entity coordination). Separate run-rate from in-year effect and show timing against covenant headroom and investment needs.

Sequencing constraints: data and systems, people, cash, commercial timing, operating stability. Classify initiatives heavy (cross-functional, behaviour-changing; at most three to five at once), medium or light (fast, often governance tightening). Use decision gates: measurable thresholds that unlock the next wave or extra investment.

## 8. Governance and reporting

Three layers: workstream (weekly, owner-led: milestones, leading indicators, risks, decisions needed); enterprise value (monthly, chaired by the chief executive: reconcile to the value bridge, reallocate); board (quarterly: capital allocation, trade-offs, risk tolerance, accountability). Principles: decision-first forums, owner-led accountability, leading indicators, a single set of definitions, light artefacts (charters, portfolio view, value bridge).

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sponsor | Set ambition, enforce accountability, ask mechanism questions rather than status questions |
| Board | Decide items needing its authority; reinforce stable definitions |
| Chief executive | Integrator and VCP owner; resolves cross-functional conflict, protects focus |
| Finance lead | Guards definitions and value-bridge integrity; links early-warning indicators to cash and covenants |

Dashboards: initiative (milestones, indicators, risks, decisions), portfolio (health against the bridge), board (performance versus equity story, decisions needed). Choose metrics that predict value and that owners can influence; define each with source, refresh rate, owner and threshold. Decision triggers: two consecutive misses on a leading indicator, guardrail breach, material loss of forecast headroom.

Escalation: level 1 initiative owner; level 2 sponsor for cross-functional conflict; level 3 chief executive for enterprise trade-offs; level 4 board or investor for capital, leadership or risk tolerance. Stop rule: an initiative missing critical milestones for two cycles without a credible recovery plan is redesigned or stopped.

## 9. People, incentives, change

Leadership fit behaviours: clarity (name what matters, changes, stops), velocity (decide on imperfect information, small tests), accountability (own outcomes, speak in drivers, propose action on variance), installation (embed improvements in standards, routines, dashboards, training). Common upgrade points: finance (cash discipline, forecasting), commercial (coverage, conversion, pricing), operations (reliability, productivity). One accountable owner with decision rights per lever.

Incentives in three layers: enterprise (EBITDA and free cash flow), value drivers (net price realisation, retention, service reliability, productivity, working-capital health), role metrics leaders control (procurement compliance, schedule adherence, pipeline creation). Balance EBITDA and cash with guardrails against starving capex or service; do not reward volume while demanding price discipline; use shared metrics for cross-functional levers; keep the metric set small and precisely defined.

Change management: make "what will stop" explicit; tailor messages (executives: portfolio logic; managers: routines; frontline: workflow); confront exception culture and hero culture; run a quarterly load reset (list each team's top five recurring obligations, drop or automate one, cap new work); normalise the emotional arc (early energy, then reality, then reinforcement).

## 10. Risk, scenarios, stress tests

Treat assumptions as hypotheses with owner, test and trigger; keep 10-15 load-bearing ones across market, commercial, operational, financial and organisational categories. Rank by impact x control: high-impact, high-control first; high-impact, low-control needs buffers and optionality. Keep an assumption log (assumption, current evidence, owner, next test, action trigger), updated monthly.

| Scenario | Definition |
|---|---|
| Base | Expected path, conservative ramp, controllable and earnable levers |
| Upside | One or two mechanisms outperform; activated by evidence with added investment and risk |
| Downside | One or two drivers underperform plus timing headwinds; tests financeability and response time |

Phase scenarios in quarters (shock quarters 1-2, stabilise 3-6, recover or rebase) and attach triggers based on leading indicators, which fire before month-end profit does. For each critical initiative plan three failure modes: slip (narrow scope, workarounds, expertise, escalate), underperformance (redesign, intensify, substitute lever) and side effects (segment, pause scaling until guardrails recover), with three response levels (corrective, scope or resource shifts, strategic). Stress test three to five combined shocks (demand, price pressure, input cost, working capital, financing) including second-order effects (lower volume worsens absorption and unit cost and distracts from initiatives); the output is a sequenced liquidity protection playbook (immediate, near term, follow-on). Adjust quarterly by diagnosing variance in driver terms and choosing intensify, redesign, re-sequence or replace; never relabel categories to hide underperformance.

## 11. First 100 days and exit readiness

First 100 days (highest leverage: access improves, attention is high, small decisions compound). Days 1-30: cash visibility, verify customer and contract baseline, leadership assessment, binding constraints, governance rhythm. Days 31-60: launch the first high-confidence wave, install indicator dashboards, share the equity story internally. Days 61-100: first proof points, first "stop doing" decisions, present the plan to the board. Avoid spending all 100 days in analysis, launching too much at once, or letting investor ownership read as a threat.

Exit readiness builds continuously: monthly dashboards on what buyers test (pricing discipline, retention, delivery reliability, margin consistency, cash conversion); clean earnings (disciplined add-backs, lower customer concentration, documented cadence); a before-and-after narrative with durable evidence. Checklist: better net price realisation with churn inside guardrails; delivery and quality shown as differentiators; lower key-person risk; cleaner reporting; consistent execution across roughly 8-12 quarters.

## 12. Use in a business plan

| VCP element | Plan section | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis | 01 Executive summary | Equity story: why the business is worth more later |
| Baseline | 10 Financial projections | Actuals and clear definitions |
| Ambition and targets | 10 Financial projections | Driver-based revenue, EBITDA and cash targets |
| Levers | 03 Products and services, 07 Marketing | Growth, margin and capital levers with mechanisms |
| Initiatives and governance | 13 Implementation timeline | Owners, milestones, indicators, cadence, decision rights |
| Scenarios | 12 Risk analysis | Base, upside, downside with triggers |
| First 100 days | 13 Implementation timeline | Launch sequence |
| Exit preparation | 11 Funding request | Return link and proof points |

East Africa adaptation: for bank-debt contexts replace the money-multiple and IRR framing with debt service cover and return on equity; keep the value bridge to show how revenue, margin and cash targets support debt service; weekly leading indicators help avoid covenant breaches; apply the leadership criteria to founders and management; treat exchange-rate shock, input-cost inflation and policy change as the most material downside scenarios (verify current conditions).
