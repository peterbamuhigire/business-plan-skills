# Digital Innovation Strategy Toolkit

**Use for:** the AI integration section (14), with links into company overview (02), products (03), market analysis (04), competitive analysis (06) and operations (08). Named frameworks: 6D strategy cycle, strategy nucleus, Three Horizons and multimodal innovation, Doblin's ten types of innovation, Blue Ocean Strategy, VRIN, Design Thinking, Transform Loop (Palfreyman) and digital maturity stages. Descriptions are the engine's own.

## 1. 6D cycle (iterative strategy)

Discover, Detect, Diagnose, Direct, Deliver, Dexterity, then repeat. The aim is strategic adaptability: a fixed purpose with continuous external scanning and recalibration.

| Step | Purpose | AI application |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Market, positioning, culture, internal performance and ambition | Market data aggregation; sentiment analysis; trend monitoring |
| Detect | Look further out for trends and signals | Anomaly detection; news and competitor feeds; price alerts |
| Diagnose | Extract themes; where to create value | Analytics; funnel analysis; unit economics modelling |
| Direct | Prioritise themes, drivers, objectives, target outcomes | Scenario and decision-support tools; stress tests |
| Deliver | Delivery plans with governance | Project automation; CRM; automated invoicing |
| Dexterity | Ongoing evaluation and recalibration | KPI dashboards; early-warning thresholds |

Plan use: name which steps AI tools strengthen, which shows investors a systematic approach.

## 2. Strategy nucleus (purpose-led)

Nested layers, centre outward: Purpose (fixed), Vision, Mission, Strategy, Values. Outer layers iterate; Purpose stays constant. Relation to the Golden Circle: Purpose is Why, Strategy is How, offer is What (see `02-company-overview/references/mission-vision-why.md`).

**Integrity test.** Ask of each strategic decision whether it serves the Purpose. Automation that saves cost but degrades customer relationships can fail the test.

Consumer and employee surveys suggest purpose affects loyalty and workplace innovation; if you cite such statistics, obtain a current dated source.

Plan use: anchor section 02 in Purpose first; in section 14, show how AI provides the data visibility and speed that let the company deliver its purpose at scale.

## 3. Three Horizons and multimodal innovation

Run all three at once (multispeed innovation) rather than in sequence; this needs organisational ambidexterity.

| Horizon | Intent | Culture | Return measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 exploit | Sustain and sweat the core; cost and profit | Lean, low risk | Margin, ROI, productivity |
| H2 extend | Diversify and grow; stretch the core | Assertive, flexible | Market share, payback |
| H3 explore | Experiment for new cores | Rapid, curious | Learning, venture building, brand |

**Planning assumption for SME allocation (adjust with rationale):** about 70 to 80 per cent of management time and budget to H1, 15 to 20 to H2 with a named owner, 5 to 10 to H3 with a learning objective and kill criteria.

**Risk.** H1 urgency crowding out H2 and H3. Dashboards that automate H1 monitoring free attention for the others. H3 items should be small, time-boxed pilots.

## 4. Doblin's ten types of innovation

- **Configuration:** 1 profit model; 2 network; 3 structure; 4 process.
- **Offering:** 5 product performance; 6 product system.
- **Experience:** 7 service; 8 channel; 9 brand; 10 customer engagement.

Audit: map where the business currently innovates. Firms competing only on type 5 are exposed to commodity pressure; advantages in three or more types are more defensible. Highest AI opportunity: process (4), service (7), channel (8), customer engagement (10).

| Type | AI overlay |
|---|---|
| Profit model | Dynamic pricing; subscription analytics |
| Network | Platform matching; supplier scoring |
| Process | Automated quality control; inventory optimisation |
| Service | Chatbots; automated follow-up |
| Channel | Personalised recommendations; mobile-first delivery |
| Customer engagement | Behavioural segmentation; loyalty triggers |

## 5. Blue Ocean Strategy

Create uncontested space by differentiating and cutting cost together.

Five steps:
1. **Map** competitive attributes against key rivals on a strategy curve.
2. **Identify** distinctive features through ideation (including Design Thinking).
3. **Isolate** features in high demand or enabling step change.
4. **Deprioritise** low-demand features, those the market is not ready for, or those the firm cannot deliver.
5. **Eliminate** obsolete or unviable features.

ERRC grid: Eliminate (factors the industry takes for granted), Reduce (well below standard), Raise (well above standard), Create (never offered). Feeds the "clear water" quality criterion in competitive analysis (06).

## 6. VRIN

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| Valuable | Meets customer need better than alternatives? |
| Rare | Few or no rivals have it? |
| Inimitable | Hard to copy? |
| Non-substitutable | Can rivals meet the need another way? |

Score each claimed advantage 0 to 4. Only 4 out of 4 supports a sustained advantage; 2 to 3 is temporary; 0 to 1 does not belong in the competitive analysis. Aim to identify one or two resources that pass all four.

Common SME failures: "quality products" (valuable, not rare); "experienced team" (substitutable); "low prices" (not rare; invites price war).

Proprietary operational data can pass all four tests, so state what data the business collects that rivals cannot easily replicate.

Assessment table columns: resource or capability | valuable | rare | inimitable | non-substitutable | score.

## 7. Design Thinking

Discover (empathise through observation and interviews), Define ("how might we..." statement), Ideate (volume before evaluation), Prototype (cheap, low fidelity), Test (watch behaviour, not stated opinion, and iterate).

| Step | AI development use |
|---|---|
| Discover | Interview users; map current non-AI workflows |
| Define | "How might we automate or improve X for [user]?" |
| Ideate | Ten or more AI feature ideas without filtering |
| Prototype | Mock-up, simulated ("wizard of oz") or spreadsheet model before code |
| Test | Observe real use; record confusion and workarounds |

Design Thinking supplies the user insight that scopes the minimum viable product (see `03-products-services/references/`). Use it for customer-facing AI so the design matches real, not assumed, behaviour.

## 8. Transform Loop (agile transformation)

Six steps: Scan, Prioritise, Learn, Experiment, Plan, Build.

- **Transformation Backlog:** score items 0 to 9 for strategic value and 0 to 9 for implementation difficulty.

| Value \ Difficulty | Low (0-3) | Medium (4-6) | High (7-9) |
|---|---|---|---|
| High (7-9) | Do first | Do second | Do third, with caution |
| Medium (4-6) | Do if resources allow | Backlog | Avoid |
| Low (0-3) | Backlog | Avoid | Eliminate |

- **Strategy first:** answer before transformation begins: where do we compete, what unique value do we deliver, how do we deliver it? (Rumelt.)
- **Five faces of agility:** strategic agility; the transform loop; experiment-mode sprints on unproven ideas; architectural flexibility (modular systems); cultural agility (changing how people work).
- **Failure modes:** starting with technology before defining the problem; treating transformation as a one-off project; underestimating cultural change; monolithic systems; skipping Learn between Prioritise and Build.

## 9. Digital maturity and AI pathway

| Stage | Definition | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Digitisation | Analogue records made digital | Low; data capturable |
| Digitalisation | Digital data improves existing processes | Medium; efficiency, visibility |
| Digital transformation | Business model reinvented digitally | High; structural advantage |

Most Ugandan SMEs sit at digitisation. Be honest about the current stage and the realistic next step.

**Self-assessment**

| Indicator | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial records | Paper or mobile money history | Accounting software | Real-time dashboards with alerts |
| Customer data | WhatsApp contacts | CRM with purchase history | Predictive churn and upsell |
| Inventory | Manual counts | Spreadsheet tracking | Automated reorder triggers |
| Marketing | Word of mouth | Social media presence | Data-driven optimisation |
| Operations | Owner-dependent | Documented processes | AI-assisted automation |

**Pathway (adapt to the business):**
- Year 1 (digitisation to digitalisation): export mobile money records to a cash-flow sheet; set up WhatsApp Business with auto-replies and a catalogue; record customer purchases in structured form; adopt basic accounting software (obtain current pricing).
- Year 2: spreadsheet demand forecast from historical sales; social analytics to find best content and channels; a basic CRM (free tiers exist); automate invoices and payment reminders.
- Year 3 onward: AI-assisted pricing from demand and competitor data; customer segmentation from accumulated history; evaluate sector-specific tools (agri-tech, fintech, health-tech); test whether proprietary data now provides a VRIN advantage.

## 10. Uganda and East Africa application

| Framework | Application |
|---|---|
| 6D | Strong Discover, weak Detect: set a quarterly Detect review using UBOS data, Bank of Uganda bulletins and sector association reports |
| Nucleus | Many SMEs have a mission but no Purpose; purpose-led positioning is increasingly expected by development-finance and NGO funders |
| Three Horizons | Agri-processor: H1 current line; H2 export entry; H3 data-driven crop forecasting service; run all with modest H2 and H3 allocation |
| Doblin | Most compete on type 5 alone; low-capital opportunities are process (lower-cost delivery) and channel (mobile-first distribution) |
| Blue Ocean | Rivals fight on price and quantity; unclaimed attributes are trust, speed and after-sale support |
| VRIN | Genuine advantages include supplier or government relationships, certifications (UNBS, organic), input access and community trust |
| Design Thinking | Users often endorse features in interviews they will not use; test prototypes with real users before investing |
| Transform Loop | Start with mobile money payments (high value, low difficulty), WhatsApp customer service (high value, very low difficulty), simple inventory tracking (medium value, low difficulty) |
| AI tools | Mobile money exports for cash-flow forecasting; WhatsApp Business automation; spreadsheet forecasting |

**Regional reference points (verify current status before citing):** agricultural advisory by SMS and USSD; satellite and farm-data credit scoring for smallholders; alternative credit scoring for micro-borrowers; transaction-data credit platforms; pay-as-you-go asset finance with IoT data. Use these to show AI can reach users without smartphones.

**Regulatory checks for AI in Uganda (verify current position):**
- Confirm whether any AI-specific regulation exists (NITA-U oversees digital infrastructure).
- The Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 applies to personal data used in AI; check registration duties with the Personal Data Protection Office.
- AI credit scoring may need Bank of Uganda approval if it is a financial service; a fintech sandbox framework exists.
- Check any UNBS position on AI product certification; ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management systems) may matter for export markets.

## 12. Quick reference

- 6D: Discover, Detect, Diagnose, Direct, Deliver, Dexterity.
- Nucleus (inner to outer): Purpose, Vision, Mission, Strategy, Values.
- Horizons: exploit, extend, explore.
- Highest-AI-impact Doblin types: process, service, channel, customer engagement.
- ERRC: Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create.
- VRIN: 4 out of 4 for sustained advantage.
- Design Thinking: Discover, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test.
- Transform Loop: Scan, Prioritise, Learn, Experiment, Plan, Build.
- Maturity: digitisation, digitalisation, transformation.
