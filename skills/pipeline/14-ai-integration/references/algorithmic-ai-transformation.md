# Process-First, Experience-Led AI Transformation

**Use for:** keeping the AI integration section practical. Two principles: AI follows process redesign rather than replacing it, and AI must improve customer and employee experience, not only cut cost. Draws on the five-step redesign sequence (McNeill) and customer-experience-led innovation practice (Goh).

## 1. Five-step redesign before AI

1. Question every requirement.
2. Delete every possible step.
3. Simplify and optimise.
4. Accelerate cycle time.
5. Automate last.

Do not recommend AI for a broken process. First ask:
- Which approval, report, handoff or data request exists only by habit?
- Which steps can be removed entirely?
- Which can be standardised before software touches them?
- Where is the true delay: people, handoffs, waiting or system friction?

Recommend workflow automation, copilots or AI agents only after these answers.

## 2. Product means the whole customer experience

Map any AI proposal across: demand generation and enquiry handling; buying or application friction; onboarding and first value; support quality and response time; repeat purchase, retention and referral. If AI lowers internal cost but harms trust, clarity or response quality, it is strategically weak.

## 3. Experience-led AI prompts

For each initiative ask:
- Which customer or employee pain point does it address?
- How does it support the vision and customer-experience ambition?
- What adoption, governance and literacy work is needed?
- Which ROI metric will prove it works?

The AI section should connect business strategy, customer-experience strategy, process redesign, adoption and literacy, and ROI measurement, not list tools.

## 4. Adoption and literacy minimum

- An owner or sponsor per use case.
- Staff training on safe, effective use.
- Clear limits for human review.
- A pilot period with success metrics.
- A feedback loop to refine or stop the use case.

## 5. Metrics

Cycle-time reduction; first-response time; error reduction; throughput increase; labour hours saved; conversion uplift; retention or repeat purchase; satisfaction or complaint reduction; payback period. Measure outcomes, not novelty.

## 6. Culture

- Urgency and accountability: named owners, explicit deadlines, regular reviews.
- Learning posture: pilots generate evidence, not publicity.
- Internal use first: use the system inside the business before making it customer-critical.

## 7. Uganda and East Africa application

- Begin with one narrow pilot tied to a painful workflow rather than a claim of full transformation.
- Strong first cases: WhatsApp handling, CRM follow-up, quoting, customer support, payment reminders, simple forecasting.
- Build training and supervision into the plan where teams are new to structured digital tools.
- State when a human takes over in any customer-facing AI, especially in credit, health and high-value B2B relationships.

Sources consulted: McNeill, J. (2026), *The Algorithm*; Goh, F. (2025), *Innovate to Elevate!*.
