# Pricing triangle, business-model pages and price-change P&L

Parent: [Products and Services](../SKILL.md).

When to read: when setting a price or rate card for sections 03 and 07 of a
business plan or a marketing plan, when testing a price change, or when
describing the business model in plain terms. Complements the fuller
[pricing strategy](pricing-strategy.md) reference and `meta-pricing-strategy`.
Method sources: Lin, L. C. (2013) *Decode and Conquer*, 2nd edn, Impact
Interview; Croll, A. and Yoskovitz, B. (2013) *Lean Analytics*, O'Reilly Media.
Figures are illustrative; tax and statutory costs go to the finance engine.

## 1. Pricing triangle

1. **Ceiling — the customer's best alternative.** What would the buyer do
   instead, and what does it cost them in total? For a new category, the cost
   of assembling a solution themselves; in an existing one, the competitor's
   price plus whatever the buyer must add for parity.
2. **Comparables.** Prices of offers the buyer actually compares, including
   informal and imported options.
3. **Floor — unit cost.** Variable cost plus a fair share of delivery cost,
   unless a deliberate loss-leader with a stated payback route.

Then choose a specific price with its strategic reason (penetration, parity,
premium), and a test plan. Surveys overstate willingness to pay; prefer a
bounded offer test, a deposit ask or a price-variant test.

Worked example (Kampala bookkeeping retainer): ceiling = a part-time in-house
bookkeeper's full monthly cost including statutory contributions (finance
engine to confirm) or the penalty exposure from late filing; comparables =
local firms' retainers; floor = hours × loaded hourly cost; strategy = an entry
package priced for SMEs in their first year of VAT registration, with tax
filing as the upgrade.

## 2. Billing unit and price signal

Match the billing unit to how buyers budget. A monthly subscription can
confuse a buyer who purchases occasionally; a per-job or per-season price may
sell better, and vice versa (Croll and Yoskovitz's job-posting example).
Price also signals quality: a price far below the comparables can undermine a
premium position.

## 3. Business-model pages

Describe the model in five interchangeable pages: **acquisition channel** (how
buyers find us), **selling tactic** (how visitors become buyers: trial, deposit,
bundle, time limit), **revenue source** (direct payment or indirect such as
commission, advertising or sponsorship), **product type** (the value) and
**delivery model** (how it reaches them). Freemium is a selling tactic, not a
model; SaaS is a delivery model. Turning one page at a time generates pivot
options for testing.

Revenue levers (Zyman's five, as cited by Croll and Yoskovitz): sell more
items, to more people, more often, for more money, more efficiently. Choose
the lever that fits the situation (repeat buyers → more often; high referral →
more people; heavy transaction cost → more efficiently).

## 4. Price-change P&L

Before cutting or raising a price, compute the net effect by customer group:

| Line | Formula | Example (gym, Ntinda) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue change on existing customers | Existing customers × price change | 600 × −UGX 100,000 = −UGX 60m |
| Revenue from new customers | New customers × new price | 120 × UGX 900,000 = +UGX 108m |
| Extra cost to serve new customers | New customers × incremental cost | −UGX 25m |
| Extra margin from behaviour change | e.g. added café or training margin | +UGX 10m |
| **Net effect** | Sum | **+UGX 33m** |

Then state the constraint that decides it (here, peak-hour capacity).

For a price rise, the share of volume that can be lost before gross profit
falls = price increase % ÷ (gross margin % + price increase %). With a 30%
gross margin and a 10% price rise, up to 10 ÷ 40 = 25% of volume can be lost
before gross profit falls.

## 5. Anti-patterns

- Cost-plus pricing with no view of the ceiling. Fix: triangle first.
- Copying a competitor's price without comparing offers. Fix: parity adjustments.
- Price cuts justified by volume alone. Fix: price-change P&L plus capacity check.
- Surveys treated as willingness to pay. Fix: behavioural price test.
