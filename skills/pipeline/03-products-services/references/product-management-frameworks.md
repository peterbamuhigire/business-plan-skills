# Product Management Toolkit for the Products and Services Section

Use this reference when Section 03 (Products and Services) and neighbouring sections must show how the product is owned, prioritised, tested, priced, roadmapped and scaled. It applies product-owner practice (Verwijs, Overeem and Lennartz) to business plans. The examples use placeholders; replace them with the plan's own verified figures.

## 1. Six stances and where they appear in the plan

| Stance | Focus | Plan section |
|---|---|---|
| Customer representative | Needs, personas, value | 05 Target Market, 03 Products and Services |
| Visionary | Vision, roadmap, goals | 02 Company Overview, 03 |
| Experimenter | Testing assumptions, innovation, pivots | 03 (R&D), 12 Risk Analysis |
| Decision maker | Prioritisation, trade-offs, accountability | 09 Management Team |
| Collaborator | Governance, budgeting, contracts | 10 Financial Projections, 11 Funding Request |
| Influencer | Stakeholder management, communication | 01 Executive Summary, 09 |
Use the table to check that someone in the team plays each stance.

## 2. Customer representative: value and feature logic

Four layers of customer value (after Bain's value work): functional (time saved, cost reduced, simplicity, integration); emotional (less anxiety, comfort, reward); life-changing (hope, self-actualisation, motivation); social impact (community benefit, sustainability, jobs). Map each feature to at least one layer; products working on several layers are stronger.
Outcome chain: company goal, then impact on the market, then customer outcome, then product feature. Work backwards, for example: goal (leading regional platform), impact (target number of users by year three), outcome (users earn a stated percentage more), features that produce the outcome (price transparency, quality grading, direct buyer connection). Show this chain for each planned feature in the R&D pipeline.

## 3. Visionary: product vision

Positioning statement (Moore's format): For [target customer] who [need or opportunity], [product name] is a [category] that [key benefit], unlike [primary alternative]; our product [differentiator]. Worked prompt for a food-processing product: for smallholders who lose part of the harvest after picking, a solar drying and packaging system that extends shelf life, unlike weather-dependent sun drying, and includes certification for export.
Hierarchy of statements:
| Level | Horizon | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| Mission | Ongoing | Why do we exist? |
| Vision | 5 to 10 years | Where are we going? |
| Product vision | 2 to 3 years | What will the product become? |
| Product goals | Quarterly | What are we achieving now? |

## 4. Roadmaps by audience

| Type | Best for | Detail | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal-oriented | Strategic alignment | Low, goals not features | Investors, board, executive summary |
| Now-next-later | Agile teams, start-ups | Medium, themes by timeframe | Team, advisers |
| User story map | Development planning | High, detailed features | Development team |
| Visual roadmap | External communication | Low, milestones and themes | Customers, partners |
| Gantt chart | Regulatory or construction work | High, dates and dependencies | Operations, implementation timeline |
Goal-oriented template: quarter or year; goal; key metric; status (development, planning, concept, vision). Sequence, for example: validate product-market fit with a paying-customer count; reach operational break-even (revenue covers variable costs); scale to the first market; expand to further districts; then regional expansion. Fill targets from the financial model.
Now-next-later: now (core features, small pilot, manual operations); next (integrations such as mobile money, wider pilot, semi-automation); later (advanced features such as AI recommendations, second market, full automation).

## 5. Experimenter: choosing tests

Innovation sources: inside-out (from internal capability), outside-in (from market needs), business-model (change how value is captured, for example one-off sale to subscription).
Truth curve: order experiments by cost and confidence and start cheap: desk research, customer interviews, surveys, landing page, concierge MVP, prototype, pilot, full launch. Invest more only when cheaper tests confirm the hypothesis.
| Technique | Cost | Time | Confidence | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desk research | Very low | Days | Low | First screening |
| Customer interviews | Low | 1 to 2 weeks | Medium | Understand the problem |
| Smoke test (landing page) | Low | About a week | Medium | Test demand |
| Concierge MVP | Medium | 2 to 4 weeks | Medium to high | Deliver the service manually |
| Wizard of Oz | Medium | 2 to 4 weeks | Medium to high | Simulate automation manually |
| A/B test | Medium | 2 to 4 weeks | High | Compare approaches |
| Pilot | High | 1 to 3 months | High | Full validation before scaling |
Hypothesis template: We believe [segment] will [behaviour] if we [change]; we will know it is true when [metric] reaches [threshold] within [timeframe].

## 6. Scaling strategies

| Strategy | Meaning | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Geographic expansion | Same product, new places | Product-market fit proven |
| Segment expansion | Same product, new customer types | Adjacent demand |
| Feature expansion | More capability for current customers | High retention, requests |
| Platform | Third parties build on the product | Strong user base, ecosystem potential |
| Vertical integration | Control more of the value chain | Supply chain bottleneck |
| Horizontal integration | Acquire complementary products | Consolidation opportunity |
| Licensing or franchising | Others replicate the model | Capital-light growth preferred |
| Partnership or distribution | Use others' reach | Cannot fund own distribution |

## 7. Three-step pricing check

1. Understand value to the customer.
2. Know the cost floor.
3. Check the market reference.
Alignment tests with red flags:
| Test | Question | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Does price reflect delivered value? | Price above perceived value |
| Cost | Does price cover all costs plus margin? | Gross margin below the level the plan needs (set the threshold from the model) |
| Competition | Is the price defensible? | Large premium with no clear differentiation |
| Segment | Can target customers afford it? | Price exceeds their budget |
| Scalability | Do unit economics improve with scale? | Costs rise faster than revenue |
Detailed methods: `pricing-strategy.md`.

## 8. Key value areas for product metrics

| Area | Measures | Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Current value | Value delivered today | Revenue, satisfaction, usage frequency |
| Unrealised value | Value not yet captured | Market-share gap, unserved segments, feature requests |
| Time to market | Speed of delivering new value | Release frequency, idea-to-launch time |
| Ability to innovate | Capacity to create future value | R&D share of spend, technical-debt ratio, team capability |
Include current and unrealised value in the financial projections; put ability to innovate in the management section.

## 9. Plan-section prompts

- Who plays each product stance in the team?
- What is the product vision statement and goal roadmap?
- Which experiments will run first, at what cost?
- Which scaling strategy fits the stage?
- Which key value metrics will be reported?
