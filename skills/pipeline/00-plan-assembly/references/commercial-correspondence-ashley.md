# Commercial Correspondence Standards

Use when drafting any business communication within or around a plan: covering letters, supplier letters, HR letters, banking applications and trade documents.

## 1. Letter structure (block style)
Block style (every line at the left margin, no indentation) is the widely used international format.
Components in order: sender's address (address only, no name); date (month in full, for example 15 March 2026, never figures only); inside address (recipient, title, building, street, town and postcode, country); attention line when addressed to a department; reference numbers above the salutation; salutation; subject line (bold or underlined) between salutation and body; body in blocked paragraphs; complimentary close; signature block (signature, typed name, job title); enclosures listed below.
| Situation | Salutation | Close |
|---|---|---|
| Name and gender unknown | Dear Sir/Madam | Yours faithfully |
| Company addressed, name unknown | Dear Sirs | Yours faithfully |
| Surname known | Dear Mr/Mrs/Ms/Dr Surname | Yours sincerely |
| Close colleague | Dear First name | Best wishes |
| US formal | Gentlemen: | Yours truly |
Rules: use Ms when marital status is unknown; "Sir" (a knight's title) differs from "Dear Sir"; "Messrs" is old-fashioned except within a company name; be consistent on commas after salutation and close. Courtesy titles: Mr, Mrs, Miss, Ms; do not abbreviate Dr, Prof or Capt in the salutation; "Esq." is rarely used.

## 2. Email and fax
- Email: salutation and close optional internally, included externally for formal messages; sending "Thank you" as a close is acceptable when less formal; send invoices and contracts as attachments; use a signature block (name, title, company, contact details).
- Fax: To, From, Date, Subject, number of pages, reference; mark URGENT or CONFIDENTIAL when needed; same standards as a letter.

## 3. Content and style
- Plan before writing: list every required point, check completeness, then sequence. Length should cover all points while respecting the reader's time.
- Sequence: first paragraph acknowledges or states purpose; middle paragraphs give key points in order, one idea each; final paragraph states next steps and invites a response.
- Standard reply template: acknowledge the enquiry, order or complaint with date and reference; answer each point; draw attention to key items (products, terms, conditions); state guarantees, delivery and payment terms; invite further contact.
- Tone polite but direct; avoid archaic phrases ("We beg to remain", "Respectfully yours"); use impersonal wording to avoid blame ("There appears to be an error").

## 4. Enquiries, replies and quotations
- Enquiry openings: ask for a catalogue and price list quoting the price basis and destination; ask whether cash discounts are allowed; ask for a prompt reply quoting trade prices.
- Incoterms: state the rule, the named place and the edition in force (verify the current edition, at present Incoterms 2020). EXW: buyer collects at seller's premises. FCA: seller delivers to the buyer's carrier, export cleared. FAS: alongside the ship at the port. FOB: on board the ship. CFR: cost and freight to the destination port. CIF: cost, insurance and freight to the destination port. CPT and CIP: carriage (and insurance) paid for any transport mode. DAP, DPU and DDP: delivery to a named destination with progressively more seller responsibility (DDP includes import duty). Older terms (DAF, DDU) are superseded. Always name the port or place, for example CIF Mombasa.
- Discounts: trade (same-sector buyers), quantity (above a threshold), cash (early payment, for example 3/10 net 30 means 3 per cent if paid in 10 days), loyalty (long-term customers).
- Quotation reply structure: thank and reference; enclose catalogue or price list with price basis and discount terms; draw attention to relevant products; state guarantee period; give delivery dates; invite further contact.

## 5. Orders and dispatch
- Order covering letter: order number and enclosed order form; accepted discount and payment terms; substitution policy (for example, do not send alternatives if out of stock); target delivery date; banking details for shipping documents.
- Packing instructions: packing material, marking of crates, handling of fragile or moving parts.
- Advice of dispatch: order number, vessel or flight, departure and arrival dates, and where shipping documents were sent (usually the buyer's bank).

## 6. Payment methods and documents
| Method | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cheque | Domestic, small | Slow across borders |
| Bank draft | Domestic, large | Bank fee; internal signature rules |
| Bank transfer (SWIFT) | International, preferred | Fast; both parties notified |
| Bill of exchange | Trade | See below |
| Letter of credit | High-value trade | Safest for both parties |
| Cash on delivery | Domestic, parcel | Collected on delivery |
| Open account or monthly statement | Trusted buyers | Credit facility (section 8) |
- Bill of exchange: drawer (seller or exporter) writes it; drawee (buyer) accepts and pays. Sight draft: payable on presentation. Term draft: payable a set number of days after sight. Acceptance (drawee signs) creates the legal obligation. Discounting: seller sells an accepted bill to a bank for immediate cash at a discount. Endorsing transfers it. Inland bills have no shipping documents; documentary bills carry invoice, bill of lading and insurance certificate.
- Letter of credit: issued by the buyer's bank to the seller and guarantees payment if conditions are met; documentary credit pays on presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading and insurance certificate; irrevocable credits (standard) cannot be cancelled without the seller's consent.
- Invoice contents: number and date; buyer's order number; description, quantity, unit price, total; Incoterm price; discount; tax on a separate line if applicable; "E. & O.E." (errors and omissions excepted). Tax treatment: verify with the Chwezi finance engine.
- Statement of account: monthly summary of invoices, debit notes, credit notes and payments with opening, running and closing balances; E. & O.E.

## 7. Complaints and adjustments
- Making a complaint: act promptly; do not apologise for writing; be specific (order number, date, exact fault); suggest a solution (credit note, replacement, collection); use impersonal wording; never use blame language. Openings: "I am writing to complain about Order No. X received on [date]"; "There appears to be an error on Invoice No. X".
- Replying: acknowledge at once and thank; apologise if justified; explain what went wrong without blaming staff; resolve (replacement, credit note, debit note, refund); confirm that such incidents are rare; for an unjustified complaint, state your case politely with evidence and decline adjustment if warranted.
- Debit note: increases the buyer's balance (under-invoicing, return of a credit). Credit note: reduces it (returned goods, overcharge, compensation).

## 8. Credit and open account
- Requesting open account: ask while placing an order, cite the length of the relationship, state preferred terms.
- Granting credit: check trading history; state terms clearly (net 30 days, or a cash discount for payment within a set time, which may suit small customers better); long-standing customers need no references; new customers give two trade references and a bank reference.
- Credit status enquiries: mark strictly private and confidential; replies should be tactful, stating facts without strong opinions.

## 9. Banking correspondence
- Request for an overdraft or loan: (1) purpose (expansion, equipment, working capital); (2) amount; (3) evidence of need (confirmed orders, growth data); (4) proposed security; (5) repayment timeline; (6) request a meeting.
- Bank's approval letter includes: amount, form (overdraft or term loan), interest rate and basis, repayment date, security, guarantor requirement, next steps (sign and return by a date).
- Declining a loan: state regret early, give a brief reason (credit climate, existing overdraft, sector risk), suggest alternatives (development finance institution, equity), close positively.
- Standing order request: account number, amount, recipient and bank, first payment date, frequency. Cancelling a cheque: number, amount, payee, reason.

## 10. Agency correspondence
- Offer to act as agent: describe your company, coverage and contacts; explain why the market suits the products; propose commission terms; state the type of agency (sole, del credere, general).
- Del credere agent: guarantees customers' debts to the principal and earns an additional commission for that risk.
- Principal's response: settle who receives payment (principal direct, or agent who remits); who holds stock; who funds local advertising; dispute resolution jurisdiction or arbitration body; contract length.
- Agent's quarterly report: market overview, sales performance, advertising spend, pricing and competitive issues, recommendations.

## 11. Memos and reports
- Memo header: To, From, Date, Subject; one topic per memo; concise, impersonal and formal; used for internal communication, policy changes, meeting requests and announcements.
- Regular report: title, introduction, main findings, conclusions, recommendations. Special report (investigation, feasibility, research): title, summary (self-contained, one page at most), introduction, methodology, findings with headings and subheadings, conclusions, recommendations. Use numbered headings for long reports (1, 2.1, 3.1.1). The summary states why the report was written, main findings, conclusions and recommendations.
- Sources: primary (interviews, surveys, questionnaires, observation) and secondary (books, publications, newspapers, databases).

## 12. Personnel correspondence
- Job advertisement: abbreviated small-ad form or full wording; state post, terms and how to apply.
- Application letter: opening (how you heard of the vacancy and the post title); background (specific, quantified education and experience); why this employer; closing (offer more information, availability). Unsolicited application: name who recommended you and ask about vacancies in a specific department. Never criticise a former employer; acceptable reasons for leaving include a new opportunity to use particular skills, a role with more responsibility, or voluntary redundancy during restructuring.
- CV: personal details, education (most recent first), work experience (most recent first), skills, two referees (one professional, one academic). Covering letter: the job and source; why you are suitable, linking the CV to requirements; availability and invitation to interview.
- Replying to applicants: invitation to interview (date, time, place, confirm attendance by a date); decline with thanks and good wishes (no obligation to give reasons).
- Offer of post: post and start date; salary and increments; terms (hours, leave, probation); written acceptance by a date; contract attached or to follow. Acceptance: state the post, starting salary and start date. Verify statutory employment terms with local labour law and the Chwezi finance engine for payroll.

## 14. East African notes
- Block style with open punctuation in addresses matches current practice; banks and development finance institutions expect professional block-style letters.
- "Dear Sir/Madam" suits a credit or branch manager addressed by title; use "Dear Mr/Ms Surname" and "Yours sincerely" for a named contact.
- Payment terms seen in practice (verify locally): cash on delivery or prepayment in most retail; thirty to sixty days open account for established suppliers; SWIFT transfers for imports; mobile money for small domestic payments (functionally equivalent to bank transfer); letters of credit for larger imports through major banks, with thresholds set by the bank.
- Bills of exchange are less common domestically and used mainly in larger trade contracts; central-bank foreign-exchange rules and approval thresholds apply (verify current requirements).
- Incoterms for regional imports: CIF Mombasa or Dar es Salaam leaves inland transport to the buyer; DDP to an inland city puts all costs including customs duty on the seller (uncommon); EXW is common in regional trade.
- Complaints: relationship maintenance matters, so word complaints even more diplomatically ("Given our long trading partnership, we are confident this was an oversight").
- Credit: savings and credit cooperatives and microfinance lenders rarely grant open account; post-dated cheques are used as an informal credit mechanism and should be acknowledged in the payment-terms section of the operations plan.
- Memos are standard in formally registered businesses; messaging-app groups often replace memos in micro-businesses, which the operations plan should note.
