# SME Operating Discipline Checklist

Small businesses fail more often from weak controls, poor cash discipline, bad hiring, unclear partnership terms, poor customer experience, and inconsistent marketing than from missing strategy vocabulary. This checklist keeps plans grounded in operating reality. It paraphrases practical guidance from *Small Business 911* and the April 2020 issue of *Start Your Own Business* magazine (OCR-derived sampling, used for corroboration only; confirm publication details before citing).

## 1. Records and visibility

- [ ] Detailed records kept for sales, purchases, stock movement, labour use, and cash.
- [ ] Accounting software (or a disciplined digital ledger) replaces memory and paper where volume justifies it.
- [ ] The owner can name the main profit drivers from the records.

## 2. Customer experience

- [ ] The plan shows how service quality and a smooth experience are delivered, not only price.
- [ ] After-sales contact and complaint handling have an owner.
- [ ] Poor experience is treated as a margin risk.

## 3. Growth with systems

- [ ] Every proposed expansion (new outlet, headcount, product line) has matching procedures, role definitions, and controls.
- [ ] Owner dependence is named as a scaling risk, with a delegation plan.
- [ ] Logistics, staffing, financing, and quality effects of growth are shown.

## 4. Partnerships and shared ownership

- [ ] Partnership terms are in writing.
- [ ] Roles, expectations, and ethical standards are defined.
- [ ] The profit-and-loss model is built before ownership shares or repayment terms are set.

## 5. Marketing basics

- [ ] Target market understood and written down.
- [ ] Branding is consistent across signage, packaging, messaging, and online presence.
- [ ] Social channels are used on a planned rhythm, not sporadically.
- [ ] A usable website exists where customers search online.
- [ ] Marketing ideas are tested and the results recorded.

These basics are an operating floor. For conversion design, use the Section 07 references `direct-response-commercial-system.md` and `direct-response-selling-playbook.md`.

## 6. Leakage and process discipline

- [ ] Shrinkage and internal leakage are named risks with controls.
- [ ] Stock checks and cash procedures are scheduled (daily cash-up, periodic counts).
- [ ] Surveillance footage or system logs are actually reviewed; unreviewed surveillance is weak control.
- [ ] Process discipline is prioritised over optimistic sales targets.

## 7. Proportionate digital adoption

- [ ] Digital tools recommended for early-stage firms are simple to adopt: mobile money, messaging-app ordering, a simple website, lightweight accounting.
- [ ] Complex stacks are deferred until volume and skills justify them.

## Blocking conditions

Reject or qualify a plan that scales headcount or outlets without systems and controls, hides owner-bottleneck risk, omits governance for shared ownership, or recommends digital tools the team cannot run.
