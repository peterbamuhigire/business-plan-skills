# Business and Competitive Analysis Methods: Selector and Reference

Use to choose the right analytical method for a plan section, justify the choice to a reader, and adapt it to low-data East African markets.

## 1. Choosing a method: the FAROUT test
Rate each candidate method Low, Medium or High on six criteria: Future orientation (does it look forward?), Accuracy, Resource efficiency (is the insight worth the collection cost?), Objectivity (freedom from bias), Usefulness (actionable for the decision maker) and Timeliness (results before the decision window closes). When combining methods (triangulation), show how one method's weakness is covered by another's strength. (FAROUT: Fleisher and Bensoussan.)

## 2. Strategic and industry methods
| Method | Use when | Outputs | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nine forces industry analysis (Porter's five forces plus government, social, technology, international) | Start of any plan: is the industry structure attractive? | Force ratings, weighted attractiveness, forces to monitor | Weighting is judgemental; static snapshot |
| Competitive positioning | Defining go-to-market position or after a rival repositions | Perceptual map, build/maintain/defend/withdraw options | Subjective axes; oversimplification |
| Business model analysis (eight steps: value proposition, target segment, competitors, value chain and cost model, value network, revenue model, critical success factors, analysis grid) | Assessing viability, auditing a rival, stress-testing a pivot | Grid per competitor, revenue model comparison, value chain map | Private cost data scarce; outside disruptors missed |
| SERVO (strategy, environment, resources, values, organisation) | Underperformance despite sound strategy | Alignment map, gaps, options | Qualitative; can become a laundry list |
| McKinsey 7S (strategy, structure, systems, shared values, style, staff, skills) | Capability gaps threatening execution; acquisition targets | Alignment matrix, most misaligned element, change priorities | No competitive dimension; diagnosis stronger than prescription |
| Critical success factors (identified via industry analysis, competitor intelligence, experts, leaders, temporal and company-specific factors, environmental scanning, judgement) | Defining what the business must do exceptionally well | Three to seven ranked CSFs, weights, gap versus best in class | CSFs change; hindsight bias; inputs confused with outcomes |
| Supply chain analysis (SCOR reference model) | Sourcing risk, cost advantage | Supply chain map, vulnerability register, cost per link | Tier-2 and tier-3 data rarely available |
| Strategic relationship analysis | Partnership planning; rival's hidden relationship capital | Network diagram, readiness assessment, rival vulnerabilities | Relationship quality hard to see |

## 3. Competitor intelligence methods
| Method | Use when | Outputs | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitor profiling (future goals, current strategy, assumptions, capabilities) | Each significant competitor | Profile, threat level, predicted next move | Scarce private data; wrong motive assumptions |
| Shadowing (teams act as named competitors) | Before launch, entry or pricing decisions | Shadow market plan, predicted responses, blind spots | Costly; over-optimism |
| Win/loss analysis (structured interviews after won and lost deals) | Falling win rate; new segment; new rival | Win/loss ratio by segment, ranked buyer criteria, competitor strengths | Candour of buyers; survivor bias; needs neutral interviewer |
| Corporate reputation analysis | Brand or trust as differentiator; crisis; acquisition | Reputation score, stakeholder map, risk register | Instrument reliability varies |
| Product line analysis (life-cycle stage per product) | Portfolio rationalisation | Product-by-stage map, invest/harvest/exit, gaps versus rivals | Contested stage judgement |
| Benchmarking (internal, competitive, functional, generic) | Setting targets; showing management knows the standard | Baseline, best practice, gap table, actions | Copycat syndrome; context differences; data sharing |
| Event and timeline analysis | Profiling a rival or target | Annotated timeline, patterns, predicted events | Sparse records; inferred causation |

## 4. Financial and quantitative methods
- Competitor cash-flow analysis: estimate a rival's capacity to fund a price war, respond to entry or invest; outputs are operating and free cash flow estimates, debt service burden and investment capacity. Limits: no published accounts for most private and African firms; proxies add uncertainty; lagging indicator.
- Statistical interpretation: percentage change, percentiles, central tendency, standard deviation, regression, correlation and significance. Give confidence ranges and separate correlation from causation; misapplication of tests is common.

## 5. Environmental and futures methods
- Country risk analysis: six dimensions (economic, transfer, exchange, location or neighbourhood, sovereign, political). Use for any export or regional expansion; ratings from commercial providers can lag events. Combine with the relevant country-context skill.
- Driving forces analysis: rank the technological, economic, social, regulatory, competitive and demographic forces changing the industry over the three-to-five-year plan horizon, with direction, magnitude and monitoring triggers.
- Technology forecasting: Delphi consensus, trend extrapolation, S-curves, historical analogy, scenarios, morphological analysis, relevance trees, technology monitoring. Long-range forecasts perform poorly; keep to plan horizon.
- War gaming (six steps: assess fit, launch, assemble teams, prepare, play, debrief): role-play competitors, customers and the firm; produces response playbooks and early-warning indicators. Depends on facilitation and honest role play.
- Indications and warning: build indicator sets, assign meaning, validate and rank against thresholds, define responses; produces an early-warning dashboard for risk and monitoring plans. Watch for indicator sprawl and false positives.
- Historiographical analysis: reconstruct a competitor's or industry's history from validated sources to find strategic patterns; do not treat the past as deterministic.

## 6. Analytical process methods
- Analysis of competing hypotheses (eight steps: identify hypotheses, list evidence, build hypothesis-by-evidence matrix, refine, draw tentative conclusions, sensitivity analysis, report, set future milestones): use when several interpretations are plausible and error is costly; guard against confirmation bias.
- Linchpin analysis: name the single assumption on which the conclusion rests, test sensitivity, assess risk if it fails and monitor it. Use as the last check before presenting; ask what one assumption, if wrong, would invalidate the plan.

## 9. Uganda and East Africa adaptations
- Data scarcity: public filings and association reports are thin. Profile competitors through visits, job advertisements, social media, supplier conversations and customer interviews (open-source intelligence replaces desk research). Use casual, trust-based debriefs for win/loss because buyers rarely explain decisions candidly in formal settings. Benchmark against regional peers (Kenya, Rwanda, Tanzania) using industry associations and development-institution or statistics-bureau data.
- Informal competition: include the informal sector as a force in nine-forces analysis; informals compete on low overhead, not efficiency; test whether formalisation is a mobility barrier. Driving forces specific to Uganda include formalisation, mobile money adoption and national identity and registration requirements.
- Country risk items to verify with dated sources: tax-invoicing system compliance penalties, exchange-rate depreciation assumptions, and import levies or sector regulation.
- Relationship intelligence: the "community knowledge layer" (suppliers, distributors, former staff, customers) is the richest source; adapt relationship and reputation analysis to social-network mapping.
- Mobile-first research: messaging broadcast lists, community groups, mobile data-collection tools (KoboToolbox, ODK) and map-listing reviews make primary research feasible.
- Regional expansion: combine country-risk analysis with the country-context skill for regulation and finance; free movement rules for community nationals are set by regional treaty (verify).
