# SaaS Exit Strategy & Strategic Optionality Reference

## 1. The Exit-Path Map

| Path | Typical valuation | When realistic | Founder economics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategic acquisition** | 4-10× ARR | $5M-$500M ARR | Full liquidity event |
| **PE rollup** | 3-7× ARR | $10M-$200M ARR; mature | Liquidity + earnout |
| **PE growth equity (partial)** | 8-15× ARR | $20M+ ARR | Partial liquidity + continued operating |
| **IPO** | 5-15× ARR | $100M+ ARR | Liquidity vests over 6-12 months |
| **Secondary** | Round price | Series B+ | Partial founder liquidity; company continues |
| **Founder dividend** | n/a | Profitable bootstrap | Annual cash distribution; equity preserved |
| **Hold forever** | n/a | Sustainable profit; aligned shareholders | Long-term ownership |

## 2. Strategic-Buyer Categories

### C. Tech conglomerates — they have M&A appetite
US/EU: Microsoft, Salesforce, Workday, ServiceNow, Atlassian, Adobe.

### D. Platform plays — strategic acqui-hires
Cloud providers (AWS, Google, Microsoft) buying capability.

## 3. Optionality-Preserving Moves (the boring discipline)

Do today to preserve maximum exit optionality later:

- **Clean cap table** — avoid dirty preferences, complex conversion ratios, super-voting structures
- **Standard preferred shares** — 1× non-participating liquidation preference is the norm; reject 1.25× or participating
- **No exclusive partnerships** — exclusivity with one strategic kills optionality with their competitors
- **Audit-ready books** — clean GAAP/IFRS financials saved deal-time later
- **IP register** — every contribution assigned (especially open-source contributions)
- **Customer contracts** with assignment rights (no "change of control" prohibition clauses without negotiation)
- **Build relationships with target acquirers** 18-36 months before you need to sell (Cohen: "the deal is built on the relationship")
- **Maintain a data room** continuously (used for fundraising; ready for M&A)
- **Geographic diversification** — single-country revenue limits buyers; multi-country opens more
- **Multi-channel revenue** — channel concentration discounts acquisition multiples

## 4. The Exit-Economics Waterfall

Model what each shareholder receives at multiple exit values:

```
                            $20M exit    $50M exit    $100M exit    $200M exit
Gross sale proceeds          $20M         $50M         $100M         $200M
- Investment banker fee 3-5% -$0.8M       -$2M         -$4M          -$8M
- Legal / transaction fee    -$0.4M       -$1M         -$2M          -$4M
- Working capital adjustment -$1M         -$2M         -$5M          -$10M
= Net to be distributed       $17.8M      $45M         $89M          $178M

Distribution (waterfall by preference):
- Debt repayment             $X          $X           $X            $X
- Series B liquidation pref  $X          $X           $X            $X
- Series A liquidation pref  $X          $X           $X            $X
- Common (founders + ESOP)   $remaining  $remaining   $remaining    $remaining

Founder take-home (each)     $___        $___         $___          $___
ESOP holders take-home       $___        $___         $___          $___
```

This waterfall surfaces the **liquidation preference cliff** — at exit values below the preference stack, founders get little / nothing.

## 5. The Founder-Investor Exit Conflict

Founders and investors often want different exits:
- **VCs**: want $100M+ exits because the math of VC fund returns requires outliers
- **DFI / patient capital**: accept $20-100M exits; care about impact + return
- **Founders**: may prefer $30-100M life-changing exits over riskier $300M moonshots
- **Early angels**: usually accept any exit at 5-10× their investment

Address this BEFORE the conflict appears. Plan dialogues with the board annually. Consider:
- Secondary at Series B (founder partial liquidity reduces pressure for moonshot)
- Drag-along carve-outs (founder veto under X exit value)
- Liquidation-preference negotiation at each round (1× non-participating only)

## 6. IPO Reality in African Markets

- **JSE (Johannesburg)** — most liquid African exchange; some tech listings (Naspers, Capitec, Bytes). Threshold typically $100M+ revenue.
- **NSE (Nigeria)** — fintech / consumer-fintech listings possible (Interswitch IPO discussion); thin tech.
- **GHX (Ghana)** — very thin.
- **NSE (Nairobi)** — Equity Bank listed but tech is thin.
- **EGX (Egypt)** — MNT-Halan-class possible.
- **Dual-listing** with Frankfurt / London / Nasdaq is theoretically possible but adds compliance cost.

IPO is rarely the realistic exit for African SaaS at <$100M ARR. Strategic acquisition is the default.

## 8. Africa / Uganda Application Notes

- **Strategic acquisitions are the dominant exit** — IPO is rare; plan accordingly.
- **Cross-border M&A complexity** — currency controls, capital-gains tax, regulatory approval. Plan 12-18 months for deal close vs 3-6 in US.
- **Tax structuring**: most African founders use Mauritius / Cayman / Delaware holding companies for tax-efficient exit. Set up early.
- **Diaspora founders** have additional optionality (US/UK tax residency may allow more efficient exit; also creates compliance burden).
- **Patient-capital DFIs** as shareholders are easier to exit (don't push for moonshot); structure governance accordingly.
- **Local strategic buyers** (telcos, banks) often pay lower multiples but provide certainty; weigh against global strategic at higher multiple but longer deal cycle.
- **Secondary market is thin** — secondary sales require negotiation with the company; build into shareholders' agreements early.
- **Asset-vs-share deal** matters in African contexts (transfer-tax, regulatory approvals); structure with this in mind.
