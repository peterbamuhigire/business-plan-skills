# Reasoning and Achievability Gate

Use this reference to make business plans logically sound, commercially convincing, and achievable. It distils critical-thinking, essential-question, logic, problem-solving, mental-model, design-thinking, and strategic-thinking material into a business-plan review workflow.

## 1. Essential Questions

| Area | Ask |
|---|---|
| Decision | What must the reader decide: lend, invest, grant, partner, approve, or operate? |
| Real problem | What customer, market, operational, or financing problem does the business solve? |
| Customer | Who buys, who uses, who pays, and who influences the purchase? |
| Evidence | What proves demand, ability to serve, price acceptance, cost realism, and execution capacity? |
| Assumptions | Which claims are not yet proven but drive the model? |
| Alternatives | What substitutes, competitors, informal options, or do-nothing choices exist? |
| Constraints | What licences, cash, skills, equipment, land, supplier, logistics, tax, technology, or governance constraints matter? |
| Downside | What breaks the plan if sales lag, input costs rise, exchange rates move, approvals delay, or founder capacity is stretched? |
| Implementation | Who does what, by when, using what resources, and with what dependency? |
| Ethics and sustainability | Who is harmed, excluded, exposed to risk, or responsible for long-term operation? |

## 2. Business Case Argument Map

Use for the executive summary, market analysis, operations, financials, funding request, and risk section.

| Plan claim | Evidence | Warrant | Assumption | Countercase | Plan implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The conclusion the reader must accept | Source, data, interview, quote, benchmark, record, or model | Why this evidence supports the claim | What must be true | Strongest rival explanation or failure mode | What changes in the plan |

Weak evidence should lead to one of three actions: gather proof, narrow the claim, or label the assumption.

## 3. Mental-Model Review

| Model | Business-plan test |
|---|---|
| First principles | What must be true for this business to create and capture value? |
| Map versus territory | Does the model simplify away market informality, regulation, logistics, culture, seasonality, or cash constraints? |
| Circle of competence | Are founders, advisers, or writers making claims outside proven competence? |
| Base rates | How do comparable businesses normally perform on margins, ramp-up, failure, churn, utilisation, and repayment? |
| Inversion | What would make the plan fail within 12 months? |
| Second-order effects | What happens after expansion, price cuts, hiring, credit sales, automation, or new branches? |
| Incentives | Why will customers, staff, suppliers, lenders, partners, and regulators behave as assumed? |
| Opportunity cost | What capital, time, or strategic option is displaced by this plan? |
| Margin of safety | What buffer exists for cost overruns, working-capital gaps, delays, and demand shortfalls? |
| Survivorship bias | Are success stories being used without failed comparable businesses? |

## 4. Design-Thinking Pass

Use when the plan proposes a product, service, technology, process, programme, or customer experience.

1. Identify the real user, buyer, payer, operator, and affected stakeholder.
2. Define the job to be done and the current workaround.
3. Test whether the problem is urgent enough to pay for or adopt.
4. Name the riskiest assumption and the smallest credible validation test.
5. Check adoption friction: trust, price, distance, literacy, payment method, onboarding, service reliability, and after-sales support.
6. Translate the result into product design, operations, marketing, and financial assumptions.

## 5. Strategic Logic

| Discipline | Business-plan application |
|---|---|
| Pattern recognition | Identify whether apparent demand is a trend, cycle, one-off spike, policy effect, or noise. |
| Systems analysis | Map suppliers, customers, regulation, labour, finance, infrastructure, and feedback loops. |
| Mental agility | Re-run the case under opposite assumptions: lower price, slower sales, stronger competitor, delayed licence. |
| Structured problem-solving | Build a MECE issue tree for market, model, operations, finance, team, and risk. |
| Visioning | State a future position that is specific, staged, and economically plausible. |
| Political savvy | Identify approvals, informal power, community acceptance, lender criteria, and stakeholder incentives. |

## 6. Business-Sense Gate

### Market Logic

- Target segment is specific enough to reach.
- Need is backed by customer behaviour, not only demographic size.
- Competition and substitutes are named honestly.
- Regulation, location, seasonality, purchasing power, and distribution are considered.

### Revenue Logic

- Revenue is driver-based: customers, units, utilisation, frequency, price, retention, and channel.
- Sales ramp follows marketing capacity, sales cycle, operating capacity, and working capital.
- Credit sales, bad debts, discounts, returns, and seasonality are handled where relevant.

### Cost and Cash Logic

- Fixed costs, variable costs, payroll, tax, rent, utilities, maintenance, transport, inventory, and financing costs are explicit.
- Gross margin is plausible for the industry and route to market.
- Cash-flow timing matches inventory, receivables, payables, tax, loan repayment, and capex timing.

### Operating Logic

- The business can produce, source, store, deliver, sell, collect, record, and report at the projected volume.
- Staffing, equipment, premises, systems, licences, and suppliers fit the plan.
- Quality control and customer service are operationally designed, not promised.

### Funding Logic

- Funding ask equals the real use of funds.
- Debt plan shows repayment source, DSCR, collateral or compensating factors, and downside resilience.
- Equity plan shows scale, return logic, valuation basis, governance, and exit or liquidity logic.
- Grant plan shows additionality, impact, sustainability, and reporting capacity.

## 7. Achievability Gate

Do not proceed to final synthesis until these are true:

- [ ] Executive summary matches the final model, funding ask, risks, and implementation path.
- [ ] Market claims support revenue assumptions.
- [ ] Operating capacity supports sales volume.
- [ ] Team capability supports the plan's complexity.
- [ ] Funding ask supports the implementation sequence.
- [ ] Risk mitigations are operational, costed where necessary, and assigned to owners.
- [ ] Financial projections reconcile with pricing, costs, staffing, capex, tax, and working capital.
- [ ] Assumptions are explicit and the riskiest ones have validation actions.
- [ ] The plan remains credible under a pessimistic scenario.

## 8. Fix Severity

Classify findings so the next action is clear:

| Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fatal gap | The plan should not be presented until fixed. |
| Major weakness | Fundability or credibility is materially reduced. |
| Evidence gap | Claim may be true but proof is missing or weak. |
| Modelling gap | Numbers do not yet reflect the business logic. |
| Wording issue | Logic is sound but communication needs tightening. |
