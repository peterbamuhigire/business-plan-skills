# SaaS Funding Stage Playbook

## 1. The Stage Ladder

| Stage | Typical ARR | Typical raise | Pre-money (US) | Pre-money (Africa) | Plan profile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bootstrap | $0-$100k | $0 (founder cash) | n/a | n/a | Lean MVP, founder-led sales |
| Pre-seed | $0-$200k | $50k-$500k | $2-5M | $1-3M | Validating PMF |
| Seed | $200k-$1M | $500k-$3M | $5-15M | $3-10M | Building first repeatable channel |
| Series A | $1-5M | $3-15M | $20-60M | $10-30M | Scaling first channel |
| Series B | $5-20M | $15-50M | $60-200M | $30-100M | Multi-channel, multi-region |
| Series C+ | $20M+ | $50M+ | $200M+ | $100M+ | Market leader / international expansion |

## 2. Capital Sources by Stage — US Default

| Stage | Typical investors |
|---|---|
| Bootstrap | Founder savings, side-income, customer prepayment |
| Pre-seed | F&F, angels, micro-VCs, accelerators (YC, Techstars, 500 Startups) |
| Seed | Seed VCs (First Round, USV, Cowboy, Initialized), TinySeed, micro-VCs |
| Series A | Series A VCs (Bessemer, Insight, Sequoia, A16z, Index) |
| Series B+ | Growth VCs (Tiger, Coatue, Insight, Iconiq, General Atlantic) |
| Late stage | Hedge funds, crossover funds, sovereign wealth |
| Exit | Strategic acquirers, PE rollups, IPO, SPAC |

## 3. Capital Sources by Stage — African Stack (different)

| Stage | Typical investors |
|---|---|
| Bootstrap | Founder savings, side-income, customer prepayment, family |
| Pre-seed | F&F, individual angels, micro-funds (Microtraction, Ventures Platform Fund, Antler Africa, Future Africa, MEST Africa) |
| Seed | African seed funds (TLcom Stage 1, Partech Africa, Norrsken Africa, P1 Ventures, Catalyst Fund, Renew Capital), accelerators (YC, Techstars, MEST, MEST, Founders Factory Africa, Antler, Plug & Play) |
| Pre-A / A | Africa-focused VCs (TLcom, Partech, Novastar, 4DX), DFIs (IFC Venture Capital, FMO Ventures, Norfund, BII Africa, Proparco Digital Africa) |
| Growth | DFIs (BII growth, FMO Massif, AfDB Boost Africa), patient capital (Acumen, Omidyar Network), international growth funds (Insight Partners select Africa) |
| Strategic / exit | Local telcos (Safaricom Spark, MTN, Liquid), regional banks (Standard Bank, Ecobank), global strategics (Visa, Mastercard, Stripe for fintech, Google for Startups Africa), PE (Helios, Development Partners International) |

## 4. What the Plan Needs at Each Stage

### Pre-seed
- Founder bios + commitment
- Problem narrative + customer interviews (20+)
- MVP demo or prototype
- Light financial model (1-2 years)
- Customer pipeline list (named prospects)
- Round terms simple (SAFE, convertible note, or small priced round)

### Seed
- ARR with growth signal ($100k+, 2-3× YoY)
- Some PMF evidence (Sean Ellis 40%; retention curve flattening)
- Repeatable channel signal (one channel showing payback <18 months)
- Unit economics — even if early, LTV:CAC directional
- Team beyond founders (first hires)
- 18-24 month plan with milestone-based budget
- Priced seed round preferred over uncapped SAFEs at this size

### Series A
- $1M+ ARR (US benchmark, slightly lower in Africa)
- 2-3× YoY growth
- Healthy unit economics (LTV:CAC 3+, payback <18-24 months)
- Scalable channel demonstrated
- Repeatable sales motion (not founder-only)
- 3-year plan with quarterly milestones
- Strong leadership team (CTO, Head of GTM at minimum)
- Data room ready
- Audited or accountant-prepared financials

### Series B+
- $5-10M+ ARR
- Growth + Rule of 40 ≥ 30
- Multiple channels working
- International expansion thesis (for Africa: regional play)
- Strong operating cadence (MBR, QBR, board pack)
- Cap table clean
- Full audit, security (SOC2 / ISO27001)

## 5. Typical Term Sheet Expectations by Stage

| Stage | Typical terms |
|---|---|
| Pre-seed (SAFE) | Cap $3-5M; no liquidation preference; pro-rata for major investors |
| Seed (priced) | 1× non-participating liquidation pref; weighted average anti-dilution; ESOP 10-15%; 1-2 investor board seats; standard protective provisions |
| Series A | 1× non-participating liq pref; broad-based weighted average; ESOP 12-15% post-money; 1-2 investor seats; standard protective provisions |
| Series B+ | Similar to A; growth funds may push for 1.25-1.5× liq pref (push back); board composition rebalances |

African terms: DFIs may have additional ESG covenants, reporting obligations, MFN clauses. Patient capital may accept lower IRR (15-20%) but want governance.

## 6. Dilution Modelling

Per round, founders typically dilute 15-25%:
- Pre-seed: 5-15% to investors + 5-10% to ESOP = 10-25% dilution
- Seed: 15-25% to investors + ESOP top-up = 20-30% dilution
- Series A: 18-25% to investors + ESOP top-up = 22-30% dilution
- Series B+: 10-20% to investors + ESOP top-up = 15-25% dilution

After 3-4 rounds, founders often own 15-30% post-money. ESOP is 12-20%. Investors hold the rest.

**Stage-up logic:** plan early-round terms to preserve founder control for later rounds. Don't optimise pre-seed valuation at the cost of follow-on viability.

## 7. The Bootstrapper Track (Walling default)

Some SaaS plans deliberately skip the venture path:
- Bootstrap → modest angel ($50-200k) → continue bootstrapping → exit at $5-50M
- Founders keep 60-90% of equity
- TinySeed-style funds invest $120-500k early then take 8-12% equity
- Profit distributions begin at maturity
- Exit options: strategic acquisition, PE rollup, SBA / SBLC-backed buyout, hold forever

For African contexts, this track is often more realistic than the VC track because of capital scarcity.

## 8. Living-Plan Cadence

- Quarterly: refresh cap table; check if next round milestones are on track
- At each major milestone: re-value; revisit fundraising plan
- Pre-round: update data room; produce investor-update letter cadence; refresh comparables
- Post-round: re-baseline plan with new capital; update MSPOT; rebalance Omissions list

## 9. Africa / Uganda Application Notes

- Pre-seed in Africa is increasingly accessible (Microtraction, Future Africa, MEST, Antler, Founders Factory, accelerator + check models).
- Seed gap: hardest stage in Africa; many companies stuck between $200k angel and $3M+ seed. Bridge with DFIs / patient capital / revenue-based finance (RBF).
- DFI growth equity is often the "Series A" of African SaaS. Plan for ESG covenants, reporting, IFC Performance Standards compliance.
- Patient capital (Acumen, Catalyst Fund, Renew Capital) accepts lower IRR (15-20%) in exchange for governance and patience.
- Local family offices and corporate venture units are an under-utilised source — typically less structured but require longer relationship-building.
- Revenue-based finance (RBF) is emerging in Africa (Pipe, Capchase clones; specific African RBF providers like Bridgeloans, MoneyShop) for SaaS that has predictable ARR.
- Grants: Tony Elumelu Foundation, GSMA Innovation Fund, Catalyst Fund Inclusive Fintech 50, Mozilla African Innovation Mradi — useful at very-early-stage as non-dilutive supplement.
- Exit market in Africa: strategic acquirers (telcos, banks) and global tech (Visa, Mastercard, Google, Meta, Stripe) are the primary buyers. IPO on JSE, NSE, GHX is theoretically possible but rare.
