# Marketing-Plan Phrase Bank

Method source: the marketing-strategy key points (positioning, pricing and margin, low-cost tactics, alliances, sufficient resources) are adapted from Debelak, D. (2006) *Perfect Phrases for Business Proposals and Business Plans*, McGraw-Hill; the separation of marketing from sales and the message-consistency discipline from Abrams, R. M. (1993) *The Successful Business Plan: Secrets & Strategies*, 2nd edn, The Oasis Press. The book has no dedicated marketing-plan chapter, so this bank is **largely written from first principles** using the same power-paragraph grammar: fact → why it matters → strategy emphasis → consequence. All templates and examples are original; figures are illustrative. "Before" lines are deliberately bad examples.

Boundary: this bank covers the wording of a marketing plan as a business document. Channel execution, content production and campaign operations belong to the social-media and digital-marketing engine; hand over the approved plan rather than duplicating its doctrine.

Each section gives: purpose, keywords, slot-templates, tone rules, before→after rewrites, an illustrative model paragraph and red flags.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**Purpose.** State the marketing task, the target, the approach, the budget and the result expected, so the approver can decide whether to fund the plan.

**Keywords:** marketing objective, target segment, positioning, core message, priority channels, budget, expected result, acquisition cost, period, owner.

**Slot-templates**
1. [Company]'s marketing task for [period] is to [win / grow / retain] [n] [customers] in [places], who now [behaviour / named alternative].
2. The plan concentrates [%] of a [UGX amount] budget on [channels] where these customers already look.
3. It is expected to deliver [n] [leads / customers] and [UGX amount] in revenue by [date], at an acquisition cost below [UGX amount].
4. [Role] owns delivery; results are reviewed [monthly] against [n] KPIs.

**Tone rules:** results and budget in the first three sentences; no channel list without numbers; one page at most.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "This comprehensive marketing plan will elevate our brand and drive growth across all channels." → After: "This plan aims to win 600 repeat grocery customers in Ntinda and Bukoto by June, within a UGX 4.8 million budget (illustrative)."
- Before (wrong): "We will create massive awareness." → After: "We expect 2,000 households to see the offer through referrals and estate notices, and 600 to become repeat customers."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** [Company] will win 600 repeat grocery customers in Ntinda and Bukoto by June. These households already buy groceries through WhatsApp but complain of late deliveries and unclear prices. The plan puts 70% of its UGX 4.8 million budget into referrals and estate partnerships, which cost least per customer in the pilot. The operations lead owns the plan and reviews cost per repeat customer every month.

**Red flags:** "brand awareness" as the only goal; no budget; no date; "all channels".

## 2. Situation Analysis

**Purpose.** Summarise where the business stands today: sales, customers, market movement, competitors and internal capacity, so the reader agrees on the starting point.

**Keywords:** current sales, baseline, customer base, share, trend, driver, competitor activity, strengths, weaknesses, constraints, last period's results.

**Slot-templates**
1. In [last period], [Company] sold [volume / UGX amount] to [n] customers, [up / down] [%] on the year before.
2. [%] of revenue came from [segment / product]; [segment] fell because [reason].
3. [Driver, dated] is changing how [customers] buy: [observed behaviour].
4. [Competitor] has [action, date], which [effect on us].
5. [Company]'s main constraint is [capacity / cash / staff / data]; this plan works within it by [choice].
6. We do not yet measure [metric]; this plan introduces [measure] from [date].

**Tone rules:** start from measured facts; label estimates; include weaknesses; keep SWOT items specific enough to act on.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "In today's fast-paced world, the retail landscape is evolving rapidly." → After: "Walk-in sales at our Jinja shop fell 12% last year while WhatsApp orders doubled to about 40 a week (sales records)."
- Before (wrong): "Strengths: quality, service, experience." → After: "Strength: 68% of last year's customers bought again within six months (POS records)."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** Jinja Bakers sold UGX 310 million of bread and cakes last year, 8% more than the year before, but walk-in sales fell 12% while WhatsApp orders doubled to about 40 a week. Two supermarket bakeries opened within a kilometre in March. The bakery's constraint is delivery: one motorbike limits it to about 40 orders a day. This plan works within that limit until the second rider is hired in quarter three.

**Red flags:** generic SWOT; no baseline figures; market commentary with no link to the business.

## 3. Market and Customer Insight

**Purpose.** Show who buys, why, how they decide and what they say, so the strategy rests on evidence about customers rather than assumptions.

**Keywords:** segment, count, location, purchase trigger, buying criteria, decision-maker, influencer, customer words, interview, survey, repeat rate, lifetime value, barrier to purchase.

**Slot-templates**
1. Primary segment: [n] [households / businesses] in [place] who [defining behaviour], reachable through [channel].
2. Secondary segment: [description], targeted from [quarter] once [condition].
3. They buy when [trigger] and choose mainly on [criterion 1], then [criterion 2].
4. In [n] interviews, [n] customers described the problem as "[their words]".
5. The main barrier to buying is [barrier]; [offer feature] removes it.
6. We will not target [excluded group] in this period because [reason].
7. A customer buys [frequency] and spends about [UGX amount] a [period], a lifetime value of about [UGX amount] over [period].

**Tone rules:** count and place every segment; quote customers where you have them; state what you do not know.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "Our customers are young, tech-savvy professionals." → After: "Our primary customers are the estimated 1,100 office workers within walking distance of the Nakawa business park who buy lunch on weekdays (count method in Appendix A)."
- Before (wrong): "Customers love convenience." → After: "Of 60 shoppers interviewed, 41 said they would order if delivery arrived within an hour; 12 said price mattered more (illustrative)."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** The plan's primary customers are the estimated 1,100 office workers in the Nakawa business park who buy lunch on weekdays. They decide by 11 am, choose on speed first and price second, and most now queue at three roadside stalls. In 60 interviews, the phrase we heard most was "I lose my whole lunch hour". An ordering link that confirms a 12:30 pick-up removes that barrier.

**Red flags:** demographic-only segments; "everyone"; insights with no source; personas invented without interviews and not labelled as hypotheses.

## 4. Positioning and Message Themes

**Purpose.** State the single position the brand will own for the target customer and the few message themes that support it, so every channel says the same thing.

**Keywords:** positioning statement, frame of reference, point of difference, proof point, brand promise, message theme, tagline, tone of voice, customer language, concede.

**Slot-templates**
1. For [target customer] who [need], [Brand] is the [category] that [primary benefit], because [proof].
2. Against [named competitor], [Brand] stands for [one attribute]; we do not compete on [conceded attribute].
3. Core promise, repeated in every channel: "[single-sentence promise]".
4. Message theme 1, [theme]: [claim] proved by [evidence].
5. Message theme 2, [theme]: [claim] proved by [evidence].
6. The tagline "[tagline]" uses words customers already use; [n] of [n] interviewees used "[phrase]".
7. Tone of voice: [two or three adjectives with a do and a don't for each].

**Tone rules:** one position, two or three themes at most; each theme needs a proof point; use customer language, not agency language.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "We are the innovative, customer-centric, premium choice for discerning clients." → After: "For Kampala clinics that lose patients to slow replies, Signal Studio is the agency that connects enquiries to bookings, proved by response-time reports for four current clients."
- Before (wrong): "Quality you can trust." → After: "Every crate graded to the buyer's specification before it leaves Namanve."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** For parents in Kira who now drive into Kampala for their children's dental care, Smile Kids is the dental practice built only for children, close to home. Two themes support that position: "Close enough for after school", proved by the 10-minute drive from the three main estates, and "Calm first visits", proved by the practice's child-only waiting area and paediatric training. We do not compete on price for adult care.

**Red flags:** stacked adjectives; a position any competitor could claim; message themes with no proof; a different message on each channel.

## 5. Objectives (SMART)

**Purpose.** Set specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound targets tied to the business plan's sales forecast, so progress can be judged and resources justified.

**Keywords:** objective, baseline, target, deadline, metric, measurement source, owner, leading indicator, lagging indicator, relevant to revenue.

**Slot-templates**
1. By [date], secure [n] paying [customers] in [place], of whom [%] come from referrals, measured through [CRM / POS].
2. Raise [metric] from [baseline] to [target] in [place] by [date].
3. Achieve [%] repeat purchase among [segment] within [n] days of first order by [date].
4. Keep customer acquisition cost below [UGX amount] while lifetime value stays at least [n] times that.
5. Hold cost per qualified lead from [channel] under [UGX amount] for [n] consecutive months before increasing spend.
6. [Role] owns each objective; progress is reported [monthly].

**SMART rewrites of vague goals**

| Vague goal (wrong) | SMART rewrite (illustrative) |
|---|---|
| Increase brand awareness | Raise prompted awareness of [Brand] among Mbarara town shop owners from 22% to 40% by December, measured by a 150-shop survey in June and December |
| Grow sales | Grow monthly sales in the Gulu branch from UGX 38 million to UGX 50 million by the end of quarter three |
| Get more customers online | Win 250 new customers through the website ordering page by March, tracked by order source in the POS |
| Improve customer satisfaction | Raise the share of customers rating delivery 4 or 5 out of 5 from 71% to 85% by June, from the post-delivery SMS survey |
| Become the market leader | Reach 20% of hardware wholesale accounts in Kikuubo by June next year (estimated at [n] accounts from the market register) |
| Boost social media engagement | Generate 60 WhatsApp enquiries a month from Facebook posts by April, tracked through a tagged click-to-chat link |
| Expand into new markets | Open one agent outlet in Lira by August and sign 80 customers there by December |
| Retain more members | Cut SACCO member exits from 9% to 5% a year by December, measured from the member register |

**Tone rules:** every objective has a baseline, a target, a date, a source and an owner; objectives add up to the sales forecast; avoid objectives the business cannot measure yet unless measurement is set up first.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "We aim to become the go-to brand for quality." → After: "Win 40% of repeat orders from the 120 pharmacies in Lira and Apac by June, measured from order records."
- Before (wrong): "Maximise ROI across all campaigns." → After: "Keep cost per new customer below UGX 8,000 for three consecutive months before increasing monthly spend."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** The plan has three objectives, each tied to the year-one sales forecast. By June, win 600 repeat customers in Ntinda and Bukoto, from a baseline of 140. Keep cost per repeat customer below UGX 8,000. Raise the share of customers ordering at least twice a month from 35% to 55%. The operations lead owns all three and reports them each month from the ordering system.

**Red flags:** "increase awareness"; no baseline; targets with no measurement source; objectives that do not reconcile with the revenue forecast.

## 6. Strategy

**Purpose.** Explain the choices that will achieve the objectives: which segment first, which position, which basis of competition, and what the plan will not do.

**Keywords:** strategic choice, focus, sequencing, basis of competition, price position, trade-off, phase, test and scale, what we will not do, strategy type.

**Slot-templates**
1. The plan wins [segment] first because [reason: highest margin / easiest to reach / proof for later segments].
2. [Company] competes on [basis], consistent with its strategy type ([strategy_type value]).
3. Phase one tests [tactic] with [n] customers; phase two scales only if [threshold].
4. We will not [option rejected], because [trade-off].
5. The price is set at [position] because [reason], giving a gross margin of [%].
6. [Named partners] give access to [n] customers at a lower cost than paid media.

**Tone rules:** state choices and rejections; link each choice to evidence; use the business plan's strategy type for emphasis.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "We will adopt a holistic, multi-channel approach." → After: "We start with referrals and estate partnerships because they cost a third as much per customer as paid social in the pilot; paid social follows only once referral growth slows."
- Before (wrong): "Our strategy is to offer the best value." → After: "We match supermarket prices on staples and charge a 10% premium on same-day delivery, which the pilot showed customers accept."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** The plan focuses on households already ordering groceries by WhatsApp, because they need no persuasion to buy online, only a reason to switch supplier. We compete on reliable timing, not price: staples match supermarket prices, and the delivery promise carries the margin. We will not open a second zone until 80% of orders in the first zone arrive on time for eight consecutive weeks.

**Red flags:** strategy as a list of tactics; no trade-offs; "multi-channel" with no priority; a strategy that contradicts the positioning.

## 7. Channel Plan

**Purpose.** Assign each channel a job, a target and a cost, so the budget follows the customers.

**Keywords:** channel, role (awareness / consideration / conversion / retention), reach, frequency, cost per lead, conversion rate, partner channel, owned channel, earned channel, paid channel.

**Slot-templates**
1. [Channel] has one job: [job], with a target of [n] [leads / sales] a [period] at under [UGX amount] each.
2. [Channel] reaches [n] [customers] a week at [location] for about [UGX amount] a month.
3. [Partner] will [display / recommend / bundle] [offer] to its [n] members in return for [commission].
4. Owned channels ([WhatsApp broadcast list / website / SMS]) are used only with customer consent.
5. We drop [channel] because [reason: audience mismatch / cost per lead in pilot].

**Tone rules:** name the place and the audience for every channel; one job per channel; show why each was chosen and at least one that was rejected.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "We will use social media, radio, flyers and word of mouth." → After: "Referral credits bring new customers (target 60 a month); estate notice boards announce the service (target 300 enquiries); the WhatsApp broadcast list drives repeat orders."
- Before (wrong): "Radio will create massive reach." → After: "Two 30-second spots a day on a Gulu station for four weeks before planting season cost about UGX 2.4 million; we will track calls to a dedicated number (illustrative; confirm current rate card)."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** Each channel has one job. Referral credits recruit new households, with a target of 60 a month at UGX 5,000 each. Notices on estate boards in six compounds announce the service. The WhatsApp broadcast list, used only with consent, carries the weekly price list and drives repeat orders. We tested flyers at two supermarkets and dropped them: they produced four orders for UGX 300,000.

**Red flags:** channel lists without jobs; no cost per result; channels chosen because competitors use them; messaging without consent.

## 8. Advertising and Media Plan

**Purpose.** Specify paid media: where, when, how often, at what cost and how response will be tracked.

**Keywords:** media, placement, flight, frequency, reach, rate card, cost per thousand, cost per response, tracking code, call-to-action, creative, test budget.

**Slot-templates**
1. [Medium] runs [n] [spots / placements] a [period] from [date] to [date], costing [UGX amount] ([rate card, date]).
2. The target audience is [segment]; [medium] fits because [audience evidence].
3. Each advert carries one call-to-action, [action], tracked through [code / number / link].
4. [%] of the media budget is a test; the rest is released only if cost per response stays below [UGX amount].
5. Adverts run in [months] to match [season / purchase trigger].

**Tone rules:** rate cards are volatile, so date them and confirm before booking; show tracking for every paid placement; test before scaling.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "An impactful advertising campaign will resonate with our audience." → After: "A four-week radio test in Luganda on one Kampala station, 14 spots a week, tracked through a dedicated phone number (confirm current rate card)."
- Before (wrong): "We will advertise widely." → After: "We will run Facebook adverts only in the 5 km around the shop, with a click-to-WhatsApp button, at a test budget of UGX 600,000."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** Paid media starts in month four, after the referral programme has shown its conversion rate. A UGX 600,000 test runs Facebook adverts within 5 km of the Ntinda depot, each with a click-to-WhatsApp button and a tagged link. If cost per first order stays below UGX 8,000 for four weeks, the monthly budget rises to UGX 1.5 million; if not, the money returns to referral credits.

**Red flags:** no tracking; no test phase; undated rate figures; "viral"; media chosen for reach rather than fit.

## 9. Content Plan

**Purpose.** Plan the content that supports the message themes and buying decisions, with formats, frequency, owners and measures.

**Keywords:** content theme, format, frequency, publishing calendar, owner, buyer question, proof content, case study, testimonial with consent, measure, repurposing.

**Slot-templates**
1. Content answers the [n] questions customers ask before buying: [question 1], [question 2].
2. Each week: [n] [format] on [theme], published on [channel] by [owner].
3. Proof content: [n] case studies of [customer type], each with a measured result and written consent.
4. Every piece ends with one next step: [action].
5. Content is measured by [enquiries / saves / replies], not by likes alone.

**Tone rules:** content serves a buying question; testimonials only with consent and without invented figures; hand production detail to the digital-marketing engine.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "We will post engaging content daily." → After: "Three posts a week: one price update, one delivery-time report, and one customer story with consent."
- Before (wrong): "Educational content will establish thought leadership." → After: "A monthly two-minute video answers the question pharmacists ask most: how to cut stock-outs of fast-moving drugs."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** Content in this plan answers the three questions customers ask before ordering: what does it cost, when will it arrive, and can I trust the quality. Each week the team posts the price list on Monday, last week's on-time delivery rate on Wednesday, and one customer story, with consent, on Friday. The measure is WhatsApp orders from each post, tracked by link.

**Red flags:** content volume targets with no purpose; "engaging content"; testimonials without consent; no measure.

## 10. Sales Plan

**Purpose.** Show how enquiries become orders: the process, the people, the targets and the conversion rates, so the revenue forecast has a mechanism.

**Keywords:** sales process, lead, qualified lead, visit, demonstration, trial, proposal, close rate, sales cycle, territory, quota, pipeline, follow-up, key account.

**Slot-templates**
1. Sales follow [n] stages: [stage 1] → [stage 2] → [stage 3] → [order].
2. [n] agents each cover [territory], visiting [n] [accounts] a week.
3. Expected conversion: [%] of visits to trial and [%] of trials to regular orders (assumptions from [pilot / records]).
4. The target of [n] accounts by [date] therefore needs [n] visits.
5. Every enquiry is answered within [time]; unconverted leads are followed up after [n] days.
6. [Role] reviews the pipeline every [week] and owns the sales target.

**Tone rules:** a numbered funnel with sourced rates; named territories; a named owner; keep marketing and sales separate but linked.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "Our dynamic sales team will aggressively pursue leads." → After: "Two agents visit 12 pharmacies each a week in Lira and Apac; from the pilot, one visit in four leads to a trial order."
- Before (wrong): "We expect strong sales." → After: "Reaching 90 regular accounts by December needs about 720 visits over six months at the pilot conversion rates."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** MedPool sells to rural pharmacies through two field agents covering Lira and Apac. Each agent visits 12 pharmacies a week; in the pilot, a quarter of visits produced a trial order and half of trial customers ordered again within a month. At those rates, reaching 90 regular accounts by December needs about 720 visits over six months, within the agents' capacity. The sales manager reviews visit and order numbers every Friday.

**Red flags:** no funnel; conversion rates with no source; sales targets that exceed the team's capacity; no follow-up rule.

## 11. Budget

**Purpose.** Show what the plan costs, by line and month, and why that amount is enough to reach the objectives.

**Keywords:** marketing budget, line item, phasing, percentage of revenue, cost per lead, cost per customer, contingency, release condition, sufficiency calculation.

**Slot-templates**
1. The marketing budget is [UGX amount] for [period], [%] of projected revenue.
2. It is sufficient because [n] leads × [%] conversion × [UGX average sale] = [UGX revenue], which meets the objective.
3. Spend is phased: [%] in [quarter], with further releases only when [metric threshold] is met.
4. The largest line is [item] at [UGX amount], because [reason].
5. A contingency of [%] covers [risk].

**Tone rules:** show the sufficiency calculation; tie every line to a channel job; date any rate or price used.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "An adequate budget has been allocated to marketing." → After: "The six-month budget is UGX 4.8 million, 6% of projected revenue; 2,000 contacts × 40% trial × 75% repeat = 600 repeat customers, the plan objective (illustrative; rates from the pilot)."
- Before (wrong): "Budget: Social media UGX 5M, Radio UGX 3M, Flyers UGX 1M." → After: add the job, target and cost per result for each line, and the release condition.

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** The six-month budget is UGX 4.8 million, about 6% of projected revenue. Referral credits take UGX 3 million, estate partnerships UGX 600,000, and a paid-social test UGX 600,000, with the remainder held as contingency. Only the test budget is at risk before results are known; the rest is spent per customer won. At pilot conversion rates the budget delivers the 600-customer objective at under UGX 8,000 per repeat customer.

**Red flags:** budget as a list with no link to results; round numbers with no basis; no phasing; no release conditions.

## 12. Implementation Calendar

**Purpose.** Schedule activities by week or month with owners and dependencies, so the team can execute and the approver can check progress.

**Keywords:** calendar, week, month, activity, owner, dependency, launch date, season, milestone, review point.

**Slot-templates**
1. [Month]: [activity], owned by [role]; depends on [prior activity].
2. Launch is set for [date] to match [season / event / payday cycle].
3. [Activity] must finish before [activity] because [reason].
4. Review points fall on [dates]; each decides whether to [continue / adjust / stop] [tactic].

**Tone rules:** real dates; one owner per activity; align with seasons, school terms, harvests, public holidays and paydays where relevant.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "The campaign will be rolled out in phases over the year." → After: "Weeks 1–2: sign estate partners. Week 3: launch referral credits. Week 6: first review of cost per customer. Week 13: paid-social test starts if the review allows."
- Before (wrong): "We will launch soon." → After: "Launch on 1 February, the first weekend after school fees are paid, when household spending recovers (assumption from last year's sales pattern)."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** The plan runs for 26 weeks from 1 February. Estate partnerships are signed in weeks one and two, and referral credits launch in week three. The first review, in week six, checks cost per new customer against the UGX 8,000 ceiling. The paid-social test starts in week 13 only if referral growth has slowed. The operations lead owns the calendar and updates it after each review.

**Red flags:** "phased roll-out" with no dates; no owners; launches that ignore seasonality.

## 13. KPI Dashboard

**Purpose.** Define the few measures that show whether the plan is working, with formulas, sources, targets and review frequency.

**Keywords:** KPI, definition, formula, data source, baseline, target, frequency, owner, leading indicator, lagging indicator, threshold.

**Slot-templates**
1. [KPI]: [formula], from [source], target [value], reviewed [frequency] by [owner].
2. Leading indicator: [metric], because it predicts [lagging result].
3. Threshold: if [KPI] is [above / below] [value] for [period], [action].

**Example dashboard (illustrative)**

| KPI | Formula | Source | Target | Review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New customers | First orders in period | Ordering system | 100 a month | Weekly |
| Cost per new customer | Marketing spend ÷ new customers | Budget ledger + ordering system | < UGX 8,000 | Monthly |
| Repeat rate | Customers with 2+ orders in 30 days ÷ new customers | Ordering system | ≥ 75% | Monthly |
| On-time delivery | Orders delivered within promised window ÷ all orders | Delivery log | ≥ 90% | Weekly |
| Referral share | New customers with referral code ÷ new customers | Ordering system | ≥ 40% | Monthly |

**Tone rules:** five to eight KPIs; every KPI has a formula and source; no vanity metrics unless they predict revenue.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "We will track engagement, reach and impressions." → After: "We track cost per new customer and 30-day repeat rate; reach is recorded only to diagnose a fall in new customers."

**Red flags:** likes and followers as primary KPIs; KPIs without formulas; targets that do not match the objectives.

## 14. Control Plan

**Purpose.** State who reviews results, how often, and what happens when a tactic misses its target, so the plan corrects itself.

**Keywords:** review meeting, frequency, variance, corrective action, stop rule, reallocation, escalation, reporting, decision rights.

**Slot-templates**
1. Results are reviewed [monthly] by [roles] against the KPI dashboard.
2. Any tactic that misses its cost-per-result target for [n] months is stopped and its budget moved to [best performer].
3. A variance above [%] against budget is reported to [role] within [n] days.
4. [Role] may reallocate up to [%] of the budget between channels; larger changes need [approver].
5. A written summary of results and decisions is circulated within [n] days of each review.

**Tone rules:** stop rules stated in advance; decision rights explicit; short written records.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "The plan will be monitored closely and adjusted as necessary." → After: "Any channel whose cost per new customer exceeds UGX 12,000 for two consecutive months is paused, and its budget moves to referral credits."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** The operations lead and the owner review the dashboard on the first Monday of each month. A tactic that misses its cost-per-customer target for two months is stopped, and its budget moves to the best-performing channel. The operations lead may move up to 20% of the budget between channels; larger changes need the owner's approval. A one-page note of results and decisions goes to all staff within two days.

**Red flags:** "monitored closely"; no stop rule; no named reviewer.

## 15. Risks

**Purpose.** Name what could stop the plan reaching its objectives and the response prepared for each.

**Keywords:** marketing risk, competitor response, channel dependency, platform policy change, capacity shortfall, reputational risk, trigger, response, owner.

**Slot-templates**
1. If [competitor] responds with [action], [Company] will [response], because [reason].
2. [%] of new customers depend on [channel]; if its cost or rules change, [alternative] takes over.
3. If demand exceeds [capacity], [Company] will [waiting list / pause promotion] rather than miss delivery promises.
4. A complaint made public on [channel] is answered within [time] by [role].
5. [Platform features or ad rules] change often; the plan is checked against current rules before each campaign.

**Tone rules:** specific triggers and responses; include the risk of success (demand beyond capacity); keep platform claims dated or phrased as checks.

**Before → after**
- Before (wrong): "Potential risks will be mitigated through robust strategies." → After: "If a supermarket launches its own delivery at lower prices, we keep our price and promote the one-hour window, which the supermarket does not offer."
- Before (wrong): "Social media is a reliable channel." → After: "Sixty per cent of new customers come through one messaging platform; we collect phone numbers with consent so SMS can replace it if access changes."

**Model paragraph (illustrative).** Three risks could stop the plan. A supermarket could launch cheaper delivery; we would keep prices and promote our one-hour window, which the pilot showed customers value most. One messaging platform brings 60% of orders, so we collect consented phone numbers as an SMS fallback. Demand could exceed our two riders; if on-time delivery falls below 85% for a week, referral credits pause until a third rider starts.

**Red flags:** "no major risks"; generic risk lists; no response owners; ignoring capacity risk.

## Close: the one-sentence plan

Test the finished plan with one sentence that names the segment, the message, the main tactics, the target and the budget:

- Template: "With [focused segment], [single message], [main tactics] and [named partners], [Company] will reach [SMART headline target] by [date] within [UGX budget]."
- Illustrative: "By focusing on WhatsApp grocery buyers in Ntinda and Bukoto, promising one-hour delivery at supermarket prices, and recruiting through referral credits and two estate managers, [Company] will win 600 repeat customers by June within UGX 4.8 million."
