# Human-English Five-Pass Standard

Write for a particular reader who has a reason to care and a decision to make. Human-sounding prose comes from attention, judgement, accurate detail, and a recognisable point of view — never from slang, invented anecdotes, random quirks, or deliberately broken grammar.

This standard was synthesised in August 2026 from the engine's own editorial practice and from language-study sources. Three sources were supplied only as title stubs and contribute nothing: Bolitho, R. *Discover English*; Mann, M. *Improve Your Skills: Writing for Advanced*; and *Natural English Reading & Writing Skills: Upper-Intermediate Resource Book*. Do not attribute any method to them until a readable copy is supplied. ## 1. The five passes

1. **Purpose and reader.** Name the reader, their situation, knowledge level, emotional temperature, and next action. State the single job of the piece.
2. **Genre and spine.** Choose the form that fits the job. Make headings describe decisions or content, not topics alone. Put the governing point where this reader needs it.
3. **Meaning and evidence.** Separate fact, interpretation, recommendation, assumption, and open question. Put proof beside the claim. Remove any claim that cannot be supported.
4. **Sentence and paragraph craft.** Prefer concrete nouns, strong verbs, exact modifiers, natural collocations, and the active voice where the actor matters. Give each paragraph one centre. Vary sentence length for meaning, not decoration.
5. **Proof and reader test.** Check grammar, spelling, names, dates, figures, links, headings, and consistency. Read aloud once; skim only the headings once. Repair every stumble, false promise, and unexplained term.

Keep the substantive revision (passes 1 to 4) separate from the mechanical proof (pass 5). A polished sentence can still sit in the wrong paragraph; a sound argument can still carry a damaging typo.

## 2. Language decisions

- Use British English by default; keep accepted local names, institutional terms, currencies, and expressions.
- Prefer the ordinary word that carries the meaning: *use* not *utilise*, *start* not *commence*, *help* not *facilitate*, *about* not *with regard to*.
- Use an advanced or technical word only after checking its meaning, connotation, collocation, and register.
- Check the recurring error families: subject–verb agreement, articles, tense and aspect, pronoun reference, relative clauses, prepositions, parallelism, misplaced modifiers, punctuation, spelling, homophones, fragments, and run-on sentences.
- Start grammar checks from meaning: what the sentence says, who acts, what time is meant, and what relationship the words must express.
- Replace unearned intensifiers and prestige adjectives (*very, really, significant, substantial, world-class, leading, innovative, seamless, robust*) with a fact, limit, comparison, or qualified judgement.
- Use connectors (*however, therefore, for example*) only when they show a real logical relationship.
- Keep lists grammatically parallel.

For collocation and precise word choice, also apply [English collocations and lexical precision](english-collocations-and-lexical-precision-2026-09-02.md): use collocations selectively and check sense, register, evidence, and audience before adopting them.

## 3. Paragraph and argument craft

A strong paragraph usually has a point, support, and a consequence or link; it has no fixed sentence count. Change paragraph length when the thought changes.

For claims that matter, run the private test **claim → evidence → warrant → countercase → implication**. The prose need not show the labels, but the writer must know what connects evidence to conclusion and what could make the conclusion fail.

In persuasive or analytical work, let the reader follow the thought: what is happening, why it matters here, what is difficult, what you recommend, and what the recommendation costs or requires. A human voice often shows in an honest qualification, a chosen example, or a sentence that names the trade-off.

## 4. Register by output type

| Output | Primary job | Voice and grammar | Main danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| App message or microcopy | Help the person complete the next step | Brief, calm, literal, respectful; say what happened and what to do | "Something went wrong" with no recovery; forced cheerfulness |
| Website page | Orient, answer, prove, invite action | Reader-first, specific, scannable; meaningful headings; claims beside proof | Keyword stuffing, inflated promises, generic welcome copy |
| Business plan | Let a lender, owner, or investor judge a proposition | Answer first; evidence, assumptions, risks, trade-offs visible | Confident prose hiding weak numbers |
| Proposal or bid | Earn evaluator confidence within the terms of reference | Direct, compliant, tailored, measurable | Copied methodology, unearned capability claims, buried compliance gaps |
| Social post | Earn attention and a useful response | Conversational but controlled; one idea, one audience, one action | Content-factory rhythm, forced humour, hashtag piles |
| Research report | Show what the evidence supports | Precise, sourced, open about limits and inference | Citation laundering, false balance, findings without judgement |
| Political essay or speech | Make a serious public argument | Clear thesis, institutional diagnosis, public-interest stakes, fair countercase | Slogans, theatrical certainty, unevidenced allegations |
| SRS or technical document | Make behaviour buildable and testable | Exact terms, explicit actors, states, constraints, test oracles | "User-friendly" or "reliable" without a measurable requirement |

## 5. Release questions

- Could this paragraph belong to ten unrelated organisations? If so, add the real actor, place, decision, constraint, example, or evidence, or cut it.
- Does the first sentence do useful work, or only announce the topic?
- Does the piece contain a judgement or selection a competent author actually made?
- Are sentence openings, paragraph shapes, and transitions mechanically repeated?
- Have polished adjectives hidden a missing number, source, owner, mechanism, or exception?
- Does the conclusion earn its place?
- Would an intelligent reader enjoy the movement of the prose, or merely tolerate it?

Do not ban a word only because models overuse it; ban default use that adds no meaning. Do not insert mistakes to simulate a human author.

## 6. Rewrite patterns

| Weak pattern | Direction |
|---|---|
| A sentence about a "fast-paced world" and "innovative solutions" | Name the pressure, the business, the solution, and the decision it changes. |
| "World-class services tailored to your needs" | State the service, the client situation, the boundary of the work, and the next step. |
| "Your request has been submitted successfully." | "Request sent. We'll email the reference number within 10 minutes." |
| "The project will enhance stakeholder engagement." | "The district team will hold a monthly review with traders and publish the actions agreed." |
| "The report explores several important issues." | "The report compares the two procurement options and recommends the one that fits the six-month budget." |

## 7. Evidence record

When the standard is applied, record: output type; reader and purpose; proof status of sources; terms checked; substantive revision completed; read-aloud and proof status; unresolved language or evidence gaps; reviewer and date. A language pass cannot certify a weak proposition, an unverified fact, or a missing human approval.
