# Business-Plan Phrase Bank: Sections 09 to 16

Method source: margin, use-of-funds and founder-financing sentence jobs adapted from Debelak, D. (2006) *Perfect Phrases for Business Proposals and Business Plans*, McGraw-Hill. All templates and examples are original. Figures in examples are illustrative, not market facts. "Before" lines are deliberately bad examples and must never be copied into a plan.

**Coverage note.** The book gives phrase guidance for margins, start-up costs and founder financing (used in 10 and 11) and only key points for management (09). Sections 11b, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16 are **written from first principles** for this engine, applying the same power-paragraph grammar; they are marked "First principles" below.

Each entry gives: (a) purpose and reader decision, (b) keywords, (c) slot-templates, (d) tone rules, (e) before→after rewrites, (f) an illustrative power paragraph, (g) red-flag phrases.

## 09 Management Team

*Partly first principles: the book lists the key points (experience or access to advisers, checks and balances, retention) but gives no phrase chapter.*

**(a) Purpose.** Show that the people running the business have done the hardest parts before, that gaps are covered, and that governance checks the managers, so the reader can judge whether this team can execute the plan.

**(b) Keywords:** role, years in, previously, led, delivered, responsible for, skills gap, adviser, board, independent director, audit committee, reporting line, key-person risk, succession, retention, vesting, remuneration.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Name], [role], has [n] years in [field], including [relevant result with a number] at [organisation].
2. On this plan [he/she] is responsible for [specific outcome].
3. Where the team lacks [skill], it is supported by [named adviser], who [relevant track record].
4. A [n]-member board, including [independent member with credential], meets [quarterly] and reviews [finance / risk / audit].
5. Together the team has already [built / sold / operated] [the hardest part of this plan].
6. [Role] will be recruited by [date] at [UGX salary band]; until then [name] covers it.
7. Key staff are retained through [bonus / share options / vesting over n years].
8. If [key person] were unavailable, [named deputy] would take over [duties]; [insurance] covers the gap.

**(d) Tone rules**
- Each bio ends with what the person will do on this plan.
- Use results, counts and named organisations; no personality adjectives.
- Name the gaps honestly and say how they are filled. A team with no gaps reads as unexamined.
- Describe governance as it is, not as it will be "in due course".

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "[Founder name] is a highly motivated, results-driven visionary." → After: "[Founder name] ran distribution for a beverage company across 14 districts for six years and grew dealer revenue by 42% in his second year (illustrative)."
2. Before (wrong): "We have a strong management team with unwavering commitment." → After: "The finance director spent eight years at a commercial bank and has led two statutory audits to unqualified opinions; an independent director chairs the audit committee (illustrative)."
3. Before (wrong): "The team will be expanded as the business grows." → After: "A production manager will be hired by March at UGX 3.5–4.5 million a month; until then the managing director supervises production directly."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> The two founders have already built and run the operation this plan expands. [Founder A] managed milk collection for a regional processor for seven years, overseeing 30 collection centres, and now runs [Company]'s chilling points. [Founder B] is a practising accountant who set up the company's mobile-money payment system for farmers. The team lacks cold-chain engineering, so it retains a refrigeration engineer on a service contract and has a named replacement supplier. A three-member board, including an independent director with dairy-cooperative experience, meets quarterly and reviews cash, collections and audit findings (all names and details illustrative).

**(g) Red flags:** "visionary", "passionate", "dynamic"; bios with no numbers; a board made only of friends and family; no mention of gaps; one person holding every critical role with no deputy.

## 10 Financial Projections

**(a) Purpose.** Translate the plan's decisions into income, cash flow and balance-sheet projections with stated assumptions, so the reader can judge profitability, cash needs, repayment capacity and sensitivity to things going wrong.

**(b) Keywords:** assumption, driver, base case, downside case, sensitivity, revenue build, unit economics, gross margin, EBITDA margin, net margin, break-even, working capital, cash runway, debt service coverage ratio, payback, industry comparison.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. Revenue is built from [n customers] × [average spend] × [purchase frequency]; each input and its source is listed in Table [x].
2. [Company] earns a gross margin of [%] and an EBITDA margin of [%], against [comparable group] at [%] and [%] ([source, date]).
3. The margin is higher because [sales mix / owned asset / sourcing advantage].
4. The margin should hold because [contract / barrier / demand exceeds supply], although [risk] could reduce it by [points].
5. The base case projects break-even in month [n] at [volume]; the downside case, with [change], moves break-even to month [n].
6. Cash is lowest in month [n] at [UGX amount]; the facility requested covers this with [UGX amount] of headroom.
7. The debt service coverage ratio is [x] in year one and [x] in year two (base case) and stays above [x] in the downside case.
8. Receipts are projected from collections, not sales: [%] of sales are paid within [n] days, [%] within [n] days, and [%] is assumed lost.
9. All figures are in [UGX], [nominal / real], and exclude [VAT / item]; see the assumptions register.

**(d) Tone rules**
- Every number traces to a decision or an assumption the reader can see. Write the assumption before the figure.
- Label margins precisely (gross, EBITDA, operating, net). Check definitions with the finance engine.
- Show a downside case unprompted; lenders trust plans that show where they break.
- Explain every step-change in the forecast with its cause (new branch, contract, price change).
- Keep narrative short; the tables carry the detail. The narrative explains, it does not repeat.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "The business will be highly profitable with healthy margins." → After: "Gross margin is 41% against about 30% for comparable wholesalers ([source, date]), because 65% of sales come from the own-brand range (illustrative)."
2. Before (wrong): "Our conservative projections show exponential growth." → After: "Revenue grows from UGX 820 million to UGX 1.6 billion over three years, driven by two new branches opening in months 7 and 19; without the second branch, year-three revenue is UGX 1.2 billion (illustrative)."
3. Before (wrong): "Profitability will have been achieved by year three." → After: "The business makes a profit in year three."
4. Before (wrong): "Cash flow is expected to be positive." → After: "Cash is lowest in month 5, at UGX 38 million, after the seasonal stock build; the overdraft limit requested covers this with UGX 20 million to spare (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> Lakeview Events earns a 46% gross margin and a 14% EBITDA margin, against 30–35% and 6–9% for the Kampala caterers in our comparison set ([source, date]; illustrative). The difference comes from owning the venue: every booking earns both venue and catering revenue. The base case assumes 70 weddings a year, the average of the last two years, and projects the loan repaid in 36 months with a debt service coverage ratio of 1.8 in year one. In the downside case, with bookings 25% lower, the ratio falls to 1.3 and repayment still holds. Weekend dates for next year are already 60% booked, with deposits received.

**(g) Red flags:** "conservative" applied to aggressive numbers; hockey-stick growth with no driver; margins with no comparison; profit shown but no cash flow; "net margin" that excludes interest and tax; no downside case; figures that do not reconcile with the funding request.

## 11 Funding Request

**(a) Purpose.** State how much money is needed, in what form, for what, how it changes the business and how it will be repaid or returned, so the reader can decide whether to lend or invest and on what terms.

**(b) Keywords:** amount requested, instrument (loan / equity / convertible / grant), use of funds, impact, repayment source, tenor, grace period, security, collateral, owner's contribution, funding to date, post-round position, ownership, valuation basis, later rounds, exit route.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Company] needs [UGX amount] to [one clear purpose].
2. The total requirement is [UGX amount]: [UGX amount] from the founders, [UGX amount] as a [bank loan / equity] and [UGX amount] from [other source].
3. The funds will buy [assets / stock / hires], listed with quotations in Table [x].
4. The investment raises [capacity / reach] from [a] to [b], which lets [Company] [serve named customers / enter named market].
5. The loan will be repaid from [cash source] over [n] months, after a grace period of [n] months, secured by [collateral, valued at UGX amount by (valuer, date)].
6. To date the founders have invested [UGX amount], all equity, in [what it bought].
7. After this round, the founders will hold [%] and new investors [%]; the company will carry [UGX amount] of debt.
8. [Company] expects to raise [amount] in [year] for [purpose], once [milestone] is reached; if not, growth will be self-funded.
9. The amount is sized to [make an impact / reach the next milestone]; a smaller sum would only [limited result].

**(d) Tone rules**
- State the amount and purpose in one sentence a reader can remember.
- Answer the two investor tests explicitly: is the money enough to make an impact, and is it justified by the size of the market?
- Make the three founder-financing statements reconcile: total need and outside portion, funding to date, position after the round.
- Match the register to the instrument: repayment and security for lenders; growth, ownership and exit for investors.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "Funding will be used to achieve our growth ambitions." → After: "UGX 650 million will open a second pack-house in Mukono and buy four refrigerated trucks, raising capacity from 4 to 12 tonnes a day (illustrative)."
2. Before (wrong): "The founders are fully committed and have invested significantly." → After: "The founders have invested UGX 45 million in equity in a pilot kiosk that has traded profitably for 11 months (illustrative)."
3. Before (wrong): "Investors can expect excellent returns." → After: "At the base-case exit multiple, an investor entering at this valuation would receive about three times the investment in year five; the assumptions and a downside case are in Table 11.3 (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> [Company] needs UGX 650 million to open a second pack-house in Mukono and add four refrigerated trucks. The expansion triples daily capacity from 4 to 12 tonnes, enough to supply all six supermarket chains that have asked for daily delivery; today it can serve only two. The founders will contribute UGX 150 million, and [Company] seeks a UGX 500 million asset loan over 48 months with a six-month grace period, secured on the trucks and the founders' land in Mukono. The founders have already invested UGX 90 million of their own money. After the loan, owners' equity of UGX 240 million will stand against UGX 500 million of debt (all figures illustrative).

**(g) Red flags:** "to scale"; no amount; no breakdown; the ask not matching the implementation budget; founders who have invested nothing; "flexible terms"; a valuation with no basis; money requested that is too small to change the business.

## 11b Grant Proposal

*First principles: the book does not cover grants.*

**(a) Purpose.** Show a funder that a defined problem affects defined beneficiaries, that the project will produce measurable results for them at a reasonable cost, and that the benefits will last after the grant, so the funder can decide whether the project fits its programme and deserves the award.

**(b) Keywords:** problem statement, baseline, beneficiaries (direct and indirect), theory of change, activities, outputs, outcomes, impact, indicator, target, means of verification, logframe, budget narrative, cost per beneficiary, co-financing, sustainability, risk and assumption, monitoring and evaluation.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. In [place], [n] [beneficiary group] [problem], measured at [baseline] ([source, date]).
2. The project will [activities] so that [n] [beneficiaries] [change] by [date].
3. If [activities] produce [outputs], and [assumption] holds, then [outcome] follows, contributing to [impact].
4. Outcome indicator: [metric], from [baseline] to [target] by [date], verified by [means].
5. Of the [n] direct beneficiaries, [%] will be women and [%] young people aged [range].
6. The grant of [amount] covers [activities]; [Company] contributes [amount] in [cash / in kind].
7. The cost per [beneficiary / outcome] is [amount], against [comparator] for [similar programme, source].
8. After the grant ends, [activity] continues because [revenue / fee / partner / government uptake] covers [cost].
9. Progress is reported to the funder [quarterly] against the logframe, with an independent review at [point].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Use the funder's own terms and structure; mirror its evaluation criteria in headings.
- Keep outputs (what the project delivers) separate from outcomes (what changes for beneficiaries).
- Quantify beneficiaries and baselines; a problem without a baseline cannot show change.
- Be commercially sober: grants reward credible delivery, not ambition.
- Show sustainability as a mechanism with money behind it, not a hope.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "This project will empower vulnerable communities and foster sustainable development." → After: "The project will train 600 women farmers in three Lira sub-counties to dry and store maize to the buyer's moisture standard, so post-harvest losses fall from about 25% to under 10% by the end of the second season (illustrative)."
2. Before (wrong): "The project is sustainable." → After: "After the grant, the drying floors are run by the three farmer groups, which charge members UGX 20 per kilogram; that fee covers maintenance and the storekeeper's wage (illustrative)."
3. Before (wrong): "We will reach many beneficiaries." → After: "Direct beneficiaries: 600 women farmers. Indirect: about 3,000 household members. The cost per direct beneficiary is UGX 410,000 (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> Maize farmers in Aromo, Ogur and Agweng sub-counties lose an estimated quarter of each harvest to poor drying and storage, according to the district production office's [year] survey. The project will build three shared drying floors and train 600 women farmers to meet the moisture standard set by the two buyers who have agreed to purchase their grain. If farmers dry and store to standard, and the buyers honour their letters of intent, losses should fall below 10% by the end of the second season, which will be verified through store records and a sample survey. The grant of UGX 246 million is matched by UGX 60 million from the implementing company (figures illustrative).

**(g) Red flags:** "empower", "transform lives" with no indicator; outputs reported as outcomes; no baseline; sustainability that depends on another grant; a budget with no link to activities; beneficiary numbers with no count method.

## 12 Risk Analysis

*First principles: the book does not cover risk.*

**(a) Purpose.** Name the risks that could stop the plan, rate them, and show the control and contingency for each, so the reader can judge whether management understands what could go wrong and has priced it.

**(b) Keywords:** risk, likelihood, impact, rating, trigger, early warning indicator, mitigation (avoid, reduce, transfer, accept), contingency, owner, residual risk, insurance, key-person risk, regulatory risk, currency risk, concentration, do-nothing case.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. The main risk is [specific risk], because [cause]; if it occurs, [measurable consequence].
2. We rate it [likelihood] likelihood and [impact] impact, based on [evidence].
3. [Company] reduces it by [control], owned by [role].
4. The early warning sign is [indicator]; when it reaches [threshold], [response] follows.
5. If the risk occurs despite the controls, [contingency] limits the loss to [amount / period].
6. [Risk] is transferred through [insurance / contract clause], covering [scope] up to [amount].
7. [Company] accepts [minor risk] because [reason], and monitors it [frequency].
8. If the business does not proceed with this plan, [do-nothing consequence].
9. The downside case in section 10 models [risk] and shows [effect on cash / repayment].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Name specific, decision-relevant risks for this business; a generic list of "market, operational, financial" risks reads as template.
- Pair every rated risk with a control and a named owner.
- Be calm and factual. Understating risks damages trust more than naming them.
- Link each major risk to the downside case in the financials.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "Risks include market, operational and financial risks, which will be mitigated through robust management." → After: "The largest risk is losing the supermarket contract that provides 45% of revenue. We reduce it by adding two chains in year one and holding a 90-day notice clause in the current contract."
2. Before (wrong): "Currency fluctuations may affect the business." → After: "Imported packaging is priced in US dollars and makes up 18% of direct cost; a 10% shilling depreciation cuts gross margin by about two points, which the downside case includes (illustrative)."
3. Before (wrong): "There are minimal risks." → After: "Power cuts stop the cold room; a 30 kVA generator covers eight hours, and stock beyond that is moved to a partner's store under a signed agreement."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> The risk most likely to damage this plan is supplier failure during the dry season, when milk volumes around Mbarara fall and traders compete for supply. We rate it high likelihood and medium impact, based on last year's intake, which fell 35% between January and March (company records, illustrative). [Company] reduces the risk by paying farmers within seven days by mobile money, faster than traders, and by holding a standby agreement with two cooperatives outside the district. The collection manager tracks daily intake against a floor of 4,500 litres; below that, standby supply is called in. The downside case in section 10 shows repayment still holds with a 40% dry-season fall.

**(g) Red flags:** "no significant risks"; generic category lists; risks with no owner; mitigation that is only "close monitoring"; risks that do not appear in the financial downside case.

## 13 Implementation Timeline

*First principles: the book does not cover implementation scheduling.*

**(a) Purpose.** Show what will happen, in what order, by whom and by when, with the gates that decide whether to proceed, so the reader can judge whether the plan is executable and when funds will be needed.

**(b) Keywords:** phase, milestone, target date, owner, dependency, critical path, go/no-go gate, deliverable, resource, budget per phase, licence approval, lead time, buffer, progress measure, 90-day plan, change control.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. Phase [n], [name], runs from [month] to [month] and ends when [measurable milestone].
2. [Milestone] is due by [date] and owned by [role]; it depends on [prior milestone].
3. [Licence / permit] must be issued before [activity]; we allow [n] weeks for approval.
4. The gate at the end of phase [n] is [condition]; if it is not met, [alternative action].
5. Phase [n] needs [UGX amount] and [n] staff, drawn down in [month].
6. In the first 90 days, [Company] will [action 1], [action 2] and [action 3].
7. Progress is measured by [metric] and reported to [board / lender] [monthly].
8. Dates include a [n]-week buffer for [known delay source: rains, customs clearance, approvals].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Every milestone has a date, an owner and a measurable completion test.
- Show dependencies; a list of dates without them looks like a wish list.
- Pad dates for known delays and say so; readers trust realistic schedules.
- Match funding drawdowns and milestones to the cash-flow forecast.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "We will embark on an ambitious roll-out in the near future." → After: "The Mukono pack-house opens in April [year], after the lease is signed in January and the cold room is commissioned in March."
2. Before (wrong): "We will build a substantial customer base." → After: "We will sign 120 paying retainer clients by December [year]."
3. Before (wrong): "Timelines are subject to change." → After: "The schedule allows six weeks for the environmental approval; if it is not issued by May, construction moves to the dry season starting in June."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> Implementation runs in three phases over 18 months. Phase one, January to April, secures the Mukono site, orders the trucks and completes the approvals; it ends when the cold room passes its commissioning test. Phase two, May to August, starts deliveries to the four new supermarket chains and hires 14 packing staff. The board will not approve phase three, the second shift, until daily volume has held above eight tonnes for six consecutive weeks. The operations manager owns the schedule and reports progress to the board and the lender each month, and each phase draws on the loan only when its gate is met (illustrative).

**(g) Red flags:** "in the near future"; "Q1-Q4" with no milestones; milestones with no owner; no dependencies; timelines that do not match the cash flow; "subject to change" with no contingency.

## 14 AI Integration

*First principles: the book predates this section.*

**(a) Purpose.** Show which workflows the business will redesign and then automate or assist with AI, what it will cost and save, and how people, data and errors are governed, so the reader can judge whether AI adds measurable value without adding unmanaged risk.

**(b) Keywords:** workflow, process redesign, automation candidate, human review, tool, integration, data source, data protection, consent, accuracy measure, error rate, time saved, cost per task, pilot, adoption, training, governance owner, fallback.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Workflow] takes [n] staff-hours a [period] today; [Company] will first [simplify / remove step] and then use [tool type] to [task].
2. [Tool], costing [UGX amount] a month, will handle [task]; [role] reviews every [output type] before it reaches customers.
3. In a [n]-week pilot, the tool [result], measured by [metric] against [baseline].
4. The expected saving is [n] hours a [period], worth about [UGX amount] (estimate: hours × loaded wage).
5. Customer data used by the tool is limited to [fields], stored in [location], and processed under [data protection obligation; confirm current law].
6. If the tool fails or its accuracy falls below [threshold], [manual fallback] takes over.
7. [n] staff will be trained by [date]; [role] owns AI governance and reports [frequency].
8. [Company] will not use AI for [decision type] because [reason: fairness, liability, accuracy].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Redesign the process first; automate last. State what was removed before what was automated.
- Name the tool category and cost; treat vendor names and features as volatile and verify them before submission.
- Quantify time and cost with their basis; do not claim transformation.
- Always state where a human reviews and what happens when the tool is wrong.
- Keep AI claims proportionate to the business; a bakery does not need an AI strategy for every process.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "We will harness cutting-edge AI to unlock efficiencies and transform our business." → After: "Staff spend about 20 hours a week answering stock and price questions on WhatsApp; an assisted-reply tool drafts answers from the price list, and a staff member approves each one before it is sent."
2. Before (wrong): "AI will revolutionise our customer service." → After: "In a four-week pilot, drafted replies cut average response time from 2 hours to 25 minutes; staff edited 30% of drafts before sending (illustrative)."
3. Before (wrong): "Our AI is fully secure and ethical." → After: "The tool sees only product, price and order-status data; no customer phone numbers or payment details are sent to it, and the operations manager reviews a sample of 50 replies each month."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> [Company]'s three sales staff spend about 20 hours a week answering price and stock questions on WhatsApp, and customers wait two hours on average for a reply. The company first cut the question volume by publishing a daily price list to its broadcast channel. It will then use an assisted-reply tool, costing about UGX 350,000 a month [verify current pricing], to draft answers from the stock system; a staff member approves every draft before it is sent. In a four-week pilot, response time fell to 25 minutes. The sales supervisor owns the tool, reviews 50 replies a month for accuracy, and switches to manual replies if errors exceed 2% (illustrative).

**(g) Red flags:** "AI-powered" with no workflow; savings with no basis; no human review; no data-protection statement; vendor features stated as fact without a check date; AI proposed for decisions the business cannot explain to customers.

## 15 Appendices

*First principles: the book does not cover appendices.*

**(a) Purpose.** Hold the evidence that supports claims in the plan, organised so a reader can verify any figure quickly, so due diligence confirms the plan rather than contradicting it.

**(b) Keywords:** appendix, exhibit, evidence matrix, source document, certified copy, registration certificate, licence, audited accounts, bank statements, letters of intent, quotations, valuation report, CVs, survey instrument, data tables, cross-reference.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. Appendix [letter]: [document], dated [date], supporting the claim in section [n] that [claim].
2. The evidence matrix below links each material claim to its source document and page.
3. [Document] is a certified copy; the original is available for inspection at [place].
4. Quotations for [asset] were obtained from [n] suppliers on [date]; the lowest compliant quotation is used in the budget.
5. The survey questionnaire, sample method and raw results ([n] respondents, [dates]) are in Appendix [x].
6. [Document] is pending; it is expected by [date] and will be supplied before [disbursement / signing].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Every appendix earns its place by supporting a specific claim; cut anything that does not.
- Title each document by what it proves, not only by what it is.
- Keep the main plan readable without the appendices, and the appendices findable without the plan.
- Flag missing or pending documents honestly with a date.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "Appendix 4: Various supporting documents." → After: "Appendix D: Signed letters of intent from three supermarket chains (dated March–May [year]), supporting the year-one sales forecast in section 10."
2. Before (wrong): "Additional information available upon request." → After: "Audited accounts for [year] and [year] are in Appendix F; management accounts to [month] are in Appendix G."
3. Before (wrong): "See attached market research." → After: "Appendix C: Shopper survey, 220 respondents at two Ntinda supermarkets, [dates]; questionnaire and tabulated results."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative, evidence-matrix introduction)**
> The appendices hold the evidence behind every material claim in this plan. The evidence matrix on the next page lists 22 claims, each with the appendix, document and page that supports it. Registration, licences and tax clearance are in Appendix A; audited accounts for the last two years in Appendix B; the rider survey and its method in Appendix C; and signed letters of intent in Appendix D. Two documents are pending: the renewed trading licence, expected in March, and the asset valuation for the security offered, expected in April. Both will be supplied before any loan disbursement (illustrative).

**(g) Red flags:** "available upon request" for core evidence; unlabelled bundles; documents that contradict the plan; expired licences with no renewal note; appendices that pad the plan without supporting a claim.

## 16 Sustainability Strategy

*First principles: the book does not cover sustainability.*

**(a) Purpose.** Show the environmental and social issues that matter to this business, how they are managed and measured, and who is accountable, so lenders, DFIs and investors can judge environmental and social risk and the durability of the business.

**(b) Keywords:** material issue, materiality, environmental and social risk, climate risk, adaptation, emissions, water use, waste, by-product, employment, gender inclusion, community benefit, grievance mechanism, indicator, baseline, target, SDG contribution, accountable officer, reporting frequency.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. The two most material issues for [Company] are [issue 1] and [issue 2], because [link to operations or customers].
2. [Climate risk: e.g. rainfall variability] affects [supply / operations]; [Company] adapts by [measure].
3. [Company] will reduce [resource use] from [baseline] to [target] per [unit] by [date], measured by [meter / record].
4. [By-product / waste] is [sold / reused] as [product], earning [UGX amount] a year and avoiding [disposal cost].
5. The business will employ [n] people by [date], of whom [%] are women and [%] are under [age].
6. Complaints from the community or workers are handled through [grievance mechanism], with a response within [n] days.
7. [Role] is accountable for sustainability performance and reports to the board [frequency].
8. The business contributes to SDG target [number] through [activity], measured by [indicator].
9. Sustainability spending of [UGX amount] is included in the capital budget (section 10) and the timeline (section 13).

**(d) Tone rules**
- Choose two or three material issues for this business; a list covering every SDG reads as box-ticking.
- Every commitment has a baseline, target, date and owner.
- Present sustainability measures that also cut cost or risk as business decisions, with the figures.
- Avoid claims such as "carbon neutral" or "eco-friendly" without a method and verification.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "We are committed to a greener future and holistic sustainability." → After: "The cold room will draw 60% of its daytime power from a 15 kWp solar array, cutting generator diesel from about 400 to 150 litres a month (illustrative)."
2. Before (wrong): "The project contributes to all 17 SDGs." → After: "The business contributes to SDG 2 (zero hunger) through reduced post-harvest loss and SDG 5 (gender equality) through its target of 50% women among contracted farmers, both measured quarterly."
3. Before (wrong): "We will empower women and youth." → After: "Of the 14 packing jobs created in year one, at least 8 will go to women; the operations manager reports the figure to the board each quarter."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> Two sustainability issues matter most to [Company]: rainfall variability, which threatens supply from its rain-fed farmers, and the energy cost of cold storage. To protect supply, [Company] is helping 20 of its 60 contracted farmers install drip irrigation, financed through deductions from produce payments, so that dry-season volumes hold above 70% of the wet-season level.
