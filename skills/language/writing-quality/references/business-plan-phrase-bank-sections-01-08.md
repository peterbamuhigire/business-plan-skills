# Business-Plan Phrase Bank: Sections 01 to 08

Method source: power-paragraph sentence jobs and section emphasis adapted from Debelak, D. (2006) *Perfect Phrases for Business Proposals and Business Plans*, McGraw-Hill. All templates and examples are original. Figures in examples are illustrative, not market facts. "Before" lines are deliberately bad examples and must never be copied into a plan.

**Coverage note.** The book gives phrase guidance for the executive summary, business concept, customers, market drivers, market size, niche, competitive advantage and marketing strategy outline (used in 01 to 07). For 02 company overview, 06 competition and 08 operations it gives only key points; those entries apply the same power-paragraph grammar from first principles.

Each entry gives: (a) purpose and reader decision, (b) keywords, (c) slot-templates, (d) tone rules, (e) before→after rewrites, (f) an illustrative power paragraph, (g) red-flag phrases.

## 01 Executive Summary

**(a) Purpose.** Compress the whole case into one to two pages so the reader decides whether to read on and, for a lender, whether to open the financials. Write it last, from the finished sections.

**(b) Keywords:** registered, based in, serves, sells, earns, gross margin, key advantage, market driver, paying customers, pilot, order book, seeks, use of funds, owner's contribution, repayment from, security offered, break-even, milestone.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Company], a [legal form] registered in [year] and based in [place], [does what] for [whom].
2. [Company]'s niche is [specific offer] for [specific customer], who today [named alternative].
3. [Company] sells [product] at [UGX price] per [unit] against a direct cost of [UGX amount], a gross margin of [%].
4. Demand is rising because [driver, dated], which is forcing [customers] to [action].
5. [Company]'s key advantage over [named alternative] is [one advantage], which matters because [customer need].
6. Since [date], [Company] has [traction: n customers, UGX revenue, n repeat orders].
7. The founders bring [n] years in [field]; [named person] previously [relevant result].
8. [Company] seeks [UGX amount] as [loan/equity/grant] to [one clear purpose], alongside [UGX amount] from the founders.
9. The loan will be repaid from [cash source] over [n] months; the projections show a debt service coverage ratio of [x] in year one (base case).
10. The investment takes [Company] from [current capacity or position] to [new position] by [date].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Every sentence carries a number or a named fact; delete any sentence that does not.
- Follow the reader's scorecard order: customer, driver, market, niche, advantage, team, economics, ask.
- Pass the "banker's restatement" test: a reader can restate the business in four sentences after one read.
- State the ask once, precisely, with its purpose. Do not bury it on page two.
- Avoid opening with the country's economy, the sector's importance or the company's mission.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "XYZ Ltd is an innovative, customer-focused company poised for exponential growth in the vibrant East African market." → After: "XYZ Ltd supplies chilled milk from 400 smallholder farmers around Mbarara to two processors, earning UGX 150 more per litre than bicycle traders (illustrative)."
2. Before (wrong): "We are seeking funding to scale our operations and realise our vision." → After: "The company seeks a UGX 480 million asset loan to open four chilling points in Isingiro and Kiruhura, raising daily intake from 6,000 to 20,000 litres (illustrative)."
3. Before (wrong): "Our experienced team is passionate about transforming agriculture." → After: "The managing director ran milk collection for a regional processor for seven years; the finance lead is a practising accountant (illustrative)."
4. Before (wrong): "The business is expected to be highly profitable." → After: "The base case projects break-even in month 14 and an EBITDA margin of 12% in year three, on the assumption that intake reaches 15,000 litres a day (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> [Company] buys milk from 400 smallholder farmers around Mbarara, chills it within two hours of milking and sells it to two processors that pay a quality premium of UGX 150 a litre. Processors pay that premium because milk from bicycle traders often arrives warm and is rejected. [Company] is the first operator in the district to offer chilling as a per-litre service, so farmers need no equipment of their own. The company seeks a UGX 480 million asset loan to open four more chilling points; the founders are contributing UGX 120 million, and the base case shows the loan repaid from collection margins within 42 months.

**(g) Red flags:** "poised for growth"; "huge untapped market"; "no competition"; "conservative projections" with no stated assumptions; an ask with no purpose; "we are seeking investors" with no amount; a summary longer than two pages.

## 02 Company Overview

**(a) Purpose.** Establish what the business is, who owns and controls it, its legal standing, history and stage, so the reader can judge legitimacy and the credibility of the story that follows.

**(b) Keywords:** registered, legal form, registration number, shareholders, shareholding, founded, premises, licences held, trading history, stage, mission, vision, business model, milestones to date.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Company] is a [private company limited by shares / partnership / cooperative] registered with [registry] in [month year], registration number [x].
2. Its shareholders are [names], holding [%] and [%]; [name] is managing director.
3. The company operates from [premises, owned/leased until year] in [place].
4. [Company] was founded in [year] after [founder] saw [specific problem, with evidence].
5. The business model is simple: [Company] [buys/makes/provides] [what] and earns [revenue source] per [unit].
6. Since [year], [Company] has reached [milestone 1] and [milestone 2].
7. [Company] holds [licence/permit], valid until [date]; [pending licence] is expected by [date].
8. [Company]'s mission is to [concrete purpose] for [whom] in [place]; progress is measured by [metric].
9. [Company] is at the [pre-revenue / early revenue / established] stage, with [n] staff and [UGX amount] in revenue for [period].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Legal facts first, story second. The reader checks legitimacy before being persuaded.
- The mission is one sentence a staff member could act on. If it could describe any firm, rewrite it.
- The origin story names a real problem and a real observation, not a feeling.
- Keep history to what explains today's capability; move the rest to an appendix.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "Our mission is to be the leading provider of world-class solutions that elevate our clients." → After: "Our mission is to get tested seed to 5,000 farmers in Lango sub-region before each planting season."
2. Before (wrong): "The company was born out of a passion for excellence." → After: "The founder started the company in 2022 after losing two maize harvests to counterfeit seed bought in Lira market."
3. Before (wrong): "We are a dynamic and growing company." → After: "The company has traded for three years, employs 11 people and recorded UGX 640 million in sales in 2025 (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> [Company] is a private company limited by shares, registered in 2022 and owned equally by its two founders. It operates a leased warehouse in Lira and holds the seed-dealer registration required for certified seed [confirm current licence name and validity before submission]. The company buys certified maize and bean seed from two licensed breeders and sells it through 38 village agents, earning about UGX 1,200 per kilogram (illustrative). Its mission is narrow and measurable: tested seed in the hands of 5,000 farmers before each planting season. It reached 2,100 farmers in 2025 and now plans to expand into Apac and Kole.

**(g) Red flags:** "leading provider" with no ranking source; "a passion for"; missing registration or ownership details; history that runs for pages; a vision that promises to "transform Africa".

## 03 Products and Services

**(a) Purpose.** Show what the customer buys, the need it meets, how it performs against alternatives and what it costs to deliver, so the reader can judge whether customers will pay and whether the business can deliver profitably.

**(b) Keywords:** product, service, specification, feature, benefit, customer outcome, price point, unit cost, gross margin per unit, warranty, service level, development stage, tested, certified, roadmap, intellectual property.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Product] lets [customer] [result] without [pain they face today].
2. Customers currently [named alternative], which costs them [UGX amount / time] and [drawback].
3. [Product] costs [UGX amount] to deliver and sells for [UGX amount], a gross margin of [%] per unit.
4. [Product] has been [tested / certified] by [body] on [date]; [result].
5. The core offer is [product]; [add-on 1] and [add-on 2] are sold as options.
6. Each [unit/visit/subscription] includes [components], delivered within [time].
7. [Company] guarantees [specific service level]; if it is missed, [remedy].
8. The next version, planned for [date], adds [feature] because [n] customers asked for it.
9. [Product] is protected by [trademark / exclusive licence / trade secret], [status].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Lead with the customer's result, then the feature that produces it.
- Name the alternative the customer uses today and compare on the criterion they rank highest.
- Put unit economics in the section; a lender wants to see margin per unit here, not only in the financials.
- Separate what exists and is sold today from what is planned. Label development stage honestly.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "Our innovative solution offers a seamless, holistic experience." → After: "Members check their SACCO balance by USSD on any phone in under 30 seconds, without visiting the branch."
2. Before (wrong): "We offer high-quality products at affordable prices." → After: "The 20-litre water filter sells for UGX 145,000, removes [tested contaminant] to [standard] (test report, Appendix C) and costs a household less than boiling water with charcoal over one year (illustrative)."
3. Before (wrong): "Our app has many features." → After: "The app does three jobs for a shop owner: records sales, reorders stock from two approved wholesalers, and issues receipts."
4. Before (wrong): "State-of-the-art equipment ensures robust performance." → After: "The mill processes 800 kg of maize an hour at a measured reject rate of 2%, against about 8% on the hammer mills farmers use today (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> [Company] sells a 20-litre ceramic water filter to households in Gulu and Lira that now boil drinking water over charcoal. The filter costs UGX 145,000, lasts two years with one cartridge change, and a household using it spends less than it would on charcoal for boiling over the same period (illustrative calculation, Appendix D). A certified laboratory test [name, date] confirms it meets the drinking-water standard for the contaminants tested. [Company]'s direct cost is UGX 88,000 per unit, a gross margin of 39%. Customers can pay in four monthly mobile-money instalments, which removes the main barrier the pilot identified.

**(g) Red flags:** feature lists with no customer result; "unique" with no comparison; no price; no unit cost; planned features described in the present tense; "patent-pending" with no application number.

## 04 Market Analysis

**(a) Purpose.** Show the size, structure and direction of the market and the driver that makes the opportunity real now, so the reader can judge whether the market is big enough and moving the right way.

**(b) Keywords:** broad market, narrow market, market size, method (top-down / bottom-up), source and date, growth rate, market driver, forced action, why now, industry structure, entry barriers, substitutes, seasonality, regulation, price trend.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Company] competes in the [broad] market for [purpose], which includes [named alternatives], and within it the [narrow] market for [category].
2. We estimate the [narrow] market in [place] at about [value] a year, calculated as [n customers] × [average annual spend] ([sources, dates]).
3. According to [source, date], [metric] grew from [a] to [b] between [year] and [year].
4. Demand is driven by [driver type: regulation / technology / lifestyle / customer demand / related market / population / business practice]: [specific driver, dated].
5. The driver forces [customers] to [action]; most now respond by [costly workaround].
6. The opportunity is recent: only since [date] has [condition] made [offer] viable.
7. The market is [fragmented / concentrated]: the [n] largest suppliers hold about [%] ([source]).
8. Demand peaks in [months] and falls in [months], which the cash-flow forecast reflects.
9. No reliable figure exists for [metric] in [place]; [Company] will measure it during [phase].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Label every market figure as broad or narrow, give its geography, source and date, and show the arithmetic.
- Name the driver and the action it forces. A trend that forces no action is background, not a driver.
- Answer "why has no one done this already?" before the reader asks.
- Prefer a small, well-derived number to a large, unsourced one. Bottom-up estimates carry more weight with lenders.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "The digital revolution is transforming business across Africa, creating unprecedented opportunities." → After: "Since [date], [authority] has required [businesses] to [action] [confirm current rule]; most small wholesalers in Kikuubo respond by hiring clerks to re-key sales."
2. Before (wrong): "The market is huge and growing rapidly." → After: "We estimate the narrow market for SME payroll processing in Kampala at about UGX [value] a year: [n] registered employers × UGX [amount] average annual spend ([sources, dates])."
3. Before (wrong): "In today's competitive landscape, demand for healthcare continues to grow." → After: "Three new estates in Kira added about [n] households between [year] and [year] ([source]); none has a paediatric clinic within 5 km."
4. Before (wrong): "Studies show that consumers prefer online shopping." → After: "In our survey of 220 shoppers at two Ntinda supermarkets in [month year], 61% had ordered groceries by WhatsApp in the previous month (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> TillSync competes in the narrow market for point-of-sale invoicing software among wholesale traders in Kampala. Using [authority, date] registration counts, we estimate about [n] wholesalers in Kikuubo and Nakawa are now required to issue electronic tax invoices from every till [confirm current scope of the rule]. Most comply by paying a clerk to re-enter each sale in the tax portal, at a monthly cost we measured at UGX 600,000–900,000 among 14 traders interviewed (illustrative). The requirement is recent, which explains why no local product yet connects existing tills directly. TillSync does exactly that, for a monthly fee below the cost of the clerk it replaces.

**(g) Red flags:** a round-number market size with no method; "billion-dollar market" for a local business; "the market is growing rapidly"; a global figure used for a district business; drivers with no date; a market defined so broadly that the company's share looks tiny and meaningless.

## 05 Target Market

**(a) Purpose.** Define the customers the business will actually serve, count them, show how they spend today and why they will switch, so the reader can judge whether enough customers exist within reach.

**(b) Keywords:** customer segment, count, location, catchment, current spend, purchase frequency, named alternative, buying criteria, decision-maker, willingness to pay, customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, repeat rate, two-year focus, persona.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Company] targets the estimated [n] [households / businesses] in [place] who currently [buy from / travel to] [named alternative] for [need].
2. They spend about [UGX amount] a [period] on [category] ([source or survey, date]).
3. They buy [frequency] and decide mainly on [criterion 1] and [criterion 2]; price ranks [position].
4. The buying decision is made by [role]; [role] influences it.
5. For the first two years, [Company] will market only to [narrow group] in [places].
6. We will not target [excluded group] in this period, because [reason: margin / capacity / distance].
7. In [n] interviews, [n] customers described the problem as "[their words]".
8. Each customer is worth about [UGX amount] over [period] (lifetime value), against an acquisition cost of [UGX amount].
9. [n] customers have already [signed letters of intent / paid deposits / joined the waiting list].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Define customers by count, place, current spending and named alternative, not by age and income alone. How customers spend matters more than how much they have.
- Restrict the target to where the company will market within two years; a tight focus reads as competence.
- Quote customers' own words where interviews exist, and give the interview count.
- Show the arithmetic for lifetime value and acquisition cost, with each input's status (fact, assumption, estimate).

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "Our target market is middle- and upper-income families aged 25–45 in Kampala." → After: "We target the estimated 3,500 families with children under 12 in Kira, Najjera and Kyanja who now drive into central Kampala for children's dental care (count method in Appendix B)."
2. Before (wrong): "Everyone needs our product." → After: "Our first customers are the 180 private clinics in Kampala and Wakiso with 20 to 150 staff; hospitals and single-doctor practices come later, if at all."
3. Before (wrong): "Customers are eager to embrace our offering." → After: "Of 40 boda riders interviewed at three Kawempe stages, 29 said a day off the road costs them more than a month's subscription (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> BodaCare targets the estimated 4,000 boda-boda riders who work from registered stages in Kawempe division (count from stage-association registers, [date]). Riders spend UGX 60,000–120,000 a month on roadside repairs and lose a day's fares whenever the bike is off the road (rider survey, n=40, illustrative). They choose a mechanic on speed first and price second. Most use roadside mechanics who hold no parts and cannot come to them. For its first 18 months BodaCare will serve Kawempe only, where its van can reach any stage within 45 minutes, and will not market to riders elsewhere until that response time holds.

**(g) Red flags:** demographic-only definitions; "the whole of East Africa"; "millennials"; no count; customers described as "eager" or "excited"; lifetime value without the formula; a target that includes everyone who might ever buy.

## 06 Competitive Analysis

**(a) Purpose.** Show who else serves these customers, directly and indirectly, and the one advantage that will win and keep share, so the reader can judge whether the business can take customers from existing options.

**(b) Keywords:** direct competitor, indirect competitor, substitute, do-nothing option, buying criteria, comparison matrix, price position, market share, key advantage, barrier to imitation, competitor response, concede, switching cost, exclusive agreement.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Company] competes directly with [named firms] and indirectly with [other ways customers get the same result, including doing nothing].
2. On the criteria customers rank highest, [c1] and [c2], [Company] leads on [c1] and matches on [c2].
3. [Competitor] is strongest on [area]; [Company] does not compete on [area it concedes].
4. [Company]'s key advantage is [one advantage], which matters because [customer need].
5. The advantage holds because [barrier: exclusive contract / location lease / licence / data / relationships] would take a competitor [period] to match.
6. Most customers today choose [indirect alternative] because [reason]; [Company] converts them by [mechanism].
7. We expect [competitor] to respond by [likely response]; [Company] will [counter-plan].
8. The table below compares price, [criterion] and [criterion] for each main competitor, from [mystery shopping / price lists, date].

**(d) Tone rules**
- State one key advantage, tied to the customer's top buying criterion. Five differentiators read as none.
- Measure the advantage against the same market definition used in section 04.
- Respect competitors. Conceding a point where they are stronger makes the rest believable.
- Include the do-nothing option and informal providers; in many East African markets they hold most of the volume.
- Anticipate the competitor response and state the plan for it.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "We have no competition." → After: "Customers now use three alternatives: informal traders, imported bulk packs and home preparation. Informal traders hold most of the volume; our advantage is certified hygiene with delivery inside two hours."
2. Before (wrong): "Our competitive advantages include quality, affordability, innovation, customer service and experienced staff." → After: "Our advantage over roadside mechanics is response time: a van reaches any Kawempe stage within 45 minutes. Riders rank this above price."
3. Before (wrong): "Competitors cannot match our unmatched service." → After: "The two established agencies price 20% below us and we do not try to match them; we win clinics that need booking integration, which neither offers."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> Kira Paediatric Dental competes directly with four general dental clinics within 5 km and indirectly with the central-Kampala specialists most families now drive to. Parents we interviewed ranked closeness and a child-friendly setting above price (n=35, illustrative). The general clinics are cheaper and we do not compete with them on routine adult care. Our advantage is that we are the only practice in the area equipped and staffed for children, with a paediatric-trained dentist and sedation-free techniques [verify local competitor offer before submission]. A general clinic would need a specialist hire and a refit to match this, which we estimate would take at least a year.

**(g) Red flags:** "no competitors"; "we are better in every way"; a matrix in which the company wins every row; competitors unnamed; no indirect or informal alternatives; no expected response.

## 07 Marketing and Sales Strategy

**(a) Purpose.** Show how the business will reach the target customers, convince them and close sales at an acquisition cost the margins can carry, so the reader can judge whether the revenue projection is achievable.

**(b) Keywords:** positioning, message, price, channel, low-cost tactic, referral, partner, sales process, conversion rate, cost per lead, customer acquisition cost, sales target, sales capacity, pipeline, launch sequence, marketing budget.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Brand] is positioned as the [category] for [customer] who want [benefit] without [pain].
2. Every channel repeats one message: [single-sentence promise].
3. [Offer] is priced at [UGX amount], [premium / parity / below] [named alternative], giving a gross margin of [%].
4. The first [n] months rely on low-cost tactics: [tactic 1], [tactic 2] and [tactic 3], each with a measurable target.
5. [Partner] will [display / recommend / bundle] [offer] to its [n] [customers] in return for [commission / benefit].
6. Sales follow [n] steps: [lead source] → [first contact] → [demonstration / trial] → [order], with [x]% expected to convert at each step (assumption, from pilot).
7. The target of [n] customers by [date] requires [n] leads at a [%] conversion rate, within a budget of [UGX amount].
8. [n] sales staff will each handle [n] accounts; [role] owns the sales target.
9. Paid advertising starts only after [referral/organic] tactics produce a conversion rate above [%].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Show the chain from budget to leads to sales to revenue; a lender tests the revenue line here.
- Name the channel and the place: the market day, radio station, WhatsApp group, association, estate.
- Lead with low-cost, high-trust tactics; paid media should follow proof, not replace it.
- Separate marketing (awareness and message) from sales (how orders are secured) and give each an owner.
- For the full marketing-plan wording, use `marketing-plan-phrase-bank.md`.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "We will leverage social media, radio, flyers and word of mouth to create awareness." → After: "For six months we will focus on the 2,400 Ntinda and Bukoto households already ordering groceries by WhatsApp, using a UGX 5,000 referral credit and weekly price lists to opted-in customers (illustrative)."
2. Before (wrong): "Our marketing strategy will drive massive growth." → After: "The target of 600 repeat customers by June needs about 2,000 contacts at a 40% trial rate and a 75% repeat rate (assumptions from the eight-week pilot)."
3. Before (wrong): "We will harness the power of digital marketing." → After: "Facebook and Instagram adverts start in month four, only if referrals cost more than UGX 8,000 per new customer."
4. Before (wrong): "Our sales team is highly motivated." → After: "Two field agents will each visit 12 pharmacies a week; the sales manager reviews visit-to-order conversion every Friday."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> [Company] will reach its first 600 repeat customers by June through the 2,400 households in Ntinda and Bukoto that already buy groceries by WhatsApp (estate-manager counts, [date]). Three low-cost tactics carry the plan: a UGX 5,000 credit for each referral, a weekly price list sent only to opted-in customers, and an agreement with two estate managers to share the offer with tenants. The pilot converted 40% of contacts to a first order and 75% of those to a second (illustrative). At those rates the plan needs about 2,000 contacts and a marketing budget of UGX 4.8 million over six months, an acquisition cost of UGX 8,000 per repeat customer.

**(g) Red flags:** a list of channels with no targets; "go viral"; "aggressive marketing"; a budget with no link to sales; conversion rates with no source; "the product will sell itself".

## 08 Operations Plan

**(a) Purpose.** Show how the business will produce and deliver what it sells, at what cost and capacity, with which people, suppliers and controls, so the reader can judge whether the operation can deliver the projected volume reliably.

**(b) Keywords:** location, premises, capacity, utilisation, process, standard operating procedure, supplier, lead time, stock holding, quality control, reject rate, fixed cost, variable cost, staffing, shift, licence, maintenance, contingency.

**(c) Slot-templates**
1. [Company] operates from [premises] in [place], chosen because [reason tied to customers or suppliers].
2. Current capacity is [n units] a [period]; the plan raises it to [n] by [date] through [investment].
3. The process runs in [n] steps: [step 1] → [step 2] → [step 3], taking [time] from order to delivery.
4. [Company] buys [input] from [n] suppliers, [named or described]; no supplier provides more than [%] of supply.
5. Quality is checked at [points] against [standard]; the measured reject rate is [%].
6. Fixed costs are kept low by [leasing / outsourcing / shared facilities]; the largest cost lines are fixed by [contract / quote] until [date].
7. The operation will be running within [n] weeks of funding, because [premises / equipment / staff] are already [secured / quoted].
8. Staffing grows from [n] to [n] by [date]; each new role is added when [volume trigger] is reached.
9. If [key supplier / power / equipment] fails, [contingency] keeps [output] at [level].

**(d) Tone rules**
- Give essentials, advantages and controls, not a procedures manual; the manual belongs in an appendix.
- Tie every operational choice to a customer need or a cost figure the financials use.
- State capacity and utilisation in numbers, and show that capacity matches the sales forecast.
- Name the operational risk and its control; readers distrust an operation with no failure modes.

**(e) Before → after**
1. Before (wrong): "Our robust operations ensure seamless delivery." → After: "Orders received by 2 pm are packed the same day and delivered by 10 am the next day by two contracted vans; on-time delivery in the last quarter was 94% (illustrative)."
2. Before (wrong): "We work with reliable suppliers." → After: "Maize comes from three aggregators in Masindi and Hoima; none supplies more than 40%, and each has a signed price schedule to December."
3. Before (wrong): "The business will have adequate capacity." → After: "The two mills can process 12 tonnes a day on one shift; the year-two forecast needs 9.5 tonnes, leaving room for a second shift before new equipment is needed (illustrative)."

**(f) Power paragraph (illustrative)**
> [Company] packs fresh vegetables for supermarkets from a leased pack-house in Namanve, 20 minutes from its three largest customers. Current capacity is four tonnes a day on one shift, and the pack-house runs at 90% of that at peak (illustrative). Produce comes from 60 contracted farmers in Mukono and Kayunga; no farmer group supplies more than a quarter of volume. Every crate is graded against the buyer's written specification before loading, and the rejection rate at supermarket docks has averaged 3% over the last six months. A second pack-house and four refrigerated trucks will raise capacity to 12 tonnes, and the site lease and truck quotations are already in hand.

**(g) Red flags:** "state-of-the-art facilities" with no capacity figure; no named suppliers; operations described with no costs; "we will hire as needed"; no contingency for power, water or supply failure.
