# Document And Investor Polish

Use this reference for business plans, executive summaries, funding requests, investor memos, board papers, proposals, covering letters, and other high-stakes commercial documents.

## Decision-Ready Standard

A premium commercial document should reduce the reader's work. It should make the decision, evidence, economics, risk, and next step clear without making the reader assemble the argument themselves.

## Executive Summary Upgrade

The executive summary must function as a compressed investment or funding case.

Require:

- opening sentence with the core commercial fact or opportunity
- business concept in plain language
- target customer and pain point
- solution and differentiator
- market size or demand evidence
- traction or validation
- economics and financial highlights
- team credibility
- funding ask and use of funds
- risk controls
- clear next step

Avoid:

- company history as the opening
- unsupported market excitement
- vague statements of quality
- financial highlights without basis
- a buried funding ask

## Business Plan Section Polish

For each section, add:

- answer-first opening paragraph
- specific figures, named places, named segments, or operating mechanisms
- link to the governing thesis of the plan
- section-specific proof or assumptions
- implications for revenue, cost, risk, operations, funding, or execution

Section writing should not read like a textbook. It should explain this business in this market under these constraints.

## Proposal And Client-Facing Documents

Use a situation-led structure:

1. Client situation.
2. Problem or opportunity.
3. Implication if unresolved.
4. Recommended approach.
5. Workplan and deliverables.
6. Proof of capability.
7. Investment and terms.
8. Decision path and next step.

Make the scope visible. Premium clients pay more readily when they can see the thinking, sequence, governance, and quality controls behind the work.

## Investor And Lender Language

Investor-facing copy should emphasise:

- growth thesis
- repeatable acquisition or distribution engine
- unit economics
- margin logic
- capital efficiency
- milestones
- team capability
- risk management
- exit, repayment, or return logic

Lender-facing copy should emphasise:

- repayment source
- DSCR or repayment capacity where available
- collateral or guarantees where relevant
- cash-flow timing
- controls and records
- borrower character and track record
- downside risk management

DFI or grant-facing copy should emphasise:

- development impact
- safeguard readiness
- governance
- implementation capacity
- measurable outputs and outcomes
- sustainability after funding

## Premium Document Texture

Premium documents usually include:

- clean signposting
- concise headings that make claims
- short paragraphs
- tables for comparisons and financial summaries
- charts or figure callouts where useful
- appendix references for high-stakes proof
- consistent terms across sections
- a final issue list where claims remain unsupported

Avoid decorative language. The document feels premium when the reader can see expert judgment and disciplined editing.

## Funding Ask Polish

A funding ask must state:

- amount
- instrument: grant, debt, equity, lease, blended finance, or other
- use of funds
- timing
- expected commercial result
- repayment, return, or impact logic
- risk controls
- documents or evidence attached
- next decision requested

Never let the reader infer the ask from surrounding prose.

## Claim Calibration

Use the right strength of language:

- Proven: historic data or verified customer results exist.
- Validated: credible pilot, interviews, pre-orders, LOIs, or repeatable evidence exists.
- Expected: projection based on stated assumptions.
- Proposed: management recommendation or plan.
- Assumed: working input that needs confirmation.

This distinction protects credibility and improves due-diligence readiness.

## Final Review Questions

- Can a busy reader understand the decision case in two minutes?
- Does the strongest point appear early?
- Are all numbers internally consistent?
- Are assumptions labelled clearly?
- Does each section advance the same thesis?
- Are risks acknowledged and answered?
- Is the next step explicit?
- Would this document support a premium fee or serious funding conversation?
