# Premium Writing Quality Gate

Use this gate before finalising any high-stakes business plan, proposal, investor document, article, landing page, service page, or client-facing copy.

## 1. Commercial Purpose

- State the reader's decision in one sentence.
- State the next action the document should earn: continue reading, approve, fund, call, request a proposal, subscribe, share, or buy.
- Remove any section that does not help the reader make that decision.
- Make the strongest commercial point visible early, not hidden in the middle.

## 2. Differentiation

- Name the category: what kind of business, offer, document, or article is this?
- Name the contrast: what ordinary alternatives do, and what this does better.
- State the specific buyer or reader segment. Avoid "everyone", "SMEs", or "customers" without qualification.
- Include at least one proof-backed differentiator: process, data, speed, access, method, cost structure, relationship, location, service model, team, compliance, distribution, or proprietary insight.

## 3. Proof And Credibility

- Support every important claim with one of: number, named place, named segment, source, customer example, operational mechanism, financial logic, qualification, or appendix reference.
- Convert vague proof into concrete proof. "Strong demand" needs orders, enquiries, waiting list, repeat purchases, interviews, occupancy, conversion rates, market data, or credible proxy evidence.
- Separate fact, assumption, projection, and recommendation.
- Use confidence language that matches the evidence. Do not make projections sound like historic performance.

## 4. Reader Value

- Translate features into outcomes using the chain: feature -> practical benefit -> commercial consequence.
- Add the "so what" after technical points. Explain why the reader should care.
- Prioritise expensive outcomes: revenue, margin, time saved, risk reduced, compliance achieved, status improved, access gained, capital protected, or decision confidence.
- For premium offers, show service quality: onboarding, diagnosis, customisation, communication cadence, delivery assurance, aftercare, and escalation path.

## 5. Structure And Flow

- Put the answer first, then the support.
- Use headings that make a claim, not labels only.
- Keep one main idea per paragraph.
- Use bullets for lists of three or more items, but make each bullet substantial.
- Make transitions logical: problem -> cause -> implication -> solution -> proof -> next step.
- If the reader must compare options, use a table.

## 6. Voice

- Write in a calm, senior, commercially literate tone.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Avoid breathless launch language, luxury cliches, vague authority claims, and fake urgency.
- Remove AI tells: generic first sentences, overused abstractions, symmetrical three-part lists, and phrases that could fit any business.
- Use British English and East African professional tone where the wider suite requires it.

## 7. SEO And AI Search

- State the topic, audience, location, and useful answer early.
- Use natural domain vocabulary, not keyword repetition.
- Include answerable sections: definition, who it is for, steps, costs, risks, examples, comparison, checklist, and next action where relevant.
- Make claims easy for search engines and AI answer engines to extract through clear headings, concise summary sentences, tables, and specific entities.

## 8. Premium Fee Test

Before delivery, ask:

- Would a client believe this took expert judgment, not prompt output?
- Does the document say something a generic competitor would not say?
- Are the strongest claims supported well enough for a sceptical reader?
- Does the structure reduce the reader's work?
- Is the writing elegant because it is precise, not because it is decorated?
- Does the ending create a clear next step?

If any answer is no, revise before finalising.
