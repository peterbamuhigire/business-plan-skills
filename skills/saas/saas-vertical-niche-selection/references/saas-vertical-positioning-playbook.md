# SaaS Vertical Positioning Playbook

## 1. The Vertical Fit Scorecard (50 points)

For each candidate vertical, score 1-5 on each dimension:

| Dimension | Question | Score (1-5) |
|---|---|---|
| Founder domain knowledge | Does the founder have material insider knowledge? | _ |
| TAM | Is the market large enough to be interesting? | _ |
| Customer pain intensity | Is the pain bad enough to drive paid adoption? | _ |
| Willingness-to-pay | Do customers have budget for software? | _ |
| Competition density | Is the category over-served? (Lower = better, scored inverse) | _ |
| Cycle-length tolerance | Can the company afford the cycle? | _ |
| Network effects within vertical | Does winning one customer help win the next? | _ |
| Channel access | Does the founder have channel access? | _ |
| Geography fit | Does the vertical map to the founder's geography? | _ |
| Regulatory complexity | Sometimes higher = moat | _ (judgement call) |
| **TOTAL** | | _ / 50 |

40+: pursue. 30-39: consider. <30: walk away.

## 2. African Vertical SaaS Opportunity Map

### Fintech / Financial Services
**Sub-niches:** B2B payments, neobanking, lending, insurance (insurtech), wealth management, remittances, FX, embedded finance.

**Examples:** Flutterwave, Paystack, Yoco, Lulalend, MoneyHash, MNT-Halan, Carbon, Kuda, Branch, Tala, Pula (insurance), Mama Money.

**African moat sources:** regulatory licences, payment-rail integrations, KYC infrastructure (Smile ID), trust.

### Agritech
**Sub-niches:** Cooperative management, supply-chain traceability, farmer-financing, equipment-sharing, climate-resilience.

**Examples:** Apollo Agriculture, Releaf, FarmCrowdy, Twiga, Tulaa, Hello Tractor, Lentera, FarmKonnect, Babban Gona.

**African moat sources:** field-officer network, donor/government partnerships, local-language support, weather-data integration.

### Logistics / Mobility
**Sub-niches:** Last-mile delivery, freight, fleet management, e-commerce fulfilment, mobility.

**Examples:** Sendy, Lori, Field Intelligence, MAX, OmniRetail (logistics layer), Sokowatch / Wasoko (B2B+logistics), Sun King last-mile.

**African moat sources:** rider/fleet network, marketplace density, address-system handling.

### Healthtech
**Sub-niches:** EHR, supply-chain, insurance / claims, telemedicine, diagnostic SaaS.

**Examples:** mPharma, Helium Health, Reliance HMO, RecoMed, Vezeeta, Field Intelligence (Shelf Life).

**African moat sources:** regulatory licences, EHR integrations with public health systems, distribution to under-served markets.

### Edtech
**Sub-niches:** Higher-ed LMS, K-12, vocational training, language learning, professional credentialing.

**Examples:** uLesson, Andela (talent + training), AltSchool Africa, Moringa School, eduMe, ShulesPlus.

**African moat sources:** local-curriculum alignment, mobile-first delivery, payment-rail integration for fees.

### Cleantech / Energy
**Sub-niches:** PAYG solar, mini-grids, e-mobility, smart meters.

**Examples:** M-KOPA, Sun King, Zola Electric, Husk Power, Ampersand, BasiGo, Roam, Wavemobile.

**African moat sources:** field-service network, embedded financing, partnerships with mobile-money, regulatory.

### Public-sector / GovTech
**Sub-niches:** Tax, payments-to-government, identity, court systems, procurement, e-government.

**Examples:** Mojaloop (payment), Smile ID (KYC), Bayanat (Egypt), BlueData (Nigeria), Mosabi.

**African moat sources:** regulatory access, security certification, multi-year procurement experience.

### HR / People / Gig Platforms
**Sub-niches:** Payroll, HRIS, benefits, gig-worker platforms, recruitment.

**Examples:** Workpay, SeamlessHR, Eden Life, PaystackHR, MoneyMaster (NG payroll), Bento Africa.

**African moat sources:** tax compliance per country, payroll-to-bank-and-mobile-money, regulatory.

### B2B Marketplaces
**Sub-niches:** MSME procurement, wholesale aggregation, agricultural marketplaces.

**Examples:** Sabi, Wasoko (Twiga acquired), MarketForce, OmniRetail, TradeDepot, Yassir.

**African moat sources:** logistics integration, embedded finance, network effects.

### Construction / Real Estate
**Sub-niches:** Project management, financing, listings, property management.

**Examples:** Estate Intel, Spleet, GMG Africa, Famasi (property management).

**African moat sources:** local data ownership, financing partnerships.

## 3. The Niche Within the Vertical (geography × sub-segment × use-case)

Specificity unlocks pricing power (Kennedy). For African SaaS:

- "Fintech for Africa" → too generic
- "B2B payments for Nigerian SME importers" → niche
- "USD-denominated escrow + FX for Nigerian SME imports from China" → super-niche → premium pricing

The intersection often produces the moat:
- Geography (country / region within country)
- × Sub-segment (size / type of customer)
- × Use-case (specific pain)
- × Channel access (how do you reach them?)

## 4. Niche-Pricing-Lift in African Context

Kennedy's general rule (100-500% lift from niche-specific positioning) applies, but in African contexts the lift can be even higher because:
- Incumbents are usually generic global tools (Salesforce, SAP) that don't fit
- Local incumbents are usually spreadsheets / paper
- Niche-fit lets you anchor pricing to value delivered, not competitive comparison

Example: a generic CRM at $30/user/month in Nigeria → a Nigerian-SME-specific CRM with M-Pesa / Paystack integration at $50-80/user/month justifies the premium.

## 5. Multi-Vertical Expansion Discipline (Walling)

Don't expand to adjacent verticals until you dominate one. Signals to expand:
- ≥40% share of your niche's serviceable market
- Stable retention and unit economics
- New vertical shares meaningful customer DNA (same persona, similar workflow)
- Capital available to fund 6-12 months of build before revenue

## 6. Vertical-by-Geography Expansion Order

Typical pattern for African vertical SaaS:
1. **Country 1 (founder's home market)** — deep niche dominance
2. **Country 2** (similar regulatory + payment-rail; e.g. Kenya → Tanzania, Uganda → Rwanda, Nigeria → Ghana)
3. **Country 3** (different region; e.g., West-East jump)
4. **Country 4-5** (regional consolidation)

Don't jump to a different vertical OR different language family until at least 3 countries deep in the original niche.

## 8. Africa / Uganda Application Notes

- **Founder domain knowledge** is the most weighted dimension in African vertical SaaS scoring — without it, the moat is hollow.
- **Regulatory complexity** in fintech, healthtech, public-sector is often the moat itself — depth into licensing creates barriers.
- **Channel access** (extension officers, sector associations, NGOs, DFIs) is often more important than digital marketing reach.
- **Multi-language** support is part of the moat (Luganda dairy; Swahili healthcare; Hausa fintech).
- **NGO / donor-funded customers** are an under-utilised first-customer segment for verticals like agritech, edutech, healthtech — but plan the commercial transition.
- **Public-sector** is a high-value but high-friction segment; if pursued, build dedicated motion.
- **Diaspora-buyer fintech** is a niche-within-niche with high-LTV characteristics (remittance, USD-denominated services, cross-border).
