# SaaS selling approach and conversation standard

This is the engine's standard for the "how we sell" part of a SaaS business plan. It starts from the buyer's understanding of their problem, derives the selling approach and organisation, and then sets conversation and meeting standards. It draws on the SaaS sales training literature, notably van der Kooij, J. et al. (2018) *The SaaS Sales Method for Account Executives* and *The SaaS Sales Method Fundamentals*, Winning by Design, which describe methodology choice by buyer state, a staged winning process, an impact-based proposal and conversation techniques (including their "TALKER" listening model). The structure, wording, rules and examples here are this engine's. Deal-size, cycle, pay and ratio figures in those sources are dated US figures; find current local evidence.

## 1. Choose the selling approach from the buyer's understanding

| What the buyer understands | Approach | Deal and cycle (describe from local evidence) | What carries the sale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knows exactly what to buy | Self-serve | Very small; minutes | Product, pricing page, onboarding |
| Knows the product type and compares price | Volume selling | Small; days | Speed, many conversations per seller |
| Knows the problem and compares a few vendors | Solution selling | Mid-size; weeks | Demonstration, competitive position |
| Senses a problem but cannot define it | Consultative selling | Large; months | Diagnosis, business case, several stakeholders |
| Does not yet see the problem | Insight-led selling | Very large; long | Education, executive access |

The approach drives channels, seller profile, quota, cycle, win rate and headcount. A plan that pairs large contracts with a very short cycle is internally inconsistent. Enterprise and public-sector buyers in East Africa often have slow procurement and several approvers, so consultative selling is usually the realistic choice for larger deals; confirm cycle lengths from the client's pipeline.

## 2. Show the whole revenue path, not only closing

Model four activities with owners and conversion assumptions: **teach the market** (content, events, thought leadership); **find fitting organisations** (target lists, outreach, booked discovery calls); **win** (stages below); **grow accounts** (onboarding, adoption, expansion, renewal). Most plans show only winning; teaching and growth are often where SaaS economics are decided.

## 3. Winning stages and the artefact each needs

| Stage | What happens | Artefact |
|---|---|---|
| Target | After each win, list look-alike organisations (segment, region, problem, desired result, the buyer's own network) | Relevance-ranked target list |
| Understand | Pain, the event forcing a decision by a date, and its cost in money, time or risk | Discovery guide |
| Show | Demonstration, workshop or proof of concept on the buyer's own use case | Demo plan |
| Support the evaluation | Help the buyer set decision criteria; trial; comparison | Evaluation plan |
| Propose | Quote and impact proposal | Proposal template (section 4) |
| Wait well | If the buyer pauses, keep teaching and watching for interest without pressure | Nurture sequence |
| Agree terms | Negotiate; handle price; test commitment | Concession matrix |
| Sign | Order, master agreement, statement of work | Contract pack |
| Close out a loss | Debrief and record lessons | Loss review |

Without a dated forcing event there is rarely urgency; train sellers to find one or build one with the buyer (examples: a tax-system integration deadline, a budget approval, a new executive). Tactics that test commitment by going quiet must never mislead. Assign each stage to a role (development representative, account executive, customer success) in job descriptions.

## 4. Impact proposal

1. The buyer's problem in their words.
2. The forcing event and timeline.
3. The cost of doing nothing, quantified.
4. The proposed solution, specific to that problem.
5. The quantified result of acting.
6. The price shown as a fraction of that result.
7. Three commercial options: base, recommended, premium.

**Illustrative example.** A Kampala school-management SaaS proposes to a 900-pupil school: fee arrears of UGX 180m a term (bursar's records), a board deadline before the next term, an expected cut in arrears through mobile-money reminders, and an annual fee equal to a small fraction of the arrears recovered, offered at three levels.

## 5. Concessions

Every concession buys something: longer term, prepayment, a case study, reference calls, co-marketing, wider scope or a faster signature. Gate sequence and approval matrix: `../../saas-gtm-motion-design/references/saas-scaling-rules-and-discount-governance.md`.

## 6. How the organisation grows

Describe stages by ARR: founder selling → first account executives with a development representative → first sales manager and customer success → pods (account executive, development representative, customer success) with revenue operations → specialisation by region, sector or customer size. State the development-representative to account-executive ratio (higher for outbound-heavy motions) and customers per customer-success manager by service level. Use current local pay and productivity figures. In some East African markets outbound prospecting is cheaper than content-led inbound, and customer success may also carry on-site implementation for larger clients.

## 7. Conversation standard

- **Customer context first:** know the buyer's role, sector, use cases and relevant client stories; arrive prepared, on time, with working equipment.
- **Channels:** phone, email, video meeting, in-person, messaging and social networks each need their own skill; place each in the buyer journey.
- **Listening and questioning:** keep a warm, measured tone; ask more than you tell; capture the buyer's exact words; dig beneath the first answer; play back your understanding before recommending anything. Stay curious.
- **Question control:** open questions widen the conversation and closed questions narrow it; situation questions map the facts and pain questions explore consequences. A useful progression is open-situation → open-pain → closed-pain (commitment) → closed-situation (clarification). Keep a question bank for each.
- **Diagnose before prescribing:** a doctor asks what happened before treating a limp; insightful questions earn trust fastest.
- **Three depths of listening:** the words; the feeling behind them; what is left unsaid (unspoken worries, internal politics, sponsorship networks, budget cycles). Train sellers for the second and third.

## 8. Sequences, plays and meetings

- Single touches form **sequences** (for example: profile view → message → email → call → second email → WhatsApp note → pause → new insight).
- Sequences form **plays** aimed at a segment (for example an account-based play for finance directors of SACCOs: tailored note, briefing invitation, multichannel follow-up).
- Plays produce meetings. Name the sequences and plays in the plan with a conversion assumption per step; they feed the Section 10 funnel.
- **Social engagement:** be where the buyers spend time online, listen to what they discuss, then contribute something useful.
- **Messaging policy:** short and specific; respect the hour; use after first contact and with permission; use for scheduling and confirmations, not first pitches. Write the policy down where WhatsApp dominates B2B contact.
- **Video meetings:** agenda, length, attendees and intended outcome in the invitation; buyer-specific material; confirmation a day before; open by linking to the last conversation; buyer talks more in discovery, seller more in demonstration; agree the next action; send a summary within 24 hours.
- **Demonstrations:** walk through the buyer's own use case in the live product rather than slides.

## 9. Checks before release

- The selling approach is stated and matches deal size and cycle.
- All four revenue activities are modelled.
- Named sequences and plays replace generic "outreach".
- Training covers listening, question control and diagnosis, not only product knowledge.
- Messaging policy and meeting protocol are written.
- Material deals use the impact proposal.
- Step conversion assumptions feed the Section 10 model.
- Required sales languages are stated where selling is multilingual.
