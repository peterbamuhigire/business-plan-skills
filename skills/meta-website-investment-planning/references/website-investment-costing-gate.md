# Website Investment And Costing Gate

Use this gate when a business plan includes a website, ecommerce system, content/SEO engine, web app, customer portal, online booking path, or website-design service line.

## 1. Website Role In The Business Model

State the website's primary role:

- Credibility: prove the business exists, is legitimate, and can be trusted.
- Local discovery: help customers find, call, visit, review, or message the business.
- Lead generation: capture qualified enquiries, applications, consultations, or quotes.
- Sales enablement: explain offers, answer objections, host case studies, and support proposals.
- Ecommerce: sell products, manage checkout, payment, fulfilment, and post-purchase flows.
- Content authority: publish guides, comparisons, resources, diagnostics, and SEO assets.
- Customer support: reduce repetitive enquiries through FAQs, documentation, forms, and status information.
- Investor/partner trust: make the business model, team, traction, governance, and proof easy to inspect.
- Software delivery: provide authenticated dashboards, portals, workflows, or self-service tools.

If the role is unclear, do not budget a full website build. Start with discovery and a minimum credible web presence.

## 2. Website Design Philosophy For Business Plans

Use concise language such as:

"The website will be developed as a measurable business asset rather than a brochure. The design process will begin with customer segments, offers, search intent, proof requirements, and conversion goals. The site will be mobile-first, fast, accessible, SEO-ready, secure, and connected to analytics so management can track enquiries, sales, and content performance after launch."

Add premium language only when the budget and operating model support it:

"For this premium positioning, the website must carry the same quality signal as the product/service itself: distinctive design, expert copy, credible proof, strong photography, clear service pathways, and a post-launch optimisation rhythm."

## 3. Stack And Operating Choice

Choose based on business logic:

| Website type | When suitable | Operating implications |
|---|---|---|
| Static-first marketing site | Content changes are periodic; speed/security/SEO matter | Low maintenance; needs developer or simple CMS workflow for updates |
| CMS website | Staff will publish pages/posts regularly | Budget for editor training, governance, backups, updates, and content QA |
| WordPress site | Client familiarity and plugin ecosystem matter | Budget for hardening, plugin updates, backups, performance tuning, and support |
| Ecommerce platform | Products are sold online with payment and fulfilment | Requires catalogue, payment, inventory, delivery, returns, customer service, tax/accounting flows |
| Custom web app/portal | Users need login, roles, dashboards, workflows, or integrations | Requires requirements analysis, security, testing, support, and change control |
| Landing-page funnel | Campaign-specific acquisition and conversion | Requires traffic source, offer, copy, analytics, follow-up, and testing plan |

Do not choose the most complex option if a simpler stack meets the business objective.

## 4. One-Time Cost Categories

Include one-time costs where relevant:

- discovery, strategy, and project management
- brand alignment and design direction
- UX architecture, sitemap, wireframes, and prototypes
- copywriting, editing, translation, photography, video, and graphics
- SEO research, metadata, schema, redirects, internal linking, and launch crawl
- frontend development and responsive templates
- CMS setup or content management workflow
- integrations: forms, CRM, email, WhatsApp, booking, payment, inventory, ERP/POS, analytics
- ecommerce catalogue setup, checkout, tax/shipping, transactional emails, test orders
- accessibility, performance, security, browser/device, and content QA
- launch, DNS/hosting migration, backups, Search Console/Bing, and handover
- training, documentation, and initial support window

## 5. Recurring Cost Categories

Include recurring monthly/annual costs where relevant:

- domain renewal and DNS management
- hosting/CDN/email hosting
- SSL if not included by hosting
- CMS/plugins/themes/licences
- analytics, heatmaps, consent tools, forms, email/CRM, automation, booking, ecommerce, payment gateway charges
- maintenance, backups, updates, security monitoring, uptime monitoring, and support
- content updates, blog/resource production, SEO monitoring, CRO testing, reporting, and campaign landing pages
- photography/design refresh and case-study production

Do not bury recurring website costs inside "miscellaneous marketing"; show them in marketing, ICT, subscriptions, or maintenance assumptions.

## 6. Planning Cost Ranges

Use these as internal planning anchors, then replace with supplier quotes, country-specific benchmarks, or the agency's current fee policy.

| Scope | Planning assumption | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum credible presence | low one-time cost plus domain/hosting | Only for small businesses needing legitimacy and contact capture; not premium |
| Foundation authority website | strategy, core pages, SEO foundation, analytics, QA, launch | Suitable for established service businesses; include copy and proof polishing |
| Growth website | deeper content, service pages, SEO clusters, lead capture, CRM/email handoff | Budget for buyer interviews, copywriting, content migration, and 60-day optimisation |
| Premium authority system | positioning, content hub, case studies, custom design system, advanced SEO/GEO, analytics dashboard, 90-day optimisation | Treat as a strategic asset; should support premium pricing and investor/partner confidence |
| Ecommerce | product data, payment, delivery, checkout, emails, analytics, customer support | Add operations and accounting controls; payment fees affect gross margin |
| Web app/portal | requirements, roles, workflows, APIs, security, testing, support | Model as software development, not website marketing spend |

For Peter/TechGuyPeter website-design services, premium is the default. Do not plan sub-premium delivery for SRS, website, or core skills work. If the budget cannot support quality, reduce scope, stage delivery, or recommend paid discovery rather than lowering the standard.

## 7. Website-Design Service Business Costing

When the business itself sells website design services, include:

- Offer tiers: Foundation, Growth, Authority, ecommerce, maintenance/optimisation retainer.
- Direct labour: discovery, strategy, copy, design, development, QA, PM, launch, support.
- Senior review cost: premium quality requires expert review, not only production hours.
- Tooling: design software, code tools, hosting test accounts, analytics, project management, QA tools.
- Sales and marketing: website, case studies, proposals, diagnostics, outreach, content, SEO, social proof.
- Delivery capacity: number of projects per month/quarter by tier, utilisation, revision load, and support obligations.
- Gross margin: price must cover labour, overhead, revisions, aftercare, non-billable sales time, and profit.
- Cash flow: deposits/milestones must finance delivery before final acceptance.
- Retainers: maintenance, SEO/content, analytics, CRO, security, backups, and campaign support should be modelled separately from project revenue.

## 8. Cross-Section Reconciliation

Check consistency:

- Products/services: website offering or website asset is clearly defined.
- Marketing: website supports SEO, content, lead generation, conversion, and follow-up.
- Operations: roles exist for updates, enquiries, maintenance, security, and reporting.
- Implementation: build, content, launch, and optimisation timeline is realistic.
- Finance: one-time and recurring costs, payment fees, maintenance, subscriptions, and staff time are modelled.
- Risk: traffic shortfall, weak content, cybersecurity, downtime, payment failure, supplier lock-in, and maintenance neglect are addressed.

## Red Flags

- Premium brand strategy with a cheap, generic, or unbudgeted website.
- Revenue forecast depends on online leads but the plan has no SEO/content/campaign budget.
- Ecommerce forecast has no fulfilment, inventory, returns, fraud, payment, tax, or customer support assumptions.
- Website-design service margins ignore revisions, project management, QA, support, sales time, and delayed payments.
- Tech stack is named but not justified by business operations.
