# Location and scope calibration

Parent: [Marketing Plan Orchestrator](../SKILL.md).

When to read: before setting objectives, choosing channels or budgeting any
marketing plan or business-plan section 07, and whenever a plan crosses a
district, national or regional boundary. Objectives and tactics must fit the
business, its location and its scope. A tactic that works in Nairobi's
Westlands may be unreachable, unaffordable or unlawful in Arua.

Facts marked with a claim ID come from the Kaizen currentness register
(checked 2026-09-23). Re-verify them through the Digital Research engine
before client release once their review date passes. Everything else here is a
check to run, not a fact to repeat.

## 1. Declare the scope first

| Scope level | Typical business | Calibration questions | Objective unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neighbourhood or trading centre | Shop, salon, clinic, restaurant, school | Walking or boda catchment; landmarks customers use; market days; competitors within reach | Parish, estate, stage, street |
| City or municipality | Delivery service, professional firm, chain of outlets | Divisions or suburbs actually served; delivery radius and time; traffic and payment realities | Division, suburb, delivery zone |
| Region or district cluster | Agro-input dealer, distributor, radio-led brand | Languages; radio stations covering the area; distributor and agent reach; seasonality (harvest, school terms) | District, sub-region |
| National | Consumer brand, telecom-linked service, national NGO | Urban/rural split; channel mix by region; regional price and logistics differences | Country with regional breakdown |
| Cross-border (EAC or wider) | Exporter, SaaS, regional distributor | Currency and FX date; tax and registration per country; data-protection regime per country; distributor terms; language; payment rails per country | Country and city, each with its own targets |

Rule: an objective is written at the scope level where the business can
actually deliver and measure. A Kampala bakery does not set "Uganda" targets.
A cross-border plan sets targets per country, never a blended regional number.

## 2. Uganda and East Africa defaults (to verify per engagement)

Use these as prompts for the evidence plan, not as findings.

- **Payments.** Mobile money is a common consumer payment route in the
  region; confirm its share from the client's own receipts, then plan offers, instalments, deposits and collection around it, and
  cost its fees through the finance engine. Card and bank-transfer share varies
  sharply by segment; check with the client's own receipts.
- **Messaging.** WhatsApp is widely used for enquiries, catalogues and
  after-sales contact, but **no credible country-level WhatsApp user figure
  for Uganda or Kenya was found** (MK-03). Do not state one unless it is cited
  from a named survey with its date and method.
- **Social-media reach.** DataReportal *Digital 2026* (published 8 November
  2025) reports Uganda's Meta ad reach at 2.40 million, measured while
  Facebook was blocked in Uganda; treat it as a floor, not the audience size
  (MK-02). Kenya's figures are reported separately (MK-01). Ad reach is not the
  same as users.
- **Radio.** FM and vernacular radio are commonly used to reach audiences
  outside the capital cities. Obtain station coverage maps, audience research
  and current rate cards; do not quote reach percentages without a dated
  source.
- **Physical realities.** Markets and market days, taxi and bus parks, boda
  stages, churches and mosques, SACCOs, associations and schools are channels
  and research venues. Outdoor advertising needs local authority permits
  (KCCA in Kampala; county governments in Kenya) — check current rules.
- **Languages.** Choose the language of each message by segment: English,
  Luganda, Runyankore-Rukiga, Luo, Lusoga, Ateso, Swahili and others. Use
  transcreation with in-community review, not literal translation (Kelley and
  Sheehan, c. 2021).
- **Seasonality.** School terms and fees, harvests, festive seasons, month-end
  pay, Ramadan and Christmas, rainy seasons affecting roads, and election
  periods all shift demand and media cost.
- **Connectivity.** Median mobile speeds are published for Kenya but not for
  Uganda (MK-04). Design landing pages and digital journeys for low-end
  devices and weak connections; route website performance work to the
  website engine.
- **Tax on advertising spend.** Budgets for foreign ad platforms may attract
  VAT and other charges; the position is partial in the register (PL-05).
  Route every tax statement to the Chwezi finance engine; do not state rates in
  the plan without its confirmation.

## 3. Data protection and direct marketing

Any plan that collects contacts, runs surveys, sends SMS, WhatsApp or email
campaigns, uses customer lists for ad matching or retargets site visitors must
include a data-protection line in its compliance note.

| Jurisdiction | What the register confirms (checked 2026-09-23) | What the plan must do |
|---|---|---|
| Uganda (PL-01) | Data collectors, processors and controllers must register with the Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO); registration is valid for 12 months. Processing personal data without prior consent is an offence. Section 26 of the Data Protection and Privacy Act gives data subjects a right to object: by written notice they can require the controller to stop processing their data for direct marketing, and the controller must respond within 14 days. | Budget and schedule PDPO registration or renewal; obtain consent when collecting data; build an objection (opt-out) route and a 14-day response procedure. Do not claim the Act "requires opt-in for marketing"; it provides an objection right, while consent governs collection. |
| Kenya (PL-02) | The Data Protection (General) Regulations 2021 allow direct marketing with non-sensitive data under conditions including a simplified, free opt-out; the right to object to direct marketing is absolute; direct-marketing email must identify the sender and give a valid opt-out address. Registration is required; small entities are exempt unless listed, and businesses "wholly or mainly in direct marketing" must register regardless of size. | Provide a free, simple opt-out on every message; honour objections without exception; register with the ODPC where required. |
| Rwanda (PL-03) | Controllers and processors must register with the National Cyber Security Authority. Direct-marketing provisions were not assessed. | Register; treat direct-marketing rules as NOT_ASSESSED until checked. |
| Tanzania (PL-04) | The Personal Data Protection Act 2022 is in force and collectors must register with the PDPC; enforcement dates and direct-marketing rules are partial or not assessed. | Register; check direct-marketing rules before launch. |
| EU-facing campaigns (CW-10) | Google tags for EEA, UK and Swiss users need Consent Mode v2 signals. | Hand implementation to the website engine; note it in the compliance line. |

Also check: sector advertising rules (health, financial products, alcohol,
betting, education claims), comparative-claim rules, and influencer
disclosure. No Uganda- or Kenya-specific influencer disclosure law was found
as of 2026-09-23; apply clear "#ad"-style disclosure as practice and check
UCC, CA and sector regulators before a campaign (AD-10). Renting or buying
contact lists is constrained by these laws; prefer the firm's own opted-in
contacts and partner co-marketing to the partner's own consented list.

## 4. Calibration checklist (run once per plan and per region)

1. Scope level declared and objectives written in that unit.
2. Payment routes and their fees reflected in offers and economics.
3. Channels chosen from evidence of use in the named place, not national averages.
4. Languages and message framing chosen per segment, with a reviewer from the community.
5. Seasonality calendar built for the place and segment.
6. Physical channels mapped (markets, stages, venues) with permit checks.
7. Data-protection registration, consent, objection and opt-out handling in the compliance note.
8. Sector and claims rules checked; tax statements routed to the finance engine.
9. Currency stated; any exchange rate carries its source and date.
10. Cross-border plans: one column per country for law, currency, channel and target.

## 5. Worked calibration (illustrative)

A solar-lantern distributor plans "national expansion". Calibration splits it:
Kampala and Wakiso (retail shops, Facebook and WhatsApp, card and mobile
money), Lango and Acholi sub-regions (Luo-language radio, market-day
demonstrations, agent network, mobile-money instalments, harvest-timed
promotions) and a Kenya pilot in Kisumu County (separate distributor, KES
pricing, ODPC registration check, Swahili and Dholuo creative). Each region
receives its own objectives, budget column and control-plan rows.
