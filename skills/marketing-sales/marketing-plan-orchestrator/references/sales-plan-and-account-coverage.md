# Sales plan, account coverage and contact economics

Parent: [Marketing Plan Orchestrator](../SKILL.md).

When to read: when writing the sales plan of a marketing plan or the sales
part of business-plan section 07, especially for B2B, high-ticket, dealer or
field-sales businesses. Marketing creates awareness and delivers the message;
sales secures the orders. Plans that describe only the first half are
incomplete. All figures below are illustrative.

## 1. Sales structure and productivity evidence

Record the evidence that makes the sales forecast believable (Abrams, R. M.
(1993) *The Successful Business Plan*, The Oasis Press, adapted):

- **Sales force:** numbers by type (inside, field employees, agents or
  independent representatives, partners); how territories, product lines or
  customer types are divided; commission basis and other incentives; who
  trains, how often; which other staff generate sales.
- **Sales process and productivity:** how prospects are found; who contacts
  them; attempts before a prospect is dropped; contacts per appointment;
  appointments per sale; calls expected per seller per period; who takes
  orders, checks credit and confirms fulfilment.
- **Sales assumptions:** the ratios above, from CRM records or a dated test,
  feed the demand-to-cash bridge in
  [marketing economics](marketing-economics-and-budget.md).

Pay commission on gross profit or contribution rather than revenue where
discounting is possible, so sellers do not buy volume with margin (Barrow, C.
(2009) *Get Backed, Get Big, Get Bought*, Capstone). Commission and payroll
treatment go to the finance engine.

Rewrite phone, WhatsApp, counter and field scripts so every human touchpoint
repeats the plan's two or three message themes.

## 2. Qualifying and grading leads

- Qualify on **process** of buying, **need**, **authority**, **money** and
  **estimated timing** (Weinberg, G. and Mares, J. (2014) *Traction*,
  S-curves Publishing).
- Grade by expected time to close: **A** within three months (most selling
  time), **B** three to twelve months, **C** beyond twelve months (marketing
  nurtures; sellers do not chase).
- Enquiries go cold within about two weeks; respond fast and **call before you
  send** material, because the right material usually changes after a short
  conversation (Hunter, V. L. with Tietyen, D. (1997) *Business to Business
  Marketing*, NTC Business Books; the half-life figure is historical, so
  measure your own).
- Never pass unqualified leads to field sellers. Track cost per qualified lead
  by source and close rate per qualified lead by seller.
- Design the funnel from the buyer's questions inward: list what buyers ask
  (does it suit me? what does it cost over time? who else uses it?), give each
  question an answer asset (page, case study, demonstration, reference call),
  and close with dated yes-or-no commitments.

## 3. B2B account coverage and contact economics

Reconcile what customers want with what they are worth (Hunter and Tietyen
(1997), adapted).

1. **Define an active customer** with money and time.
2. **Grade buyer groups by expected value**, not last year's sales. Remove
   house accounts (bespoke plans) and very small occasional buyers (low-cost
   reactive stream). Split the rest by count into five grades, for example AA
   about 5%, A 15%, B 25%, C 25%, D 30%. Promote look-alikes of higher grades
   for a trial uplift.
3. **Set a sales-expense-to-revenue (E/R) target per grade**, agreed with the
   finance owner.
4. **Build the contact matrix:** start from the contact customers say they
   value; if the cost exceeds the E/R target, move content from visits to
   calls to digital updates until it fits. Lower grades get fewer, denser
   contacts.
5. **Capacity check:** total the visits and calls the matrix requires and
   compare with seller capacity (visits a day × selling days × sellers). Close
   gaps with lower-cost value contacts, not by assuming more sellers.
6. Break the matrix into a monthly calendar and a weekly contact plan.

Example (office-supplies distributor, Kampala; costs: digital update UGX
1,500, inside-sales call UGX 20,000 of staff time, field visit UGX 200,000;
E/R target 4%):

| Grade | Expected revenue per buyer group | Contacts a year | Cost per group | E/R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AA | UGX 60m | 24 digital, 12 calls, 8 visits | UGX 1,876,000 | 3.1% |
| C | UGX 8m | 12 digital, 4 calls | UGX 98,000 | 1.2% |
| C with 2 visits added | UGX 8m | as above + 2 visits | UGX 498,000 | 6.2% (fails) |

Judge the system on E/R, retention and account penetration, not on the
response rate of individual mailings.

## 4. Key-account portfolio

For the few largest accounts, classify by the account's profit-growth
potential and the supplier's current strength (McDonald's typology, as
presented in Marcos, J., Guesalaga, R., Hough, A. and Vincent, R. (c. 2025)
*The High-Performing Key Account Manager*, Kogan Page):

| Type | Potential and strength | Resourcing |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic | High potential, high strength | Senior owner, joint annual plan, multi-level contact |
| Star | High potential, lower strength | Pursuit budget to build position |
| Status | Lower potential, high strength | Delivery excellence and margin protection |
| Streamline | Low potential, low strength | Cost-to-serve control, productised or lower-touch service |

In family-owned groups, "strength" often depends on access to the owner;
score relationships per stakeholder. For strategic accounts, write the value
case in the customer's own metrics (cash released, cost removed, revenue
enabled), agree a baseline, and review realised value quarterly. The book's
uncited statistics are excluded.

## 5. Growing inside accounts

Grow in this order: more product lines to the same buyer → colleagues with the
same need → other functions at the same site → other sites or subsidiaries
(Hunter and Tietyen's account cube). Keep a penetration grid per key account
(bought, not bought, blocked) and tie each addition to a named business
problem. Launch new offers to existing customers first; they carry the lowest
risk.

## 6. Captive universes

Where the target universe is small and knowable (licensed insurers, commercial
banks, SACCOs above a size threshold, private hospitals, international schools,
registered manufacturers), build the universe list from regulator registers
and association member lists and run it as a named-account programme rather
than mass advertising. Check each register's terms of use and data-protection
rules before contact.

## 7. Sales plan acceptance checks

- Sales force, territories, incentives and training are stated.
- Conversion ratios come from records or a dated test and feed the forecast.
- Lead qualification, grading and response-time rules are defined.
- B2B plans show the grade table, contact matrix, E/R check and capacity check.
- Key accounts are classified and resourced by type.
- Commission basis and headcount reconcile with the P&L.
