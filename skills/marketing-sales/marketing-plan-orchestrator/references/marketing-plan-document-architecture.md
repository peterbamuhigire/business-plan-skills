# Marketing plan document architecture

Parent: [Marketing Plan Orchestrator](../SKILL.md).

When to read: before outlining or drafting any standalone marketing plan, and
when auditing one for missing sections. Each section below states its purpose,
the decision it supports, the required tables and the reference that holds the
method. Scale depth to the engagement (see "Scaling rules"), but never drop a
section's decision; a one-line "not applicable because…" is acceptable, silence
is not.

Method sources are cited where a procedure comes from a book. The books are
concept inputs; every figure, platform fact, law and benchmark must come from
the client's records, dated research or the engine's currentness register.

## Build order versus reading order

Draft in the order the evidence arrives, not the order the reader sees:

1. Situation analysis and customer research (sections 2–5).
2. Objectives and strategy (sections 6–8).
3. Programmes: channels, advertising, digital, content, sales, retention (9–14).
4. Economics, budget and cash bridge (15–16).
5. Implementation, KPIs, control and risk (17–20).
6. Executive summary last (section 1), then appendices.

Stutts's sequencing rule applies to the programmes: research → plan →
creative → test → launch. No creative or scaled media budget is committed
before the customer insights report and the approved plan exist, and no scale
spend happens before a capped test has read out (Stutts, P. (2021) *The
Undefeated Marketing System*, Lioncrest/Scribe).

## Section-by-section architecture

| # | Section | Decision it supports | Required content and tables | Method reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Executive summary | Approve, fund or revise the plan | Business outcome, target segments, positioning line, 3–5 SMART objectives with dates, chosen strategy in one paragraph, budget total and its P&L reconciliation, first 90-day actions, top three risks, the ask | [Phrase bank](../../../language/writing-quality/references/marketing-plan-phrase-bank.md) |
| 2 | Situation analysis | What in the environment and the business constrains or opens choices | PESTEL reduced to 3–6 key drivers with direction and effect; five forces with driver evidence; internal resources and capabilities; EFAS, IFAS and SFAS weighted tables on one stated scale; SWOT converted to a TOWS matrix | [Situation and market toolkit](situation-and-market-toolkit.md); `meta-strategic-factor-analysis` |
| 3 | Market and customer | Which customers, in which buying situations, for what reasons | Segmentation bases; segment attractiveness matrix; target-market checks (describable, relevant to buying, large enough, reachable) with an attribute-justification column; buying situations and sensitivities; payer versus user; customer insights report summary | [Situation and market toolkit](situation-and-market-toolkit.md) |
| 4 | Market sizing | Is the reachable demand large enough to meet the objectives | Market ladder (potential → available → qualified available → served → penetrated); top-down and bottom-up estimates with the gap explained; estimation driver tree; sanity check against a known reference | [Estimation trees](../../../pipeline/04-market-analysis/references/estimation-trees-and-sanity-checks.md) |
| 5 | Competitors | Whom the buyer compares us with and where we can win | Direct, indirect and category competitors (including informal traders, imports and do-it-yourself); weighted customer-perception grid and operational grid; market-share distribution; barriers with strength and duration | [Situation and market toolkit](situation-and-market-toolkit.md) |
| 6 | Positioning, brand and message | What single idea the brand will own and how it is said | Positioning statement with a checkable reason to believe; five buyer-benefit questions with one or two chosen; 2–3 message themes selected from the full claim list by importance × proof × distinctiveness; positioning failure-mode check; brand contact-point list | [Situation and market toolkit](situation-and-market-toolkit.md) |
| 7 | Objectives | What success means, by when, at what cost | Cascade: business → marketing → communication → channel objectives; each SMART and location-calibrated; baseline with source and date; line in the sand with its source; action if missed | [SMART objectives builder](smart-objectives-builder.md); [Location and scope calibration](location-and-scope-calibration.md) |
| 8 | Strategy | How the objectives will be reached | STP choice and coverage pattern; strategy-clock position; growth route (penetration, market development, product development, diversification) and method (organic, alliance, acquisition); push versus pull; marketing mix (4Ps for products, 7Ps for services) with a decision per P | [Situation and market toolkit](situation-and-market-toolkit.md); `meta-strategic-options-evaluation` |
| 9 | Channel plan | Which acquisition channels deserve money now | Bullseye across the 19 traction channels: one idea per channel, A/B/C rings, three inner-circle test cards, focus rule, decay watch and re-run trigger | [Traction channel Bullseye](traction-channel-bullseye.md) |
| 10 | Advertising and media plan | What to say, where, how often and at what cost | Advertising objectives; budget triangulation (percentage of sales, objective-and-task, share of voice) with a minimum-effective-level floor; reach and frequency targets; flighting pattern; audience-cost media table (CPM and cost per interested thousand); creative brief; message-before-execution test plan | [Advertising and media plan](advertising-and-media-plan.md) |
| 11 | Digital layer | How owned and paid digital channels carry the strategy | Channel roles, website and conversion path, measurement set-up, consent and privacy handling; detail handed to `digital-marketing-strategy` and the social-media engine | `digital-marketing-strategy`; social-media engine handoff (below) |
| 12 | Content plan | What the brand publishes to earn attention and trust | Content pillars mapped to message themes, formats per channel, cadence, owners, approval route, reuse plan; production detail in the social-media engine | Social-media engine handoff |
| 13 | Sales plan | How demand becomes signed revenue | Sales structure and force; process stages with conversion ratios; lead qualification and grading; for B2B, account grading, contact matrix and sales-expense-to-revenue check; capacity reconciliation | [Sales plan and account coverage](sales-plan-and-account-coverage.md) |
| 14 | Retention and customer value | How customers stay, buy again and refer | Active-customer definition; CLV method; onboarding (assimilation); early-warning triggers; market-at-risk calculation; referral mechanism; maximum affordable cost per lead | [Marketing economics and budget](marketing-economics-and-budget.md) |
| 15 | Direct-response economics | Which direct campaigns pay | Campaign pro-forma P&L per offer; net profit per order; orders per thousand to break even for each medium; test → retest → extend → roll-out ladder | [Marketing economics and budget](marketing-economics-and-budget.md) |
| 16 | Budget and demand-to-cash bridge | Is the plan affordable and does it reconcile with the P&L | Two-phase budget (activity, then segment/geography/time); monthly budget by vehicle; demand-to-cash bridge; reconciliation of the plan budget with the P&L marketing line; contribution and cash by scenario | [Marketing economics and budget](marketing-economics-and-budget.md); Chwezi finance doctrine |
| 17 | Implementation calendar | Who does what, when, in what order | Month-by-month calendar with owners; critical path from the traction goal; dependencies; explicit not-doing list | [Traction channel Bullseye](traction-channel-bullseye.md) (critical path) |
| 18 | KPI dashboard | How progress is seen | One metric that matters (OMTM) per stage; 3–5 guard-rail metrics; leading and lagging tags; vanity-metric test; definitions with numerator, denominator and window | [KPI dashboard and control plan](kpi-dashboard-and-control-plan.md) |
| 19 | Control plan | How the plan is steered | Control table: action, owner, reviewer, frequency, KPI, tolerance band, corrective action; weekly war-room cadence; monthly, quarterly and trigger reviews | [KPI dashboard and control plan](kpi-dashboard-and-control-plan.md) |
| 20 | Risks and contingency | What could break the plan and the pre-agreed response | Risk register (area, risk, likelihood, impact, trigger, response, owner); competitor-response scenarios; compliance risks (data protection, advertising claims, sector rules) | This file, "Risk and contingency" |
| A | Appendices | Evidence a reviewer needs | Research instruments and samples; source register with dates; calculations; media rate evidence; test cards; approvals register; glossary of metric definitions | [Evidence discipline](evidence-discipline-for-marketing-claims.md) |

## Section standards that apply everywhere

- Every number carries its class (verified fact, management assumption,
  estimate, projection or target) and its source and date. See
  [evidence discipline](evidence-discipline-for-marketing-claims.md).
- Every objective and target passes the [SMART quality test](smart-objectives-builder.md)
  and the [location and scope calibration](location-and-scope-calibration.md).
- Every programme links upward to an objective and downward to a budget line,
  an owner and a KPI. A programme without all four is removed or parked.
- Money is in the client's currency (UGX by default) with the date of any
  exchange rate used.
- Prose follows the [marketing-plan phrase bank](../../../language/writing-quality/references/marketing-plan-phrase-bank.md),
  `writing-quality` and `anti-ai-slop`.

## Scaling rules

| Engagement | Pages (excl. appendices) | Keep in full | Compress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro-business or single-outlet launch | 6–10 | Objectives, target market tests, channel Bullseye, 90-day calendar, budget reconciliation, three KPIs | Situation analysis to a key-driver list and a short SWOT/TOWS; media plan to one table |
| SME annual plan | 15–25 | All 20 sections | EFAS/IFAS to top 5–7 factors each; one competitor grid |
| Corporate, multi-region or cross-border | 25–40 plus appendices | All sections, with a region-level calibration table and media plan per region | Nothing essential; move detail to appendices |
| Campaign plan inside an existing strategy | 4–8 | Objectives cascade, audience, message themes, media plan, budget, KPI and control table | Situation to a one-paragraph brief linking to the parent plan |

## Digital marketing and advertising handoff (cross-engine)

The social-media engine (`social-media-skills`) is now the **digital marketing
and advertising engine**: advertising strategy, media planning, build
specifications, optimisation and attribution, alongside channel playbooks and
content. Boundary:

- **This engine owns** the plan-level advertising objectives, the advertising
  budget envelope, the media-mix decision (which media families and how much
  to each) and the reconciliation of all three with the P&L marketing line, the
  demand-to-cash bridge and the KPI and control plan.
- **The digital marketing and advertising engine owns** detailed media plans
  (vehicle-level buys, schedules, targeting), creative briefs and production,
  campaign build specifications, in-flight optimisation, and measurement and
  attribution models.
- Detailed media plans, creative briefs and attribution results return to this
  engine as summaries that reconcile to the approved envelope; any variance
  above the tolerance band in the control plan comes back for a decision here.

Hand over:

| Handed over | Artefact | Owner after handoff |
|---|---|---|
| Target segments, message themes, positioning line, proof points | Message brief (one page) | Social-media engine for execution; this engine keeps the positioning |
| Advertising objectives, budget envelope, media-mix decision, KPI definitions and lines in the sand | Advertising and channel brief table | Digital marketing and advertising engine for detailed media plans, creative briefs, build specifications, optimisation and attribution; this engine for envelope, mix and control |
| Consent, privacy and claims constraints | Compliance note | Both; legal review where a claim or data use is material |
| Results for the control plan | Monthly KPI and attribution export in the agreed definitions | Digital marketing and advertising engine supplies; this engine interprets against the plan |

Website builds and landing-page implementation go to the website engine;
visual formatting of the plan document goes to the design-system engine. Resolve
each engine through the global routing table.

## Risk and contingency

Minimum risk areas: demand shortfall; competitor response (price cut, copycat,
spend increase); channel decay or platform policy change; creative fatigue;
delivery capacity failure after a demand spike; cash timing; data protection
and consent failures; advertising-claim challenges; reputational incident;
supplier or distributor failure; key-person dependency. For each, state the
trigger that activates the contingency and the pre-agreed response (for
example, "if cost per qualified enquiry exceeds UGX 60,000 for two consecutive
weeks, pause the channel and move 50% of its budget to the next test card").
