# Channel selection and the critical path

Parent: [Marketing Plan Orchestrator](../SKILL.md).

When to read: when choosing acquisition channels for a marketing plan or business-plan section 07, when a client arrives asking for one "obvious" channel, when growth flattens, and when turning an action list into a dependency-ordered implementation calendar.

This is the engine's channel-selection method: consider the whole channel universe, shortlist by evidence, test three in parallel, then concentrate. The ring-based shortlisting idea (the "Bullseye" method) and the argument that founders should consider every channel family come from Weinberg, G. and Mares, J. (2014) *Traction*, S-curves Publishing, which catalogues nineteen channels. The grouping, test card, thresholds and East African forms below are this engine's; the book's US prices and platform examples are not used.

## 1. Principles

- No one can predict the best channel in advance; test before committing.
- Look across **every** channel family before choosing; teams overuse what they know and avoid what feels awkward.
- Test **three** channels at once, cheaply, then **concentrate** on the one the numbers favour.
- **No tracking, no test:** unique links, codes, WhatsApp keywords, dedicated lines or a "how did you hear about us?" field come first.
- **Channels wear out** as they crowd; keep a small continuing test budget and re-run the method when growth flattens.
- Know whether a channel **captures existing demand** (search, directories, marketplaces) or **creates it** (social, radio, events, PR); judge creating channels on the audience they build and its later conversion.
- **Threshold that matters:** state what a channel must deliver to be worth running at this stage (for example, "at least 40 qualified enquiries a month at no more than UGX 60,000 each"). Reach needed = target customers ÷ expected conversion.

## 3. Procedure

1. Confirm the traction goal, stage, budget, allowable cost per customer (from [marketing economics](marketing-economics-and-budget.md)) and tracking readiness.
2. Write at least one realistic idea for every row. Score each on likelihood of working (1–5), expected cost per customer, customers available at that cost before saturation, and time to test.
3. Place each idea in a ring: **inner** (most promising now), **middle** (possible), **outer** (long shot).
4. Take three inner-ring ideas and write a test card for each.
5. Run the tests together with weekly read-outs. A test uses two or three variants; fine-tuning comes only once a channel shows promise.
6. Decide: concentrate, extend the test, or drop, with the reason recorded.
7. Hand the winner to an optimisation plan and set the re-run trigger (for example two months of flat growth or rising cost per customer).

With a very small budget, test one at a time and say so.

## 4. Test card

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Channel and idea | e.g. sponsored posts in three parenting WhatsApp Channels in Wakiso |
| Assumptions under test | Cost per qualified enquiry ≤ UGX [x]; at least [n] enquiries a month available; enquirers match the segment |
| Spend cap and duration | UGX [amount]; [n] days |
| Tracking | Unique link, QR code, promo code, WhatsApp keyword or dedicated line |
| Success threshold | e.g. at least [n] qualified enquiries at ≤ UGX [x] each |
| Owner | Named role |
| Result and decision | Concentrate, retest or drop, with reason |

**Illustrative example.** A Mukono poultry-feed maker's inner ring: agent commission for village stockists, Luganda radio call-in sponsorship during the brooding season, and a WhatsApp group for farmers who buy chicks from two hatcheries. After four weeks, the agent route delivers qualified stockists at a third of the radio cost and receives 60% of the channel budget.

## 5. From traction goal to critical path

1. State the traction goal and what it changes (break-even, loan eligibility, investor readiness, a second outlet).
2. List candidate milestones: hires, features, partnerships, campaigns, approvals, stock.
3. Keep only those strictly needed to reach the goal.
4. Order them by dependency; prefer buying or borrowing over building.
5. Date each and give it an owner; add padding.
6. Put everything else on a **not-doing list** published with the plan.
7. Ask weekly, "are we on the path?"; review deeply at each milestone.

## 6. Anti-patterns

- Choosing channels by fashion or habit. Fix: one idea for every row before ranking.
- Testing without tracking. Fix: tracking is the first test-card field.
- Scaling acquisition into a leaking business. Fix: fix activation and retention first.
- Assuming a working channel lasts. Fix: wear-out watch and re-run trigger.
- An action list with no dependencies. Fix: critical path plus not-doing list.
