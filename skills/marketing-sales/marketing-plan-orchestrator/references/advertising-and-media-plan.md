# Advertising and media plan

Parent: [Marketing Plan Orchestrator](../SKILL.md).

When to read: when a marketing plan or business-plan section 07 includes paid
advertising (radio, TV, print, outdoor, paid social, search or sponsorship),
when setting or defending an advertising budget, or when writing the
plan-level creative direction. This reference owns the plan-level advertising
objectives, budget envelope, media-mix decision, scheduling logic and
measurement plan, reconciled to the P&L. The digital marketing and advertising
engine (`social-media-skills`) owns detailed media plans, creative briefs and
production, build specifications, optimisation and attribution; see the
handoff boundary in the marketing-plan document architecture.

Sources: Kelley, L. D. and Sheehan, K. B. (c. 2021) *Advertising Management in
a Digital Environment*, Routledge; Stockwell, J. and Shaw, H. M. (1994)
*Direct Marketing Checklists*, NTC Business Books; Stutts, P. (2021) *The
Undefeated Marketing System*. Reach, frequency, flighting and GRP
definitions are standard media-planning terms. All cost figures are
illustrative; use current rate cards.

## 1. Advertising objectives

Derive them from the communication objectives in the
[SMART cascade](smart-objectives-builder.md). Each campaign has **one primary
objective**: awareness, consideration, enquiries, sales, retention or
reputation. Each objective names its audience, place, baseline, target, date
and measure. Platform objective menus change; use the current menu (for
example, Meta's six objectives as checked on 2026-09-23, AD-01) only in the
execution brief.

## 2. Budget triangulation

Never set the budget by one method or by "what we spent last year". Show three
methods side by side and explain the chosen figure:

| Method | Calculation | Weakness to state |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage of forecast sales | Forecast revenue × category advertising-to-sales ratio (local ratio if available; otherwise a labelled assumption) | Treats advertising as a result of sales, not a cause |
| Objective-and-task | Target outcome → reach and frequency or enquiries needed → cost via CPM or cost-per-enquiry assumptions → add production, research, contingency | Only as good as the conversion assumptions |
| Competitive share of voice | Estimated category spend × intended share of voice; to gain share, share of voice above share of market is usually needed (Kelley and Sheehan cite "2 × SOV ≈ 1 × SOM" as a case heuristic — treat it as a hypothesis) | Local competitor spend is hard to observe; build estimates from ad libraries, media monitoring and observation |

Then set:

- **Minimum effective level (floor):** below it, the campaign is invisible.
  If the budget is below the floor, concentrate on fewer places, segments or
  months rather than spreading thin.
- **Diminishing-returns ceiling:** beyond it, extra money buys little.
- **Answers to the three budget questions:** what do we get for this sum? what
  happens if it rises by X%? what happens if it falls by X%?

## 3. Two-phase allocation

**Phase 1, by activity:** paid media, production, research and testing,
contingency (with a named purpose: overrun, opportunistic buy or channel
trial). Kelley and Sheehan's illustrative split is media 75%, production 15%,
research 5%, contingency 5%; set the client's own split. Decide separately
whether agency fees, staff time and tools sit inside the campaign budget.

**Phase 2, by segment, geography and time:** weight places using a category
development index (CDI = place's share of category sales ÷ place's share of
population × 100) and a brand development index (BDI, the same for the
brand). High CDI with low BDI suggests opportunity; high with high, defend;
low CDI with high BDI, maintain; low with low, avoid. Do not advertise where
distribution is weak. Reserve deliberate test cells (raised or cut spend in
one place) to read the effect.

## 4. Reach, frequency and scheduling

- **Reach:** share (or number) of the target audience exposed at least once in
  the period.
- **Frequency:** average exposures per person reached.
- **Effective reach:** share reached at or above the frequency judged
  necessary for this message. The "3+" industry default is contested; set the
  level per brand and message complexity, and test it.
- **Gross rating points (GRPs):** reach % × average frequency (target rating
  points when measured on the target audience).
- **Flighting pattern:** **continuity** (steady presence, for frequently bought
  products), **flighting** (bursts with gaps, for seasonal demand or tight
  budgets) or **pulsing** (a low base with bursts, for steady demand with
  peaks). Tie bursts to buying moments (school terms, harvests, pay days,
  festive seasons).
- **Frequency caps and creative rotation** prevent fatigue in digital channels;
  review weekly.

## 5. Media selection and the audience-cost table

Test each medium against four questions (Stockwell and Shaw): does it reach
our target (check the audience breakdown, not the title)? does it produce
response? do competitors use it? is its audience data current and audited?
Then compare **cost per interested thousand** (CPIM = cost ÷ in-target
audience × 1,000), not raw CPM. A niche title with a higher CPM can be cheaper
per interested reader. When adding a second medium, cost it only against the
**incremental** in-target people it adds.

A medium whose audience is more than half in-target is a primary medium; one
with roughly 30% or more is secondary. Keep a low-share medium when it is the
only route to a segment.

| Medium and vehicle | Audience and in-target share (source, date) | Role (primary, secondary) | Unit and cost | CPM | CPIM | Reach and frequency planned | Flight dates | Response device and code | Objective served |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 6. Creative brief (one page)

Core fields: project scope and the problem the brand faces; business and
communication goals; target audience with demographics **and mindset**;
current perceptions; positioning and value proposition; single core message;
timeline. Optional: KPIs, competitors, past learning, mandatory elements,
budget (a budget, not a format — let the creative team choose the format).

Brief test: single-minded? logical? contains an insight or brand truth?
specific to this brand? commits to a point of view? A brief is also an
agreement: major changes are renegotiated, not improvised.

Creative review: re-read the brief aloud; judge against the brief and the
business, not taste; comments come only from people who read the brief and
carry a reason; separate off-brief (reject), on-brief but weak (iterate) and
taste (drop). Copy skeleton for direct response: attention through the main
benefit → about the reader → selling points, proof and guarantee → a clear,
easy action (Stockwell and Shaw's HEY-YOU-SEE-SO order).

## 7. Test before scale

1. Test the **message** (alternative benefit statements) before the
   **execution** (the ad itself).
2. Build a test matrix: 2–3 angles per value theme × 2 formats; cap the test
   budget (commonly a small share of total media; set the band with the
   finance owner) and the duration.
3. Read results by segment; kill losers early; retest the best two or three;
   then scale.
4. Diagnose "liked but not linked": good recall and likeability but weak
   message playback or brand linkage means fixing the branding and single
   message, not raising the media weight.
5. Low-cost pre-tests (WhatsApp polls, intercepts with 30–50 target people,
   small split tests) are directional, not normed; say so.

## 8. Measurement architecture (four levels)

| Level | Measures | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Message | Insight strength; message test results | Tested before production |
| Communication | Unaided and aided awareness, consideration, attribute shifts, by segment | Survey waves with the same design |
| Media | Planned versus delivered impressions, reach, frequency, effective reach, CPM, CPIM, CTR, cost per enquiry | Post-buy reconciliation every flight |
| Business | Trial, repeat, basket, margin, incremental sales against a control (hold-out place or period) | Isolate advertising from operations: a stock-out or broken website is not a media failure |

A campaign that wins on one level and fails on another is not a success.
Break results down by segment; averages hide weak groups.

## 9. Compliance

Comparative claims must be evidence-based and cleared by legal review; name
category rivals ("the long queue at the banking hall") rather than competitors
where possible, and never disparage. Sector rules apply to health, financial
products, alcohol, betting and children. Endorsements need disclosure (see
[location and scope calibration](location-and-scope-calibration.md), AD-10).
Advertising is an expense for accounting purposes; route treatment and any tax
on platform invoices to the finance engine (PL-05).
