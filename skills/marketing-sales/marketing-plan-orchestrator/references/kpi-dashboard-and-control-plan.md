# KPI dashboard and control plan

Parent: [Marketing Plan Orchestrator](../SKILL.md).

When to read: when writing sections 18–19 of a marketing plan (KPI dashboard and control plan), the metrics dashboard of business-plan section 07, or a retainer reporting framework. The evidence ladder and model-specific metric kits are in [evidence ladder and model metrics](../../../meta-strategy/meta-market-validation/references/lean-analytics-stage-and-model-metrics.md).

This is the engine's own dashboard and control design. It has five parts: pick the current focus number, admit only metrics that pass four tests, tie every target to an action, control every programme through a table with owners and tolerance bands, and run a fixed operating rhythm. The idea of focusing a team on one number at a time, with guard-rails, follows the analytics-for-start-ups literature (Croll, A. and Yoskovitz, B. (2013) *Lean Analytics*, O'Reilly Media); the rungs, tests, tables and cadence below are this engine's.

## 1. Choose the focus number for the current rung

Place the business on the evidence ladder, then choose **one focus metric** (often abbreviated OMTM) for the period: the number that measures the riskiest assumption now. Keep three to five **guard-rail** metrics that must not worsen while the team pushes the focus metric.

| Rung | Question | Typical focus metric | Move up when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Problem proven | Do enough people feel a real problem and already spend effort on it? | Share of interviewees raising it unprompted; pre-orders or deposits | At least 15 problem interviews per segment show consistent pain and willingness to pay or refer |
| 2 Repeat use | Do the right customers come back without being chased? | Repeat purchase or active-use rate by cohort | Retention holds at the level the model needs |
| 3 Customers bring customers | Do customers refer others? | Referrals per active customer; share of customers who refer | Referral measurably lowers effective acquisition cost |
| 4 Revenue that pays | Does each customer return more than they cost, repeatedly? | Contribution per customer; CLV to CAC; months to recover CAC | Unit economics meet the model |
| 5 Scale | Can channels and places grow without hand-feeding? | Payback by channel and region; cost to serve | At least two proven channels and a working operating rhythm |

Do not buy growth before rung 2: paid acquisition into a product customers do not repeat wastes cash. The focus metric changes as each constraint is solved. A team is in trouble if it cannot name its focus metric, names one that does not fit its rung, names several, or does not know today's value.

## 2. Four admission tests for every metric

| Test | Passes when |
|---|---|
| Comparable | It can be compared across periods, cohorts, segments or places |
| Memorable | The whole team can recall and discuss it |
| Rate | It is a ratio or rate, not a running total |
| Decision-linked | The action on a rise, a fall or no change is written down before data arrives |

Tag each metric as **leading** (predicts, for example qualified pipeline or enquiries by 5 p.m.) or **lagging** (explains, for example churn or monthly revenue); as **exploratory** or **reporting**; and, where a link is claimed, as **correlation** or **cause** (cause needs a controlled test).

**Vanity screen.** Do not accept as growth evidence: page views, visits, unique visitors, followers and likes, time on site, emails collected and downloads, unless the business model sells that attention (a media business sells views) or the metric rates a single piece of content. Replace with behaviour rates: share of followers reached who enquired; share of enquiries that paid; repeat purchase within 30 days.

## 3. KPI table

| KPI | Definition (numerator ÷ denominator, window) | Type | Baseline (source, date) | Line in the sand and its source | Owner | Review cadence | Action if hit | Action if missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The line comes from model arithmetic, a sourced benchmark or the firm's own trend (see [SMART objectives builder](smart-objectives-builder.md)). Never lower a line only to pass.

## 4. Four measurement levels

Report message, communication, media and business results separately, and separate marketing from operations failures (see [advertising and media plan](advertising-and-media-plan.md), section 8).

## 5. Control table

For every programme in the plan. The idea of a control table that names steering measures, standards and corrective action follows the strategic-management literature (Wheelen, T. L., Hunger, J. D., Hoffman, A. N. and Bamford, C. E. (2018) *Concepts in Strategic Management and Business Policy*, 15th edn, Pearson); the columns are this engine's.

| Action or programme | Objective served | Owner | Reviewer | Frequency | KPI and line | Tolerance band | Corrective action outside the band | Budget line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules: owner and reviewer are different people; tolerance bands are set before launch; corrective actions are specific ("move half this channel's budget to test card B"), never "review the strategy"; each row maps to a budget line.

## 6. Operating rhythm

A practitioner synthesis drawing on campaign-discipline ideas in Stutts, P. (2021) *The Undefeated Marketing System*; the table is this engine's.

| Rhythm | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| Daily in launch and campaign weeks | Channel owners | Spend pacing, frequency, cost per qualified enquiry, creative fatigue, enquiry response time |
| Weekly review (30–45 minutes) | Marketing lead, sales lead, channel owners | Test read-outs by segment; stop or scale decisions; lead quality from sales; focus metric and guard-rails against lines; blockers; "are we on the critical path?" |
| Monthly | Marketing lead with finance | CLV and repeat rate; budget against actual and the P&L line; channel mix; competitor watch |
| Quarterly or six-monthly | Owner or board | Refresh the customer insights report; revalidate strategy and objectives; reallocate budget |
| On a trigger | Owner | Re-survey and reopen research, plan and message after a shock: price change, competitor launch, regulatory change, election period, crisis |

Do not abandon the plan because of noise; change it when data shows a real shift.

**Sequencing gate for every new campaign:** research, then plan, then creative, then a capped test, then launch. Refuse tactic-first requests ("we need a TikTok campaign") until the first two steps exist.

**Three-level governance for larger teams:** the owner reviews three key assumptions monthly; managers review three actions per assumption weekly; teams run and log three experiments per action.

## 7. One-page decision memo for mid-course changes

Purpose (the problem and its urgency); three to five decisive facts; three markedly different options including "hold and monitor"; the recommendation with three reasons; the action plan (amount, approvals, dates, measurement); and an if-then contingency. Decide at the confidence the evidence allows; "more research" is an option only when it is cheap and fast (a memo form used in advertising management, for example Kelley, L. D. and Sheehan, K. B. (c. 2021) *Advertising Management in a Digital Environment*, Routledge).

**Illustrative example.** A Mbale coffee exporter on rung 4: focus metric months to recover CAC on new buyers (target 4, from model arithmetic); guard-rails: repeat order rate, gross margin, on-time shipment. Control row: "sample-lot programme, owner export manager, reviewer finance lead, weekly, cost per qualified buyer at most USD 150, tolerance plus or minus 20%, outside band move half the budget to the trade-fair test card."

## 8. Anti-patterns

- A 40-row dashboard with no focus metric. Fix: one focus metric plus guard-rails; the rest in an appendix.
- Running totals reported as growth. Fix: rates per period, by cohort.
- Blended averages hiding cohort decline. Fix: cohort tables aligned by months since first purchase.
- Targets with no agreed action. Fix: action-if-hit and action-if-missed columns.
- Control rows with the same owner and reviewer. Fix: separate the roles.
