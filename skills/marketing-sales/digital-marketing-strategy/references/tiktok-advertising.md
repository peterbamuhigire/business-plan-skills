# TikTok Marketing: Paid Campaigns, Content and Organic Growth

Use this reference when a plan includes short-form video advertising or organic growth on TikTok. Advertising minimums, formats, eligibility thresholds and benchmarks change frequently; treat every figure below as a dated planning assumption and verify it in the current advertising manager and creator tools.

## 1. Channel case and fit test

New advertising platforms tend to follow a cycle: an early period of cheap, unfamiliar, high-variance traffic; maturity as advertisers learn the rules and costs rise; then full-price competition for late entrants (an argument made by Marshall and Yu about search, Facebook and TikTok). The case for early investment is audience familiarity, a content library and algorithmic learning before the platform crowds. Verify current local adoption (strongest among about 18 to 35-year-olds in East Africa) before relying on it.

Advertising is not influencer work: the model is a modest weekly content routine plus a controlled ad budget.

When to use which channel:

| Scenario | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Established business, proven offer | TikTok plus Facebook | Repurpose winners; diversify traffic |
| New business, unproven offer | Facebook or messaging first | Cheaper testing and faster feedback |
| B2C, ages 18 to 35 | TikTok primary | Concentrated audience |
| B2B professional services | LinkedIn plus Facebook | Lower B2B intent on TikTok |
| Local geographic targeting | TikTok plus Facebook | Both target locally |
| Immediate leads | Facebook ads or messaging | Established conversion infrastructure |
| Six-to-twelve-month brand building | Organic plus boosted posts | Compounding audience |

Caution: TikTok amplifies a proven funnel; do not use it to test an unproven offer.

## 2. Economics first: the traffic-conversion-economics triangle

Every channel runs on traffic (people who see it), conversion (persuading them to act) and economics (customer value against cost). Design backwards: define what a customer must be worth, then derive the conversion and traffic required.
- 80/20 logic: a minority of content produces most results (find and amplify it); a small share of customers spends many times the average (plan for VIP economics).
- Break-even model: before scaling, reach break-even on acquisition (first-sale value at least equal to acquisition cost), then scale. Cost per acquisition should stay at or below an internally set share of first-sale value (a common rule of thumb is about 30 percent).
- Lifetime-value question: if a machine turned money into customers, how much would you feed it? The limit is customer lifetime value; a business with high lifetime value can outspend rivals on acquisition.

## 3. Content strategy

### Repurpose winners
1. Identify the best-performing content from the last 30 and 90 days on every platform.
2. Cut it to about 15 to 23 seconds.
3. Run it as an in-feed ad at a small daily budget.
4. Test until roughly one in ten videos wins; pause the rest; scale winners.

### Content factory
One long interview or demonstration yields several clips, a short video, a text excerpt and a messaging tip. One testimonial yields a short video, a social post and a review request. One walkthrough yields a series of short daily clips. Example: a ten-minute kitchen tour can yield about eight pieces (preparation clips, staff and ambience clips, a "how we make it" piece and a customer reaction).

### Why / how / what structure

| Type | Purpose | Examples | Funnel stage | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Why | Emotional connection | Testimonials, origin story, problem solved | Top | Cold |
| How | Value and trust | Tips, tutorials, demonstrations, interviews | Middle | Warm |
| What | Offer | Product, price, call to action, differentiation | Bottom | Hot (retargeting) |

Keep at least three of each type live at once and replace the weakest in each row with a new test.

### Fifteen-second ad anatomy
- Seconds 0 to 5: hook (on-screen text plus voice) that stops the scroll and shows the pain or pleasure.
- Seconds 5 to 10: solution, the offer described plainly.
- Seconds 10 to 15: one clear call to action (message, link, visit).
Hook patterns: a single reason a target audience struggles or succeeds; "five things [audience] should know"; "ever wonder how [outcome]"; a relatable story opener; "how to [result] without [obstacle]". Tip-list template: improve an outcome with three tips, list them, then invite a personalised consultation or offer.

### Production standards
Recent smartphone; always vertical 9:16; natural light or a ring light indoors; sound quality matters more than image quality (clip-on microphone); text overlays and captions on; movement and scene changes hold attention; keep authenticity over polish, since raw phone-shot content often converts better than studio work.

### Content types for small businesses

| Type | Ease | Conversion potential | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder talking | Very easy | High | Why I started, who I serve |
| Customer testimonial | Easy | Very high | Happy customer at delivery |
| Behind the scenes | Easy | High | Kitchen, production floor, farm |
| Before and after | Medium | Very high | Renovation, haircut, cleaning |
| Educational tip | Easy | Medium to high | "Three things to know before buying X" |
| Industry commentary | Medium | Medium | Local news relevant to the sector |
| Product demonstration | Medium | High | Unboxing, taste test |

Localisation: use the local language or a mix with English, local landmarks and familiar settings; testimonials from recognisable community members carry the most trust.

## 4. Paid formats and campaign structure

| Format | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| In-feed ads | Native ads between organic videos | Awareness, traffic, leads, conversions |
| Spark ads | Boost an existing organic post (yours or a creator's) | Amplify winners, creator collaborations; paid and organic engagement combine on one video, adding social proof |
| Top-of-app takeover | Full-screen at app open | Large brand campaigns; not for small businesses |
| Branded hashtag challenge | User participation | Mass awareness; high minimum |

Small businesses should use in-feed and Spark ads only.

Structure: campaign (objective, total budget), ad group (targeting, placement, daily budget, subject to a platform minimum), ads (video, copy, call to action).
Common objectives: video views (top of funnel), traffic (site or messaging link), conversions (purchase, form, message), lead generation (native form, no website).
Budget discipline: control spend at ad-group level; review weekly and do not leave campaigns running unreviewed; plan the weekly cost as daily minimum x days; test about two weeks before scaling and allow two to three weeks for the algorithm to optimise.

Targeting: demographics, interests and behaviours, custom audiences (pixel visitors, uploaded lists), lookalike audiences from best customers. The algorithm often does better with broad targeting plus strong creative.
Remarketing first: begin with retargeting (visitors, list, engagers); expand to cold audiences through lookalikes.

## 5. Metrics

| Stage | Metric | Definition | Planning benchmark (verify) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | CPM | Cost per thousand impressions | Varies widely by targeting |
| Awareness | Video view rate | Views divided by impressions | Higher is better; about half is a commonly cited good level |
| Engagement | Engagement rate | (Likes + comments + shares) / impressions | Set your own baseline |
| Consideration | Cost per view, cost per click | Spend divided by views or clicks | Compare with other channels |
| Conversion | Cost per acquisition | Spend divided by conversions | At or below the set share of first-sale value |
| Conversion | Return on ad spend | Revenue divided by spend | About 3x minimum as a rule of thumb |

Video completion rate matters more than raw engagement because the algorithm rewards watched-to-the-end videos; do not pad length.

## 6. Organic growth mechanics (Willis)

- Ranking signals: user interactions (completion is strongest, then likes, comments, shares, follows), video information (captions, hashtags, sounds, on-screen text) and device or account settings (least weight).
- Distribution logic: new videos are shown to a small test audience first; strong early engagement releases them to larger pools. Each video is an independent test, so posting frequency and the first-three-second hook are the highest-leverage factors.
- Trends: check the discovery page daily, follow five to ten high performers in the niche, move within days (trend life is often one to three weeks), and prefer local trends (local music, national events, farming seasons, school terms) over imported ones.
- Engagement tactics: reply to every comment in the first hour; use reply-with-video features (stitch, duet) to reach other creators' audiences; join niche hashtag challenges.
- Collaboration: swap content with creators in adjacent, non-competing niches, including local food, lifestyle and farming creators.
- Frequency: about daily during the growth phase, four to five times a week once established; use in-app analytics to post shortly before peak activity.
- Thumbnails and captions: expressive cover text; two or three short sentences, three to five mixed hashtags, end with a question.
- Live streams: available once follower thresholds are met (verify); useful for launches, Q&A, tours, demonstrations, advice sessions, and recommended separately from normal videos; virtual gifts can convert to revenue.

### In-app boost tool versus the ads manager
The in-app promote feature boosts an existing organic video from a phone with a low daily minimum, simple objectives (views, profile visits, followers, site visits), automatic or manual targeting and one-to-seven-day durations. Use it to test a video already performing organically. Use the ads manager for full objectives, pixel tracking, custom audiences and retargeting, and for conversion-focused campaigns.

### Creator marketplace and creator payments
- Creator marketplace: brands post briefs, creators apply or are invited, terms are agreed in-platform with analytics on reach and audience. Its creator pool leans towards other regions; for East Africa, direct outreach to local micro-creators is often more practical. Obtain current local rate quotes.
- Creator payment programmes: pay per thousand views once eligibility thresholds (age, followers, views, country) are met; per-view rates are small, so treat as supplementary. Verify country eligibility. Primary creator income normally comes from brand deals, own products, affiliate marketing and digital products.

## 7. Set-up costs and budgeting (assumptions)

Equipment: existing smartphone, a ring light, a clip-on microphone, a free editing app. Budget stages: minimum viable (one ad group at the platform minimum), recommended starting budget (one ad group for about five days), then scale on evidence. Obtain current local prices for equipment and platform minimums.
