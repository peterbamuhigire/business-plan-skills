# B2B Social Media: Lead Generation and Return Measurement

Use this reference when a business-to-business plan needs a social media section that justifies itself in leads and revenue rather than followers.

## 1. Return model

Social media can lower the cost of acquiring a customer and raise the customer's lifetime value at the same time, which few channels do.

- Return on investment (%) = (TLV - COCA) / COCA x 100.
- TLV (total lifetime value) = average sale value x number of purchases over the relationship.
- COCA (cost of customer acquisition) = (all marketing costs + all sales costs) / customers acquired in the same period.

### Cost inputs for COCA
- Marketing: pro-rated staff time and overhead, agency fees, paid media, tools (email, CRM, analytics), content production.
- Sales: salaries, commissions, travel, demonstration costs.

### Channel-level COCA procedure
1. Allocate staff cost to the channel by share of time; add overhead and tools.
2. Divide by customers attributed to the channel in the period.
3. Compare across channels (organic content versus paid social, for example).
4. Match the period to the sales cycle: with a 90-day cycle, judge a quarter's spend against the previous quarter's leads.

### TLV worked pattern
Services firm: average project value x projects per year x years retained. Supply firm: average monthly order x months retained. Use the business's own figures; illustrative amounts in the plan must be labelled as assumptions.

Decision rule: a high TLV supports a higher COCA. State the acceptable COCA-to-TLV ratio in the plan and test each channel against it.

### Why social media moves both terms
- Lower COCA: organic content is owned or earned attention that accumulates, reducing paid-media dependence.
- Higher TLV: education before and after the sale reduces disappointment, builds loyalty, repeat purchase and referral.

## 2. Five-step lead-generation process

1. Get the basics right: professional profiles, consistent branding, responsive inbox, working website or landing page.
2. Maximise discovery: search-optimised posts, keyword-targeted, shareable formats (how-tos, checklists, case studies).
3. Make conversion available everywhere: every item carries a next step (WhatsApp link, enquiry form, free consultation).
4. Test small and stop failures early: formats, times, offers.
5. Optimise for lead flow: measure by channel, content type and segment; move budget and time to the best performers.

## 3. Sharing mix rule of thumb

In a run of about fifteen posts: roughly ten curate or share others' useful content, four are original thought leadership or behind-the-scenes, one is directly promotional. The aim is value before asking. Local adaptation: curated sector news, customer messages shared with permission, and production behind-the-scenes.

## 4. Content types and lead power

| Content | Lead power | Local feasibility | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search-optimised articles | High, slow | High | Free to produce; compounding traffic |
| Short educational video (60 to 90 seconds) | Very high | High | Works on WhatsApp and Facebook |
| Case studies | Very high | High | Recognised client names add proof |
| WhatsApp how-to broadcasts | High | Very high | Strong open rates; builds trust |
| Guides and ebooks | High for B2B | Medium | Needs design; good for professional services |
| Webinars or live sessions | High for B2B | Medium | Best with a sizeable invited audience |
| Testimonials | High | Very high | Low cost, credible in trust-based markets |
| LinkedIn articles | Medium to high | Medium | Reach is lower locally; useful for professional services |

## 5. Attribution

- First-action attribution credits the first touch (which channel creates interest).
- Last-action attribution credits the final touch (which tactic closes).
- Minimum viable method: ask every new customer "How did you hear about us?" and log it in a spreadsheet for six months before buying attribution software.
- Track a funnel end to end, for example post, page visit, WhatsApp enquiry, quote, payment (mobile money), referral. Record which post drove the enquiry, days from first contact to payment, and whether the customer referred others.

## 6. LinkedIn tactics for B2B

1. Optimise founder and senior-staff profiles; headline states the value offered, not a job title.
2. Company page shows client results, team expertise and sector insight.
3. Join and contribute to relevant sector groups.
4. Personalised connection requests and direct messages, more effective after engaging with the prospect's content.
5. Post about three times a week: insights, case studies, questions.

Metrics: connection acceptance rate, post engagement rate, message response rate, leads attributed to LinkedIn.

## 7. Email and WhatsApp opt-in nurturing

Opt-in is a legal requirement in formal markets (check the applicable data-protection rules) and a quality filter.
- Build the list with a "join our tips group" call to action, a lead magnet (guide, price list, template) and post-sale opt-in requests.
- Nurture sequence: week 1 welcome plus best content; week 2 case study; week 3 educational piece; week 4 soft offer (consultation, demo, quote); afterwards apply the sharing mix.
- Put social links in message footers and invite subscribers to a community group.

## 6b. Roadblocks and responses

| Roadblock | Response |
|---|---|
| No time | Assign about two hours a week per channel; batch content monthly |
| Customers are not on social media | Verify by searching for the segment before assuming |
| Nothing to say | Answer the ten most common customer questions |
| Compliance blocks posts | Involve compliance early; use pre-approved templates |
| It is free | Budget staff time, tools and design |
| Cannot tell whether it works | Set up COCA tracking first; review revenue per channel quarterly |

## 8. KPIs and plan-section prompts

KPIs: leads by channel, COCA by channel, TLV, COCA-to-TLV ratio, conversion rate by stage, days to close, referral rate.

Prompts: What is the target COCA and TLV? Which two channels will be funded first and why? How will leads be attributed? Who owns the content calendar and the inbox? What is the opt-in and consent process?
