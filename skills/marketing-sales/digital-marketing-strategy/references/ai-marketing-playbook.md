# AI-Assisted Marketing: Capability Map and Adoption Plan

Use this reference when a business plan includes AI-assisted marketing. AI amplifies a sound strategy; it does not replace customer knowledge, creative judgement or human oversight. A weak strategy run through AI fails faster.

## 1. Scope test

AI marketing means using machine learning and generative tools to find patterns in customer data, automate repetitive tasks, personalise messages at scale and optimise ad delivery.

It does not replace:
- field research and customer interviews;
- brand voice and factual accuracy (AI drafts, a person approves);
- data hygiene and monitoring (models need clean inputs and oversight);
- ethical review (bias, privacy, transparency).

## 2. Capability map by function

For each function, state the use case, the minimum data needed, the tool tier and the KPI it should move.

| Function | What AI does | Minimum viable start | Graduate when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer analysis and segmentation | Predictive analytics (next purchase, churn, acceptable price); sentiment analysis; behaviour-based clustering (for example loyal high-value, price-sensitive one-off, referrers); image recognition of brand mentions | Manual segments on the WhatsApp list: existing customers, warm leads, cold enquiries | A CRM holds roughly 500 or more customer records |
| Personalisation | Product recommendations, dynamic email content, ad-creative variation, subject-line testing | Segmented WhatsApp broadcasts by customer type (new enquiry, first-time buyer, repeat, VIP) | The email list passes about 200 contacts; use send-time optimisation |
| Chatbots and service | 24/7 response, instant replies, parallel conversations, lead qualification | WhatsApp Business auto-reply: greeting, FAQ links, hours | Enquiry volume justifies a flow-based or language-understanding bot (about 30 a day is a workable trigger) |
| Social media | Best-time scheduling, sentiment monitoring, automated ad-audience expansion, content-performance prediction, hashtag and keyword suggestions | Batch-draft a week of captions with a generative assistant, schedule in a free suite, review results weekly | Volume or channel count exceeds manual scheduling |
| Email | Behaviour segmentation, subject-line optimisation, send-time optimisation, dynamic content, triggered sequences | Free-tier email platform with welcome and post-purchase sequences | List and revenue justify a paid automation platform |
| SEO | Keyword research, content optimisation, conversational (voice) query mapping, competitor keyword gaps, first drafts | Local search first (section 3) | Organic search becomes a material acquisition channel |
| Content | Ideation, first drafts, repurposing, translation and localisation, performance analysis | Brief, generate, human edit, optimise, publish, measure | The content calendar exceeds team capacity |
| Automation and lead scoring | Score leads on behaviour; run nurture sequences | Spreadsheet score (1 to 5 on budget fit, urgency, authority) | Lead volume needs CRM automation |
| Sales enablement | Prospect identification against the ideal customer profile, qualification, forecasting, personalised outreach, call transcription and coaching | Assistant-drafted quotes and proposal introductions | A defined pipeline and history exist to forecast from |

## 3. Procedures

### Chatbot design
1. Define what the bot handles (FAQs, hours, availability, qualification) and what goes to a person (complaints, custom orders, negotiation).
2. Seed it with the ten most frequent questions.
3. Use language understanding rather than keyword matching once on an intermediate or advanced tier.
4. Provide a visible route to a human at all times.
5. Tiers: free WhatsApp Business auto-reply; flow-based tools with decision trees; messaging API with language understanding and CRM integration. Price each tier with a dated vendor quote.

### Drip and lifecycle sequences (planning templates; tune to the sales cycle)
- New lead: welcome, educational content, case study or proof, offer.
- Post-purchase: thanks, usage guide, replenishment reminder, loyalty offer.
- Abandoned basket (if the business has one): reminder within hours, second reminder next day, incentive after about three days.
- No response after about three weeks: move to a low-priority nurture list.

### Lead scoring (illustrative; calibrate against closed deals)
Assign points to signals such as a pricing-page visit, a resource download, repeated email opens, a direct WhatsApp enquiry and fit with the target segment. Set a threshold above which the lead is contacted immediately. Review the weights quarterly against actual conversions.

### Content production with AI
1. Brief: audience, goal, tone, key messages.
2. Generate a first draft.
3. Edit for brand voice, facts and local context.
4. Optimise for search and add a clear call to action.
5. Publish and measure.

Repurpose each researched piece into several formats (short tips, social posts, a video script, email paragraphs). Human-review any translation into local languages.

### Local search priority order
1. Complete and maintain the Google Business Profile.
2. Research ten to fifteen local search terms customers actually use.
3. Optimise the home page and product or service pages.
4. Publish a small number of long-tail articles each month.
5. Run a review-generation routine.

Write FAQ content in natural, conversational language, since spoken queries and voice messages are conversational.

## 4. Benchmarks (planning assumptions only)

Email benchmarks commonly quoted for planning: open rate above about 20 percent, click-through above about 2.5 percent, unsubscribes below about 0.5 percent per send. These vary by sector, list quality and mail-client privacy changes. Re-verify with a dated source and replace with the business's own baseline after the first three sends. Track revenue per email by campaign. Do not quote vendor personalisation-uplift statistics without a citation and date.

## 5. Ethics and risk controls

1. Targeting bias: review who the ads do and do not reach; exclude no protected group.
2. Privacy: use only data customers knowingly provided; do not buy third-party data of doubtful origin; check the applicable data-protection law (in Uganda, the Data Protection and Privacy Act; verify current requirements).
3. Transparency: disclose AI-generated content where audiences would reasonably expect to know; never fabricate reviews.
4. Output bias: models trained mainly on Western data can produce culturally wrong output; a person with local knowledge reviews everything before publication.
5. Dependency and quality: keep prompt templates, an approval step and a monitoring cadence.

## 6. Implementation roadmap (template for a small business)

| Period | Action | Cost tier |
|---|---|---|
| Quarter 1 | Set up a generative assistant with prompt templates for the five most common content types; configure the WhatsApp Business auto-reply | Free or low |
| Quarter 2 | Enable automated ad-audience optimisation inside existing ad spend; install web analytics with conversion events | Within ad budget |
| Quarter 3 | Basic email automation (welcome and post-purchase); weekly brand and competitor alerts | Free tier |
| Quarter 4 | Evaluate a chatbot if enquiry volume justifies it; implement lead scoring and align it with sales | Obtain a dated quote |
| Year 2 | Move to full marketing automation if list size and revenue justify the subscription | Obtain a dated quote |

Tool ladder: spreadsheet and WhatsApp (start); free CRM tier (growing); automation platform with scoring (scaling); enterprise CRM (advanced). Subscription prices change often; record the source and date for each.

## 7. Plan-section prompts

- Which three marketing tasks will AI take over, and which KPI moves as a result?
- What customer data exists today, and how is consent recorded?
- What human review gate applies before publication?
- What is the monthly tool cost against the labour hours saved?
- What is the fallback if a tool or platform changes its terms?
