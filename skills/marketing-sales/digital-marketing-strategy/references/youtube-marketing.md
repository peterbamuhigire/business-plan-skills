# YouTube Marketing: Channel Set-Up, Content, Search, Advertising and Monetisation

Use this reference when a plan includes video search, tutorials or a YouTube channel as a lead-generation asset. YouTube is a search engine and a compounding content library: a well-made video keeps producing enquiries for years at no extra cost, unlike a campaign that stops when the budget does.

Platform thresholds, formats, revenue rates, audience figures and specifications change often. Every number below is a planning assumption to verify in YouTube Studio, Google Ads and a dated audience source before it enters a plan.

## 1. Why and when

- Discoverability: videos are indexed by YouTube and by Google, so a business can appear on the first page of search without a website. Feed-based platforms reach followers and paid audiences and decay quickly; search-based video accumulates views.
- Compounding: views typically build over months as the algorithm matches the video to similar searches. Model a video as a permanent sales asset, not a campaign.
- Local opportunity test: search the ten most likely customer queries in the target language and city. If few local businesses answer them with video, competition is thin. Check audience size and mobile share for the target country with a dated source.
- Fit: strongest for products and services that need demonstration or explanation (agriculture, building, food, solar, trades, finance guidance). Weaker for pure impulse retail.

## 2. Channel set-up checklist

1. Create a Brand Account under a custom business name so several people can manage it without sharing personal credentials.
2. Profile image: the logo, legible as a small circle.
3. Banner: business name, tagline, website and upload schedule; keep key content inside the safe area for all devices; use a template tool.
4. Channel trailer (60 to 120 seconds) for non-subscribers: viewer problem, what the channel provides, who it is for, subscribe prompt; feature a recognisable face.
5. About section (indexed text): open with "[Business] is [what you do] for [whom] in [place]", list the weekly content topics with the benefit of each, invite subscription, add contact details; place primary keywords naturally in the first hundred words; add website, messaging link and social links.
6. Channel keywords: ten to fifteen phrases (industry plus country, industry plus city, service type, audience problem). They have modest ranking effect now but help categorisation.
7. Playlists named with searchable phrases: getting started, customer success stories, how-to and tips, product or service showcase, behind the scenes. Playlists lengthen sessions, which signals quality.
8. Community posts (once eligible): audience questions to inform topics, previews of upcoming videos, quick tips, polls.

## 3. Content strategy

### Video types

| Type | Business use | Typical length | Search value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tutorial or how-to | Step-by-step instruction on a local problem | 5 to 15 min | Very high |
| Product demonstration | Shows the product in use | 3 to 8 min | High; converts best |
| Case study or testimonial | Real customer result | 5 to 10 min | High |
| Behind the scenes | Process, team, farm or factory tour | 5 to 12 min | Medium |
| FAQ | Answers the questions customers ask | 3 to 8 min | High |
| List or round-up | "Seven most profitable X" | 5 to 12 min | Very high |
| News commentary | Regulation or market changes | 3 to 7 min | Medium |
| Review | Tests products or tools | 8 to 15 min | High |
| Comparison | Side by side, for example two options for the same job | 6 to 12 min | High |
| Live stream | Q&A, events, launches | 20 to 60 min | Medium |

Priority: tutorials and FAQs give the most durable search traffic; demonstrations convert the largest share of viewers to enquiries.

### Cadence by stage
- Launch (about three months): one to two videos a week to build a library of roughly twelve to twenty.
- Growth (months four to twelve): weekly.
- Established: weekly long video plus a short vertical video, since shorts aid discovery and long videos retain and convert.
Consistency matters more than frequency. One quality video a week takes roughly three to five hours for one person with a phone and free editing software.

### Titles
Formula: primary keyword phrase plus a benefit, qualifier (year, place, number) or intrigue. Lead with the keyword, not the brand; keep to roughly 60 characters so mobile does not truncate; numbers often lift click-through; avoid clickbait, which is penalised when viewers leave at once. Title patterns: how-to, list, question, local, problem-solution.

### Thumbnails
Click-through rate drives views (doubling it doubles views on the same impressions). Rules: expressive face close-up if possible; three to five large words in contrasting colour, readable at small size; bright backgrounds; 1280 x 720 pixels, 16:9, under 2 MB; consistent fonts and colours across the channel; leave a curiosity gap (show the reaction or "before", not the full result); always upload a custom thumbnail. Tools: free design apps.

### The first fifteen seconds
1. Seconds 0 to 3: pattern interrupt (unexpected image, bold claim, surprising fact, question, or start mid-action; no logo animation or "welcome to my channel").
2. Seconds 3 to 7: name who the video is for and the problem.
3. Seconds 7 to 12: credibility signal.
4. Seconds 12 to 15: promise of what the viewer will know or do by the end.

## 4. Search optimisation

Keyword research methods: autocomplete suggestions; the channel's own search-terms report; trend comparison tools filtered by country; free tiers of video keyword extensions (volume and competition estimates); competitor channels' most-viewed titles. Algorithms now read spoken content and understand topics, so cover the subject thoroughly and say the keyword naturally; keyword stuffing no longer works.

Description template: first two sentences hold the primary keyword and the summary (shown before "show more", roughly the first 100 to 150 characters); timestamped chapters; two or three paragraphs of natural detail with secondary keywords; resources mentioned; contact block (messaging link, email, website, social page); subscribe prompt; three to five hashtags at the end. Aim for at least about 250 words, 400 to 500 for competitive terms.

Tags: five to ten; first tag the exact primary keyword, then close variations, then broader category, then channel and brand names.

End screens (last 5 to 20 seconds): subscribe button plus a recommended video or playlist. Cards: at natural moments, no more than about three, linking to related videos or playlists.

Captions: upload manual captions (also in local languages); the transcript is indexed.

## 5. How ranking and recommendation work

Two systems: search (where a video appears for a query) and discovery (home feed, up-next, suggestions). Search views carry higher purchase intent; discovery grows the channel faster.

| Signal | Weight | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate | Very high | Target around 5 to 10 percent (verify) |
| Average view duration and audience retention | Very high | Aim for about half the video on average |
| Total watch time | High | Older videos compound |
| Session watch time | High | Playlists and end screens keep viewers on YouTube |
| Subscriber activity | Medium | Inactive subscribers hurt |
| Likes, comments, shares | Medium | Comments count more than likes |
| Completion rate | Medium | Helps shorter videos |
| Post-watch actions | Medium | Subscribe, like, share |
| Transcript and spoken words | Indexing | Say the keyword naturally |
| Video quality | Baseline | Upload at least 1080p |

Test loop: YouTube shows the video to a small audience, measures click-through then retention, and expands distribution if both are strong. Strong click-through with weak retention pulls distribution back (clickbait penalty); weak click-through stops testing.

Current features to use: short vertical videos integrated with the main algorithm; disclosure of realistic AI-generated content; podcast uploads; second-by-second retention heatmaps (find and fix the exact drop-off point).

## 6. Monetisation and lead generation

Primary use for most small businesses: the channel as a permanent lead generator. Ad revenue is secondary.

### Platform programmes
- Partner programme: a standard tier (about 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months) and a lower entry tier with fewer features (about 500 subscribers with recent uploads and watch hours). Verify current thresholds and country eligibility.
- Revenue per thousand views (RPM) varies by audience country and niche; East African audiences earn well below North American or European ones (verify current figures). Model ad revenue as monthly views x RPM / 1,000 and treat it as supplementary.
- Channel memberships (monthly fee for member-only content) for loyal repeat customers; Super Thanks and Super Chat (low revenue, builds community); merchandise shelf (check country availability).

### Other revenue
- Sponsored content once there is a defined niche audience (input suppliers, hardware and paint retailers, ingredient and equipment suppliers pay for reach); obtain local rate quotes.
- Affiliate links with disclosure ("this video contains affiliate links"), through local e-commerce programmes, global marketplaces or direct supplier arrangements.
- Diaspora-oriented content for higher RPM and interest in origin-country products.
- Email or messaging list built from viewers, for monetisation independent of the platform.

### Lead generation model
1. Publish videos that answer the ideal customer's questions.
2. End each with a clear, spoken call to action (not only in the description).
3. Treat each view by a potential customer as a warm lead who has consumed minutes of your expertise.
4. Track which videos generate messages or visits with tagged links.
5. Use a clickable link on the video where the programme allows; otherwise description link plus verbal call.

### Conversion paths
- Video to messaging: verbal call in the last minute, direct messaging link in the description, pinned comment with the number.
- Video to website lead form: description link to a specific landing page that continues the video's topic, with a lead magnet.
- Video to email list: a free downloadable resource.
- Channel to community to purchase: community posts, live Q&A; viewers who have watched five to ten videos are much warmer.

### Call-to-action by video type

| Video | Viewer intent | Call to action |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness or tutorial | Curious | Subscribe and follow on messaging or social |
| Problem-solution | Starting to consider | Free guide or free consultation |
| Product demo | Evaluating | Contact for pricing or order online |
| Testimonial or case study | Ready to buy | "Get the same result: message us now" |

Script: "If this was useful, click subscribe so you don't miss the next one, and if you need help with [problem], the number is in the description. Message us today."

## 7. Analytics and diagnosis

| Metric | Target (verify) | If below target |
|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate | About 5 to 10 percent | Redesign thumbnail; revise title |
| Average view duration | About 40 to 60 percent of length | Improve hook; trim dead segments |
| Audience retention | About 50 percent | Use the heatmap to locate drop-offs |
| Watch hours | Growing monthly | Raise cadence; promote |
| Impressions | Growing | Improve click-through; promote |
| Subscriber growth | Positive | Add subscribe prompts |
| RPM | Depends on niche and country | Target higher-value niches |
| Traffic sources | Search and suggested dominate | If only subscribers, improve search optimisation |
| Demographics | Match target customer | Adjust topics |

Underperformance diagnosis:
1. Very low impressions in the first week: the video is not being tested widely; review title and tags against real searches; seed initial watch time from messaging groups and social posts.
2. High impressions, low click-through (under about 3 percent): redesign the thumbnail (use the built-in thumbnail test) and sharpen the title.
3. Good click-through, weak retention in the first 30 seconds: rewrite the opening so it delivers the title's promise.
4. Good retention, few enquiries: add an explicit spoken call to action, a messaging link in the description and a card.
5. Strong start then decay: normal for topical content; for evergreen tutorials update the title with the current year, add new information or a short video linking to the original.
Weekly routine: overview of the last 28 days, retention heatmap per video, traffic sources, search-terms research tab for new topics.

## 8. Advertising on YouTube

Managed through Google Ads.

| Format | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| In-stream skippable | Before or during videos; skippable after five seconds; charged only for meaningful views | Awareness, demonstrations, reach |
| In-stream non-skippable | Short, watched in full | High-impact messages |
| Bumper | Six seconds, non-skippable | Brand recall alongside other campaigns |
| In-feed discovery | Thumbnail in search results and suggestions; charged on click | High-intent viewers, tutorials, demonstrations |
| Shorts ads | Between vertical videos | Younger audiences |
| Homepage masthead | Reserved buy, high minimum | Large brands only |

Small businesses: in-stream skippable and in-feed. In-feed is often better value because the viewer opted in.

ABCD structure for in-stream ads: Attract (hook in the first seconds; do not waste them on a logo), Brand (name or logo early), Connect (deliver value or emotion), Direct (clear call to action). The first five seconds are the unskippable window; show the end result, make a bold claim or ask a direct question.

In-feed creative: thumbnail rules as for organic; title written like a search-optimised title; two short description lines.

### Targeting
- Geography: country, region, city or radius (for a city business, a radius around the centre); separate campaigns for different countries, languages or currencies.
- In-market audiences (actively researching a category); affinity audiences (long-term interests); custom-intent audiences built from recent Google search terms (strong link between search behaviour and video ads); remarketing to site visitors, video viewers and subscribers (highest return).

### Campaign structure and budget
Campaign (objective, geography, daily budget), ad group (audience, placements), ads (creative, call to action). Starter set: brand awareness (in-market and affinity), competitor and category research (custom intent, in-feed), remarketing (warm audiences). Test for about two weeks with a fixed total test budget; record cost per view, cost per click and cost per lead, then scale on evidence. Cost per view and per click vary by market; obtain them from the live account.

## 9. B2B and B2C approaches

- B2C: reach customers early with problem-aware, tutorial and entertaining content; high frequency for recall; track reach, subscriber growth and view-to-message conversion.
- B2B: buyers rarely browse video to find suppliers, but use it for post-sale support (installation and set-up guides that cut support calls), relationship building, pre-sales content the sales team shares, authority positioning, case studies with client organisations, thought leadership, and recruitment culture videos; track shares by sales staff, time on channel and form submissions from descriptions.

## 10. Local adaptation

- Language: most business video is in English; audiences for Luganda, Kiswahili, Kinyarwanda and other local languages are large and underserved. Film in English for reach and add local-language captions, or make a short-video series in the local language from each long video. Verify search demand per language and country.
- Niche selection: compare search demand with the volume and quality of existing content; pick one niche, publish twenty or more focused videos in three months. Candidate topics: poultry, maize and piggery farming, construction costs and building guides, local restaurants and delivery, tax and social-security guidance, solar installation, motorcycle taxi business, local fashion.
- Mobile-first: most views are on phones. Film horizontal for standard videos and vertical for shorts; keep text in the centre third; use captions (many watch on mute); prefer close-ups; deliver one idea at a time.
- Data cost: viewers weigh data cost, so make titles and thumbnails communicate full value, avoid filler, use shorts for quick value, and upload at 1080p unless higher is necessary.
- Set-up kit (obtain current local prices): existing smartphone, tripod, ring light, clip-on microphone, free editing and thumbnail tools.

## 11. Production workflow for one person

1. Pre-production (about one hour): keyword check, five to eight bullet outline (not a full script), background and light, equipment check.
2. Production (30 to 60 minutes): one continuous set-up where possible; cheat sheet rather than script; film the intro last; capture three to five B-roll clips.
3. Post-production (30 minutes to two hours): cut dead air, lower-third name, chapter markers, captions, export at 1080p MP4, 16:9, H.264.
4. Upload (15 to 20 minutes): title, full description, tags, custom thumbnail, playlist, schedule two or three days ahead, add cards and end screens after processing.
Batching: film four videos in one day and publish over four weeks; one set-up serves all four, publishing stays regular during busy operational weeks, and topics can cross-reference.

## 12. Promotion beyond the platform

- Messaging: share the link with the broadcast list on publication and in relevant groups (with permission); add the channel link to the business profile.
- Facebook: share as a link with the two or three best points as copy; do not upload the full video natively, since that splits views.
- Website: embed each video on its matching page to lift time on page and views.
- Email: use a thumbnail image with a play overlay linking to the video (video embeds are often suppressed).
- Collaborations with channels in complementary niches: mutual features.
- Community: link the new video in comments of older, related videos; reply to every comment in the first 48 hours.

## 13. Quick-start plan (template)

- Months 1 to 3: create the channel and About text (week 1); research about twenty topics (week 2); batch-produce four videos and thumbnails (weeks 3 and 4); publish weekly with playlists and end screens (from week 5); review click-through and retention (weeks 9 and 10); produce the next batch and start promotion (weeks 11 and 12).
- Months 4 to 6: steady weekly output, add shorts from long videos, reply to comments within a day; set a subscriber target from evidence.
- Months 7 to 12: pursue partner-programme eligibility; approach sponsors once the audience is defined; add affiliate links; refresh titles and thumbnails on the top five videos; add lead capture (email or messaging).

## 14. Plan-section prompts

- Which customer questions will the first twenty videos answer, and which keywords do they target?
- Who produces, edits and publishes, at how many hours a week?
- Which conversion path is used and how are leads tracked?
- What are the retention and click-through targets and the review cadence?
- Is paid promotion planned, with what test budget and stop rule?
