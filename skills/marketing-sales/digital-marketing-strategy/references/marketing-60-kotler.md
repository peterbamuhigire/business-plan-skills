# Immersive and Phygital Marketing: Horizons, Generations and Next-Stage Channels

Use this reference when a plan needs to position its marketing on the maturity curve (from omnichannel to immersive), address younger customer generations, or decide which emerging technologies to plan for. The framework of "Marketing 1.0 to 6.0" (Kotler, Kartajaya and Setiawan) is named and applied here with brief attribution; the applications are the engine's own.

## 1. Maturity ladder

| Stage | Focus | Typical technology | Planning use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 Product-driven | Features and specifications | Mass media | Historical baseline |
| 2.0 Customer-oriented | Satisfaction, relationship management | Internet, CRM | Contact database, messaging apps |
| 3.0 Human-centric | Values, mission, sustainability | Social media | Community trust, alignment with the Sustainable Development Goals |
| 4.0 Digital | Content plus omnichannel | Mobile, AI | Content plus messaging-led omnichannel |
| 5.0 Technology for humanity | AI-assisted personalisation | AI, data | Generative tools, automated ad optimisation |
| 6.0 Metamarketing | Physical and digital merged | Sensors, extended reality, spatial computing, blockchain | Early-adopter horizon |

Placement rule: most small businesses in East Africa execute stage 4.0 now; stage 5.0 tools are immediately accessible; stage 6.0 elements suit a multi-year experimentation horizon. State the current stage in the plan and do not budget for capabilities two stages ahead.

## 2. Channel progression

- Multichannel: separate online and offline channels for different customers.
- Omnichannel: integrated journey (research online, buy in store).
- Metamarketing: digital enhances physical and physical authenticates digital.

Example loops to map: short video, then QR scan in store, then mobile-money payment, then an unboxing shared in a status update; or social ad, then walk-in and QR menu, then a map review, then owned photos; or portfolio on a visual platform, then visit, then messaged care tips, then referral.

## 3. Three-layer model

1. Enablers: sensors (real-time data in physical spaces), AI (personalisation from data streams), spatial computing (interactive displays, gesture interfaces), augmented and virtual reality (virtual try-on, furniture placement, virtual showrooms, training), blockchain (community-owned platforms, tokenised loyalty). AI and augmented reality run on smartphones now; sensors, spatial computing and blockchain need infrastructure and belong on a three-to-five-year horizon.
2. Environments: extended reality (QR codes, overlays, interactive displays, shoppable pop-ups) and virtual worlds (mainly gaming and social entertainment for the youngest customers).
3. Experiences: multisensory marketing (ambience, music, signature scent, tactile packaging), spatial marketing (proximity notifications, location offers, in-store overlays), virtual-world brand activations.

## 4. Younger customers ("phygital natives")

| Trait | Implication |
|---|---|
| Pragmatic, price-aware, research-driven | Show value and price plainly; avoid hype |
| Authenticity-seeking | Behind-the-scenes, unfiltered customer content, founder-led video |
| Short-form video native | Lead with video; hook in three seconds |
| Comfortable with AI | Chat assistants, personalised broadcasts |
| Community-oriented | Groups and peer referral over mass media |
| Experience over possessions | Market the experience of using the product |
| Sustainability-aware | Show ethical sourcing, local jobs and community impact, with proof |

Younger consumers adopt adult buying behaviour earlier; treat them as sophisticated buyers.

## 5. Five trends to test

1. Short-form video and micro-moments (brief intent-driven moments on a phone): status videos, short demonstrations, always with a visible call to action; shoppable video where available.
2. Community-based social media: join and contribute to relevant groups; build a branded customer community; trusted member referrals often convert better than paid ads.
3. Conversational commerce: catalogue, auto-reply and broadcast list on a business messaging account plus mobile money completes the loop; automation takes first enquiries, people close complex deals.
4. Social commerce: marketplaces and in-app shops; check platform availability locally; "link in bio" plus messaging conversion is the practical fallback.
5. Algorithmic personalisation: use broad targeting and strong creative and let the ad platform optimise using customer data.

## 6. Three-horizon roadmap template

- Horizon 1 (years one to two): consistent online and offline presence, one brand voice and value proposition, mobile money as the payment bridge.
- Horizon 2 (years two to four): QR codes linking products and spaces to content, location-based messages for walk-ins, in-store digital displays and shoppable social content, a chat assistant for first-line service.
- Horizon 3 (year four onwards): augmented-reality try-on (fashion, furniture, cosmetics), virtual tours (property, hospitality, motor), tokenised loyalty, virtual-world presence for the youngest audiences.
Gate each horizon on customer evidence and cash, not on novelty.

## 7. Sustainability as positioning

Alignment with the Sustainable Development Goals is commercially relevant for younger buyers. Evidence to show: local sourcing and supply-chain transparency; number of local jobs; waste, energy and packaging practices; women's empowerment; partnerships with local non-profits. Rule: proof over claims; performative claims are detected and punished.

## 8. Plan-section prompts

- Which maturity stage is the business at, and which single step up is funded?
- Which phygital loop applies to the customer journey?
- What evidence would justify moving to the next horizon?
- Which sustainability claims can be documented?
