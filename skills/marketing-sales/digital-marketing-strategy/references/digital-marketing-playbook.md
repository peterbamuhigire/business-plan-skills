# Digital Channel Selection, Budgeting and Measurement

Use this reference to choose digital channels, set objectives, allocate a limited budget and specify measurement in a business plan. Digital is the main discovery route for most small businesses; the planning problem is prioritisation, not whether to participate.

## 1. Strategy definition

A digital marketing strategy states how the business will:
1. reach the target audience;
2. achieve specific, measurable goals;
3. allocate budget and staff;
4. measure and optimise over time.

Without it, effort spreads across too many channels.

### SMART objectives

| Test | Question | Example form |
|---|---|---|
| Specific | Is the outcome defined? | "Generate a stated number of WhatsApp enquiries per month" |
| Measurable | Can data track it? | A dedicated number or tracked link |
| Attainable | Do budget and team support it? | Checked against capacity |
| Relevant | Does it serve the business goal? | Leads must convert at target margin |
| Time-bound | Is there a deadline? | "By the end of the second quarter" |

Goal types and their measures: traffic (sessions), leads (enquiries, form fills, messages), conversion (rate, cost of customer acquisition), awareness (reach, impressions), retention (repeat-purchase rate, customer lifetime value).

## 2. Channel selection

For each channel record: role in the funnel, audience fit, time to results, cost, and primary metric. Fund only the channels the audience actually uses.

| Channel | Role | Key tactics | Time and cost note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search optimisation | Capture existing demand | Keyword research on terms customers type; on-page (titles, descriptions, headers, image text); helpful content; technical basics (speed, mobile, HTTPS, crawlability); earned links (directories, associations, press) | Allow at least three to six months; low running cost once built |
| Local search | Location-based demand | Complete Google Business Profile, ask every satisfied customer for a review | Often under-used by local competitors; verify by auditing competitors |
| Paid search and paid social | Fast lead generation | High-intent keywords; audience targeting by demographics, interests, location, behaviour; retargeting; A/B tests; start with a small daily budget, measure acquisition cost, scale what is profitable | Metrics: cost per click, cost per lead, return on ad spend, conversion rate |
| Content | Attract and retain | Answer the ten most common customer questions and objections; repurpose across formats; show process transparency (how the product is made, quality checks) because trust-sensitive customers want proof | Measure engagement and lead contribution |
| Social media | Reach, community, service | Post consistently (at least about three times a week on the primary platform); answer comments and messages within a day; join relevant groups; never buy followers | Track leads, not likes |
| Email | Formal and business nurture | Opt-in list, segmentation, personalised subject lines, two to four sends a month as a starting point, clear call to action | Targets to verify: open about 20 percent, click about 2 percent |
| Influencer | Borrowed trust | Tiers by follower count (nano, micro, macro, mega); micro and nano usually give better engagement per unit cost for local businesses; unique code or link per influencer; pay on results where possible | Obtain current rate cards |
| Video | Demonstration and trust | Demonstrations, behind-the-scenes, testimonials, how-to, site visits (farm, factory) | A smartphone, good light and a free editor are enough |

### Platform priority by audience
Rank each platform by audience reach, best use and primary metric: Facebook (broad B2C and B2B), WhatsApp Business (direct sales, service, nurture), YouTube (demonstrations, how-to), TikTok (younger audiences, awareness), Instagram (visual products), LinkedIn (professional services), X (real-time events; low priority for most small businesses). Verify the current audience data for the target market before ranking.

Email versus messaging: email for formal, B2B and long-form communication; messaging apps for informal consumer contact. Do not conflate them.

## 3. Advanced tactics

- Algorithmic advertising: ad platforms now optimise delivery automatically. Use broad targeting with strong creative and let the system find converting audiences; manual interest targeting matters less.
- Mobile first: test every page and ad on a phone; keep load time short (a common target is under three seconds; data costs make this important); use vertical creative; avoid small text; WhatsApp Business functions as a mobile-native CRM; offer mobile-money payment links to reduce checkout friction. Most social access is on mobile in the region; verify the current share.
- AI tools: drafting (posts, emails, descriptions), image generation, chatbots, send-time optimisation, analytics insights. Edit for brand voice and accuracy (see ai-marketing-playbook.md).

## 4. Measurement

### Web analytics
Install analytics on every page before launch. Review acquisition (where traffic comes from), engagement (pages and events), conversion (enquiry form, messaging click, purchase) and retention (return visits).

### Platform analytics
Use each platform's native tools for reach, engagement, leads, watch time and follower growth.

Principle: optimise for business metrics (leads, enquiries, sales), not vanity metrics (likes, followers).

### Optimisation loop
1. Run the campaign or publish.
2. Measure reach, engagement, leads, sales.
3. Identify the best performers.
4. Move budget and time to them.
5. Revise or stop the rest.
6. Repeat monthly.

### Budget split rule for constrained businesses
Roughly 70 percent on proven channels, 20 percent on tested channels being scaled, 10 percent on experiments. Never stake everything on one untested channel.

## 5. Budget framework by monthly spend band

Bands are relative; convert to the plan currency and re-check against current prices.

| Budget band | Allocation guide |
|---|---|
| Minimal | All owned channels: messaging business account, organic social, business profile, content creation |
| Low | About 60 percent owned, 30 percent paid social, 10 percent tools and design |
| Moderate | About 40 percent owned, 40 percent paid (social and search), 15 percent content and influencers, 5 percent analytics tools |
| High | Full channel mix, search investment, a dedicated content creator, regular professional photography or video |

## 6. Plan-section prompts

- Which two or three channels are funded first, and what evidence supports the choice?
- What is the SMART objective per channel?
- What is the expected cost per lead and per customer, and how will it be verified?
- Who produces content, and at what weekly time cost?
- What is the review cadence and the rule for cutting a channel?

Sources consulted: Robinson, D., The Digital Marketing Playbook for 2023 (2023); general practitioner practice. Prices, audience shares and benchmarks are planning assumptions requiring dated verification.
