# Strategic audit: diagnostic modules for East African SMEs, NGOs and growth firms

Parent: [Strategic Audit](../SKILL.md)

When to read: when running the audit workflow in the parent skill and choosing which questions to ask, how to grade findings and how to lay out the report.

This is the engine's own audit design. It organises the diagnosis into seven modules that follow the order in which evidence normally becomes available, then gives a function checklist, a severity scale and report templates. The general idea of auditing an organisation from performance through governance, environment, strategic factors, alternatives, implementation and control follows the strategic audit tradition in Wheelen, T. L., Hunger, J. D., Hoffman, A. N. and Bamford, C. E. (2018) *Concepts in Strategic Management and Business Policy*, 15th edn, Pearson. The module questions, owner-family, NGO, mobile-money and EAC prompts, scale and templates here are this engine's. Select the questions that bear on the mandate and add others where needed.

## 1. Seven modules

Mapping to the parent skill's step numbering: its Parts I, II, III, IV, V and VI correspond to modules M1 to M6, and its Parts VII and VIII (implementation, evaluation and control) are combined in M7.

| Module | Purpose | Questions to ask (select) |
|---|---|---|
| M1 Results and stated direction | Establish where the organisation stands and what it says it is doing | Three-year trend in revenue, gross margin, operating profit, cash and share or reach; for NGOs funds mobilised, cost per beneficiary and outcomes. Are mission, objectives, strategies and policies written, or only implied by budgets and behaviour? Do they agree with each other, with the environment and with every country served? |
| M2 Governance and leadership | Test who decides and how well | Board, trustees or owners: composition, independence, skills, meeting frequency and quality. Owner-managed firms: who holds decision rights, how family members are employed and paid, whether succession is planned. Management: skills, delegation, ownership, fit for the next stage; is there a qualified finance lead or adviser? NGOs: board-management separation, donor-restriction oversight, conflict-of-interest register |
| M3 Outside world | Identify opportunities and threats | Natural: climate, water, power reliability. Societal, technology and legal by country: verify, never assert, tax changes in annual Finance Acts, mobile-money rules and fees, data-protection registration, EAC and AfCFTA trade rules, exchange-rate exposure, public-sector payment delays. Industry: five forces plus complementors, the strategic customer, key actors. Summarise as an external factor table |
| M4 Inside the organisation | Identify strengths and weaknesses | Structure: fit with strategy; is decision-making concentrated in one person? Culture: beliefs, stories, control systems, a one-line name for the prevailing mindset ("we are traders, not marketers"). Resources by function (below). Summarise as an internal factor table and note which strengths pass a resource-advantage test and which activities could be outsourced |
| M5 Decision-critical factors | Concentrate on what matters | Carry forward fewer than ten weighted, rated factors with durations. Are the mission and objectives still right? Rule: a factor is not named here for the first time; it must already appear in M3 or M4 |
| M6 Options and recommendation | Show what else could be done | Could better execution of the current strategy meet the objectives? Name two or three feasible alternatives (growth, stability, retrenchment; cost or differentiation; partnering) with the evidence that makes each feasible. Leave the choice to `meta-strategic-options-evaluation` unless the mandate asks for one |
| M7 Delivery and control | Test whether anything will happen | Programmes, owners and dates; costed budget with priorities (the budget is the last feasibility test); changed procedures and removal of redundant ones; timely unit-level reporting with leading measures; standards, tolerances and rewards aligned to strategy; could anyone hit a KPI without achieving the goal behind it? |

Factor tables: `../../meta-strategic-factor-analysis/SKILL.md` (Wheelen–Hunger 1–5 scale, weights summing to 1.00). Working order: M1 → M2 → M3 and M4 → M5 → M6 → M7.

## 2. Function checklist (ask of every function)

1. What are the function's objectives, strategies, policies and programmes, explicit or implied by performance and budget?
2. Do they fit the mission and the environment?
3. How is it performing, and what are the trends?
4. Does its analysis support the decisions now pending?
5. Does it give competitive advantage?
6. How does it compare with similar organisations?
7. Does it use accepted methods?
8. Does it adapt to each country or region served?
9. Does it consider environmental and social sustainability?
10. What part does its manager play in strategy?

Functions to cover: marketing and sales, finance, operations and logistics, people, technology and data, and fundraising (NGOs). Marketing prompts: position and mix by segment; dependence on a few customers; where share is gained or lost; life-cycle stage of main products; use of segmentation and research.

## 3. Severity scale

| Severity | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Threatens solvency, licence, funding or safety within twelve months, or blocks the decision the audit serves | Fee collection below debt-service need; an unlicensed regulated activity |
| High | Materially weakens strategy or results within one to three years | Stated and realised strategy diverge; all decisions rest with one person |
| Medium | Reduces efficiency or growth; fixable within the normal plan cycle | KPIs report only lagging results |
| Low | Good-practice improvement | Mission statement too broad |

Grade by consequence and likelihood, not by how easy the fix is.

## 5. Report outline

1. Summary: three to five findings by severity and the decision they affect.
2. Mandate, scope, decision date, evidence base and limitations.
3. Modules M1 to M7, each opening with its conclusion.
4. Decision-critical factors and the alternatives worth screening.
5. Findings register and not-assessed register.
6. Numbered exhibits (factor tables, ratios, worksheets).

**Method sentence for proposals:** "The diagnostic works through seven modules, from results and stated direction, governance, the outside world and the organisation's own resources, to decision-critical factors, options, and delivery and control. Every conclusion traces to evidence and a graded factor."

**Illustrative example.** A Masaka dairy cooperative audit finds (M2) all cash decisions held by the chair, severity High; (M4) a cold-chain asset that gives an advantage over neighbouring cooperatives; (M7) monthly reports showing litres collected but not payment delays, severity Medium; the summary links these to the board's pending decision on a processing plant loan.
