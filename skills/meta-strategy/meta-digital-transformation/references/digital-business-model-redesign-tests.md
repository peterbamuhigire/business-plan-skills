# Digital Business-Model Redesign Tests

Digitisation is not strategy until it changes how demand is understood and served, how value is created, and how costs behave. This reference paraphrases three sources: Rogers, D. L. (2016) *The Digital Transformation Playbook*, Columbia University Press (five strategy domains); Molenaar, C. *Demand-Driven Business Strategy: Digital Transformation and Business Model Innovation* (supply-driven to demand-driven redesign; confirm year and publisher before citing); and *Business Models for E-Commerce* (transaction and revenue models; confirm author details before citing).

## Test 1 — Five-domain strategy check (Rogers)

| Domain | Question | Evidence the plan must show |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Does the plan treat customers as connected networks that influence each other? | Advocacy, referral, review, and participation mechanisms; digital touchpoints along the path to purchase; customer data treated as an asset |
| Competition | Could a platform, partner, or outsider change the basis of competition? | Direct against partner-enabled model; pipeline against platform potential; whether to own, join, or use an ecosystem; risk of disintermediation or re-intermediation; co-operation with competitors where useful |
| Data | What data is captured, why, and how does it improve decisions or experience? | Data captured, its use (insight, personalisation, prediction, new value), integration across silos, security and trust boundaries |
| Innovation | Are digital bets staged as experiments? | Minimum viable prototypes, assumption tests, iteration and scale-up gates |
| Value | How does digitisation change the value proposition, not only internal efficiency? | Current proposition, emerging threats, digital options to extend or reshape it, how the business adapts before it is forced to |

Keep data strategy proportionate: many SMEs need basic CRM, inventory, and service-data discipline before advanced analytics.

## Test 2 — Demand-driven redesign (Molenaar)

1. **Supply-driven or demand-driven?** Supply-driven firms push what they have; demand-driven firms organise around what customers want, when, and how they signal it. Reject "we will build a website", "we will use an app", or "we will advertise online" unless demand sensing and service change with it.
2. **Demand sensing:** state how browsing, behavioural, social, and transaction data will detect needs and support prediction.
3. **Outside-in design:** start from the customer signal and map backward into fulfilment, supply, and communication.
4. **Role choice:** remain a pipeline, or become a co-ordinator, marketplace participant, subscription service, or service layer.
5. **Ownership to access:** test subscription, leasing, service wrap-arounds, and product-as-a-service for resilience and cash visibility (relevant to equipment, mobility, solar, irrigation, logistics, software, and B2B services).
6. **Network position:** consider network effects, ecosystem position, direct knowledge of customer behaviour, and selling through channels that hold better data rather than treating channels as endpoints.

## Test 3 — E-commerce model specification (Business Models for E-Commerce)

State the specific digital transaction model; "sell online" is not a model. Answer:

- What value proposition is delivered online, to which segment?
- Which revenue model applies: direct online sales, marketplace commission, subscription or service contract, transaction fee, B2B ordering or procurement, B2B2C?
- How does the cost structure change as the model digitises?
- What resources and systems are required?
- What monetary and non-monetary value is created?

Consider three families:

- **Demand-side:** storefronts, online sales, customer-facing channels.
- **Supply-side:** e-procurement, sourcing, auctions, supplier integration.
- **Collaborative:** shared planning and replenishment, product lifecycle management, supplier and customer information exchange, workflow and knowledge-sharing tools.

Require both directions of value: revenue enhancement (conversion, reach, retention) and cost reduction (transaction, inventory, administrative cost, cycle time). Supply-side digitisation can pay back quickly where procurement leakage, stock-outs, and manual reconciliation hurt margins.

## East African application checks

Test, do not assume: whether mobile money, messaging-app ordering, lightweight storefronts, and agent-assisted commerce outperform a full e-commerce stack for the target segment; whether marketplace and commission models account for trust, fulfilment reliability, and payment friction; whether platform participation (aggregators, mobile-money ecosystems, marketplaces) beats a stand-alone build; and whether experiments are sized for constrained capital and noisy market information.

## Pass criteria

- [ ] Claims tested across all five domains.
- [ ] Demand sensing and outside-in flow mapped.
- [ ] Specific transaction and revenue model named.
- [ ] Revenue and cost effects both quantified.
- [ ] Supply-side and collaborative options considered where supplier complexity is high.
- [ ] Digital initiatives staged as pilots with decision gates.
- [ ] Recurring, access, or platform alternatives compared with one-off sales where relevant.
