# Marketing Plan Handbook operating loop

## Source and evidence boundary

This is an independently authored operational synthesis of Marian Burk Wood,
*The Marketing Plan Handbook* (2003). It preserves durable planning concepts
but does not reproduce the book. The book's examples, software references,
market data, platform practices, legal context, and terminology are historical.
They are not current authorities. Current market, policy, regulatory, platform,
technology, lifecycle, and financial claims must pass the
`digital-research-skills` currentness gate and be recorded as `NOT_ASSESSED`
when the required evidence is unavailable.

## Use the plan as an adaptive customer-first marketing plan operating system

Treat a marketing plan as a short-horizon operating instrument connected to the
business plan, not as a static annual document. Start with the customer and
market, connect choices to the business model and capacity, document the
decision, execute a bounded program, measure results, diagnose variance, and
adjust. Write the executive summary last so it reflects the completed logic.

The plan should make these relationships inspectable:

`situation evidence -> market choice -> positioning -> objectives -> programs -> budget and capacity -> measures -> corrective action`

Every program must support an objective, every objective must support a stated
direction, and the financial plan must reconcile with the operating plan.

## Current-situation evidence pack

Build the situation from three views, keeping facts, interpretations, and
assumptions separate:

- **Internal:** mission, resources, capabilities, offerings, prior results,
  customer relationships, partners, suppliers, employees, constraints, and
  warning signs.
- **Micro environment:** customers, competitors, channel members, suppliers,
  partners, and other stakeholders. Examine alternatives, switching, power,
  service expectations, and observed behaviour.
- **Macro environment:** demographic, economic, ecological, technological,
  political-legal, and social-cultural conditions. Route every time-sensitive
  or jurisdictional claim through Digital Research.

Use SWOT only as a compact decision summary of sourced evidence. Test the
important competitor claims, identify the source and date for each material
input, and record what would change the conclusion. Desk research can frame a
question; customer conversations, observed behaviour, transaction records, or
controlled tests are needed for claims about demand and willingness to act.

## Market ladder and customer understanding

Name the market denominator before sizing or forecasting it:

1. **Potential market:** everyone who might have the need.
2. **Available market:** those with access and ability to buy.
3. **Qualified available market:** those meeting the relevant eligibility or
   fit conditions.
4. **Target or served market:** the portion intentionally pursued and reachable.
5. **Penetrated market:** the portion already buying or using the offer.

Describe needs at the stated, unstated, and latent levels; alternatives and
the cost of inaction; buying roles; triggers; constraints; perceived benefits;
and perceived total cost. For business buyers, distinguish initiators, users,
influencers, approvers, purchasing roles, and access or control roles. Never
turn market potential into demand, or interest into a purchase forecast.

## Segment evaluation, target, and position with a decision matrix

Segment on meaningful differences in need, behaviour, context, or buying
process. Use demographic or geographic variables only when they explain a
decision. Evaluate candidate segments with a weighted matrix covering:

- opportunity and economic attractiveness;
- competitive superiority and evidence of a right to win;
- fit with mission, capabilities, capacity, and resources; and
- external threats, access difficulty, and downside risk.

Record weights, scores, evidence, confidence, and sensitivity to disputed
assumptions. Choose concentrated, undifferentiated, or differentiated
coverage deliberately; sequence a rollout when capacity cannot support all
segments. Positioning must state the target, problem or desired outcome,
distinctive value, proof, and meaningful alternative. It should drive product,
price, place, promotion, service, and measurement choices, and be reviewed
when customer priorities or competitive conditions change.

## Strategy pyramid and program design

Keep the hierarchy visible:

`direction and goals -> marketing objectives -> strategy -> tactics -> programs -> tasks and owners`

Separate marketing objectives from financial objectives. Marketing objectives
may cover acquisition, retention, satisfaction, distribution, units, share,
development, or fulfilment. Financial objectives may cover sales, margin,
profitability, return, cash contribution, or break-even. Each objective needs a
baseline, target, time window, owner, evidence source, and decision threshold.

Design programs across the full promise: product or service, price, place or
channel, promotion, customer service, and internal enablement. Connect price
to perceived value, cost floors, break-even, competition, capacity, and
positioning. Connect channel choices to service level, inventory, data,
returns, payment, control, and relationship risk. Pretest material messages
and measure behaviour, not exposure alone. Use ethical, substantiated claims.

## Forecast, budget, schedule, and metric contract

Forecast by relevant market, segment, product, channel, and cost driver. Make
best, worst, and most-likely scenarios explicit; identify the assumptions that
drive the range and revise the forecast as evidence changes. Combine suitable
statistical, causal, time-series, expert, or sales-input methods while
recording bias and uncertainty.

Prefer objective-and-task budgeting when a defensible response model is not
available. For every program, show the task, timing, owner, required people,
vendor or channel dependency, budget, expected outcome, and capacity impact.
Reconcile program budgets to the financial model and working-capital needs.

Build a metric contract backwards from the objective and customer journey.
Use leading and lagging measures. Where possible, segment acquisition,
retention, satisfaction, conversion, fulfilment, distribution, unit sales,
share, margin, and cash contribution by product, channel, segment, customer,
price, or promotion. A traffic or reach number alone is not evidence of value.

## Control loop and marketing audit

Run the operating loop:

`objective -> standard -> measure -> compare -> diagnose variance -> corrective action -> remeasure`

Use four complementary control views:

- **Plan control:** progress against sales, share, cost, and program targets.
- **Profitability control:** contribution, margin, return, and cash by relevant
  product, segment, channel, or customer view.
- **Productivity control:** effectiveness of sales, promotion, channels,
  logistics, and product-management effort.
- **Strategic control:** periodic marketing audit, customer-relationship review,
  social responsibility, and response to unexpected events.

Track acquisition, retention, defection, satisfaction, complaints, win/loss
patterns, and changing customer perceptions. A marketing audit should inspect
mission, objectives, strategy, information quality, offering, pricing,
channels, communications, sales, stakeholder relationships, results, and
contingencies. Define escalation thresholds and pre-agreed responses for
material variance. Preserve mixed or negative evidence; it is a learning
signal, not a reason to rewrite the baseline.

## Adoption checklist

- [ ] Current situation separates internal, micro, and macro evidence.
- [ ] Material claims have source, date, scope, confidence, and owner.
- [ ] Market ladder denominator is named before sizing or forecasting.
- [ ] Target segment scores show criteria, weights, evidence, and sensitivity.
- [ ] Positioning names the audience, outcome, difference, proof, and alternative.
- [ ] Objectives have baselines, targets, dates, owners, and decision rules.
- [ ] Programs connect product, price, place, promotion, service, and enablement.
- [ ] Forecasts include scenarios and an invalidation or revision trigger.
- [ ] Budget, schedule, capacity, and financial model reconcile.
- [ ] Metrics include leading and lagging measures plus guardrails.
- [ ] Corrective action, contingency, audit, and re-audit ownership are explicit.
- [ ] Currentness review is complete, with unresolved items marked `NOT_ASSESSED`.
