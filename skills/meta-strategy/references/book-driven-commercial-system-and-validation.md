# Book-driven commercial system and validation

This reference is an independent synthesis of durable principles from the supplied business-system, market-validation, franchise, product-marketing, finance, supply-chain, and strategy books.

## Whole-system commercial view

Evaluate a business as a connected system: customer value, rivalry, offering, revenue, bargaining power, costs, customer base, imitability, and adaptability. Do not optimise one box while creating a hidden constraint elsewhere. State the trade-off, affected measure, time horizon, and sensitivity.

## Market and offer validation

Define the best-fit customer by observable characteristics and trigger, problem, alternative, buying process, willingness to pay, and success condition. Compare best, worst, active, inactive, won, and lost customers. Use interviews and behavioural evidence to test a hypothesis; label anecdotes, samples, and uncertainty. Treat adjacent markets as separate hypotheses until evidence supports transfer.

## Repeatable operating system

Document the repeatable promise, critical standards, roles, handoffs, training, support, quality checks, supplier dependencies, and local adaptations. Preserve a non-negotiable core while allowing a bounded edge for local learning. Replication is a controlled operating model, not a copy of surface-level branding.

## GTM and narrative

Keep one source of truth for audience, positioning, not-targeted segments, stages, readiness, ownership, enablement, risks, feedback, and measures. Use a human customer problem and proof of change to make the value legible; do not substitute storytelling for evidence.

## Finance and network discipline

Connect the commercial case to cash, working capital, capacity, inventory, service levels, and capital requirements. Use scenarios and sensitivity for demand, price, margin, lead time, capacity, and working-capital assumptions. Record what would invalidate the model.

## Currentness gate

Current market, regulatory, accounting, technology, platform, and competitor claims require Digital Research verification and appropriate cross-engine review. Books are concept inputs, not authorities for current rates, laws, standards, or product capabilities.
