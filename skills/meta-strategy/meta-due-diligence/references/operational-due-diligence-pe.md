# Operational Due Diligence Readiness for Private Equity and DFI Capital

Task reference for making the operations, management, financial-projection and valuation sections of a business plan withstand operational due diligence (ODD) by private equity, development finance institution (DFI) or impact investors. Use it to (a) test the business before approaching investors, (b) decide which evidence to place in the plan and appendices, and (c) plan post-investment reporting.

## 1. What ODD tests

ODD assesses non-investment risk: whether the organisation can execute its strategy without losing money to fraud, system failure, weak controls or regulatory breach. Investment due diligence asks whether the business will earn returns. Complete diligence needs both; a return case without an operational review is incomplete. ODD is a continuing diagnostic, not a one-off event, and it involves judgement as well as checklists.

### Ten operational risk categories

| Category | Scope |
|---|---|
| Cash controls | Authorisation, segregation of duties, transfer protocols |
| Transaction life cycle | Execution, confirmation, settlement, reconciliation |
| Valuation | Method, consistency, independence |
| Transparency and reporting | Frequency, accuracy, format |
| Liquidity management | Working capital, cash forecasting, stress |
| Technology and systems | Software, hardware, data integrity, information security |
| Legal and compliance | Regulatory status, compliance infrastructure |
| Counterparty oversight | Bank and vendor relationships |
| Service-provider quality | Auditor, legal counsel, administrator, custodian |
| Continuity and recovery | Continuity and disaster-recovery plans, backups, succession |

## 2. Process and scope

1. Investor states what it needs to learn and why.
2. Programme design: scope (core or expanded), resources, framework.
3. Document collection (list below).
4. Site visit: management interviews, physical inspection.
5. Analysis: operational opinion, red flags, scoring.
6. Continuing monitoring: periodic and event-triggered re-review.

Core scope: firm overview and size, transaction flow, cash oversight and transfer controls, compliance infrastructure, valuation policy and process, legal and regulatory status, service-provider quality, reporting. Expanded scope adds: technology, information security, depth of human capital, continuity and recovery, operational connectivity, tax practice, insurance, counterparty management, and review of administration agreements.

### Document request list (assemble before the approach)

- [ ] Due diligence questionnaire response
- [ ] Audited financial statements, minimum three years
- [ ] Offering or investment memorandum
- [ ] Shareholders' or partnership agreement
- [ ] Compliance manual or policies
- [ ] Valuation policy and procedures
- [ ] Continuity and disaster-recovery plan
- [ ] Organisation chart
- [ ] Service-provider list (auditor, counsel, administrator, custodian, bank)
- [ ] Insurance certificates
- [ ] Employee confidentiality and non-compete agreements
- [ ] Performance track record
- [ ] Background-check consent forms
- [ ] All side letters with other investors
- [ ] Systems inventory
- [ ] Litigation, past and pending

Signalling: delay, redaction or deflection when documents are requested is itself evidence. Investors will not rely on management's word alone; supply documents, not assertions.

## 3. Red-flag screen

Run this against the business before investors do.

**Valuation:** resistance to periodic valuation or transparency; no written valuation policy; the same person makes investment decisions and sets values; method inconsistent with IPEV guidelines or IFRS/GAAP; restated historical performance; illiquid unobservable-input assets with no third-party check.

**Cash:** sole signatory on transfers; management fees drawn at will or in advance without board approval; transfers to related-party or owner accounts; no audit trail from cash movement to authorised decision; commingling of business and personal or manager funds.

**Documentation and compliance:** no compliance manual or officer, even part-time; no written continuity plan; past regulatory sanctions, even if resolved; no record of how investment decisions were made; legal documents drafted without counsel; unusually broad indemnity or exculpation clauses.

**Governance and people:** unmitigated key-person risk; high turnover in finance or operations leadership; departed executives unreachable as references; principals' outside interests competing with the business; no independent board oversight; background checks revealing litigation, sanctions or misstated credentials.

**Reporting:** audited statements missing, late or inconsistent between years; an unqualified audit opinion accepted without reading the notes (an unqualified opinion can coexist with matters needing further work); unaudited performance figures circulated; NAV or equivalent not independently calculated; investor reporting less than quarterly.

## 4. Valuation that survives scrutiny

Two questions are asked: how the business measures its own performance, and how it values individual holdings or the enterprise.

### Standards to name

- IPEV Guidelines (International Private Equity and Venture Capital Valuation Guidelines): fair value is the price at which an orderly transaction would occur between market participants at the reporting date.
- GIPS (CFA Institute) for IRR and performance presentation. Annualised IRR solves for r in: sum of CFi / (1+r)^(ti/TD) + VE / (1+r) = VB, where CFi are interim cash flows, VE the ending value and VB the beginning value.
- IFRS 13 / ASC 820 fair value: an exit price, not entry cost. Three-level hierarchy: Level 1 observable market prices; Level 2 observable inputs for similar assets; Level 3 unobservable inputs (most private holdings), which require expanded disclosure of method and assumptions.

### Method selection

| Method | Use |
|---|---|
| Price of recent investment | Seed and early stage; reliability decays with time |
| Earnings or revenue multiples (EV/EBITDA, P/E, EV/EBIT) | Established businesses with identifiable earnings |
| Net assets | Property-holding vehicles, funds of funds |
| DCF of the business | Established and early-stage companies |
| DCF of the investment | Near realisation or flotation |
| Industry benchmarks | Cross-check only |

Adjust enterprise value for surplus assets and excess liabilities, deduct instruments ranking ahead in liquidation, and allocate across the capital structure. For early-stage businesses, re-examine value at milestones: financial (revenue growth, profitability path, burn rate, covenant compliance), technical (development phases, patents, test cycles), and market (customer research, launch, share changes).

### Valuation process checklist

- [ ] Written valuation policy and procedures
- [ ] Valuation committee with terms of reference
- [ ] Investment decision-maker separate from valuer
- [ ] At least quarterly valuation
- [ ] Consistent method across periods; changes documented and justified
- [ ] Independent valuer for Level 3 assets, at least periodically (check independence, qualifications, IPEV/IFRS 13 consistency)
- [ ] Documented output: method, inputs, comparables, conclusion
- [ ] Board or committee sign-off before investor reporting

### Valuation appendix checklist

- [ ] State the method
- [ ] Give source and date of each comparable multiple
- [ ] Show the earnings or revenue base multiplied
- [ ] Apply and document illiquidity and minority discounts where relevant
- [ ] Show sensitivity to the key variable at plus and minus 20 per cent
- [ ] Record independent professional review
- [ ] State consistency with prior periods
- [ ] Disclose factors that may overstate value (current losses, pending litigation)

### Manipulations investors look for

Holding "recent investment price" long after fundamentals have changed; multiples from different markets, geographies or dates without adjustment; speculative synergy or control premia; terminal growth above industry norms; failing to deduct debt or working-capital deficits when moving from enterprise to equity value; carrying assets at cost above current market value. Principle: document every assumption and show the comparables. Cross-refer to the valuation skill (`meta-finance/meta-valuation`) and the Chwezi finance engine for accounting treatment.

## 5. Liquidity and cash controls

Liquidity risk has three layers: entity liquidity (cash for obligations and capital calls), operating-company working capital, and stress liquidity (surviving a severe revenue fall). Demonstrate:

- [ ] Payment authorisers separate from payment executors
- [ ] Dual authorisation above a stated threshold
- [ ] Monthly bank reconciliation, independently reviewed
- [ ] Cash reserve policy (a planning assumption is three to six months of operating runway; justify for the sector)
- [ ] Capital-call process (fund structures)
- [ ] No commingling of business and personal or related-party funds
- [ ] Every cash movement traceable to an authorised decision
- [ ] Liquidity stress case beyond the base case

Failure pattern to avoid: fees withdrawn at will, double-billed expenses and investor money treated as the manager's own cash, enabled by absent dual authorisation and independent oversight.

## 6. Financial statements, audit and internal control

- Statements must follow IFRS (or GAAP); non-standard formats are not acceptable to institutional investors.
- Audit standard: International Standards on Auditing (ISA). Service-organisation assurance standards vary by jurisdiction; ISAE 3402 is the international reference.
- Components reviewed: balance sheet (composition, leverage, off-balance-sheet items); income statement (revenue recognition, expense classification, management fees); cash flow statement; changes in equity or net assets; schedule of investments with method per holding; financial highlights; notes (accounting policies, related-party transactions, contingent liabilities, litigation, subsequent events).
- Opinion types: unqualified (still read the notes and supporting schedules; materiality thresholds mean small items may not be flagged); qualified (explain the exception); adverse or disclaimer (generally disqualifying).
- Internal control (COSO as reference framework):
  - [ ] Documented authorisation matrix by value
  - [ ] Recording, authorising and reconciling done by different people
  - [ ] Annual controls review
  - [ ] Written conflict-of-interest policy
  - [ ] Compliance manual (conduct, personal dealing, inside information)
  - [ ] Records-retention policy aligned to law

Verify tax and accounting statements with the Chwezi finance engine.

## 7. Governance and compliance

- [ ] Genuinely independent board members, with minutes (decisions, quorum, votes)
- [ ] Key-person clause naming individuals, triggers (death, incapacity, departure) and consequences, plus a succession plan
- [ ] Compliance function: dedicated officer, or an outsourced adviser for small firms
- [ ] Conflict-of-interest policy
- [ ] Defined management-to-board information flow and frequency
- [ ] Compliance manual updated annually; personal-dealing policy; restricted list; pre- and post-trade review (where securities are traded); communications-monitoring policy; annual training
- [ ] Regulatory registrations current; sanctions absent or fully explained

Legal document set: formation documents, investment memorandum, subscription documents, all side letters disclosed, service-provider contracts (audit engagement, legal retainer, administrator), insurance certificates.

## 8. Operational infrastructure

**Technology:** state the accounting or ERP system, who customises it and who supports it; backup frequency and restore testing; information security (firewalls, access control, password policy, penetration testing); tested recovery procedure.

**Continuity and recovery:**
- [ ] Written plan, with last test date
- [ ] Technology and personnel both tested
- [ ] Alternative work location
- [ ] Data backup facility
- [ ] Backup power
- [ ] Cross-site coordination if several locations

**Service providers** (evaluate quality, not only existence):
- Auditor: independence, reputation, experience in the sector, tenure.
- Legal counsel: jurisdictional expertise, independence from management.
- Administrator: independent calculation of value, independent reconciliation, data-feed process.
- Custodian: independent holding of assets, direct balance confirmation, service-level agreement.

**Insurance:** directors and officers, professional indemnity, key-person life and disability, fidelity or crime. Be ready to explain how limits were set, who the carriers are and whether claims were made.

## 9. Compensation and alignment

Explain how principals and key staff are paid and whether pay links to performance; whether pay is competitive enough to retain talent; and whether it vests over time. State principals' own investment as a figure or percentage; vague wording is treated as no disclosure. Vesting types: time, performance (for example earnings targets) and cliff.

## 10. Evidence to place in the plan

**Operations plan:** process maps of core workflows; systems inventory (accounting software, banking platform, reporting tools); cash-management policy (authorisation levels, dual-signatory threshold, reconciliation frequency); continuity summary (loss of a key system, facility or person); service-provider list; quality-control process and its documentation.

**Management team:** full CVs with references; key-person risk statement and succession; board composition with independent directors named; compliance officer or function; compensation structure including performance-linked elements; conflict-of-interest disclosure; licences and registrations.

**Financial projections:** audited statements (or management accounts with an audit timeline); written assumptions; valuation method and comparable evidence; working-capital adequacy; stress scenario (test a 20 to 30 per cent revenue shortfall as a planning assumption); schedule of related-party transactions; description of who approves spend and who reconciles accounts.

## 11. Minimum documentation standards

| Document | Must contain | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Audited statements | IFRS/GAAP, opinion, notes, schedule of investments, related-party disclosures | None; overdue; non-standard |
| Valuation policy | Method hierarchy, approval, revaluation frequency, dispute resolution | No policy; investor also values |
| Compliance manual | Conduct, personal dealing, inside information, complaints | No manual or officer |
| Continuity plan | Triggers, roles, alternative site, backup, last test | None; never tested |
| Shareholder or partnership agreement | Economics, key-person clause, voting, exit, anti-dilution | Draft only; no independent legal review |
| Cash authorisation policy | Dual-signatory thresholds, approval chain, documentation | Single signatory; no audit trail |
| Organisation chart | Reporting lines, control of functions | Absent; overlapping roles |
| Service-provider agreements | Scope, fees, service levels, termination | Verbal only |
| Board minutes | Decisions, quorum, meeting frequency | No meetings; founder decides everything |
| Insurance certificates | D&O, E&O, key person, crime; rated carriers | None; inadequate |
| Background checks | Employment, qualification and litigation verification for principals | Not done; refused |
| Decision file | Record of how investment decisions were made | None |

## 12. Uganda and East Africa adaptation

Most SMEs operate with thin compliance infrastructure, few large audit firms outside Kampala, informal or mobile-money-based records, limited accounting software and founder-controlled boards. Standards are not lowered; show equivalent intent and process by proportionate means.

- **Audit:** an auditor who is a member of ICPAU; IFRS is the required framework. If a full audit is unaffordable, a review engagement by a qualified accountant is the minimum, supplemented by mobile-money statements, bank reconciliations and EFRIS records. Re-verify current requirements with the Chwezi finance engine.
- **Valuation:** apply multiples from East African comparables (regional deal databases, central bank and sector research) and document each source. Illiquidity discount for unlisted equity is a planning assumption (a range of roughly 15 to 30 per cent is often used); re-verify with a dated source.
- **Cash controls:** dual authorisation is achievable on business mobile-money and bank accounts; show it.
- **Compliance:** a two-to-three-page ethics and compliance policy (conduct, conflicts, cash handling) is far better than none; DFIs commonly require it.
- **Governance:** one independent non-executive director (retired banker, sector expert, respected professional) materially improves the risk profile; document role and attendance.
- **Key person:** the management talent pool is shallow, so investors press on this. Name a deputy or succession path and buy key-person insurance from a rated carrier.
- **Continuity:** a one-to-two-page plan covering owner incapacity, loss of premises and data loss; cloud accounting gives basic recovery.
- **Service providers:** use members of recognised bodies (ICPAU, Uganda Law Society, URSB-registered secretaries).

Three commonest deal-killers: (1) no current audited IFRS statements for two to three years; (2) the founder as sole cash signatory with no reconciliation; (3) no written governance documents (shareholder agreement, minutes, valuation policy, compliance manual).

## 13. Post-investment monitoring

Plan to provide: quarterly financial reports against budget; annual audited statements within 90 to 120 days of year end; board minutes within 30 days; immediate notice of litigation, regulatory action, key-person departure or material adverse change; annual operational review; covenant compliance certificates (debt service cover) where debt exists; consistent periodic valuation updates. Operational risk is managed continuously across each category, not only at the point of investment.

## 14. "Investor-ready operations" checklist

**Governance:** [ ] independent director attending documented meetings [ ] professionally drafted shareholders' agreement [ ] key-person clause and succession [ ] written conflict policy.
**Financial control:** [ ] IFRS audited statements, two to three years [ ] ICPAU-member auditor [ ] dual-signatory cash authorisation [ ] monthly reconciliations reviewed by someone other than the preparer [ ] no commingling.
**Valuation:** [ ] written method [ ] enterprise value from observable comparables [ ] illiquidity and minority discounts where relevant [ ] independent review [ ] sensitivity analysis.
**Compliance and legal:** [ ] written ethics policy [ ] registrations current (tax, company registry, sector licences) [ ] no undisclosed litigation or sanctions [ ] service agreements in writing.
**Infrastructure:** [ ] accounting system in use [ ] tested backups [ ] written continuity plan communicated to key staff [ ] written job descriptions supporting segregation of duties.
**Reporting:** [ ] monthly management accounts within 15 days of month end [ ] budget-versus-actual variance analysis [ ] capital and operating expenditure tracked separately [ ] related-party transactions disclosed and at arm's length.
