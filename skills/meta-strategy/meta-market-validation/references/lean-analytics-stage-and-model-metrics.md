# Evidence ladder: what to measure at each stage and for each business model

Parent: [Meta Market Validation](../SKILL.md).

When to read: when choosing what to measure in a validation programme, a
marketing plan's KPI dashboard, a business plan's traction evidence or a
living-plan review; when interviewing customers to validate a problem; and
when judging whether a venture is ready to spend on growth.

This is the engine's evidence ladder. It rests on one idea: the right metric
depends on the business model and on how far the venture has proved itself.
That idea, and the practice of focusing on one key metric at a time, are
argued in Croll, A. and Yoskovitz, B. (2013) *Lean Analytics*, O'Reilly Media,
which uses its own five stage names. The engine's rungs, model table,
interview protocol and examples below are this engine's own; the book's
2008–2012 US benchmarks are not used as targets. Rebuild numbers from the
client's records, tests or model arithmetic.

## 1. The five rungs

| Rung | Question the evidence must answer | Typical key metric | Move up when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem proven | Do enough people feel this problem badly enough to act, and are they already spending effort on it? | Share of interviewees raising it unprompted; deposits or pre-orders | Consistent pain across at least 15 interviews per segment, with commitment signals |
| 2. Repeat use | Do the right customers come back without being chased? | Repeat purchase or active-use rate by cohort | Retention holds at the level the model needs |
| 3. Customers bring customers | Does word of mouth or referral lower acquisition cost? | Referrals per active customer | Referral measurably cuts effective CAC |
| 4. Revenue that pays | Does each customer return more than they cost, repeatedly? | Contribution per customer; CLV : CAC; months to recover CAC | Unit economics meet the plan |
| 5. Scale | Can channels and places grow without hand-holding? | Payback by channel and region; cost to serve | Two or more proven channels and a working operating rhythm |

Do not buy growth before rung 2: paid acquisition into a product customers do
not repeat wastes cash. The key metric changes as each constraint is solved.
The KPI dashboard rules (one key metric, guard-rails, good-metric test) sit in
the marketing orchestrator's
[KPI dashboard and control plan](../../../marketing-sales/marketing-plan-orchestrator/references/kpi-dashboard-and-control-plan.md).

## 2. Metrics by business model and rung

| Model | Rung 2 (repeat use) | Rung 3 (referral) | Rung 4 (revenue) | Rung 5 (scale) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Online or WhatsApp shop | Repeat purchase within 90 days; basket size | Share of orders from referrals | Contribution per order after delivery and payment fees; CAC by channel | Payback by region; stock availability |
| Subscription or SaaS | Active accounts by cohort; logo and revenue churn | Referrals per active account | CLV : CAC; months to recover CAC; upgrade rate | Payback by channel; support cost per account |
| Two-sided marketplace | Buyer repeat rate; seller listing activity | Buyers and sellers recruited by users | Take rate; transaction value; leakage off-platform | Share of searches with an available listing, by area |
| Media or content | Returning audience; engaged time on content pages | Shares per piece | Revenue per thousand views; sponsorship yield | Audience loss rate; syndication |
| Mobile app | Day-1, day-7 and day-30 return; weekly active share | Share of active users who share | Paying share; revenue per paying user | Cost per engaged user by source |
| Mobile-money agent network | Agents transacting weekly | Agent-recruited agents | Commission per agent; float cost | Agents per trading centre; float rebalancing cost |
| SACCO or savings product | Members depositing on N days a month | Member-referred members | Loan-book yield after arrears; cost per member | Branch or agent reach; arrears by branch |
| NGO programme | Beneficiary retention or completion | Community referrals | Cost per outcome | Cost per outcome at scale; partner delivery share |
| Restaurant or hospitality | Repeat visits; bookings by 5 p.m. as an early signal | Reviews and referrals | Staff cost ÷ previous day's takings; margin per cover | Second-site economics |
| Tour operator | Repeat and referred bookings | Guest referrals and reviews | Margin per booking after commissions | Direct versus agent channel mix |

## 3. Definitions to state in the plan

- **Churn:** customers lost ÷ customers at the start (stable base); ÷ average customers in the period (moderate growth); by cohort (fast growth). Separate free from paying customers.
- **Average life** ≈ 1 ÷ periodic churn; **CLV** = revenue or contribution per period × average life.
- **Months to recover CAC** = CAC ÷ monthly contribution per customer.
- **Referral rate** = invitations per customer × acceptance rate; the time a referral cycle takes matters as much.
- **Revenue per visitor** is often a better optimisation target than conversion rate alone.
- **Break-even tests:** each customer covers its acquisition and delivery; CAC is recovered within the stated months; the business breaks even at operating level; it could survive with no new marketing.

## 4. Four analyses the engine asks for

1. **Cohort view.** Tabulate revenue or orders per customer by calendar month, re-cut by joining month, then align by months since joining. The aligned view shows where value drops (for example after month one), which may change the key metric from "new customers" to "month-two reorder".
2. **Online shop mode.** Measure 90-day repurchase for recent first-time buyers and classify the shop as acquisition-led, mixed or loyalty-led, with local bands set from the client's cohorts. Loyalty schemes pay only in loyalty-led shops; acquisition-led shops invest in efficient new-buyer channels and referral.
3. **Early-signal search.** Split a cohort into stayers and leavers at day 60 or 90, compare first-week behaviour, shortlist two or three early behaviours, check correlation, then run an experiment that moves the behaviour and see whether retention follows.
4. **Change the market before the product.** When a product stalls, test another market or business model before rebuilding: review old assumptions, drop markets whose mechanics cannot work, run 10–15 problem interviews per candidate market, and define markets by real buying similarities (industry, place, buying process, budget cycle, regulation), not labels such as "SMEs".

## 5. Problem interviews (rung 1 protocol)

- **Sample:** at least 15 interviews per segment for the problem, and again for the solution. If 15 cannot be found, selling to them will be harder.
- **Avoid leading:** ask neutrally, even the opposite of what you hope; seek commitment (a deposit, pre-order or introduction) instead of "would you use this?"; ask "why?" several times and tolerate silence; use a second person to note body language.
- **Broad, then narrow:** first let people name their own problems and count unprompted mentions; later, with new people, ask them to rank specific problems.
- **Score each interview** on: ranked our problem highly; already trying to solve it; engaged; agreed to follow up; offered referrals; offered to pay. Compare high and low scorers to find the real segment.
- **Good-enough check:** count how many already have an acceptable workaround; displacing it needs a large improvement.
- **Consent:** recruitment through WhatsApp groups, associations, market intercepts or ads follows data-protection rules (see the orchestrator's location and scope calibration).

## 6. Surveys at scale

For each question write the decision it will change and the result that counts as good. Put segmentation questions first, numeric questions next and open questions last. Test comprehension with three target people who explain each question back. Recruit with two or three neutral ad angles (name the audience, name the problem, name the value) and compare cost per completed response. Ask permission to re-contact.

**Illustrative example.** A Kampala meal-prep start-up: rung 1 — 18 interviews with working parents in Kira, 12 raising weekday dinners unprompted and 7 paying a deposit; rung 2 — 40 pilot households, target at least three orders a week for eight weeks; rung 3 — "gift a week" referrals per active household; rung 4 — contribution per order after boda delivery and mobile-money fees against CAC by channel; rung 5 — a second kitchen in Entebbe.

## 7. Anti-patterns

- Importing old foreign benchmarks as targets. Fix: model arithmetic or local trend.
- "Would you use this?" questions. Fix: ask for commitment.
- Surveys before interviews. Fix: interview until you know what to ask.
- Buying traffic before repeat use is proven. Fix: pass rung 2 first.
- Treating correlation as cause. Fix: a controlled test on the early signal.
