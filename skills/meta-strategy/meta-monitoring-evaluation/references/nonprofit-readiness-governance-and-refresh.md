# Nonprofit Readiness, Governance, and Refresh Reference

Use for nonprofit, grant-funded, public-interest, and mission-led plans. This is a synthesis of *The Nonprofit Guide to Strategic Planning*, not a replacement for the organisation's constitution, donor agreement, law, or board policy.

## Readiness gate

| Check | Evidence | Decision owner | If missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mandate, scope, key questions | Approved planning mandate | Sponsor/board | Stop scope decision |
| Mission and stakeholder purpose | Mission, beneficiary voice, partner/funder input | Board and management | Mark mission fit unassessed |
| Governance and decision rights | Bylaws, board role, sponsor, project lead, escalation path | Board chair/CEO | Stop approval design |
| Baseline and data gaps | Programme, finance, people, risk, stakeholder, and external evidence index | Project lead/M&E lead | Create a data-recovery action |
| Capacity and workload | Available staff, budget, timeline, access, confidentiality controls | CEO/project lead | Re-scope or phase |
| Implementation connection | Annual plan, budget, owners, KPI dashboard, review calendar | Management | Do not release a strategy-only plan |

## Governance boundary

The board owns mission, strategic direction, risk appetite, stewardship, executive accountability, and formal approval. Management owns diagnosis, implementation, operating decisions, monitoring, and corrective action. Stakeholder participation supplies evidence and legitimacy; it does not remove accountable decision rights.

## M&E learning register

Capture: observation, source, interpretation, affected assumption, decision, owner, due date, indicator, threshold, and follow-up evidence. Review monthly for operational exceptions, quarterly for implementation and resource decisions, annually for strategy and budget alignment.

## Early refresh triggers

Review for an early refresh when there is a material change in funding or financial resilience, leadership or governance, regulation, beneficiary/community need, programme results, demand, staffing/capability, geographic scale, technology, partner confidence, or stakeholder trust. Separate immediate continuity and safeguarding actions from the longer strategic review.

## Acceptance

A plan is ready for approval only when it states the governance route, resource implications, implementation owner, M&E cadence, refresh triggers, stakeholder evidence, unresolved gaps, and the next formal reconsideration point.
