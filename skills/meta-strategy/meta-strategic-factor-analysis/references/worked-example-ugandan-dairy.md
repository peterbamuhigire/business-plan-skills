# Worked example: Mbarara dairy processor (illustrative)

Parent: [Strategic Factor Analysis](../SKILL.md)

**Status: illustrative.** The company is fictional. Every number, weight, rating and market statement below is invented to show the method. None of it is a market fact. Before any client use, replace each row with sourced evidence and run the Digital research engine on every external factor.

Scenario: a mid-sized processor in Mbarara buys raw milk from smallholders, makes yoghurt and UHT milk, and sells through two Kampala supermarket chains and upcountry distributors. The owner wants a marketing plan and a bank facility for a second chilling centre. Rating scale throughout: **Wheelen and Hunger 1-5 response scale; average firm = 3.0.**

## 1. Key drivers (from the PESTEL scan)

| ID | Driver | Direction | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | Rainfall variability and feed prices | Rising volatility | Raw-milk cost and volume swing by season; supply contracts matter more than plant size |
| D2 | Urban demand for packaged yoghurt | Growing (VERIFY with current retail data) | Shelf presence in Kampala is the growth route |
| D3 | Regional processors competing on UHT price | Intensifying | Price is the incumbents' weapon; avoid a UHT price war |
| D4 | EAC market access for dairy | Open in principle, certification needed (VERIFY current EAC and national rules) | Export is an option only after certification |

## 2. Five forces plus complementors (summary)

Industry: packaged dairy (yoghurt and UHT) sold through formal retail in Uganda. Rivalry High (larger regional processors, price-led UHT). Entry Medium (cold chain and listings are barriers). Substitutes Medium (informal raw milk). Buyer power High (two supermarket chains are the strategic customers). Supplier power Medium, rising in drought. Complementors: milk-collection cooperatives and mobile-money agents who settle farmer payments.

## 6. TOWS

| Option | Quadrant | Factors | Option | Four-criteria result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SO-1 | SO | F4 × F3 | Freshness-led yoghurt positioning in Kampala: 48-hour plant-to-shelf promise backed by the chilled network | Passes |
| ST-1 | ST | F4 × F1 | Forward supply agreements and silage support with four farmer cooperatives | Passes |
| WO-1 | WO | F6 × F3 | Move supermarket and distributor collections to scheduled mobile-money settlement to fund Kampala growth | Passes (supporting) |
| WO-2 | WO | F7 × F5 | Appoint an operations and export manager to lead certification and a Kigali distributor search | Passes only if funded |
| WT-1 | WT | F6 × F2 | Exit the lowest-margin UHT lines where regional processors are strongest; do not match their prices | Passes |

## 7. Alternatives passed to options evaluation

- **Alternative A (focused growth):** SO-1 + ST-1 + WO-1. Grow yoghurt in Kampala on freshness; secure supply.
- **Alternative B (regional expansion):** WO-2 + SO-1. Certify and enter Rwanda through a distributor within two years.
- **Alternative C (retrench and consolidate):** WT-1 + ST-1 + WO-1. Shrink UHT, repair margin and cash, defer growth.

These are mutually exclusive in their use of the next UGX facility and management time, so `meta-strategic-options-evaluation` can screen them with SAFe.
