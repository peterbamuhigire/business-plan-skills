# Situation scan: PESTEL to key drivers, and five forces with complementors

Parent: [Strategic Factor Analysis](../SKILL.md)

Use this procedure for workflow steps 2 and 3. Its output is the raw candidate list that the weighted tables reduce. Method sources: Johnson, G., Whittington, R., Scholes, K., Angwin, D. and Regnér, P. (2017) *Exploring Strategy*, 11th edn, Pearson (PESTEL with nonmarket analysis, key drivers, five forces with complementors, industry definition); Wheelen, T.L., Hunger, J.D., Hoffman, A.N. and Bamford, C.E. (2018) *Concepts in Strategic Management and Business Policy*, 15th edn, Pearson (natural-environment and climate lens, sixth force, strategic myopia warning).

## 1. PESTEL scan with market and nonmarket tags

| Step | Action | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Scan Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Ecological and Legal factors. Use published sources and at least three outside interviews (customers, suppliers, officials, trade bodies), not managers alone. | Raw factor list with source per line |
| 2 | Tag each factor **market** (customers, rivals, suppliers) or **nonmarket** (regulators, politicians, NGOs, media, communities). | Market/nonmarket column |
| 3 | For each factor, mark whether it is an opportunity, a threat or both, and its likely scale (small, medium, large). | O/T direction and scale |
| 4 | Where the state is a customer, owner, regulator or supplier, place the sector on the political-exposure grid: direct state involvement (low/high) against exposure to civil-society pressure (low/high). | Exposure quadrant and nonmarket actions |
| 5 | Note the economic-cycle position for cyclical sectors (discretionary spend, high fixed costs). Never extrapolate the current growth rate. | Cycle note |
| 6 | Distil three to five **key drivers**: the few factors with high impact on this industry. | Key-driver table |

### Key-driver table template

| Driver ID | Key driver (one line) | Evidence and source | Direction over horizon | Implication for this organisation | Candidate EFAS factor? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | | | | | Yes / No |

Slot sentence: "The single most consequential change in [industry] over the next [n] years is [driver]. For [organisation] it means [implication], so the plan [specific response]."

### East African prompts (all are checks to run, never facts to assert)

- Political and legal: tax changes in the annual Finance Acts, licensing and sector regulators, data-protection registration, local-content rules. Resolve through the Digital research engine and the `country-context/` files; do not quote rates from memory.
- Economic: exchange-rate exposure (UGX, KES, TZS against USD), lending rates, mobile-money penetration, public-sector payment delays.
- Ecological: drought, rainfall variability, power-supply reliability, lake and water levels for fisheries and hydro-dependent users. Cross-link `12-risk-analysis` climate references.
- Social: household cash cycles (harvest, school-fee terms, salary day), urbanisation, language of purchase.
- Nonmarket: "small worlds" of interconnected procurement and investment elites; name the plan's insiders (board members, advisers, local partners).
- Guard against strategic myopia: assign one reviewer to argue the unwelcome signals.

## 2. Five forces plus complementors

### Define the industry first

State the product scope, customer scope, geography and value-chain stage. Treat two activities as separate industries when they differ in more than one force, or differ greatly in one. Too broad ("food") or too narrow ("chapati stands on one street") both mislead.

### Rate each force on named drivers

| Force | Drivers to rate | Rating (H/M/L) | Evidence | Direction in 3-5 years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rivalry | Number and balance of rivals, industry growth, fixed costs, lumpy capacity, exit barriers, low differentiation | | | |
| Threat of entry | Scale and network effects, access to channels and supply, expected retaliation, licences, incumbency (brand, location, raw materials) | | | |
| Substitutes | Price-performance of the substitute, switching cost | | | |
| Buyer power | Buyer concentration, share of buyer's spend, switching cost, buyer information, backward integration; name the **strategic customer** (retailer, funder, government) | | | |
| Supplier power | Concentration, input importance, switching cost; include landlords, labour and finance as suppliers | | | |
| Complementors (sixth force) | Products or partners that raise the value of yours (mobile-money agents, boda-boda delivery networks, bus operators for a ticketing app) | | | |

A High force is a threat to profitability; a Low force is an opportunity. One very adverse force can remove the industry's profit even if the others are benign.

### Rigour gate

- The industry definition is written down.
- Each force is rated on named drivers with evidence.
- The strategic customer is named.
- Complementors are considered.
- A three-to-five-year direction is given for each force.
- Low entry barriers are treated as a warning, not a reason to enter, unless the plan shows how barriers will rise in the entrant's favour.

## 3. Hand-off to the weighted tables

Carry forward fifteen to twenty candidate O and T factors from the key drivers and force ratings, and fifteen to twenty candidate S and W factors from the internal review (functions, capabilities, finances, team, culture). Each candidate keeps its evidence line and source grade:

- Source reliability A (fully reliable) to E (doubtful).
- Item credibility 1 (confirmed by independent data) to 5 (doubtful).

Record the grade as, for example, "B2". Factors with grade D or E, or credibility 4 or 5, may be kept only with a `VERIFY` flag.
