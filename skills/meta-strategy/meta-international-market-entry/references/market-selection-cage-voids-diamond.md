# Market selection: Diamond, CAGE, institutional voids and defender retaliation

Parent: [International Market Entry](../SKILL.md)

Procedures paraphrased from Johnson, G., Whittington, R., Scholes, K., Angwin, D. and Regnér, P. (2017) *Exploring Strategy*, 11th edn, Pearson, chapter 9, which presents Yip's globalisation drivers, Porter's Diamond, Khanna and Palepu's institutional voids, Ghemawat's CAGE distance and the competitive retaliation matrix of MacMillan, van Putter and McGrath. The templates and East African prompts are this engine's own. **Every country statement is a check to run through the Digital research engine and the `country-context/` files, recorded with source and date. None of the prompts below is a fact.**

## 1. Why expand: drivers

| Driver | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Market | Do customers abroad have similar needs? Do regional or global customers want one supplier? Does the marketing transfer? |
| Cost | Are there scale gains, country cost differences, or favourable logistics (value-to-weight ratio)? |
| Government | What trade barriers, regional-bloc rules, standards and liberalisation apply? |
| Competitive | Do rivals already operate across borders and cross-subsidise? |

Small home markets push firms to internationalise. Expansion to escape a declining home market, without a transferable advantage, is a warning sign.

## 2. Porter's Diamond: find the home-based advantage

| Element | Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Factor conditions | Which skills, inputs or infrastructure at home give an edge? | |
| Demand conditions | Have demanding home customers forced higher quality or innovation? | |
| Related and supporting industries | Which local suppliers, clusters or complementors strengthen the firm? | |
| Firm strategy, structure and rivalry | Has domestic competition sharpened the firm? | |

Then run the advantage through VRIO. Only an advantage that is valuable, rare, costly to imitate and organised for is worth exporting.

## 3. Three-layer country screen

### Layer 1: PESTEL country ranking

Rank candidates on political risk (including nationalisation, boycotts, elections), purchasing power and middle-class size, workforce, contract enforcement, IP protection, corruption and security. Use the key-driver method from `meta-strategic-factor-analysis`.

### Layer 2: Institutional voids

Voids are missing market institutions the firm relies on at home.

| Void | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Product markets | Reliable distributors, retail chains, market research, advertising media |
| Labour markets | Recruitment agencies, training institutions, skills certification |
| Capital markets | Bank credit, trade finance, payment and FX systems |
| Regulation and contracts | Predictable licensing, courts, contract enforcement, standards bodies |
| Soft infrastructure | Business services: accountants, lawyers, logistics providers |

High voids favour a local partner, a distributor or a joint venture, or they justify the firm filling the void itself as a service (and pricing that in).

### Layer 3: CAGE distance (specific to this firm)

| Dimension | Prompts | Distance (Low / Medium / High) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cultural | Language of business and purchase, religion, norms, management style | |
| Administrative and political | Shared bloc membership, legal system, colonial and trade ties, institutional voids, political relations | |
| Geographic | Physical distance, road, rail, air and lake corridors, border crossing time, sea access | |
| Economic | Income gap, cost differences, base-of-the-pyramid opportunity, currency | |

Distance is firm-specific: a firm with Swahili-speaking staff and Kenyan distributors is closer to Kenya than one without.

### East African prompts (checks only)

- EAC and AfCFTA provisions for the product: tariff treatment, rules of origin, standards and certification, non-tariff barriers. See the 04 trade references and verify current status.
- Exchange-rate and repatriation arrangements; mobile-money interoperability across borders.
- Registration, local-partner, work-permit and data-protection requirements in the target country.
- Language: English, Swahili, French and Kinyarwanda, Arabic, Lingala as relevant.
- Security and corridor reliability for South Sudan and eastern DRC routes.
- Low-distance bridge: entering through an established home-country trader network, a chamber-of-commerce desk, or diaspora customers.

## 4. Defender retaliation

For each candidate score three things (1 low to 5 high):

| Country | Attractiveness to us | Defender reactiveness (how important the market is to the incumbent) | Defender clout (share, ties to retailers, government, finance) | Net view |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| | | | | |

A market with high attractiveness but a highly reactive, powerful defender may rank below a slightly less attractive, less-defended one. The defender may retaliate in the entrant's home market or another market; plan for it.

## 6. Service-business caution

Services face extra difficulty abroad: regulation of professions, cultural sensitivity, and the need for local presence. An agency or consultancy should plan for a local partner or senior local hire and a longer ramp-up than a product exporter.
