# Entry-mode choice and staging

Parent: [International Market Entry](../SKILL.md)

The entry-mode ladder, the mode trade-off table and the alliance checklist paraphrase Wheelen, T.L., Hunger, J.D., Hoffman, A.N. and Bamford, C.E. (2018) *Concepts in Strategic Management and Business Policy*, 15th edn, Pearson, chapters 6 and 9, and Johnson, G., Whittington, R., Scholes, K., Angwin, D. and Regnér, P. (2017) *Exploring Strategy*, 11th edn, Pearson, chapters 9 and 11. The staged-plan template is this engine's own. Tax, structure and legal questions for any mode go to the Chwezi finance doctrine and legal review.

## 1. Entry modes from low to high commitment

| Mode | Commitment | Control | Risk | Speed | Fits when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct or indirect export (including e-commerce and agents) | Low | Low over marketing | Low | Fast | Demand unproven; product travels; voids manageable |
| Distributor or sales agent | Low | Low to medium | Low | Fast | Distribution is the main void; partner reaches customers |
| Licensing | Low | Low over quality and technology | Low | Fast | Brand or know-how can be protected; never license the distinctive competence |
| Franchising | Low to medium | Medium via operating system | Medium | Fast | A replicable operating system exists; royalty model fits |
| Management contract or build-operate-transfer | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | Public-private or infrastructure projects |
| Joint venture | Medium | Medium, shared | Medium | Medium | Local ownership or relationships are needed; voids high |
| Acquisition | High | High | High | Fast to presence, slow to integrate | A suitable local business exists; integration capability present |
| Greenfield subsidiary | High | High | High | Slow | Proven demand; control of quality or IP essential |

Born-global firms and digital businesses can skip stages; most SMEs move from export or distributor to partnership to presence as evidence grows.

## 2. Choosing the mode

1. List the voids from the market-selection screen and the control the firm must keep (quality, brand, customer data, IP).
2. Shortlist three modes that address the voids.
3. Score each on commitment, control, risk and speed against the objective and budget ceiling.
4. Check the finance and legal questions each mode raises (registration, permanent establishment, withholding, transfer pricing, repatriation) and route them to the finance doctrine; mark them `not assessed` until answered.
5. Choose, and record why the other two lost.

## 3. Partner and alliance checklist

Use for distributors, licensees, franchisees and joint ventures.

- Clear purpose and value for both partners; compatible goals, complementary capabilities.
- Tasks assigned to each partner's strengths; no head-to-head competition in the partnership's scope.
- Partnering risks named at formation; mutual dependence rather than one-sided reliance.
- Incentives for cooperation; agreed monitoring, information sharing and customer-response tracking.
- Cross-cultural competence and exchange of people where the relationship is deep.
- Dispute resolution, renegotiation and an agreed exit written into the contract.
- The distinctive competence is protected; the partner cannot become a competitor with it.
- Stakeholders in both countries accept the arrangement.

## 4. Controlling the foreign operation

- Multi-country, locally adapted operations: looser controls, budgets plus non-financial measures (share, relationships with regulators, staff morale), and separation of the unit's results from the manager's performance where conditions differ.
- Integrated regional operations: tighter central controls with cost and revenue centres.
- Decentralise culture-sensitive functions (marketing, people); centralise finance, IT and product standards.

Every stage names its evidence and its stop rule. Figures for investment and margin come from the model owner, not from this reference.

## 6. Localised illustration (fictional)

A Mbarara dairy processor considers Rwanda. Stage 0 verifies product standards, certification and import permits through the Digital research engine. Stage 1 appoints one Kigali distributor for yoghurt only, with a six-month target of listings in a set number of outlets. Stage 2 considers a chilling-and-distribution joint venture only if stage 1 meets its target. A greenfield plant is not considered within the plan horizon.
