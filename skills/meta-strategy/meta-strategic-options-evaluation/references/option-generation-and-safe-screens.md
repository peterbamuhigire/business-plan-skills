# Option generation and SAFe screens

Parent: [Strategic Options Evaluation](../SKILL.md)

SAFe (suitability, acceptability, feasibility), the screening techniques, break-even market share and funding by life-cycle stage follow Johnson, G., Whittington, R., Scholes, K., Angwin, D. and Regnér, P. (2017) *Exploring Strategy*, 11th edn, Pearson, chapters 8, 11 and 12. The four criteria for alternatives and the "strategies to avoid" list follow Wheelen, T.L., Hunger, J.D., Hoffman, A.N. and Bamford, C.E. (2018) *Concepts in Strategic Management and Business Policy*, 15th edn, Pearson. Tables and examples are this engine's own.

## 1. Gap analysis first

Gap = desired performance − projected performance of the current strategy. A small gap favours improved execution; a large gap justifies new direction or method. State the gap in the plan's own metric (revenue, margin, beneficiaries reached).

## 2. Four sources of options

| Source | Prompt | Example options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TOWS cells | Which SO, ST, WO and WT candidates survived the four criteria? | Forward supply contracts (ST); freshness positioning (SO) |
| Ansoff directions | Penetration, product development, market development, diversification. Brainstorm each quadrant, even to reject it. | Win more supermarket shelf (penetration); drinking yoghurt line (product development); Rwanda (market development) |
| Strategy clock | Which position (low price, hybrid, differentiation, focused differentiation) and path? | Enter at 12 o'clock, move to 1 o'clock |
| Methods | Organic, alliance (distribution, co-branding, consortium), acquisition | Distribution alliance in Kigali instead of own depot |

Rules:

- Always include "improve execution of the current strategy" as an option.
- Penetration is constrained by retaliation in slow-growth markets and by competition law. Under severe constraint, retrenchment to the most valuable segments is a valid option.
- Market development fails when old products are offloaded into a new market; the product must meet the new market's critical success factors.
- Diversification to spread risk is legitimate mainly for owner-managers whose wealth is concentrated in the firm; otherwise require shared costs, shared capabilities or a clear dominant logic.
- For SMEs the real method choice is usually ally or build. Buy suits hard assets; people and brand acquisitions carry valuation and culture risk.

### Buy, ally or build: four factors

| Factor | Buy | Ally | Build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Urgency | Fast | Fast | Slow |
| Uncertainty | Failed purchase can be resold | Losses shared; can include a buy option | Failure is unsaleable |
| Type of capability | Hard assets best | Culture and control issues | Keeps culture consistent |
| Modularity | Must buy the whole company | Only the relevant unit | New unit |

### Strategies to avoid (red-team list)

Follow the leader; hit another home run; arms race for share; do everything; throwing good money after a losing hand. Name any that an option resembles.

## 3. Suitability screens

### 3.1 Ranking matrix (required)

Options as rows; SFAS factors as columns; mark ✓ (option addresses or uses the factor), ✗ (conflicts), ? (unclear). Optional weights from the SFAS.

| Option | F1 | F2 | F3 | F4 | F5 | F6 | F7 | ✓ | ✗ | Grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| A | | | | | | | | | | A / B / C |
| B | | | | | | | | | | |
| C | | | | | | | | | | |
| Current strategy improved | | | | | | | | | | |

Grade A: clearly suitable. B: suitable with conditions. C: unsuitable, drop. The numbers are only as good as the discussion behind them; record the reasons.

### 3.2 Scenario screen

Test each surviving option against two or four named scenarios built from two independent, high-impact, high-uncertainty drivers. Prefer options that survive most scenarios; record contingency triggers for the rest. Avoid three scenarios labelled optimistic, base and pessimistic.

### 3.3 VRIO screen

Does the option rest on a capability that is valuable, rare, costly to imitate and organised for? An option built on parity capabilities earns parity returns.

### 3.4 Life-cycle and position screen

| Position \ Industry stage | Development and growth | Maturity | Decline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strong | Invest aggressively; build share | Defend; harvest weak lines | Harvest or consolidate |
| Middling | Strengthen through alliance or niche | Find a niche; improve cost | Selective exit |
| Weak | Niche or partner | Seek alliance, acquirer or exit | Exit |

## 4. Acceptability: risk, return, reactions

### 4.1 Risk

- **Sensitivity:** vary each key assumption (volume, price, input cost, capacity use, exchange rate) and find the one that breaks the objective. Price erosion is often more damaging than cost inflation.
- **Break-even market share:** fixed costs ÷ unit contribution = break-even units; break-even units ÷ defined market size in units = required share. Compare with the share held by today's leader. A lower required share means a less demanding option.
- **Liquidity:** fast-growing firms fail paying suppliers before customers pay. Check the cash low point per option.
- **Gearing and cover:** lender tests of debt to equity and interest or debt-service cover. Obtain the lender's current thresholds; do not assume them.

### 4.2 Return

Obtain from the model owner: payback, NPV at the agreed discount rate, economic profit (operating profit after tax less a capital charge), and for social or public projects a cost-benefit view. Use staged real-options logic only where the investment is genuinely staged. All valuation mechanics sit with `meta-valuation` and the Chwezi finance doctrine.

### 4.3 Stakeholder reactions

| Stakeholder | Typical concern | Question to answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owners or owner-family | Control, dilution, succession, lifestyle | Does the option change who controls the firm? |
| Lenders | Security, gearing, cover, track record | Will the bank finance it on current terms? |
| Regulators and government | Licences, local content, tax | Does the option need a new permit or trigger a new obligation? |
| Staff | Jobs, workload, skills | Who gains and who loses? |
| Key customers and channels | Channel conflict, price change | Will a distributor or supermarket react against it? |
| Donors (NGOs) | Mission drift, restrictions | Does the option fit the grant terms? |

Four questions per stakeholder: how does the option affect them; how much of what they want do they get; what will they do if they do not get it; how likely are they to do it?

## 5. Feasibility: funds, people, integration

| Test | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| Funds | Amount, timing of need from the cash plan, named source and its stage fit |
| People and skills | Named roles, hires with dates, training, management bandwidth |
| Resource integration | Suppliers, partners, systems and licences ready when needed |

Funding fit by business stage (qualitative):

| Stage | Funding need | Risk | Likely sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start-up | High | High | Owner funds, family, angels, grants, patient capital |
| Growth | High | High | Growth equity, term debt against assets, supplier credit, DFI lines |
| Maturity | Low to medium | Low to medium | Bank debt, retained earnings |
| Decline or turnaround | Low | Medium | Secured debt, asset sales, retained earnings |

## 6. Recording the choice

Option sentence: "We considered [n] options. [Option] is preferred because it is suitable ([SFAS factors it answers]), acceptable ([risk, return and stakeholder evidence]) and feasible ([funds, people, integration]). We rejected [option] because [failed screen and reason]."

Four qualifications to state where they apply: criteria can conflict and still need judgement; the options must be consistent as a package; implementation may reveal new facts, so name review triggers; real decisions are rarely fully systematic, so record the judgement openly.
