# Strategy-statement gate (35 words or fewer)

Parent: [Strategic Options Evaluation](../SKILL.md)

The three-element strategy statement is Collis and Rukstad's, used here as presented in Johnson, G., Whittington, R., Scholes, K., Angwin, D. and Regnér, P. (2017) *Exploring Strategy*, 11th edn, Pearson, chapter 1. Johnson et al. treat the ability to give a clear strategy statement as a test of managerial competence. The gate rules, slot template and examples below are this engine's own.

## 1. The three required elements

| Element | Must state | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective | A measurable end: metric, target and date (for example margin, share, households served) | It is a slogan ("be the leading provider") or has no date |
| Scope | Customer (who), geography (where) and activities done in-house versus by partners (vertical scope) | Any one of the three is missing, or the customer is "everyone" |
| Advantage | Why the firm will win in that scope: the distinctive capability and the benefit it gives | It names a threshold quality ("quality products", "good service", "experienced team") |

## 2. Gate rules

1. Count words in the final sentence or sentences; numbers and hyphenated compounds count as one word each. **More than 35 words fails.**
2. Missing objective, scope (any of customer, geography, activities) or advantage fails.
3. An advantage that rivals already offer fails. Test it with VRIO against named competitors.
4. The statement must match the chosen option in the SAFe record and the SFAS factors it answers.
5. Values and mission language do not belong in the statement; they go to section 02.
6. A failed gate blocks drafting of sections 01, 02 and 07 and the marketing-plan strategy until a passing statement is agreed with the owner.

## 3. Slot template

"By [date], [objective with metric] for [customer scope] in [geography], doing [in-house activities] ourselves [and partnering for (outsourced activities)], winning through [distinctive capability] that gives [customer benefit]."

## 5. Examples (fictional)

**Fails (42 words, no date, threshold advantage):** "Our mission is to become the leading provider of high-quality dairy products in Uganda and beyond, delighting our customers with excellent service and quality products made by our experienced and passionate team, while contributing to the livelihoods of farmers and the community."

**Passes (30 words):** "By 2028, lift gross margin to 26% selling fresh yoghurt to Kampala supermarket shoppers, collecting and processing milk ourselves, winning through the only daily chilled farm-to-shelf network in western Uganda."

**Fails (scope missing activities and geography):** "To reach 5,000 subscribing offices by 2027 through our unique dispenser service." Fix: add where, what the firm does itself, and why the dispenser service is hard to copy.

## 6. Where the statement is used

- Opens section 02 (company overview) and closes section 01 (executive summary).
- Heads the marketing-plan strategy and section 07.
- Is the filter for later sections: any programme that serves none of objective, scope or advantage is questioned.
- Client-intake probe: "In two sentences, who do you serve, where, and why do they choose you over [named rival]?" If the owner cannot answer, the plan's first job is to create the statement.
